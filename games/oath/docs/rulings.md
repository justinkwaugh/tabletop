# Rulings

The code cites the Law of Oath as `R-<section>`. A citation ending in `-H1` or `-H2` names one of the
house rulings below, where the Law is silent or ambiguous. `R-X` names a rule of this implementation.
A ruling marked _provisional_ is a question put to the publisher's community; it stands until answered.

- **R-1.16-H1:** the sites' favor at setup is paid last, after every other payout (R-1.5, R-1.6, R-1.11, R-1.15). When the bank cannot cover every site's printed favor, the Chancellor splits all that it holds among the sites, none above its print, as R-1.16 says ("the Chancellor chooses how to place it"); the split is the Chancellor's own setup choice (R-X.1). A site revealed later in play takes as much of its printed favor as the bank still holds (R-9.3).
- **R-1.19.a-H1:** with no Chronicle behind it, a game's cards in play are 54 denizens, nine of each suit taken at random, and the five Visions: 59 cards, from which R-1.19.a composes the world deck in R-8.8's piles. R-8.4 and R-8.5 add and remove the same number of denizens each Chronicle, so the count holds for a Chronicle's life. A game set up with the retired fixed first-game deck keeps that deck.
- **R-2.2.2-H1:** an adviser that "counts as" more advisers counts so in every count an adviser is part of. An adviser is a denizen card (R-2.2.2) and the cards you rule include your advisers (R-10.21), so Marriage, "two hearth advisers", is two hearth cards you rule for Fabled Feast, as it is two advisers for Trade, the Conspiracy and Military Parade. It still counts as one toward the adviser limit, as its text says. In games created before revision 16 (R-X.4) Fabled Feast counts it once.
- **R-2.11-H1:** the Oathkeeper title is judged after each Law action, and a Campaign's Steps 1–8 (R-5.5) are one: it is judged once the Campaign ends, before any "after … campaign" power, each a new action, against the holder from before the Campaign. Each binding exchange and each Relic Thief take is its own moment, judged even inside a Campaign (Relic Thief's Q&A: "at each moment that relics change hands"). A take is judged at the answer that lands it: the thief's roll, or Jinx's answer when the roll waits on it; a roll that waits on Jinx, or takes nothing, is no moment. Each change is recorded as its own System Action (`TransferOathkeeper`) in games created at the turn-flow revision or later. In games created before revision 12 (R-X.4) the title is judged after every step, so it can change hands mid-Campaign.
- **R-3.3-H1:** the end die is the Chancellor's roll. In games created at the turn-flow revision or later, a round that ends in rounds five to seven with the Chancellor or a Citizen holding the title waits in `EndOfRound` on the Chancellor's `RollEndDie`; until it is rolled, the last seat's turn can be undone.
- **R-3.2-H1:** a tie is not a win: a Vision's "the most" means strictly more than every other player.
- **R-3.2-H2:** False Prophet's warband marks a Vision its player now also has revealed: any revealed Vision, their own included, and beside a Vision of their own either goal met is a win (R-3.2-H1 still applies). "If it is ever discarded" is the Vision: discarding it kills the warband, and its player plays or discards the Vision as if they had searched. With no revealed Vision, or an empty bank (R-9.3), no warband is gained.
- **R-4.1.1-H1:** the People's Favor never drops below one favor during the Wake.
- **R-4.1.4-H1:** an Opportunity site offers only what its own card prints.
- **R-4.3-H1:** in games created at the turn-flow revision or later, the Rest Phase waits on its player only while they hold a Rest power they can use (one with a choice the engine accepts, a bank power needing a bank with favor); otherwise the Rest resolves as a System Action after End the Act Phase.
- **R-4.3.4-H1:** a Supply gain stops at the track's last space; the excess is lost. The Supply not spent that R-4.3.4 adds at the Rest is the Supply marker as it stands then, as on the board: a refresh to full during the turn (Long-Lost Heir, Royal Ambitions, Ballot Box, Bewitch, Martial Culture, Ancient Pact, a Citizenship offer accepted, R-6.7, R-6.8) and a gain move the marker, and only what is spent after them is gone. So a marker at the leftmost space at the Rest stays there. In games created before revision 24 (R-X.4) it is the Supply at the turn's start, plus what gains added, less what was spent this turn, so a refresh to full on your own turn saves nothing.
- **R-5.1.4-H1:** Inquisitor's "you play it" is any play R-5.1.4 allows the Conspiracy, as the card's Q&A answers "If I find the Conspiracy, can I play it facedown to my advisers?" with "Yes!": faceup, with the optional take, and then to the box (R-5.1.4.IV); facedown to the finder's advisers, discarding one first at the adviser limit (R-5.1.4.II), with no take and seen by the table; or discarded.
- **R-5.5.1.a-H1:** a Citizen whom R-5.5.1.a puts outside the Empire for a Campaign still adds the warbands at the site Captains names to their force, and those warbands do not defend it, as Captains' Q&A rules ("do I still add the warbands at the site to my force? A: Yes. They do not get added to the defending force"). Wild Allies prints the same words and has no Q&A of its own, so it is read the same way. Vow of Union keeps its own Q&A: such a Citizen adds no purple warbands from the sites they rule.
- **R-5.5.1.a-H2:** R-5.5.1.a's scope holds for every power from the Campaign's declaration, its Step 1 (Law 5.5.1), to its end, the banish being its Step 7 (R-5.5.7.III). A Citizen it puts outside the Empire does not rule the Empire's sites or the cards there, since Imperial players share them only as Imperial players (R-6.6.3, R-10.28-H1), and is an enemy of the Imperial players (R-10.7: two Imperial players are the only pair who are not). So a persistent power at the Empire's sites is not theirs (Jinx, "If you rule Jinx"; Spell Breaker; Tome Guardians; Boiling Lake; Grasping Vines; Toll Roads, whose toll goes to the Chancellor, R-10.4); an adviser's enemy test counts them as an enemy (Beast Tamer, Ancient Bloodline); they rule no nomad card there for Lost Tongue; Vow of Union does not hold them at a site only the Empire rules; they do not rule an Imperial Narrow Pass; and leaving a Shrouded Wood the Empire rules, its ruler picks where they go (R-11.7). The site Captains or Wild Allies names gives them its warbands for their force only (R-5.5.1.a-H1), not its cards. Powers that trigger "after" a Campaign (Herald, Sneak Attack) come once it has ended, outside its scope. In games created before revision 14 (R-X.4) these powers ignore the scope.
- **R-5.5.2-H1:** the Campaign modifiers ruled by the defender and by every Ally are active (Law 5.5.2: "Activate all the Campaign modifiers (Vow of Peace, etc.) ruled by the defender and all Allies"), so an Ally's Vow of Peace forbids the attacker's sacrifice as the defender's does. An Ally is a player who joined the defending side: the Chancellor when an Imperial player defends, or a Citizen the defender admitted. This is the Law's own step, not a reading of "you": R-10.28-H1 is unchanged. In games created before revision 13 (R-X.4) only the defender's Vow of Peace counts.
- **R-5.5.2-H2:** a Citizen whom R-5.5.1.a puts outside the Empire for a Campaign, and who campaigns from the site Captains or Wild Allies names, need not target that site: Captains' Q&A ("If I am a Citizen and attack another Citizen or the Chancellor, do I still add the warbands at the site to my force? A: Yes. They do not get added to the defending force, and you do not need to target the site") is the card's own answer, read for Wild Allies too, as R-5.5.1.a-H1 reads its force half. Both of Law 5.5.2's demands at "your site" are waived for them: the site itself when the defender rules it, and at least one target there, since with the defender's pawn elsewhere the site would be the only target there and the answer would mean nothing. They still declare at least one target; any site the defender rules, the named site among them, stays open to them, and the defender's pawn and favor, relics and banners only when that pawn is at the named site ("Act as if your pawn is there"). The answer is asked and given for a Citizen, so an Exile or the Chancellor using either card keeps both demands. In games created before revision 14 (R-X.4) both demands bind that Citizen too.
- **R-5.5.3-H1:** defending bandits use the cost-free battle plans at every site they rule, not only at the targeted ones.
- **R-5.5.3-H2:** Hospital catches every kill of its user's warbands while the Campaign is open, whatever the order of that player's plans: a plan's kill (Bear Traps, the bandits' Bear Traps), a sacrifice (Wrestlers, R-10.22) and a kill a card makes during the banish (Boiling Lake, Grasping Vines). Its text reaches "any of your warbands [that] would be killed", and its Q&A holds them until "the end of the campaign"; the banish is the Campaign's Step 7 (R-5.5.7.III). Law 5.5.3 lets a side "use any battle plans they rule, once each", with no order among them, so a player's Hospital holds before any of their plans resolves. Bear Traps' Q&A counts its kill for Cursed Cauldron, so a kill a card makes during the Campaign is a Campaign kill: without Hospital it counts for the enemy's Cauldron, once. In games created before revision 13 (R-X.4) these kills skip Hospital, and the banish's kills do not count for the Cauldron.
- **R-5.5.3-H3:** Code of Honor binds its whole side whatever order the side answers in. Once anyone on the defending side has used another battle plan, no one on it can use Code of Honor; once it is used, no one else on the side uses any. Code of Honor's Q&A asks whether the Chancellor using it as an Imperial Ally to a Citizen defender means "the Citizen defender can't use other battle plans" and answers "Yes. This is an errata from the rules as written", and R-10.28-H1 makes it bind the side; Law 5.5.3 gives the defender and the Allies no order ("the defender and any Allies may use any battle plans they rule"), so the order they answer in cannot decide it. The attacker's plans are the other side's and do not count. Peace Envoy's "You cannot use other battle plans" binds its user alone (R-10.28-H1). In games created before revision 13 (R-X.4) an Ally's Code of Honor is accepted after another player on its side has used a battle plan.
- **R-5.5.3-H4:** the bandits' compelled plans pass the same bans and added costs as a player's. The bandits are the attacker's enemy (R-10.29; Tome Guardians' Q&A: "the bandits consider all players enemies"), and a "cannot" beats R-5.5.3's "the bandits use all battle plans they rule that have no cost" (R-9.2): a plan a persistent power forbids against its holder is not used (True Names, Beast Tamer). A plan an enemy's card gives an added cost is no longer a plan with no cost, and the bandits could not pay it anyway (R-10.3-H1): it is not used either (Gleaming Armor, Insect Swarm). Bans and added costs are applied before Code of Honor stands alone, so a banned Code of Honor leaves the bandits their other plans. In games created before revision 14 (R-X.4) the bandits use every cost-free plan they rule whatever these cards say.
- **R-5.5.4-H1:** the defender's after-roll powers are settled before the attack is rolled. Law 5.5 makes Step 4, Roll Defense ("The defender rolls their defense pool"), come before Step 5, Roll Attack, and Jinx is used "after you roll [attackDie] or [defenseDie]", so a defender who rules Jinx decides on their reroll with only the defense on the table; the attack is rolled once they have answered, and the attacker's Jinx is offered after it, as before. An Ally rolls nothing (Law 5.5.4; "you" is the user alone, R-10.28-H1), so an Ally who rules Jinx is not offered a reroll of the Campaign's dice. In games created before revision 14 (R-X.4) both rolls are made first, and the attacker and then the defender are asked.
- **R-5.5.5-H1:** the attacker chooses which of their own warbands die, from their board or a site in their force: the skulls' kills, the sacrifice and the defeated half, each picked after the roll. The skulls' pick is asked only when it can matter ("For each skull attacker immediately kills one warband in their force"): a skull kills, the force stands in more than one place (Captains, Wild Allies, Vow of Union; the bandits are never in it) and the skulls leave some of it alive. The attacker then names how many die in each place, exactly the skulls; otherwise they die from the board first, then the sites. In games created before revision 7 (R-X.4) the skulls' kills follow an order declared with the Campaign, before the roll.
- **R-5.5.5.b-H1:** "you're victorious now" (Hearts and Minds, Peace Envoy) decides the Campaign at Step 3: Steps 4 and 5 are skipped outright, and the other side is defeated. No dice are rolled, so there is no comparison and no sacrifice; the enemy is defeated in R-5.5.6's full sense and kills half their force, and Steps 6 to 8 then run as printed. With no roll the Campaign stays undoable (R-X.3), uniquely among Campaigns: the step that decides it reveals nothing once no defending plans are left to wait on. In games created before revision 17 (R-X.4) that step reveals, as every Campaign step did.
- **R-5.5.6-H1:** in games created at the turn-flow revision or later, a defeated defending side is asked to choose its losses only when the choice can change the outcome. Otherwise the engine kills the default and the Campaign moves on, since every survivor goes to its board (R-5.5.6). From revision 7 the choice changes the outcome when the dead go to more than one bank (the force mixes owners) or the survivors to more than one board, so a lone Exile or the Chancellor is not asked because part of their force stands on their own board. From revision 13 it also changes the outcome when Hospital would hold the dead of one part of the force and not another's, or for another player (R-10.28-H2): a Citizen's targeted site and their Chancellor Ally's board, with either of them using Hospital. Before revision 7 (R-X.4), any part of the force on a board made the engine ask.
- **R-5.5.6-H2:** a defeated attacker moves the survivors at the sites in their force (the site Captains or Wild Allies names, Vow of Union's sites) to their own board, whatever their colour, as R-5.5.6 reads: "They move all the other warbands in their force to their board." R-5.5.7.I's placement is for targeted sites after a victory, and a force site is not targeted (Captains' Q&A: "you do not need to target the site"); R-10.13 sends the Empire's warbands to the Chancellor only when they are killed. So a defeated Citizen's surviving Imperial warbands there go to the Citizen's board. With every survivor on one board, the attacker's choice of which warbands die changes something only when their force mixes owners. In games created before revision 7 (R-X.4) those warbands went to the Chancellor's board.
- **R-5.5.7.II-H1:** Relic Hunter's "You may put any relics you take on the bottom of the relic deck" reaches every relic its user takes at Law 5.5.7's "Take All Targeted Relics and Banners": a facedown relic at a targeted site and a relic taken from the defender's board alike, each kept or sent down as its user chooses. It applies only when the attacker used Relic Hunter as a battle plan in that Campaign, and only to relics taken in it; a banner is not a relic and is always seized. A relic sent down leaves the defender as one taken does, and goes to the bottom of the relic deck instead of the attacker's board; an Obsidian Cage sent down returns its warbands as R-10.13-H1 rules, the attacker placing the Empire's. Relic Thief's Q&A counts a relic taken from its holder in a Campaign as taken, but a relic sent down is not there to take: Relic Thief is offered only the relics the attacker kept.
- **R-5.5.8-H1:** the battle plans discarded at a Campaign's end (R-5.5.8) follow the Glossary's "Discard" card by card: a plan at a site leaves from that site's region, an adviser from its holder's pawn region, and the secrets on them go to the attacker, who orders the cards for each pile. Wild Mounts' substitute card (R-7.5-H1) is discarded the same way.
- **R-6.4-H1:** the Grand Scepter's holder knows every Reliquary relic, since they may peek at will: from setup, and at once when the Scepter is given to them (its Q&A: given is not taken); a holder who took it learns them once the card's lock lifts ("You cannot use this if you took it on this turn"), when the turn it was taken on ends, which is the next turn of the game and may be another player's; a former holder keeps what they saw. In games created before revision 12 (R-X.4) a holder who took it learns them at once.
- **R-6.7-H1:** when an Exile's own warbands run short of the Imperial ones they replace on their board (R-6.7, R-6.8, R-9.3), the purple left over goes back to the Chancellor's bank, as R-6.6.2 removes a new Citizen's leftovers. In games created before revision 5 (R-X.4) it stays on the board as Imperial.
- **R-6.8-H1:** while nobody holds the Grand Scepter, which Relic Hunter may put on the bottom of the relic deck ("any relics you take"; only Fae Merchant excepts the Scepter), Self-Exile is refused: Law 6.8 gives its favor "to the player who holds the Grand Scepter", and a Give needs a player to receive it (Law 10.11), so no other payee or a free Self-Exile is inferred (R-9.1). The holder's powers have no user either: nobody peeks in the Imperial Reliquary (R-6.4), offers Citizenship (R-6.6.1) or exiles a Citizen (R-6.7) until the Scepter is drawn and held again.
- **R-7-H1:** a card's standing effect lasts while the card is in play; only the components it moved (favor burned, warbands killed, tokens placed) outlive it, since they record it themselves. Salt the Earth's "this site's capacity is now 1" holds while the card is at the site, which within a game is to the game's end: the card is locked (R-7.2.2).
- **R-7.1.1-H1:** a relic in a player's bank is in reach the way an adviser is; a relic is held, never ruled.
- **R-7.1.1-H2:** Small Friends ("Act as if your pawn is at any site with a [beast] card.") names one faceup site with a Beast card when it is declared for a Trade, and the Trade acts as if the pawn is there instead of at its own site. The Trade is made with a card at that site, so the pawn's own site is no Trade site unless it is the one named; the modifiers in reach are that site's cards and the cards the player holds or rules (advisers, relics, the cards at sites they rule), not the pawn's own site's. A Map Library at the named site counts the pawn as there, and its region is the named site's. Small Friends itself is used from where the pawn really stands, since access to a modifier is judged when it is declared (Law 7.4, 7.4.1): a Small Friends lying at the pawn's site may name another Beast site, and only the other declared cards are judged as if the pawn were there. Undeclared, Small Friends reaches nothing; with no faceup site holding a Beast card there is none to name, and it cannot be declared. Its Q&A: "Can I use Trade modifiers at the site my pawn is physically at, in addition to the site I am acting as if I am at? Nope! Only the new site." From revision 19 (R-X.4); before it, holding the card reached every Beast site at once, declared or not, and the pawn's own site stayed a Trade site with its cards in reach.
- **R-7.1.2.a-H1:** a card's occupancy bar is checked once, before the cost is paid.
- **R-7.1.3-H1:** Obsidian Cage's "your enemy's force" is the force as R-5.5.6 finds it, the survivors at the targeted sites and on the boards alike, as Sticky Fire reads the same words; bandits are never moved (R-7.6.5). Its Action costs nothing and moves at least one warband, an Exile's to their own board and the Empire's to any Imperial board (R-6.6.3), split as its holder chooses.
- **R-7.1.3-H2:** Gambling Hall ("Roll 4[defenseDie] and take X[favor] equal to the total [shield] result from any one favor bank") is used with no bank: the dice are rolled, any Jinx reroll is answered first (Jinx's Q&A lets it reroll these dice), and then the roller chooses the bank. The total counts 2x faces as multiplying, as in a Campaign (the card's Q&A). The roller takes the total, or what the chosen bank holds if less (R-9.3), from one bank that holds favor; a bank with none is not offered. With one bank holding favor it is taken from with no question; with none, or a total of 0, nothing is gained. A Vow of Kinship holder's favor goes to the Nomad bank as any gain does (R-10.10-H1). From revision 21 (R-X.4); before it the bank was named at the use, before the roll.
- **R-7.1.4-H1:** an always-on power without the persistent braid is continuous and binds only its holder: faceup advisers and held relics.
- **R-7.1.4-H2:** a persistent power's "you may" is a trigger: its holder is asked, and the interrupted turn waits.
- **R-7.1.4-H3:** Sneak Attack ("After another player's campaign, you may campaign") is offered once at the end of each Campaign another player makes, a free Campaign from Second Wind included, and only when a legal Campaign against that player exists (R-5.5.1, R-5.5.2). Inside the Sneak Attack's Campaign, out of turn, "end your Act Phase" (Martial Culture) ends nothing, and Second Wind's free Travel and Campaign are kept as R-X.4 describes for revision 7.
- **R-7.2.2-H1** _(provisional)_: Pilgrimage leaves a locked denizen at the site, and it does not count toward the number drawn.
- **R-7.3.3-H1:** Bewitch's "have more [secret] (even on cards) than the Chancellor" counts the secrets on a player's board and on every card that is theirs: their faceup advisers, the relics they hold, and the cards at the sites they rule (R-10.21). The Chancellor's are counted the same way. A token on a card has no owner; Self-Exile's count (R-6.8) takes the secrets on every denizen, relic and edifice card because its text names no owner, and Bewitch's "you have" names one, so it takes the cards that are theirs. In games created before revision 8 (R-X.4) only the secrets on faceup advisers count.
- **R-7.3.3-H2:** Saddle Makers ("After another player plays a [nomad] or [order] card, you gain [favor][favor] from the matching favor bank") pays after the play's site favor and Homeland payout, which R-7.3.3 resolves first, and before the played card's When Played power, where its Q&A places it. The order shows nowhere, since the Nomad and Order Homelands pay a secret and warbands, not favor. In games created before revision 4 (R-X.4) it pays after the When Played power.
- **R-7.4-H1:** Master of Disguise ("Act as if you had another player's advisers instead.") gives its user the named player's faceup advisers in place of their own for the whole Trade it modifies (its Q&A: "you fully replace your advisers with another player's advisers for the current Trade action"). The continuous advisers that bind that Trade are the lent ones, so the user's own Vow of Poverty and Vow of Kinship do not bind it ("Can I use this to get around Vow of Poverty or Vow of Kinship? Yes."), and the lender's do. Under the user's own Vow of Kinship, the favor the Trade gains (the Trade for favor, Careless, Secret Signal) goes to their board, and what it pays (a Trade for secrets' two favor, its modifiers' costs, Curfew's toll) comes from their board alone; under the lender's, the gains go to the Nomad bank and the payments may come from it. Favor so gained stays on the Vow holder's board, which they still use with the bank (R-10.10-H1). The user's relics still bind, and outside that Trade nothing changes. A Trade modifier that asks about "your" advisers reads the lent ones too, as the Trade's matching count does (Marriage is the lender's two Hearth advisers): The Old Oak's "if you have any [beast] advisers" is met by the lender's faceup Beast adviser, not the user's own, and Cup of Plenty's "a card that matches any of your advisers" by a lender's faceup adviser of the traded card's suit, not the user's own. For Vow of Kinship, The Old Oak and Cup of Plenty from revision 23 (R-X.4); before it, the user's own Vow of Kinship bound the Trade and the lender's did not, and The Old Oak and Cup of Plenty read the user's own advisers.
- **R-7.4.3-H1:** R-7.4.3's "a modifier cannot be discarded during the major action it is modifying" bars discards imposed from outside a modifier's own resolution. A discard the card prints for itself fires on its schedule: the "At end, discard" battle plans at Step 8 (R-5.5.8), and Mercenaries and Traveling Doctor at Step 6 when their side is defeated.
- **R-7.5-H1:** Wild Mounts' "any one beast card you rule" cannot be a locked card (R-7.2.2). The bandits take it as its Q&A rules: if they would discard two or more Nomad battle plans, they discard instead the first Beast card they rule, searching site by site from the top of the Cradle down through the regions and passing over a locked one. Nobody is asked.
- **R-7.5.4-H1:** a battle plan's ± is by side, adding for the attacker and subtracting for the defender.
- **R-7.6.1-H1:** Martial Culture's "you may become a Citizen" is the choice to use the plan: its player declares it with their other battle plans before the roll, as Sticky Fire's Q&A reads its own "may" as "if you choose to". Using it costs nothing and does nothing else; an Exile who uses it and defeats another Exile becomes a Citizen (R-7.6.1) with the other plans' outcomes, before Second Wind, the order its Q&A sets.
- **R-7.6.3-H1:** a card's binding exchange enforces what changes hands at once, not promises of later actions, and has no manual override. From revision 28 the Tribunal's exchange may also carry a written promise for each side, which the engine records and enforces none of; a promise alone is something exchanged. A promise is 1 to 200 characters (UTF-16 units), with no space at either end and no control character but the line break. Only the offer's two players read its promises until it is accepted; the accept opens a public deal between them, so it cannot be undone. A player is party to at most five open deals, either side counted: an offer with a promise is refused when either player already has five, when it is made and again when it is accepted. Other exchanges carry no promise. Either party to an open deal may, as a minor action in their own Act Phase, claim it kept, which asks the other party to confirm: kept closes the deal, not kept leaves it open. A deal is claimed at most once in a turn of the game (its round and whose turn it is), by either party, so the other party may claim it in their own turn. Either party may instead leave it unconfirmed on their own turn, asking no one, which closes it for both; the record keeps the deal with both promises. When the game ends, the deals still open are listed in the ending action's record and dropped.
- **R-7.6.3-H2:** at The Gathering, proposals come after the pawns have moved, one per present player in turn order.
- **R-7.6.3-H3:** The Gathering played to another site (the People's Favor, R-5.1.4.I) gathers at the site it is on, and its own player is asked to join there too, in turn order from the Chancellor like the others: its text reads "any players in turn order may put their pawn on this site", and nothing leaves its player out. A player whose pawn is already there is not asked. In games created before revision 16 (R-X.4) it gathers at its player's site, and its player is never asked.
- **R-7.6.5-H1:** the Bandit Crown's bandits are its holder's warbands except at sites an enemy's warbands rule: the holder rules every faceup site holding no warbands, with the cards and battle plans there, and the bandits at a targeted site join the defending force when the holder is the defender or an Ally. They count toward R-5.5.6's half, but only warbands die; they are never killed, moved or sacrificed, and never attack. For a Chancellor or Citizen holder they are Imperial warbands, as the card's Q&A says, so the Empire rules those sites (R-6.6.3); in games created before revision 5 (R-X.4) they are the holder's alone.
- **R-7.6.5-H2:** the Grand Mask's "Imperial site" is a faceup site the Empire rules: one holding purple warbands, or an empty site an Imperial holder's Bandit Crown holds (R-7.6.5-H1; from revision 5, R-X.4). During the Exile wearer's turn they rule the cards there, except the battle plans, which the Imperial players keep.
- **R-9.2-H1:** "ignore the powers of sites" (Portal, Forest Paths) reaches every site power that bears on that travel, the destination's, the origin's and the Narrow Pass's, as Forest Paths' Q&A rules ("It lets you get around things like the Narrow Pass"), but not the powers of cards at either site, so Toll Roads still charges (Portal's and Forest Paths' Q&As). A class-wide ignore names every rule in its class, so Portal and Forest Paths enter The Hidden Place without flipping a secret. Where a card's own Q&A rules on a site, it stands over this reading (R-11.7-H4).

One its holder cannot take, with no site to travel to or no legal defender where the pawn then stands, is skipped without asking. There is one held turn: a step out of turn taken while it is held adds what waits behind it to the turn's queue, so held turns never stack. Before it, the grant was lost, since its player had no Act Phase to use it in. Revision 7 also lets one use of Witch's Bargain deal with several players at its user's site, as its Q&A rules, each named once with the secrets and the pairs of favor given them, the totals held together against what the user holds once the cost is paid (R-7.1.2), so the secret the cost places cannot be given; before it, a use dealt with one player, and that secret could be given, leaving its user fewer than none. Revision 7 also asks the holder of the Dragonskin Drum, when a power moves their pawn (the Whistle, Palanquin, a banish, a Shrouded Wood ruler's pick, Brass Horse), whether to gain its one warband, as its Q&A rules a player made to travel does: once the pawn has arrived, after any Toll Roads answer that let the move happen, and not when the bank that would give the warband is empty (the Empire's for a Citizen). Before it, a move a power made gave the Drum's holder nothing. Revision 8 holds Salad Days to R-7.1.3: while three banks hold favor, each bank it names must hold some, and while fewer do, it names every one that does, an empty bank making up the three. Before it, an empty bank could be named while another held favor. Revision 8 also refuses Forgotten Vault's burn at the Darkest Secret's last secret ("not last"); before it, the burn was accepted, spent the Action and burned nothing. Revision 8 also counts, for Bewitch, the secrets on every card that is its player's, and on every card that is the Chancellor's (R-7.3.3-H1); before it, only those on each one's faceup advisers. Revision 8 also has Captains and Wild Allies name only a site where a Campaign from there has a defender (R-5.5.1: another player who rules it or whose pawn is there, or the bandits where no player rules it, with a target they can declare), and no site while a power forbids their user to campaign (Vow of Peace), judged once the card's cost is paid, so Wild Allies' secret is not counted on for The Hidden Place's flip; before it, any site they reach could be named, and the free Campaign from one with no defender could only be given up. Revision 9 holds Ring of Devotion's holder to "You cannot place warbands at sites" in a Deed Writer exchange, as its Q&A rules for every case ("All of them!"): they cannot take a site, since the new ruler moves warbands in from their board, at least one without the Bandit Crown (Deed Writer's Q&A); with the Crown they may take one and move in none. Giving a site away places nothing, so they still may. Before it, the Ring's holder moved warbands onto a site they took. Revision 10 counts Animal Host among the Beast cards "at any sites" only when it is played to a site, and Fabled Feast among "the Hearth cards you rule" only when it is ruled, as an adviser or at a site its player rules; a Citizen rules a site the Empire's warbands hold (R-6.6.3). Before it, Animal Host counted itself when played to the advisers too, and Fabled Feast when played to a site its player did not rule. Revision 10 also counts Riots played to a site among the cards on the map whose most common suit it discards, as its Q&A rules ("count and discard cards at all sites"; "other" spares Riots alone from the discard), both when the play is checked and when it resolves; played to the advisers it is neither counted nor discarded. Both count the map Riots resolves on: a card discarded before the site play (the People's Favor, the Great Slum, Crop Rotation) is not counted, Land Warden's kept card played to a site before Riots as the second card is, and a suit named that is not among the most common on that map is refused. Before it, Riots left itself out of the count wherever it was played, and its check counted a card discarded first that its resolve did not. Revision 10 also leaves two cards at one site where they are when a power swaps them (R-10.25: each goes to the other's place, which is its own), so Great Herd played to a site and swapped with a Nomad card at that site changes nothing, as the card's text allows the pick ("a Nomad card at any site"). Before it, the site was left holding Great Herd twice, and the other card was lost. Revision 10 also leaves its holder's own advisers out of Enchantress's targets while it is held as an adviser, and refuses one sent: swapped among one player's advisers, nothing moves, and the use would only burn its secret. Used from a site it still offers them, since that swap moves both cards. Before it, the holder's own advisers were offered, and the swap left the holder with Enchantress twice and lost the other card. Revision 11 counts Pied Piper toward its holder's advisers once held, as its Q&A rules ("it does not prompt a discard when you play it, but it still counts toward your total number of advisers"): played faceup, or moved by its Action to another player, it prompts no discard, and the next adviser played discards down to the limit (R-7.6.4). So Family Wagon played after it keeps Pied Piper or one other non-Nomad, as the Wagon's Q&A rules, and a holder of Pied Piper and two others keeps a fourth adviser from The Gathering only by a discard. Before it, Pied Piper never counted, held or arriving, so its holder kept a fourth adviser. Revision 11 also settles the adviser limit after a swap (Enchantress, Twin Brother, Horned Mask), as Enchantress's Q&A rules ("They will need to discard a card from their advisers, but not the Enchantress") and R-7.6.4 does for a changed limit: each player whose advisers the swap changed and who is now over their limit is asked, in turn order from the swap's user, to discard down to it. A card the swap gave them is never one of the discards, and a Pied Piper it gave them is not counted on its arrival. Each discard must bring the player nearer the limit in its turn: one that lowers the excess, or a limiter such as Family Wagon after which the limit can still be met (a Nomad under the Wagon goes only once the Wagon has gone), and the question asks for the fewest discards that meet it. The discards go to the pile of the holder's region, and the secrets on them to the swap's user (R-10.5); a facedown adviser discarded is not named. Before it, a swap asked nothing, so Twin Brother or Enchantress bringing Family Wagon, or Enchantress taking the place of Pied Piper, left a player over the limit. Revision 11 also settles the limit after an exchange (R-10.8: "You may Give to the prompted player and vice versa", so both gives are one exchange, judged on its result): an exchange that leaves a party over their limit is accepted, and each party whose advisers it changed and who is now over is asked to discard down, as above, never a card the exchange gave them, faceup or facedown. That covers a fourth adviser received, Family Wagon received over two non-Nomads, and Family Wagon given away so that the giver's Nomads count. Each party discards as the acting player of their own discard, as a player does who discards to play an adviser (R-5.1.4.II), so the secrets on it come back to them (R-10.5); they are asked in turn order from the proposer, before any question already waiting (The Gathering's next proposal). A Pied Piper the exchange gives is not counted on its arrival: "This card ignores the adviser limit", and R-9.2 lets that supersede the limit however it arrives; held, it counts. Before revision 11 the adviser limit was judged on each side alone, against the receiver's advisers before the exchange; at every revision a side so refused is now accepted when the receiver ends within the limit on the result (a one-for-one exchange at the limit, Family Wagon given away for a non-Nomad), which only widens what is accepted, and what was accepted stays accepted, so before revision 11 Family Wagon received over two non-Nomads still asks nothing. Revision 11 also moves a Deed Writer exchange's sites in the card's order across both sides ("Old ruler moves warbands to board, and new ruler moves warbands from board"): every site handed over sends its old ruler's warbands home, and only then does each new ruler move in from their board, so a player whose only warbands stand on the site they give can take the other player's site, whoever proposed; the terms are judged on that same board, and a receiver still short after their own sites come home is refused. Before it each side's sites moved in turn, the proposer's first, and each receiver's board was judged as it stood; the order is gated because a Citizen's board holds their own warbands and the Empire's, and warbands coming home first can change which of them the board holds most of, and so which a recorded exchange moved in. Revision 24 reads R-4.3.4's Supply not spent from the Supply marker at the Rest (R-4.3.4-H1), so a refresh to full on your own turn is saved; before it the Rest read the turn's Supply less what it spent, and a gain was added to that turn's Supply as it moved the marker.
