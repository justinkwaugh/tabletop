# Rulings

The code cites the Law of Oath as `R-<section>`. A citation ending in `-H1` or `-H2` names one of the
house rulings below, where the Law is silent or ambiguous. `R-X` names a rule of this implementation.
A ruling marked *provisional* is a question put to the publisher's community; it stands until answered.

- **R-2.11-H1:** the Oathkeeper title is re-evaluated after every action, so it can change hands mid-Campaign. Each change is recorded as its own System Action (`TransferOathkeeper`) in games created at the turn-flow revision or later.
- **R-3.3-H1:** the end die is the Chancellor's roll. In games created at the turn-flow revision or later, a round that ends in rounds five to seven with the Chancellor or a Citizen holding the title waits in `EndOfRound` on the Chancellor's `RollEndDie`; until it is rolled, the last seat's turn can be undone.
- **R-3.2-H1:** a tie is not a win: a Vision's "the most" means strictly more than every other player.
- **R-4.1.1-H1:** the People's Favor never drops below one favor during the Wake.
- **R-4.1.4-H1:** an Opportunity site offers only what its own card prints.
- **R-4.3-H1:** in games created at the turn-flow revision or later, the Rest Phase waits on its player only while they hold a Rest power they can use (one with a choice the engine accepts, a bank power needing a bank with favor); otherwise the Rest resolves as a System Action after End the Act Phase.
- **R-4.3.4-H1:** a Supply gain stops at the track's last space; the excess is lost.
- **R-5.5.3-H1:** defending bandits use the cost-free battle plans at every site they rule, not only at the targeted ones.
- **R-5.5.5-H1:** the attacker chooses which of their own warbands die, from their board or a site in their force: the skulls' kills in an order declared with the Campaign, the sacrifice and the defeated half picked after the roll.
- **R-5.5.6-H1:** in games created at the turn-flow revision or later, a defeated defending side is asked to choose its losses only when the choice can change the outcome: the force mixes owners, or part of it stands on a board. Otherwise the engine kills the default and the Campaign moves on, since every survivor goes to its board (R-5.5.6).
- **R-6.4-H1:** the Grand Scepter's holder knows every Reliquary relic, since they may peek at will: from setup, and at once when the Scepter changes hands; a former holder keeps what they saw.
- **R-6.7-H1:** when an Exile's own warbands run short of the Imperial ones they replace on their board (R-6.7, R-6.8, R-9.3), the purple left over goes back to the Chancellor's bank, as R-6.6.2 removes a new Citizen's leftovers. In games created before revision 5 (R-X.4) it stays on the board as Imperial.
- **R-7.1.1-H1:** a relic in a player's bank is in reach the way an adviser is; a relic is held, never ruled.
- **R-7.1.2.a-H1:** a card's occupancy bar is checked once, before the cost is paid.
- **R-7.1.4-H1:** an always-on power without the persistent braid is continuous and binds only its holder: faceup advisers and held relics.
- **R-7.1.4-H2:** a persistent power's "you may" is a trigger: its holder is asked, and the interrupted turn waits.
- **R-7.2.2-H1** *(provisional)*: Pilgrimage leaves a locked denizen at the site, and it does not count toward the number drawn.
- **R-7.5-H1:** Wild Mounts' "any one beast card you rule" cannot be a locked card (R-7.2.2). The bandits take it as its Q&A rules: if they would discard two or more Nomad battle plans, they discard instead the first Beast card they rule, searching site by site from the top of the Cradle down through the regions and passing over a locked one. Nobody is asked.
- **R-7.5.4-H1:** a battle plan's ± is by side, adding for the attacker and subtracting for the defender.
- **R-7.6.3-H1:** a card's binding exchange enforces what changes hands at once, not promises of later actions, and has no manual override.
- **R-7.6.3-H2:** at The Gathering, proposals come after the pawns have moved, one per present player in turn order.
- **R-9.4-H1:** a player may let another peek at any time, except while the game waits on that player's own decision.
- **R-10.2-H1** *(provisional)*: simultaneous triggers resolve clockwise from the acting player.
- **R-10.3-H1:** favor a power would move to the bandits is burned (R-10.4): the bandits have no board or bank to gain it. Military Parade and Battle Honors used by victorious bandits take their favor out of the banks to the supply; no player burns it, so Vow of Renewal does not take it.
- **R-10.4-H1:** a trigger on "any player" doing something fires only when a player performs it. R-2.5.3's Seize penalty is burned by the banner itself ("the People's Favor burns two favor"), so in games created at revision 5 or later Vow of Renewal's "whenever any player burns" does not take it. The Vow still takes every burn a player performs: costs, Recover's burns, a victorious Campaign's half of the defender's favor, Slander, Revelation and a toll a player pays toward the bandits.
- **R-10.5-H1** *(provisional)*: favor and secrets on the denizens Pilgrimage moves return as on a discard, favor to the matching bank and secrets to the acting player.
- **R-10.11-H1:** a banner given, by Citizenship terms or Ancient Pact, changes hands and nothing else happens: R-2.5.3's penalty is for a banner taken, per the banners' Q&A.
- **R-10.28-H1:** "you" is the user alone, as R-10.28 defines it: Imperial players share the sites the Empire rules and the cards there (R-6.6.3), and nothing else they hold. Three cards follow their own Q&A instead: Fire Talkers counts the Darkest Secret held by any Imperial player when its user is Imperial, and Code of Honor and Specialist each bind the whole side.
- **R-11.2-H1:** a relic taken through a Homeland site is taken as a Recover takes it, so "after a player takes relics" powers fire.
- **R-X.1:** every choice arrives as explicit input from the player it belongs to: another player's permission, a Citizen's joining a defence and a defeated defending side's losses are each that player's own answer to a request; the engine never infers one.
- **R-X.3:** undo stops at an action that revealed information: (a) it advanced the random stream, (b) it moved a card into or out of the vault, or (c) it showed a player a card they had not seen (a faceup play or flip, a Dream Thief swap, a facedown adviser exchanged).
- **R-X.4:** a game keeps the rules of the revision it was created under (`oathRevision`, set at initialization). A state without one was created before the first revision and keeps that flow to its end, so its stored actions replay unchanged. Revision 1 is the turn flow. Revision 2 counts the secret a Trade for favor places in the total of what the Trade costs (R-7.1.2), so a Trade the player cannot pay in full is refused; before it, a Master of Disguise Trade for favor with one secret was accepted and left the player at minus one. Revision 2 also applies Search modifiers to a facedown adviser's play (R-6.1: "Restrictions and modifiers apply"): the holder's mandatory ones that act on the play (Book of Records on a site play) and any declared with it that act on the play itself; one that changes a Search's draw, cost or pile is refused, since this play draws nothing, and Welcoming Party is refused by its own text. Before it, such a play took no modifier. Revision 3 charges Forced Labor's toll on a facedown adviser's play or discard, as its Q&A rules (R-6.1 is "as if you searched"): the favor is listed with the play, totalled with its modifiers and the Conspiracy's secret, and without a favor to give the play is refused. Revision 3 also gives Land Warden's second card the Search's carried modifiers, so Book of Records gains a secret, not favor, for each card played to a site. Revision 3 also totals a side's battle-plan costs (R-7.1.2): the plans are paid in the order declared from one holding, each printed cost and then any cost an enemy's card adds (Gleaming Armor, Insect Swarm), after the secret The Hidden Place flips, and a declaration that holding cannot pay is refused. A favor burned while its payer holds Vow of Renewal comes straight back to them and pays the next plan (its FAQ makes a burn free for its holder). And with Land Warden, Wild Cry and Welcoming Party look at both cards played and pay once if either qualifies (R-7.4.2, "only once"). Before it, a facedown play paid no toll, the second card took no modifier, each battle plan was checked alone, and Wild Cry and Welcoming Party looked at the kept card only. Revision 4 holds Vow of Silence and Vow of Renewal to Recover (R-10.23: a Seize is any other take), so their holder may target the banner the vow names in a Campaign or take it with the Conspiracy; and Vow of Silence's holder gains the secrets placed on the Darkest Secret, Magician's Code's two included. Before it, the vows refused every take of their banner, and Vow of Silence counted only the secrets paid. Revision 4 also lets a "spend no Supply" power ignore the cost and any increase to it, whichever is declared first (R-7.6.2): Tents, A Fast Steed, Special Envoy, Portal, Forest Paths and a Buried Giant flip take a Decadent Chancellor to the Hinterland for nothing. Before it, Decadent's +1 was added after them, so each cost 1. Revision 4 also pays Saddle Makers once the card is played, before its When Played power, as its Q&A rules; before it, the When Played power resolved first. Revision 4 also starts Revelation's round with the Chancellor, as its Q&A rules; before it, the round started with the player of the card. Revision 4 also takes Mushrooms' one card as the count other Search modifiers add to, so Mushrooms with Augury draws two in either order declared, as their Q&As rule; before it, Augury declared first drew one. Revision 4 also counts, for Cursed Cauldron, every enemy warband killed in the Campaign, as its Q&A rules: the skulls' kills, sacrifices (Wrestlers' too), Bear Traps' and R-5.5.6's, but not the warbands Hospital saves (those it holds for a site its user no longer rules are killed, and count); before it, the Cauldron counted R-5.5.6's kills alone, Hospital's saved ones included. Revision 4 also runs Military Parade and Battle Honors for victorious bandits, burning the favor (R-10.3-H1); before it, they did nothing for the bandits. Revision 4 also has the bandits take Wild Mounts' swap (R-7.5-H1), and defeated bandits who used Traveling Doctor resolve it (R-5.5.6), so it is discarded; they have no warbands for its "kill no warbands" to spare. Before it, the bandits discarded their Nomad battle plans as printed, and a defeat left Traveling Doctor at their site. Revision 5 returns the Imperial warbands an Exile's own cannot replace to the Chancellor's bank (R-6.7-H1); before it, they stayed on the Exile's board. Revision 5 also asks The Gathering's two rounds, the pawns and then the proposals (R-7.6.3-H2), in turn order from the Chancellor, as its Q&A rules. Revision 5 also refuses a faceup locked adviser in an exchange (R-7.2.2), and a site exchanged to a player who already rules it (Deed Writer's Q&A: a Citizen and the Chancellor "both rule them already"). And Military Parade counts Marriage as two hearth advisers (R-5.1.4.IV). Before it, The Gathering asked from its player, a locked adviser changed hands, a Citizen and the Chancellor could exchange a site, and Military Parade paid one favor for Marriage. Revision 5 also burns the two favor a Seize takes off the People's Favor (R-2.5.3) to the shared bank, where Vow of Renewal does not take them (R-10.4-H1); before it, the Vow's holder took them. Revision 5 also withholds Careless's favor on a Trade for secrets from a holder of Vow of Poverty, as the Vow's Q&A rules ("You cannot gain favor from Trade"), unless Master of Disguise lends another player's advisers for that Trade. Before it, the holder gained that favor. Revision 5 also counts only the defense dice Giant Python's targets add, not the Oathkeeper's, and applies it when its holder is the Chancellor allied to a defending Citizen, as its Q&As rule; a Citizen holding it does not bind an attack on the Chancellor, since they join after the targets are declared. Before it, Python counted the Oathkeeper's dice and bound only its defender. Revision 5 also checks Billowing Fog's and Traveling Doctor's "ignore powers that kill all of your force" before Sticky Fire's kill (R-9.2), and Sticky Fire's favor is still given; before it, Sticky Fire killed the force through either card. And it makes Book Burning burn facedown secrets too, keeping one, a faceup one when there is one; before it, Book Burning burned faceup secrets only. Revision 5 also counts the whole force in a battle plan (R-10.9): Encirclement and Zealots count the attacker's warbands at the sites Wild Allies, Captains or Vow of Union bring into the force, a defender's Encirclement compares its force with that whole force, and an attacker's plan judges the defending force from the site the Campaign acts from, as the roll does. Before it, the plans counted the attacker's board alone and judged the defending force from the attacker's pawn.
