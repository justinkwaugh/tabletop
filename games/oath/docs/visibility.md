# Oath visibility

Oath registers state and Action projection. A protected Game conceals the vault: the world deck in order, the three discard piles in order, the relic deck, the site deck, the Dispossessed, and the identity of every facedown site and relic (R-9.4). Public state carries what the rules make public: each discard pile's count, the top card back of the world deck and of each pile, whether the world deck is exhausted, the Visions Drawn track, and every slot that holds a facedown card.

The vault is one host-only field of canonical state. Rule methods reach it through `requireVault()`, so a projected client that runs into a concealed read fails the guarded execution and defers to the host. Setup shuffles the vault from the protected random stream; the three sites that start faceup are dealt from the public stream, since the rules show them to everyone. A master seed reproduces the whole deal.

A player's hand is owner-known. Each player carries a public `handCount`; `handIds` reaches only that player. The setup deal is initialization, so the opening hands exist in the initial state and never cross the Action log. The Search record's `metadata.draw`, the cards a Search or setup choice discards, and the cards a power peeks at are actor-known on the Action record; so is `metadata.reveal`, what a power or a play read from the vault. So are the cards a power or the end of a Campaign puts on a discard pile: they ride the record as `pileDeposits`, a public region with actor-known ids. A setup choice, a route to Citizenship and an answered question record theirs as actor-known `discardedCardIds` beside a public `discardPileRegion`. A card a pending question shows to the player it asks (the relic Family Heirloom draws, the Reliquary relic Skeleton Key shows, the denizens a Pilgrimage player is to stack) is protected on the question under the `oath.askedPlayer` policy; the Pilgrimage answer gives positions in that list, so its record names no card. A drawn or revealed card that the rules make public (a relic taken, a site flipped) is written into public state by the Action that reveals it.

A facedown adviser is owner-known (R-2.2.2). Every adviser row is public and names its card only when faceup; `adviserIds` names each row's card to its holder alone. The records that name a facedown adviser are actor-known: a Search's `keptCardId`, `secondPlay.cardId` and `discardedAdviserCardId`, the `discardedAdviserCardId` of a False Prophet player's answer, a setup choice's `adviserCardId`, and the card R-6.1 plays. A Vision that was revealed stays named when a False Prophet player plays it facedown, since every player saw it. A play that shows its card names it in public metadata (`playedCardId`, `secondPlayedCardId`). A power that looks at or moves another player's facedown adviser (Inquisitor, Ivory Eye, Dream Thief) names it by its place among that player's advisers, and the host resolves the card; so does an exchange at The Gathering, whose terms name adviser rows on both sides and are checked against the public rows alone, so an accepted or refused proposal says nothing about a facedown card. A facedown adviser has no power, so a player's own is refused as the source of an Action power, a modifier or a battle plan. The Inquisitor's two outcomes differ in public (the favor on it goes to the holder unless the adviser is the Conspiracy), so its question and summary name the Conspiracy when it is found, and the actor-known `peeked` names any other card.

A relic a Campaign takes from a site is shown to the victorious attacker as a peek when the battle is won, so the choice to send it to the bottom is made seeing it; it is named in public once the attacker holds it (`relicsTaken`), and one sent to the bottom stays in the actor-known `relicsToDeckBottom`. Relic Breaker's relic goes to the bottom by its slot: the host moves it and no record names it, its user's included. Fae Merchant's public summary names a relic only if it was kept or already held. Truthful Harp's draw and kept card are public on the Search and SearchResolve records (`revealedDraw`, `revealedKeptCardId`).

A peek leaves the relic facedown: its identity stays in the vault and the slot names no card. The peeking player keeps what they saw in the owner-known `peekedRelics`, keyed by slot; the public `peekedRelicSlotIds` says which slots they have seen, which is what R-6.3's second peek from any site reads. The Peek record's `metadata.relicCardId` is actor-known.

Letting another player peek (R-6.1, R-9.4, R-6.6.1) is its own Action, `LetPeek`, and an Undo barrier. It names who is shown under the public `toPlayerId`. A shown facedown adviser's `subject.cardId` is visible to its holder and to the player shown, under the `oath.shownPlayer` policy. A Reliquary relic the Grand Scepter's holder lets an Exile see is read from the vault into `metadata.relicCardId`, which only the Exile sees. It is also recorded in the Exile's `peekedRelics`, as their own peek would be; the holder already knows it.

What a player has seen stays in state, owner-known ([retained knowledge](retained-knowledge.md)). The Ivory Eye's site peeks are kept in `peekedSites` beside the public `peekedSiteSlotIds`. An Ivory Eye or Inquisitor peek at another player's facedown adviser adds the viewer to that row's `shownTo`, as `LetPeek` does; a row handed over by Dream Thief or at The Gathering keeps its `shownTo` and adds the player who gave it. Oracular Pig's cards are kept in `knownWorldDeckTop`, Scryer's and Tavern Songs' in `knownDiscardPiles` by position from each pile's bottom, and relics a player saw go to the bottom of the relic deck in `knownRelicDeckBottom`. Every move of those stacks goes through `util/knowledge.ts`, which keeps each player's record in step with it. No discard in chosen order and no default Card choice can name a facedown adviser.

Warbands are keyed by owner, never by colour: every holding (sites, cards, boards, banks, the Campaign's groups and the consent and move records) counts an Exile's warbands under that player's id and the Empire's under `IMPERIAL_WARBANDS`. The Empire's return to the Chancellor's bank (R-10.13), and who may use them is decided from status (R-6.6.3, R-5.5.1.a). A player's `color` is presentation only; no rule reads it.

## Execution

Draws and reveals are engine output. An Action that consumes the vault resolves its result in `apply` from canonical state and records it in the Action's `metadata`, which the engine strips from anything a client submits, and commits what it sends back into concealment at the end. No submitted field is read as a vault result, and `revealsInfo` follows only what the engine itself resolved. Replaying the record from the same canonical state reproduces the same result. Both steps call `requireVault()`, so such an Action never runs on a client: it always defers to the host. It sets `revealsInfo`, which makes it an Undo barrier, when it disclosed or concealed something or rolled (R-X.3): a first peek at a slot, a Travel to a facedown site, a relic taken from the vault, a card sent to a pile, a die roll from the protected stream, a card played or flipped faceup, a Dream Thief swap, a facedown adviser exchanged. The public top backs and the world deck's emptiness follow every draw, including Oracle's Vision and Cracked Horn's cards under the deck.

Valid-action discovery reads another player's advisers through their public rows only, so a player's projection offers the same actions as canonical state. The Reliquary is the Grand Scepter holder's to know (R-6.4-H1): initialization records all four relics in the Chancellor's owner-known `peekedRelics`, and after any Action whose holder has not seen every covered space (read from the public `peekedRelicSlotIds`) the engine reads the rest from the vault into the new holder's, which makes that Action host-only and an Undo barrier. No Peek targets the Reliquary; which slots a player has seen is public.
## Exploration

A projected perspective cannot explore: the runtime registers no population hook. Host View, hotseat and local branches start from canonical state through `createFromCanonicalState` (`definition/gameExploration.ts`), which deals the world deck, the discard piles, the Dispossessed, the facedown sites and the facedown relics afresh from the branch's new protected stream. It keeps what the table shows: the top back of the deck and of each pile, the Visions within R-8.8's reach of the top, every relic a player has peeked at, and every facedown site a player has peeked at with the Ivory Eye (`peekedSiteSlotIds`).

## Compatibility and publication

Oath is new at version 0.0.1 and has no stored Games to carry forward. Canonical state is validated in full on load, and fields that were once optional are now written empty when a Game is created, so a state saved by an earlier development build does not load; no migration is provided. Publish the Logic and UI Artifacts together: the UI reads projected state and hidden-information metadata the matching Logic writes. The visibility-capable backend and Site Frontend are prerequisites; Oath adds no host bridge interface. Do not run a protected Oath Game on a runtime without visibility registration.

## Verification

Logic tests cover Action schema coverage, reproducible protected initialization, vault omission for player and spectator projections, owner-only hands in state and Action records, a Pilgrimage whose draw from the Dispossessed reaches its player alone, both when it goes straight to the pile and while it waits on the ordering question, and guarded execution deferring a Search and a Pilgrimage to the host. They also cover a facedown adviser kept by a Search, kept at setup, displaced by a Search and discarded by R-6.1; a peek at a site relic; the Reliquary known to the Scepter's holder from setup and taught to a new holder in canonical execution; a facedown adviser shown to one other player and a Reliquary relic shown to an Exile; an Inquisitor peek at another player's facedown adviser; and a projection offering the same actions as canonical state while other players hold facedown advisers. They cover a relic Skeleton Key shows and leaves, the Conspiracy an Inquisitor finds, the adviser a False Prophet player discards to make room, and a relic Relic Hunter sends to the bottom; what each peek leaves in its player's state, how each stack move keeps it, and that it reaches its owner alone; and a hydration round trip of canonical state and of every perspective's projection from setup through a facedown Search play. The engine suite runs every hidden-information Action through the vault in canonical state, including replay of a two-turn walk.
