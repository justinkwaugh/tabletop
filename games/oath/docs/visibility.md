# Oath visibility

Oath registers state and Action projection. A protected Game conceals the vault: the world deck in order, the three discard piles in order, the relic deck, the site deck, the Dispossessed, and the identity of every facedown site and relic (R-9.4). Public state carries what the rules make public: each discard pile's count, the top card back of the world deck and of each pile, whether the world deck is exhausted, the Visions Drawn track, and every slot that holds a facedown card.

The vault is one host-only field of canonical state. Rule methods reach it through `requireVault()`, so a projected client that runs into a concealed read fails the guarded execution and defers to the host. Setup shuffles the vault from the protected random stream; the three sites that start faceup are dealt from the public stream, since the rules show them to everyone. A master seed reproduces the whole deal.

A player's hand is owner-known. Each player carries a public `handCount`; `handIds` reaches only that player. The setup deal is initialization, so the opening hands exist in the initial state and never cross the Action log. The Search record's `metadata.draw`, the cards a Search or setup choice discards, and the cards a power peeks at are actor-known on the Action record; so is `metadata.reveal`, what a power or a play read from the vault. So are the cards a power or the end of a Campaign puts on a discard pile: they ride the record as `pileDeposits`, a public region with actor-known ids. A setup choice, a route to Citizenship and an answered question record theirs as actor-known `discardedCardIds` beside a public `discardPileRegion`. A card a pending question shows to the player it asks (the relic Family Heirloom draws, the Reliquary relic Skeleton Key shows, the denizens a Pilgrimage player is to stack) is protected on the question under the `oath.askedPlayer` policy; the Pilgrimage answer gives positions in that list, so its record names no card. A drawn or revealed card that the rules make public (a relic taken, a site flipped) is written into public state by the Action that reveals it. A Search's public `stoppedOnVision` and a power's public `visionDrawn` (Oracle) say a Vision was drawn, which the public Visions Drawn track and the Vision's back already show (R-2.7.1, R-9.4); the card stays in the actor-known `draw` or `reveal`. A power's public `summary` names every seat it speaks of by id, and its public `targetPlayerId` names the one seat it acted on, which the summary already says. A sacrifice's public `sacrificedOwner` and an Action power's public `warbandOwner` name whose warbands were sacrificed, or killed, gained, moved or replaced by the power, when they were one owner's, which the boards and banks already show; each is optional, and a record made before it has none.

A facedown adviser is owner-known (R-2.2.2). Every adviser row is public and names its card only when faceup; a facedown row shows `vision: true` when its card is a Vision, and each player's public `handVisions` counts the Visions in hand, since a Vision's back differs from a denizen's (R-9.4); `adviserIds` names each row's card to its holder alone. The records that name a facedown adviser are actor-known: a Search's `keptCardId`, `secondPlay.cardId` and `discardedAdviserCardId`, the `discardedAdviserCardId` of a False Prophet player's answer and of an Inquisitor player's answer playing the Conspiracy facedown, a setup choice's `adviserCardId`, and the card R-6.1 plays. A Vision that was revealed stays named when a False Prophet player plays it facedown, since every player saw it; so does the Conspiracy Inquisitor found when its finder plays it facedown. A play that shows its card names it in public metadata (`playedCardId`, `secondPlayedCardId`). A power that looks at or moves another player's facedown adviser (Inquisitor, Ivory Eye, Dream Thief) names it by its place among that player's advisers, and the host resolves the card; so does an exchange at The Gathering, whose terms name adviser rows on both sides and are checked against the public rows alone, so an accepted or refused proposal says nothing about a facedown card. A facedown adviser has no power, so a player's own is refused as the source of an Action power, a modifier or a battle plan. The Inquisitor's two outcomes differ in public (the favor on it goes to the holder unless the adviser is the Conspiracy), so its question and summary name the Conspiracy when it is found, and the actor-known `peeked` names any other card.

A relic a Campaign takes from a site is shown to the victorious attacker as a peek when the battle is won, so the choice to send it to the bottom is made seeing it; it is named in public once the attacker holds it (`relicsTaken`), and one sent to the bottom stays in the actor-known `relicsToDeckBottom`. Relic Breaker's relic goes to the bottom by its slot: the host moves it and no record names it, its user's included. Fae Merchant's public summary names a relic only if it was kept or already held. Truthful Harp's draw and kept card are public on the Search and SearchResolve records (`revealedDraw`, `revealedKeptCardId`).

A peek leaves the relic facedown: its identity stays in the vault and the slot names no card. The peeking player keeps what they saw in the owner-known `peekedRelics`, keyed by slot; the public `peekedRelicSlotIds` says which slots they have seen, which is what R-6.3's second peek from any site reads. The Peek record's `metadata.relicCardId` is actor-known.

Letting another player peek (R-6.1, R-9.4, R-6.6.1) is its own Action, `LetPeek`, and an Undo barrier. It names who is shown under the public `toPlayerId`. A shown facedown adviser's `subject.cardId` is visible to its holder and to the player shown, under the `oath.shownPlayer` policy. A Reliquary relic the Grand Scepter's holder lets an Exile see is read from the vault into `metadata.relicCardId`, which only the Exile sees. It is also recorded in the Exile's `peekedRelics`, as their own peek would be; the holder already knows it.

What a player has seen stays in state, owner-known ([retained knowledge](retained-knowledge.md)). The Ivory Eye's site peeks are kept in `peekedSites` beside the public `peekedSiteSlotIds`. An Ivory Eye or Inquisitor peek at another player's facedown adviser adds the viewer to that row's `shownTo`, as `LetPeek` does; a row handed over by Dream Thief or at The Gathering keeps its `shownTo` and adds the player who gave it. Oracular Pig's cards are kept in `knownWorldDeckTop`, Scryer's and Tavern Songs' in `knownDiscardPiles` by position from each pile's bottom, and so is every card a player discarded or drew and discarded without the table seeing it; every card the table saw go onto a pile (a card leaving play faceup, a revealed Vision, a public discard order, Brass Horse's reveal) is kept in the public `seenDiscardPiles`, and under Truthful Harp, which shows the cards but not their order, as one of a set; cards Cracked Horn sends under the world deck are kept in `knownWorldDeckBottom` and, under Truthful Harp, in the public `seenWorldDeckBottom`; and relics a player saw go to the bottom of the relic deck in `knownRelicDeckBottom`. Every move of those stacks goes through `util/knowledge.ts`, which keeps each player's record in step with it. No discard in chosen order and no default Card choice can name a facedown adviser.

Other public facts the state carries for exploration:
- Every card that goes onto a discard pile or under the world deck shows its back, so the table's records (`seenDiscardPiles`, `seenWorldDeckBottom`) hold a `{ back }` entry for each place whose card the table cannot name, and a set entry carries its place's back too. Setup's seeded discards are denizens. When every Vision left in the world deck lies in the table's known bottom, Oracle's draw is the highest of them, and every record drops that place.
- `worldDeckDrawn` counts the cards drawn from the world deck's top, and `worldDeckVisions` the Visions in the world deck, since every back is seen leaving and going under.
- A hand Truthful Harp revealed has `handShown`, which opens `handIds` to everyone until the hand is set again.
- A facedown adviser the table saw go down (Harp's kept card, False Prophet's Vision, the Conspiracy Inquisitor found and its finder played facedown) has `seen`, which opens its `shownCardId` to everyone. A row Inquisitor found was not the Conspiracy has `notConspiracy`. The Conspiracy Inquisitor found names its row (`index`) on the question.
- Relics everyone saw go to the bottom of the relic deck are in `seenRelicDeckBottom`. A relic drawn off a known bottom stays known: on a site slot in its knowers' `peekedRelics` (which grants no R-6.3 peek) or in the public `seenRelics`, and on a relic question to the players in its `seenBy`, whom the `oath.relicSeenBy` policy shows its `relicCardId`.

Warbands are keyed by owner, never by colour: every holding (sites, cards, boards, banks, the Campaign's groups and the consent and move records) counts an Exile's warbands under that player's id and the Empire's under `IMPERIAL_WARBANDS`. The Empire's return to the Chancellor's bank (R-10.13), and who may use them is decided from status (R-6.6.3, R-5.5.1.a). A player's `color` is presentation only; no rule reads it.

## Execution

Draws and reveals are engine output. An Action that consumes the vault resolves its result in `apply` from canonical state and records it in the Action's `metadata`, which the engine strips from anything a client submits, and commits what it sends back into concealment at the end. No submitted field is read as a vault result, and `revealsInfo` follows only what the engine itself resolved. Replaying the record from the same canonical state reproduces the same result. Both steps call `requireVault()`, so such an Action never runs on a client: it always defers to the host. It sets `revealsInfo`, which makes it an Undo barrier, when it disclosed or concealed something or rolled (R-X.3): a first peek at a slot, a Travel to a facedown site, a relic taken from the vault, a card sent to a pile, a die roll from the protected stream, a card played or flipped faceup, a Dream Thief swap, a facedown adviser exchanged. The public top backs and the world deck's emptiness follow every draw, including Oracle's Vision and Cracked Horn's cards under the deck.

A protected client never hydrates a record it receives: it applies each one by the forward patch the projection carries, and undoes it by the undo patch, so a record's omitted fields are never read. The history spec checks that every projected record from Peek, LetPeek, Search and SearchResolve carries both patches and replays to each seat's view.

Valid-action discovery reads another player's advisers through their public rows only, so a player's projection offers the same actions as canonical state. The Reliquary is the Grand Scepter holder's to know (R-6.4-H1): initialization records all four relics in the Chancellor's owner-known `peekedRelics`, and after any Action whose holder has not seen every covered space (read from the public `peekedRelicSlotIds`) the engine reads the rest from the vault into the new holder's, which makes that Action host-only and an Undo barrier. No Peek targets the Reliquary; which slots a player has seen is public.
## Exploration

A player or a spectator can explore from their own projection. `createFromProjectedState` (`util/exploration.ts`) keeps every card the projection names where it is: the public board, the explorer's hand and advisers, the advisers shown to them, the relics and sites they peeked at, the question fields asked of them, what they remember of the world deck's top, the discard piles and the bottom of the relic deck, and what the whole table saw go onto each pile (`seenDiscardPiles`). It deals every other card afresh from the exploration's randomness: nine denizens of each suit less those named, with the Visions not yet seen; the relics no one holds; and the sites not faceup. Each hidden place takes as many cards as the public counts give it, and each held card and facedown adviser a card with the back the table sees (hands by `handCount`, the discard piles by `discardPileCounts`, Pilgrimage's waiting cards by the question's `cardCount`), the world deck takes the rest, the Visions the Visions Drawn track has not counted stay within R-8.8's reach of its top, and every public top back is kept. The Dispossessed's size is public (`dispossessedCount`) and it is dealt that many unknown cards; in this title it is always empty. A Pilgrimage from an empty Dispossessed draws back exactly the denizens everyone saw leave, so its question names them (`among`, sorted) and exploration deals only their order; once stacked, the table's record holds them as a set. The result names no master seed and passes the canonical validator. A spectator's projection names no owner-known card, so their branch keeps only the public board.

Host View, hotseat and local branches start from canonical state through `createFromCanonicalState` (`definition/gameExploration.ts`), which reshuffles each hidden list within itself from the branch's new protected stream. It keeps what the table shows: the top back of the deck and of each pile, every back the table saw go onto a pile or under the deck (a card trades places only with one of its own back), the Visions within R-8.8's reach of the top, every relic and site a player has peeked at, and every position of the world deck, the discard piles and the relic deck where the table or a player knows which card, or which set, lies.

## Compatibility and publication

Oath has stored Games to carry forward. A Game keeps the rules of the revision it was created under (R-X.4): initialization sets `oathRevision`, and a state without one was created before the first revision and keeps that flow to its end, so its stored actions replay unchanged (`rulings.md` lists what each revision changes); `definition/recordedGames.spec.ts` replays stretches of a game recorded under logic 0.2.0 and expects the same processed actions and the same state. No migration rewrites a stored Game. Canonical state is validated in full on load. Publish the Logic and UI Artifacts together: the UI reads projected state and hidden-information metadata the matching Logic writes. The visibility-capable backend and Site Frontend are prerequisites; Oath adds no host bridge interface. Do not run a protected Oath Game on a runtime without visibility registration.

## Verification

Logic tests cover Action schema coverage, reproducible protected initialization, vault omission for player and spectator projections, owner-only hands in state and Action records, a Pilgrimage whose draw from the Dispossessed reaches its player alone, both when it goes straight to the pile and while it waits on the ordering question, and guarded execution deferring a Search and a Pilgrimage to the host. They also cover a facedown adviser kept by a Search, kept at setup, displaced by a Search and discarded by R-6.1; a peek at a site relic; the Reliquary known to the Scepter's holder from setup and taught to a new holder in canonical execution; a facedown adviser shown to one other player and a Reliquary relic shown to an Exile; an Inquisitor peek at another player's facedown adviser; and a projection offering the same actions as canonical state while other players hold facedown advisers. They cover a relic Skeleton Key shows and leaves, the Conspiracy an Inquisitor finds, the adviser a False Prophet player discards to make room, and a relic Relic Hunter sends to the bottom; what each peek leaves in its player's state, how each stack move keeps it, and that it reaches its owner alone; and a hydration round trip of canonical state and of every perspective's projection from setup through a facedown Search play. Exploration from each player's and a spectator's projection is covered at the first Act Phase, mid-Search, after a Search's discards and with a relic question pending after Oracular Pig's peek, for both decks: every hidden place keeps its size, every card is dealt exactly once, what the explorer knows stays fixed, the branch hydrates and plays on, and two sources differing only in hidden cards give the same branch. Every registered action type is sampled with every optional field present and projected for its actor, another seat and a spectator: none of its protected values reaches the other two. The engine suite runs every hidden-information Action through the vault in canonical state, including replay of a two-turn walk.
