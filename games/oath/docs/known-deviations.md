# Known deviations

Where this implementation still differs from the Law of Oath or a card's published Q&A. The code
cites the Law as `R-<section>`; `docs/rulings.md` holds the house rulings, which are readings, not
deviations. Each line names the card or rule, what the engine or UI does, and what governs.

## Card powers

- **Battle-plan costs:** in a game created before revision 3 (R-X.4) each plan's cost is checked alone against the untouched holding, so a side can declare plans it cannot pay in full: the payment throws, or Gleaming Armor's added secret leaves its payer below zero.
- **Vow of Renewal:** in a game created before revision 5 (R-X.4) its holder takes the two favor a Seize burns off the People's Favor; R-10.4-H1 burns them to the shared bank.
- **Deed Writer's warbands:** in a game created before revision 5 (R-X.4) each site's warbands are checked alone against the new ruler's board, so terms can promise more warbands than the board holds in all: accepting them throws.
- **Deed Writer, sites both ways:** in a game created before revision 11 (R-X.4) each receiver's board is judged before their own sites' warbands come home, so a player whose only warbands stand on the site they give cannot take the other player's site; the card has old rulers move warbands home before new rulers move in.
- **Small Friends:** in a game created before revision 19 (R-X.4) the modifiers at the pawn's own site stay usable, and those at every Beast site are opened without being declared; its Q&A allows only the one site it acts at (R-7.1.1-H2).
- **Vow of Poverty with Careless:** in a game created before revision 5 (R-X.4) the Vow's holder gains Careless's favor on a Trade for secrets, and Secret Signal's one more with it; the Vow's Q&A withholds Careless's, so the Trade gains none for Secret Signal to add to.
- **Dragonskin Drum:** in a game created before revision 7 (R-X.4) it fires only on a Travel action, not on travel a power causes; its Q&A counts that travel.
- **Ancient Binding:** in a game created before revision 5 (R-X.4) it burns faceup secrets only, so every facedown secret stays; Book Burning's Q&A gives the same words the meaning "face up or down".
- **Obsidian Cage:** in a game created before revision 5 (R-X.4), warbands of an Exile who has since become a Citizen return as that player's own; the Q&A makes them Imperial. In a game created before revision 17, a Cage sent to the bottom of the relic deck (Fae Merchant) returns its warbands to their banks; its Q&A returns them to boards of their colour, as its Action does (R-10.13-H1).
- **Bandit Crown:** in a game created before revision 5 (R-X.4), the bandits serving a Chancellor or Citizen holder are that holder's alone: the other Imperial players do not rule the sites they hold, and those sites are not Imperial sites; the Q&A makes them Imperial warbands.
- **The Grand Scepter:** in a game created before revision 5 (R-X.4), one given in an exchange on a turn another player took it is locked for its new holder until the turn ends; its Q&A lets a given Scepter be used at once.
- **Boiling Lake on a board of two colours:** in a game created before revision 5 (R-X.4), where a board can hold its player's own warbands beside Imperial ones (R-6.7-H1, R-6.6.2, the Obsidian Cage), "kill two warbands on your board if able" kills from one colour only, its player's own first: a board with one warband of that colour loses one, though warbands of the other colour are there to kill.
- **Military Parade with Marriage:** in a game created before revision 5 (R-X.4) it counts Marriage as one hearth adviser and gains one favor for it; its Q&A pays one favor per adviser of a suit, and Marriage counts as two (R-2.2.2-H1).
- **Gambling Hall:** in a game created before revision 21 (R-X.4) the favor bank is named at the use, before the roll; from revision 21 it is chosen once the roll stands, after any Jinx reroll (R-7.1.3-H2).
- **Witch's Bargain:** in a game created before revision 7 (R-X.4) it deals with one player per use, though its Q&A allows several, and its user may give away the secret its cost placed, leaving fewer than none. In a game created before revision 32 each player at the site is named once and the totals are held against what the user holds once the cost is paid, so a token taken in one exchange cannot be given in another of the same use, though each exchange may use what its user holds at that moment; its History also names an exchange of none ("0 favor for 0 secrets"). In a game created before revision 33 an exchange that would take more favor or secrets than the other player holds is refused, though Law 9.3 takes as many as there are (R-9.3-H1).
- **Land Warden:** the second card played gets no When Played choices and no Deep Woods or Wastes relic, takes no card discarded first before it (by Crop Rotation, the Great Slum, or the People's Favor's holder, R-5.1.4.I), so it cannot use a space so freed, and cannot be played to another site by New Growth or the People's Favor. In a game created before revision 32 (R-X.4) it is played only to a site or as an adviser, never to the Revealed Vision space, though the card plays two cards drawn and the Revealed Vision space is one of a Search's plays (R-5.1.4.III); from revision 32 an Exile's Vision may go there. In a game created before revision 3 (R-X.4) it takes none of the Search's modifiers, so Book of Records' holder gains favor for it, and Wild Cry and Welcoming Party look at the kept card only. A When Played power that draws a hidden card (Family Heirloom, Pilgrimage, the citizenship relic takes) is not resolved for it, and the action records that in `secondWhenPlayed`; the table offers only the second plays the engine accepts. In a game created before revision 12 the Search records the kept card's favor alone, so its History does not count the second card's. In a game created before revision 31 the second card cannot be played as an adviser at the adviser limit, since it takes no adviser discard (R-5.1.4.II). In a game created before revision 33 (R-X.4) a second card played to a site or as an adviser is judged by Gossip and Ruined Temple on the state before the kept card's play, though it is played after that play's power (R-7.3.3-H3): Gossip discarded first by the kept card still bars a facedown adviser, and Gossip kept to a site an enemy of the player rules does not.
- **Salad Days:** in a game created before revision 8 (R-X.4) it accepts an empty favor bank while another bank holds favor (R-7.1.3). From revision 8 until revision 16 the banks are judged before a site play takes its own favor (R-5.1.4.I), so Salad Days played to a site with one favor in the Hearth bank may name that bank and gain nothing from it while another bank holds favor. From revision 16 they are judged on the banks the play leaves (R-7.3.3), and that naming is refused.
- **Forgotten Vault:** in a game created before revision 8 (R-X.4) a burn at the Darkest Secret's last secret is accepted and burns nothing; the card says "not last".
- **Bewitch:** in a game created before revision 8 (R-X.4) "(even on cards)" counts only the secrets on faceup advisers, its player's and the Chancellor's (R-7.3.3-H1).
- **Captains, Wild Allies:** in a game created before revision 8 (R-X.4) they may name a site where no player can defend (R-5.5.1); the cost is paid and the free Campaign from there can only be given up.
- **The Tribunal:** its exchange binds what changes hands at once, not promises of later actions (R-7.6.3-H1).
- **Vow of Kinship:** in a game created before revision 18 (R-X.4) Nomad-bank favor is always put into the People's Favor Wake, and Plague Engines and Dissent force it too; its Q&A makes it optional (R-10.10-H1).
- **Forced Labor:** in a game created before revision 6 (R-X.4) not charged for Oracle's draw, and before revision 3 not for an R-6.1 play either; both Q&As charge it.
- **R-6.1:** in a game created before revision 2 (R-X.4), a facedown adviser's play takes no Search modifier, so Book of Records' holder gains favor on a site play and New Growth, Crop Rotation, Wild Cry, Bracken and Cracked Horn cannot be used on it. From revision 2 the engine applies them: Book of Records, being mandatory, applies by itself, and the table offers each optional one in the step of the play it changes.
- **Encirclement, Zealots:** in a game created before revision 5 (R-X.4) they count only the attacker's board, not the whole force (R-10.9), and an attacker's plan judges the defending force from the attacker's pawn, not the site Wild Allies or Captains names.
- **Wrestlers:** in a game created before revision 22 (R-X.4) the engine picks the sacrificed warband, its user's board first, else the first targeted site, and an Ally's board is never offered; from revision 22 its user picks the warband at any place in the defending force, an Ally's board that adds to the force included (R-10.22, its Q&A).
- **Hospital, the Campaign's other kills:** in a game created before revision 13 (R-X.4) Hospital does not catch Bear Traps' kill (the bandits' included), Wrestlers' sacrifice (even with Hospital declared first), or the kill Boiling Lake or Grasping Vines makes during the banish: each is killed past it, and the banish's kill does not count for Cursed Cauldron. Hospital's text reaches any of its user's warbands that would be killed while the Campaign is open (R-5.5.3-H2).
- **Hospital, the Empire's warbands at a site:** in a game created before revision 13 (R-X.4) the Empire's warbands killed at a site in a Campaign are the Chancellor's for Hospital, so a Citizen's Hospital misses those at their Vow of Union, Captains or Wild Allies site and at their targeted sites, and the Chancellor's Hospital holds them instead, as their Ally or when they attack the Chancellor through Captains; the warbands in a force are its player's (R-10.28-H2).
- **Buried Giant to The Hidden Place:** in a game created before revision 20 (R-X.4) one flipped secret pays both flips: the Travel is free and ignores the Narrow Pass, and a power's move past the Narrow Pass into The Hidden Place takes one; each site's power takes its own (R-11.13-H2).
- **Master of Disguise with Vow of Kinship:** in a game created before revision 23 (R-X.4) the borrower's own Vow of Kinship still binds the Trade: the favor it gains goes to the Nomad bank, and its payments may come from that bank, while a lender's Vow of Kinship does not bind it. From revision 23 the lent advisers' Vow decides, as their Vow of Poverty does (R-7.4-H1, Master of Disguise's Q&A).
- **Master of Disguise with The Old Oak:** in a game created before revision 23 (R-X.4) The Old Oak's "if you have any [beast] advisers" counts the borrower's own faceup advisers, so a lender's Beast adviser does not open it and the borrower's own does. From revision 23 it counts the lent advisers, as the Trade does (R-7.4-H1).
- **Master of Disguise with Cup of Plenty:** in a game created before revision 23 (R-X.4) Cup of Plenty's "a card that matches any of your advisers" reads the borrower's own faceup advisers, so a lender's adviser of the traded card's suit does not waive the Supply and the borrower's own does. From revision 23 it reads the lent advisers, as the Trade does (R-7.4-H1).
- **Toll Roads:** in a game created before revision 6 (R-X.4), never asked or paid on travel a power causes (a banish, the Whistle, Palanquin's carried player, a Shrouded Wood ruler's pick, Brass Horse), nor on a Travel out of a Shrouded Wood an enemy rules, whose ruler may pick a Toll Roads site. In such a game a pawn banished from a Shrouded Wood also has its favor burned, and the victor's "if you're victorious" plans resolved, before the ruler picks the site (R-5.5.7.III travels first, R-5.5.8 follows). From revision 6, Palanquin's user has already moved when its carried player, leaving a Shrouded Wood, refuses the toll.
- **Ring of Devotion:** in a game created before revision 9 (R-X.4) its holder still moves warbands from their board onto a site they take in a Deed Writer exchange; the Ring reads "You cannot place warbands at sites.", and its Q&A applies that to every case. In a game created before revision 31 its holder's Messenger and Warning Signals still move warbands to a site, from their board or from another site they rule, and offer those sites; a warband reaching a site is placed there. From revision 31 both move warbands only from a site to the board for the Ring's holder; Warning Signals stays a plan they may use, moving nothing (R-9.2). In a game created before revision 32 its holder's Hospital still places the warbands it holds on its site at the Campaign's end; an Exile holding it who accepts Citizenship still has their warbands on the map replaced with purple ones; and a winner holding it still places one of their warbands at each site an accepter rules in the Chronicle (R-8.2). From revision 32 none of these is placed (R-5.5.3-H5, R-6.6.2-H1, R-8.2-H2).
- **Animal Host, Fabled Feast:** in a game created before revision 10 (R-X.4) Animal Host played to the advisers counts itself among the Beast cards "at any sites", and Fabled Feast played to a site its player does not rule counts itself among "the Hearth cards you rule"; neither card is then one of the cards its text counts.
- **Riots:** in a game created before revision 10 (R-X.4) Riots played to a site leaves itself out of the most common suit on the map, so a Discord tie or lead it makes is missed; its Q&A counts every card at a site and spares only Riots from the discard. Its check there also counts a card discarded before the site play, which its resolve does not, so the suit named can be one the resolve no longer finds most common.
- **Riots, Land Warden's second card:** in a game created before revision 26 (R-X.4) Riots played as the second card is judged on the map before the kept card's own When Played power (Dazzle, Threatening Roar, Salt the Earth, Pilgrimage), and resolves on the map after it, still counting a kept card that power discarded. A tie that power makes is then settled for the first suit counted, not by the player, and a lead it makes is refused as a tie. From revision 26 both count the map after it; the second card still names no suit, so a tie there refuses it. At every revision Great Herd's swap, a choice the table asks after the placements, is not counted before Riots: kept as an adviser and swapped with a Nomad card at a site, it changes the map Riots resolves on.
- **Great Herd:** in a game created before revision 10 (R-X.4) Great Herd played to a site and swapped with a Nomad card at that same site leaves the site holding Great Herd twice, and the other card is lost; a swap of two cards at one site moves nothing (R-10.25).
- **Enchantress:** from revision 10 (R-X.4), held as an adviser, it does not offer its holder's own advisers, which its text ("any faceup adviser") allows: that swap moves nothing and only burns the secret. In a game created before revision 10 it offers them, and the swap leaves the holder with Enchantress twice and loses the other card.
- **Pied Piper:** in a game created before revision 11 (R-X.4) it never counts toward its holder's advisers, so a holder of Pied Piper and two others keeps a fourth adviser and Family Wagon played after it keeps both; its Q&A counts it once held and exempts only its own arrival.
- **Enchantress, Twin Brother, Horned Mask:** in a game created before revision 11 (R-X.4) a swap that leaves a player over their adviser limit asks no discard, so they keep the extra adviser; Enchantress's Q&A and R-7.6.4 have them discard down to the limit.
- **The Gathering:** in a game created before revision 11 (R-X.4) an exchange that leaves a player over their adviser limit is refused when the receiver alone would be over before giving anything back, and otherwise accepted with no discard (Family Wagon received over two non-Nomads, or given away so that Nomads count); R-10.8 makes both gives one exchange, and, as R-5.1.4.II rules a play, an exchange that ends over the limit is accepted and the player discards down to it.
- **Silver Tongue:** in a game created before revision 18 (R-X.4) it leaves an empty matching favor bank out of its choices, so its Rest cannot take from one; R-9.3 lets it take 0 there, as Vow of Obedience and Vow of Poverty may.
- **Bewitch, Salad Days, the play's own gains:** in a game created before revision 16 (R-X.4) their When Played checks read the state before the play, though R-7.3.3 resolves the play's gains first: the secret Book of Records or a secret Homeland (Standing Stones) gives, the favor the site and a favor Homeland (Fertile Valley) take, and the tokens a card discarded first or an adviser discarded to the limit gives back (R-10.5) are left out, so Bewitch's yes is refused and not offered when only that secret puts its player ahead, and Salad Days' three banks are judged on the banks before the play.
- **Salt the Earth, The Gathering at another site:** in a game created before revision 16 (R-X.4) each played to another site (the People's Favor, New Growth) acts at its player's site, not at "this site": Salt the Earth clears and caps its player's site, and The Gathering gathers there and never asks its player (R-7.6.3-H3). Salt the Earth's play there is judged as any other card's, so a full site is refused and a locked card there does not bar it.
- **Salt the Earth, a card discarded first:** in a game created before revision 16 (R-X.4) Salt the Earth played to its player's own site, faceup from a Search or a facedown adviser (R-6.1), takes any denizen on the map as the card discarded first, without the People's Favor, the Great Slum or Crop Rotation (R-5.1.4.I, R-11.10), and discards it.
- **Salt the Earth, Land Warden's second card:** in a game created before revision 29 (R-X.4) the second card played to your site is judged on the site as it stood before the kept card. So with Salt the Earth kept there, a second card is accepted at the site Salt the Earth has just set to capacity 1, and with a locked card kept there, Salt the Earth is accepted as the second card and leaves the locked card in place. From revision 29 both are refused. The other way round, in a game created before revision 31 Salt the Earth played second to your site needs room there for both cards, so it is refused when the kept card fills the site's last space, though Salt the Earth ignores the site's capacity; from revision 31 it is accepted.
- **Truthful Harp, a drawn card that discards itself:** in a game created before revision 29 (R-X.4) a card the Harp showed drawn, kept or played second with Land Warden, that its own When Played power discards with no order to choose (Threatening Roar played to a site in your region, the only card it discards) lies in the table's record of the pile as a card of its back alone, though the table saw it. From revision 29 the table knows it is that card.
- **Land Warden, the kept card at another site:** in a game created before revision 16 (R-X.4) a kept card the People's Favor sends to another site still takes a space at its player's own site, so a second card played there is refused unless two spaces are left, though Land Warden's Q&A lets both cards use the People's Favor. The fix only widens what is accepted, so such games would replay the same without it; it is gated with the rest of revision 16.
- **Land Warden, the kept card's card discarded first:** in a game created before revision 31 (R-X.4) a card the kept card discards first at its player's own site (the People's Favor, the Great Slum, Crop Rotation) is counted as staying when both cards are played there, so the second card is refused when the discard leaves the site's last space for it. From revision 31 it is accepted. The fix only widens what is accepted.
- **Land Warden, the kept card's When Played power at your site:** in a game created before revision 31 (R-X.4) a card the kept card's When Played power puts off the map at its player's own site (Dazzle, Threatening Roar) is counted as staying when both cards are played there, so the second card is refused when that power leaves the site's last space for it, though the second card's own check reads the site after that power. From revision 31 it is accepted. The fix only widens what is accepted.
- **A Search modifier in use, discarded:** in a game created before revision 31 (R-X.4) a modifier a Search uses may be discarded during that Search, though Law 7.4.3 bars it (R-7.4.3-H1): as the adviser discarded to meet the limit for the kept card (Land Warden, Augury, Wild Cry and the rest), in the discard question after a swap (Twin Brother), or as the card discarded first before a site play (Welcoming Party, with Crop Rotation, the People's Favor or the Great Slum), or among the cards a card played during that Search discards at sites (Salt the Earth, Riots, Dazzle, Threatening Roar). The discarded modifier still applies to that Search. From revision 31 each such discard is refused, and a mass discard leaves the modifier where it is.
- **A facedown adviser's play, its modifier in use discarded:** in a game created before revision 31 (R-X.4) a modifier declared for an R-6.1 play may be discarded during that play (R-7.4.3-H1): as the adviser discarded to meet the limit (Wild Cry, with Family Wagon turned faceup), in the discard question after a swap (Twin Brother), as the card discarded first before a site play (Bracken, Wild Cry), or among the cards the card played discards at sites (Threatening Roar). The discarded modifier still applies to that play. From revision 31 each such discard is refused, and a mass discard leaves the modifier where it is.
- **Two modifiers answering the same question:** in a game created before revision 31 (R-X.4) Errand Boy and the Observatory naming different piles, or Bracken and the Cracked Horn, may be declared together on a Search, and Bracken and the Cracked Horn on a facedown adviser's play (R-7.4-H2): the first declared names the pile drawn from, or where the discards go, and both are paid for. From revision 31 the one declared second is refused unless their answers agree.
- **Oracle, the Vision's play or discard:** in a game created before revision 31 (R-X.4) Oracle's use takes no Search modifier, so the Vision it draws is discarded to the next region's pile even with the Cracked Horn or Bracken in reach, though its Q&A lets the Cracked Horn take it (R-6.1, R-7.4.3-H1).
- **False Prophet, the Vision's play or discard:** in a game created before revision 32 (R-X.4) the play or discard of the Vision takes no Search modifier and pays no toll, so a discarded Vision goes to the next region's pile even with the Cracked Horn or Bracken in reach, and Forced Labor charges nothing, though its Q&A and Oracle's apply both to a play "as if you searched" (R-3.2-H2, R-6.1).
- **A Search modifier that cannot act on a play "as if you searched":** in a game created before revision 33 (R-X.4) Great Spire is accepted on Oracle's use, on a facedown adviser's play (R-6.1) and on False Prophet's play or discard, and its secret is paid, though none of them draws a denizen to swap; New Growth and Crop Rotation are accepted on a play that is not to a site, and Welcoming Party on Oracle's use, though none of them can act there (R-7.4). From revision 33 each is refused before anything is paid.
- **Homesteaders and Resettle at the Great Slum, discarding themselves first:** in a game created before revision 33 (R-X.4) Homesteaders or Resettle used at the Great Slum may be the card discarded there first, so the card it moves takes its space at a full Great Slum, though the card whose power moves a card there is never the one discarded first (R-11.10-H2). From revision 33 neither is offered, and naming itself is refused before anything is paid.
- **Family Wagon, an adviser play's discard:** in a game created before revision 31 (R-X.4) an adviser play at the limit (a Search's kept card, Land Warden's second card, a facedown adviser played faceup, a Vision or the Conspiracy played facedown from a question) checks only how many advisers it discards, so under Family Wagon a Nomad discarded for a non-Nomad, or the Wagon discarded over Nomads that then count, is accepted and leaves the player over their limit. From revision 31 the discards must leave the player within it.
- **A Search's modifiers, after the When Played power:** in a game created before revision 31 (R-X.4) a Search's modifiers (Welcoming Party, Wild Cry, Hunting Party) resolve after the kept card's When Played power and Land Warden's second play, and a facedown adviser's modifiers after its power, though Law 7.3.3 resolves modifiers first (R-7.3.3-H3). So Dissent does not take Welcoming Party's favor, and Salad Days is judged on the banks before Welcoming Party's take. From revision 31 they resolve before the power.
- **Salt the Earth, after the Chronicle takes it:** in a game created before revision 31 (R-X.4) a site keeps Salt the Earth's capacity of 1 after the Chronicle builds an edifice in its place or discards it with a ruin, into the next game, and the build in its place is offered even at the Drowned City, which then holds Festival District with no capacity. From revision 31 the site has its own capacity once the card is gone (R-7-H1), and that build is not offered.
- **Salt the Earth, a relic Ancient Bloodline locks:** in a game created before revision 31 (R-X.4) Salt the Earth may be played to a site that holds a relic Ancient Bloodline locks for its player (a site an enemy holding Ancient Bloodline rules), from a Search, as Land Warden's second card, to another site with the People's Favor, or from a facedown adviser (R-6.1), though the card says "You cannot play Salt the Earth to a site with any locked cards" and Ancient Bloodline locks "denizens and relics at sites you rule" (R-7.2.2): only the denizens there are judged. From revision 31 it is refused.
- **Homesteaders at the Great Slum, a locked card discarded first:** in a game created before revision 31 (R-X.4) Homesteaders offers and accepts a locked card at the Great Slum as the card discarded first (Marriage, Salt the Earth, or a card at a site an enemy holding Ancient Bloodline rules), though a locked card "cannot be discarded, moved, or swapped" (R-7.2.2, R-11.10): the card stays and the adviser is moved only if the site still has room. From revision 31 a locked card is not offered and naming it is refused, as Roving Terror already refuses it.
- **The Horned Mask and Great Herd at the Great Slum:** in a game created before revision 32 (R-X.4) a swap that moves a card to the Great Slum offers no card there to discard first, though the Great Slum's "When playing or moving a card to this site" covers a swap's move (R-10.25). From revision 32 each offers one, as Homesteaders does (R-11.10-H1).
- **Two powers that each give a card discarded first:** in a game created before revision 32 (R-X.4) a site play discards one card first at most, though each of the Great Slum played to, Crop Rotation and the People's Favor gives one, and Crop Rotation's Q&A lets it be used along with the People's Favor. From revision 32 one card per power may go (R-5.1.4-H2).
- **Palanquin to a facedown site, the move refused:** in a game created before revision 31 (R-X.4) Palanquin reveals a facedown site it names as it is used, before the carried player is asked to flip a secret (the Buried Giant past the Narrow Pass, R-11.13-H1), so a refusal, which moves neither pawn, still leaves the site faceup with its relics and tokens placed, though R-5.6.2 reveals a site only when a pawn arrives there. From revision 31 the site is revealed as the pawns arrive, after a yes, and a refusal reveals nothing. A move that asks nothing first, or one out of a Shrouded Wood whose ruler sends the carried player elsewhere (R-11.7), still reveals it at the use, where the user's pawn is put. Before revision 31, a facedown site the use reveals that turns out to be The Hidden Place then asks the carried player's flip, so a refusal leaves it revealed with no one there; from revision 31 the site is judged as it was named, facedown, as a Travel to it is, and both pawns go with no flip asked.
- **Great Herd, Roving Terror, Enchantress, their own lock:** in a game created before revision 16 (R-X.4) each moves itself out of a site where it is locked for its user (R-7.2.2): Great Herd played to a site an enemy holding Ancient Bloodline rules is offered and makes its swap, and Roving Terror there moves to the site whose card it discards, though Great Herd's Q&A says a locked card cannot be moved or swapped and Ancient Bloodline's leaves such cards stuck at the site for all but its ruler. Enchantress has the same guard, but an adviser-only card reaches no site in a legal game.
- **Mushrooms at the Marshes:** in a game created before revision 16 (R-X.4) the Marshes' one fewer card is lost under Mushrooms' one, so Mushrooms there draws one card, two with Augury, three with Greedy, though the one fewer stacks on Mushrooms as Augury's one more does (R-11.11-H1).
- **Fabled Feast with Marriage:** in a game created before revision 16 (R-X.4) Fabled Feast counts Marriage as one hearth card its player rules, though Marriage counts as two hearth advisers and an adviser is a card its holder rules (R-2.2.2-H1).
- **Hearts and Minds, Peace Envoy, Undo:** in a game created before revision 17 (R-X.4) the Campaign step that one of them decides is marked as revealing information, so it cannot be undone, though no dice are rolled and R-5.5.5.b-H1 keeps it undoable.
- **Family Heirloom, Dowsing Sticks, the kept relic:** in a game created before revision 17 (R-X.4) keeping the relic drawn is not marked as revealing information, so it can be undone, though the keep shows the table a relic only its player had seen (R-X.3(c)), so its player may undo the keep and send the relic, now seen, to the bottom of the relic deck.

## Rules

- **R-2.11, a Campaign's title:** in a game created before revision 12 (R-X.4) the Oathkeeper title is judged after each step of a Campaign, not once it ends, so it can pass through a third player between the defeat and the spoils (or through nobody), leave R-2.11.b's choice to that player instead of the holder from before the Campaign, ask the holder mid-Campaign for a choice the spoils make moot, make Chaos Cult take favor for each pass, and come back to a holder on its Oathkeeper side when it should have kept its Usurper side; a Campaign's Steps 1–8 are one action, and Fae Merchant's Q&A counts "after you have completed this action" (R-2.11-H1).
- **R-4.3.4, a refresh to full on your own turn:** in a game created before revision 24 (R-X.4) the Rest saves the Supply at the turn's start less what was spent this turn, not the marker, so a refresh to full on your own turn is lost at the Rest: after Long-Lost Heir, Royal Ambitions, Ballot Box, Bewitch, Martial Culture or Ancient Pact, a Citizenship offer accepted or a Self-Exile, the player rests on their refresh space alone (a Citizen on the Chancellor's Supply).
- **R-5.5.1.a:** in a game created before revision 5 (R-X.4), a Citizen whom it suspends still shares the Empire's sites in battle plans and the attacking force: they may use plans at sites only the Empire rules, Great Crusade, Kindred Warriors and Wild Mounts count the cards there, Fire Talkers counts the Darkest Secret another Imperial player holds, Warning Signals moves warbands at those sites, Hospital places on them, and their force counts the Empire's warbands at a Vow of Union site. A Wild Allies or Captains site they target also defends with the Empire's warbands there, which their force counts too (R-5.5.1.a-H1).
- **R-5.5.1.a, site powers:** in a game created before revision 14 (R-X.4) a Citizen whom R-5.5.1.a suspends still rules the cards at the Empire's sites for persistent powers and the Narrow Pass, and still counts as an Imperial player's friend for their enemy tests; Vow of Union still holds them at a site only the Empire rules, and banished out of a Shrouded Wood the Empire rules, they are not treated as leaving an enemy's Wood (R-11.7; R-5.5.1.a-H2).
- **R-5.5.2, an Ally's Vow of Peace:** in a game created before revision 13 (R-X.4) only the defender's Vow of Peace forbids the attacker's sacrifice; an Ally's does not (R-5.5.2-H1).
- **R-5.5.2, the site Captains or Wild Allies names:** in a game created before revision 14 (R-X.4) a Citizen outside the Empire for the Campaign must still declare a target at the named site, and must target it when the defender rules it (R-5.5.2-H2).
- **R-5.5.3, Code of Honor on the defending side:** in a game created before revision 13 (R-X.4) an Ally may use Code of Honor after another player on the defending side has used a battle plan, and the side keeps both (R-5.5.3-H3).
- **R-5.5.3, the bandits' plans:** in a game created before revision 14 (R-X.4) the bandits use every cost-free battle plan they rule, though True Names or Beast Tamer forbids it or Gleaming Armor or Insect Swarm adds a cost (R-5.5.3-H4).
- **R-5.5.4, the defender's reroll:** in a game created before revision 14 (R-X.4) the attack is rolled with the defense, before the defender who rules Jinx decides whether to reroll, and the attacker is asked first (R-5.5.4-H1).
- **R-5.5.5:** in a game created before revision 7 (R-X.4), the attacker declares the order their skulls' kills come from with the Campaign, before the roll, and the kills follow it at the roll; R-5.5.5 has them killed once the skulls are rolled (R-5.5.5-H1).
- **R-5.5.6:** in a game created before revision 7 (R-X.4), a defeated Citizen attacker's surviving Imperial warbands at the sites in their force (Captains, Wild Allies, Vow of Union) go to the Chancellor's board; R-5.5.6 moves them to the attacker's own board (R-5.5.6-H2).
- **R-6.4, the Grand Scepter's Reliquary:** in a game created before revision 12 (R-X.4) a player who takes the Grand Scepter (in a Campaign, by Recover or by Relic Thief) is shown every Reliquary relic at once, on the turn they took it; the card cannot be used on that turn, and its use "Peek in the Imperial Reliquary" with it (R-6.4-H1).
- **R-6.6.2, R-6.6.3:** in a game created before revision 5 (R-X.4), a Citizen who gains warbands through a power gets their own, not Imperial ones; Muster already gives Imperial ones.
- **R-10.28, Peace Envoy on the defending side:** in a game created before revision 14 (R-X.4) a defender's or Ally's Peace Envoy locks the rest of the defending side out of their battle plans, as Code of Honor does (R-10.28-H1).
- **R-11.2, the Homeland ledger out of turn:** in a game created before revision 16 (R-X.4) a Rest clears only the resting player's record of the cards they discarded at a site this turn, so a discard made in another player's turn (the "At end, discard" plans of a Campaign out of turn: Sneak Attack, Second Wind) still bars that player's Homeland gain at the site in their own next turn, though the Homeland asks only about "this turn".
- **R-11.2, a relic Homeland after a discard first:** in a game created before revision 16 (R-X.4) a card played to a relic Homeland (the Deep Woods, the Wastes) after a card of the same suit there was discarded first (the People's Favor, Crop Rotation) rightly gains no relic, but the relic is taken out of the vault and shown to the player all the same, and its slot stays on the map with nothing behind it. Such a game is already broken: a later Recover, Peek or Relic Hunter Campaign of that slot fails. From revision 16 the relic stays facedown in its slot.
- **R-11.4, Plains and Mountain:** in a game created before revision 13 (R-X.4) a targeted site's attack die is added or taken after the battle plans. Rangers' 4+ and Peace Envoy's payment and check leave out a Mountain's defense die from an empty pool, and a plan's ±1 at 0 attack dice is counted before the site's (R-11.4-H1).
- **R-11.7, Shrouded Wood:** in a game created before revision 6 (R-X.4), a Travel out of a Wood an enemy rules pays the Wood's 2 with the Travel, before the site is known, so Decadent is not applied, Tyrant does not fire at the site picked, and the ruler may pick a site the traveller could not have paid for.
- **R-11.7, a power's move out of the Shrouded Wood:** in a game created before revision 15 (R-X.4) the Whistle pulls a pawn out of a Wood an enemy rules straight to its user's site, and the Brass Horse's user picks their own site there; the Wood's ruler picks neither (R-11.7-H1).
- **R-11.7, cards on the Travel out of the Shrouded Wood:** in a game created before revision 15 (R-X.4) nothing is declared on a Travel out of a Wood an enemy rules: Tents, Forest Paths, A Fast Steed, Special Envoy and the Dragonskin Drum are refused, the ruler always names a site, and the Travel is refused while no site is payable without a card (R-11.7-H2).
- **R-11.7, Way Station at the Shrouded Wood's pick:** in a game created before revision 15 (R-X.4) Way Station is ignored when the Wood's ruler picks its site for the traveller's own Travel: its ruler pays the Wood's price, and anyone else is never offered the favor instead (R-11.7-H3).
- **R-11.7, Portal at the Shrouded Wood:** in a game created before revision 15 (R-X.4) Portal used from its own site, a Shrouded Wood an enemy rules, is refused: the ruler picks the site and the Wood's 2 Supply is owed (R-11.7-H4).
- **R-11.13, a power's move and the flip:** in a game created before revision 15 (R-X.4) the Whistle, the Brass Horse, Palanquin and a banish never move a pawn to The Hidden Place, or from the Buried Giant past the Narrow Pass, since the moved player is never offered the flip (R-11.13-H1).
- **R-9.4:** the state records every back that goes onto a pile, but the table draws only the top card's back and the count.
- **R-9.4, Exploration:** a card known only as "one of these places" is forgotten: after a Search of two or more cards whose hand others knew, a card kept facedown unseen may be the kept card or among the discards; and a card known to be in a hand only as one of a set (drawn from a set's places) is forgotten when that hand is discarded. The records place such a card nowhere, so a player's or spectator's exploration deals it anywhere, and in a Randomized game may deal it out of the game (the nine denizens per suit are then drawn afresh). Every other known card keeps its place.
