# Ride the Rails: the table, the map and the panel

This contract fixes what players see and press. It follows `docs/ui-interaction-visual-contract.md`, `docs/game-ui-layout.md`, `docs/user-interactions.md`, `docs/phone-rendering.md` and the game UI animation skill. Rules are cited as `R-x.y` and the engine's conventions as `R-X.n`, labels listed in `games/ride-the-rails/docs/rules-interpretations.md`; the rules themselves live in the logic package's code. Colours, shapes and fonts are in `games/ride-the-rails-ui/docs/art-plan.md`.

The code and its tests are the source of truth: where this file and they differ, they win and this file is corrected.

The choices a player would notice are gathered in [Design choices](#design-choices), each with the alternative not taken.

## The principle

1. **The table is for looking.** The map draws everything a hex holds, so there is no detail to open. A railroad's card (on a phone, and in full screen, its card in the build panel), or a share in a seat card, spotlights that railroad: its cubes stay and every other railroad's fade. In full screen the railroad cards and the seat cards are outside the dialog, so at every width only the build panel's cards spotlight, on a building turn.
2. **A miss does nothing.** A press on a hex or city that is not lit changes nothing: it places, picks and opens nothing.
3. **The panel chooses.** Every choice without a place on the map is a button in the action panel: the share to take, Skip or Done, Ride. Ride also floats on the map beside the route it sends.
4. **Departure: a lit hex is picked on the map.** Placing a locomotive and choosing a journey are choices of a place: a hex among 386, a city among 51. Listing them in the panel would make the player read a map as a list, and `docs/game-ui-layout.md` binds players to act where the thing is. So a hex or city is picked by pressing it, but only while it is lit. A press on an unlit hex keeps rule 2, so a near miss never places anything. When a pick needs a follow-up (which railroad), a popup beside the hex asks it and the panel confirms nothing more: the popup's choice sends the action.

### Images and data

There are no images. The map, cubes, passengers, legend, seat and railroad cards and cover are drawn in code from the logic package's map data and the game state (`art-plan.md`). A railroad's piece, which the rules call a locomotive, is drawn as a flat rounded square in the railroad's colour with a dark edge and no letter. The board is a light, printed board on a pale mist table in the site's light and dark themes alike: cream land on a thin grid, a hatched sea, grey cities with cream centres, and no city names. Twelve questions of the look (palette, start mark, hex colouring, bonus mark, piece, passenger, start cue, the railroads in a lit hex, how a lit hex stands out, a city's centre, the map's frame, a journey's start) are each one pick in `utils/boardLook.ts`; the options not picked stay drawn in code for comparison (`art-plan.md`, Palette).

### Seat card

The players tab shows one card per seat, in the order the phase under way is played: the reverse of turn order while shares are taken (R-2.2), turn order otherwise (R-2.3). When the order changes, once the new state is shown, the cards slide to their new places. A card is a **paper card** on the table: edged on the left in the seat's colour, a seat-coloured disc and the name, then six share slots, one a round (R-2.1, R-3.1), filled with the seat's shares as cubes in railroad order (Red, Blue, Orange, Yellow, Purple, Black), an empty slot a dashed square; the money is the card's large figure, on the right. There is no turn position and no caption. The acting seat's card has a 3 px ring in its seat colour drawn inside its edge, so the side panel, which clips what overflows it, shows the ring whole. Pressing a held share spotlights that railroad (Railroad spotlight). While the deciding seat drafts a journey, each seat it would pay shows a pending "+$N" chip under its money (dividends plus fare, from the Journey preview). The card takes the board's paper and ink (`--rtr-panel`, `--rtr-ink`; `art-plan.md`, Palette).

## Visual intents

### Pick conventions

- **Lit:** a hex or city the deciding seat may press now, in a building turn and a journey alike, is a hole in the scrim. While a map pick is open, a light scrim (the paper's ink at 25%) lies over the land, never the sea, and each lit hex shows through it undimmed, with no ring and no fill. Panel-only steps never dim the map. (`BOARD_LOOK.litHex` keeps a deeper scrim, a thin white ring, a pale wash and a lit city painted cream as options.)
- **Lit for a start only:** a city where every placement it offers is a railroad's start is lit the same way. The board offer still tells the two kinds apart (its `start` ring), for a look option that draws them differently.
- **Picked** (an amber ring on a dark edge, over a soft amber fill): a staged hex. Pressing it again unpicks it.
- **A journey's start** (a navy ring on a white edge, the passenger grown in the middle of the hex): a marker, never a pick. Navy is a marker colour, so the start cannot be read as a city to press next.
- **Next city of a journey:** lit, with a large chevron disc centred on the city per railroad that can make the link (in the railroad's colour, the chevron in its ink, side by side where two railroads link it), pointing east, or west where the city lies west of the route's last city.
- **No ghost cubes.** A lit hex shows nothing of the railroads that may go there; the hex's accessible name says them, and the popup asks when there are two (U-2). (`BOARD_LOOK.litRailroads` keeps a ghost cube per railroad as an option.)
- **Gain and cost marks**, one wordless pill each on a lit hex, edged in the halo: a darker gold "$12" or "$8" up and left of the centre (some placement there completes the Transcontinental, with the price it would pay), and a rose "−1" at the bottom centre (a mountain while no mountain has been used this turn: the limit would drop). Chicago's $2 and a $5 still to be had are not marked again, since the city's own pill shows them; the hex's accessible name still says them. Marks come from the logic package's rule helpers, so a mark and what the engine pays never disagree.
- **Keyboard and touch.** Lit hexes are buttons in the accessibility tree, named by place and effect ("Chicago: place Orange or Black, $2"; a plain or mountain by the nearest city and the compass point it lies in from it, "Mountain west of Denver: place Red, −1"; where two hexes would share a name, both take a finer compass point and then their distance in hexes, "Plain two hexes west of Rapid City", so no two hexes share a name; a placement that ends the turn, in a long-distance hex or in Perth or Brisbane, says so last, "Perth: place Orange, $50, ends the turn"), reached by Tab in id order, pressed with Enter or Space. A lit hex's hit target is the whole hex. Nothing depends on hover: every hover effect also has a press.
- **No control floats over a pick target:** a floating control takes the first clear spot beside its anchor, and stays hidden when there is none. Clear means its box, as drawn at the current zoom and pan, overlaps no lit hex (a journey's start among them), no city a journey's route reaches (a press there cuts the route), no mark or chevron a lit hex draws, not its own anchor's hex and not the route's link count: each carries `data-keep-clear`, and `clearOfPicks` (`utils/popupPlacement.ts`) measures them on screen (`docs/phone-rendering.md`, Measuring under zoom). The control is placed again whenever the view or the picks change, so it comes back once a spot clears. The map's Ride button follows this rule ([Ride on the map](#ride-the-rails-choose-a-journey-riding-the-deciding-seat)).

### Look: railroad spotlight

**Trigger:** hovering (pointer) or pressing (touch, keyboard) a railroad's card (on a phone, its card in the build panel) or a held share in a seat card; a second press, or pressing another railroad, ends or moves it. **Shows:** every other railroad's cubes fade to a fifth of their strength; this railroad's stay whole. Nothing else on the board changes: no scrim, and a map pick's scrim and lit set stay as they are. **Unaffected:** lit hexes, staged picks, the panel.

### Take a share (`TakingShares`, the deciding seat)

The panel prompt reads "Select a share below". The share buttons are one per railroad available this round (R-1.4) in railroad order, each the same button (`ShareButton.svelte`) wherever it stands: its supply as a cube and the number left, with no words. On desktop the railroad cards above the board are the buttons, and the panel keeps only its prompt; on a phone the buttons stand in one row below the prompt, with the seat's own shares as dots in its colour under the count ([Phone layout](#phone-layout)). In full screen the dialog leaves the cards out of reach, so at every width the buttons stand in that row in the panel in the toolbar, reached by press and by keyboard. A railroad not yet available is not shown; one with an empty supply is a dashed button, disabled (R-1.1). Each button's accessible name is the whole choice: "Take a Red share, 16 left, you hold 1", or "Red: none left". Pressing an enabled button sends `TakeShare` (one press), and the share's locomotive lifts from that button's cube. The map is not dimmed and nothing on it is lit.

### Build: place a locomotive (`Building`, the deciding seat)

- **Lit:** every hex where some railroad the seat holds may be placed now (the placement test, rules R-3.2 and R-4.1 to R-4.2), with its marks. Starts light only the start cities each railroad's rule allows (R-4.2); until Yellow's first placement, its starts are Chicago and the westernmost occupied cities that hold exactly one locomotive, and after it Yellow lights only next to its own track (R-4.2.3). While Black is unstarted, nearly every city is lit for its start.
- **Press a lit hex with one possible railroad:** sends `PlaceLocomotive` at once. The locomotive lands, the cubes already in the hex slide to their new slots, any bonus is paid, and the lit set refreshes.
- **Press a lit hex with two or more:** the hex becomes picked and a popup beside it shows one button per possible railroad (U-2), with no words: the railroad's cube; a pill at its corner only when that placement earns a bonus ("+$5" green, "+$2" gold, "+$12" or "+$8" for completing the Transcontinental; a Black start in an empty $5 city uses the bonus up, so it shows none, R-4.3.2); and a small peak at its foot when the hex is the turn's first mountain, which lowers the limit (R-3.2.4). Each button's accessible name says the whole choice: "Place Orange, 8 left, pays $5", "Start Black, 6 left, pays nothing: a start uses up the $5", "Place Purple, 22 left, mountain: one fewer placement this turn, completes the Transcontinental: pays $12", and in a long-distance hex, Perth or Brisbane "…, ends the turn" (R-8.2); the popup is named "<place>: which railroad?". Choosing a button sends `PlaceLocomotive`. Undo, or pressing the picked hex again, closes the popup.
- **Panel:** on desktop, the prompt "Choose spaces on the map to build track", then on the next line the count "2 / 5" in large figures (R-3.2.1, R-3.2.3), the rose "−1" pill once a mountain has lowered the limit (R-3.2.4; the map's mountain reduction, 1 on the USA; the count then reads "2 / 4"), and the button. On a phone, and in full screen at every width (the dialog leaves the cards out of reach), the count, the −1 and the button stand on one line, and below them a card per railroad the seat may place now (`buildableRailroads`, the set that lights the hexes, so a railroad that empties or has no legal hex drops out), each its cube and the number left. The count's and the −1's accessible text says them in words ("2 of 4 placed, Mountain used: limit 4"). A press on a card holds that railroad's spotlight and narrows the zoom, as a desktop card does; placed locomotives lift from these cards. One button: Done, which reads Skip before the first placement (R-3.2.1 "may"). There is no other mountain or limit text. When nothing more is legal the turn ends by itself (`FinishBuilding`), and the panel goes to the next view.
- **This turn's placements** are marked for every viewer with a thin seat-coloured bar under each locomotive placed this building turn, so others can follow the turn as it happens. The bars clear when the building turn ends.
- **Zoom to the lit hexes** (the deciding seat's view only, desktop and phone; `BuildZoom` and `buildZoomRequest` in `utils/boardFocus.ts`). When the turn's offer first lights hexes, the board zooms to fit them all: their boxes' union, a hex width wider on every side and kept inside the board. It never zooms in past a hex 64 px wide (`BUILD_ZOOM_MAX_HEX_PX`) nor past the board's largest zoom, and never out below the view's resting scale: the whole map on desktop, and on phones the middle third's scale, a hex at least 37 px. A view that rests on the whole map moves only for a fit that draws a hex at least 1.1 times as wide as the rest does (`MIN_BUILD_ZOOM_GAIN`); a smaller gain would cut the board's edges for a hex a pixel or two wider, so the whole map stays, and it stays through the turn's next lit hexes while every one is on it. On a phone, lit hexes too far apart to fit at the resting scale keep it, centred on those nearest the view's centre, as many as fit together. Building stays hex first (U-2): there is no railroad step.
    - **Held spotlight:** pressing a railroad's card (on a phone, its build panel card) or a seat card's held share narrows the zoom to that railroad's lit hexes, and releasing it returns to all of them. A railroad with no lit hex this turn moves nothing. A hovered spotlight never moves the view.
    - **After a placement:** the view stays while every lit hex is still whole inside it (for lit hexes too far apart to fit, those nearest its centre), and fits again only once one has left it, so the view does not jump at every press.
    - **By hand:** a pan, a zoom, or a key focus that pans to a hex lets the zoom go until the next turn or the next held spotlight change.
    - **Turn end** (Done or Skip, or `FinishBuilding`): the view returns to where it was before the turn's zoom: the whole map on desktop, the resting view on phones (the middle third). In hotseat play the next seat's zoom follows instead, and the view from before the first zoom is kept for the last seat's end. A pan or a zoom holds only until the next turn or held spotlight change, and the last turn's end returns to the view from before the first zoom even if that seat moved the view by hand. The next phase's picks span the map, so it opens on the view the table had before building.
    - With reduced motion every move jumps instead of gliding. Watchers, History View, Take a share and Riding keep their views; Riding keeps the phone's pan (Phone layout). Verified by `utils/boardFocus.spec.ts` and `tests/autoZoom.spec.ts`.

### Ride the Rails: choose a journey (`Riding`, the deciding seat)

A journey is a staged selection of two stages: the start, then the links (a stacked stage, `docs/user-interactions.md`, Stacked stages). A seat with no journey to make never sees this view: the Riding state passes for it ([No journey possible](#no-journey-possible)).

- **Start.** Lit: each city holding a passenger from which at least one link leaves. The panel prompt reads "Choose a passenger". Pressing one picks it: its passenger grows from its place in the hex's top right to the middle of the hex (a local transition of the staged route, `board/StagedRouteLayer.svelte`, about a quarter of a second, none with reduced motion) and the city takes the navy start ring (Pick conventions). No railroad is asked at the start.
- **Extend.** Lit: every city one more link can reach from the route's last city without reusing a hex, each with its chevrons (Pick conventions); its accessible name names the railroads that can make that link. Pressing a lit city that one railroad links pushes that link. Only where two railroads link the same pair of cities does a popup beside the city offer a cube button per railroad, with a gold "+$N" pill for what the journey would then pay the seat, and an accessible name that says it ("Link by Orange, you +$5"). Each link takes its shortest way (fewest hexes; a tie goes to hex order) that leaves the later links possible, so a one-link draft never detours and keeps its hexes when a link is added unless the new link needs one of them. The route draws as a line through the cubes it rides (the link's railroad's cube in each hex), each link in its railroad's colour over a dark halo, those cubes drawn again on top; each new link draws itself from the city before to the city it reaches. The link count sits in a dark disc centred on the route's last city. No dot marks the cities visited.
- **Cut back.** Pressing a city the route already reaches cuts the route back to there; pressing the start cuts it back to the start alone, and pressing the start when it has no link drops the draft. A cut is one stacked stage (`linksKeptAt`, `cutDraftLinks` in `model/journeyStage.ts`), so Undo puts the cut links back.
- **Panel:** before a start, the prompt; then the route by its ends, with the number of cities between them once it passes any ("Seattle → Boise, via 1 city"), "2 links · fare $3", then one chip per seat the journey pays, the rider's own among them, in seat colour with "$N", and **Ride**, which sends `RideJourney` and is enabled from the first link.
- **Ride on the map.** From the first link a Ride button also floats beside the route's last city, 6 px from its hex, and is hidden while a link popup is open (`board/BoardPopups.svelte`). It never covers a pick (Pick conventions, No control floats over a pick target): it tries the side the route did not come in from, then the side across, below and above (on a phone below, above, then those two sides), then the corners nearest its first side, and stands at the first that is clear and inside the board's view. Where none is, as on the whole map beside a crowded city, it hides until a pan or zoom clears a spot, and the panel's Ride still sends the journey.
- **After Ride** the draft is put away so the passenger can travel on the flight layer, but the panel keeps showing the sent journey (`sentJourney`) until the new Game State is shown, so it never asks for a new start while the journey plays.
- **Undo** pops the last link or cut, then the start, one per press.

### No journey possible

R-3.3.1: a seat with no journey to make passes by itself. On its turn the Riding state takes `PassJourney` as a System Action; since no journey for one seat means none for anyone, every later seat passes in the same cascade and the round ends. There is no Pass button and no view for it: the panel goes from the last journey to the next round. The log shows each pass ("Ben passed: no journey was possible").

### Last journey

After a journey, the seat now deciding starts its turn on a clean board: in Live View the last journey is not drawn for that seat. Every other viewer (the seat that rode included) sees it until the next action is made, through the cubes it rode, thinner than a staged route but over the same dark halo, so it reads in every railroad colour (Yellow on plain hexes included). The waiting view and the log still name the ride. In History View it shows the displayed action's journey, for every viewer. It never takes presses.

### Watching (waiting view)

For a seat that is not deciding:

- **Take a share:** "‹Name› is taking a share", the seats still to take in phase 1's reverse order, and the last share taken ("Ben took Blue").
- **Build:** "‹Name› is building · 2 of 5 placed", and the railroads they may build: only those with a legal placement now, so a railroad they hold with nowhere left to go is not listed. Their placements appear live with this turn's bars.
- **Ride:** "‹Name› is choosing a journey". After each journey, its line, a long route by its ends: "Ben: Seattle → Chicago, via 2 cities, 6 links, fare $7; you +$3".
- Every waiting view ends with a line of seat chips in turn order with each seat's money, since money decides turn order (R-2.5.1).

### End of round and game end

- **End of round** has no view of its own: it happens in the last journey's (or pass's) cascade. The history description and the log show the new turn order, with each seat's money, and the railroad made available.
- **Game end:** the action area shows the winner or winners (R-6.2: every seat tied for most) in a larger line with each winner's dot ("Ada wins with $44", or "Ada and Cora share the win with $44" and "tied for most money: both win"), then a small table of every seat's final money, most first: place, dot and name left-aligned, money; the winners' rows bold. It shows no shares.

### History View

The action area shows the history description for the displayed step. Nothing is lit, no scrim, no popups, no staged route; the last-journey line and this-turn bars follow the displayed state. The railroad spotlight, the bonus chips' descriptions and the Transcontinental line's toggle still work, since they only look, the descriptions reading the displayed state; the claimed line follows the toggle wherever the displayed state has a claim.

## Coexistence and precedence

One semantic derivation, the **board offer** (Shared visual state), decides the lit set, the scrim, the marks and the popups. Map layers draw its result.

| Overlap                                       | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A map pick and railroad spotlight             | Coexist. The pick's scrim and lit set stay; the spotlight only fades the other railroads' cubes.                                                                                                                |
| The railroad popup and Undo                   | Undo closes the popup first (it is the latest manual stage).                                                                                                                                                    |
| The link popup and the map's Ride button      | The Ride button hides while a link popup is open, so the two never stand together. Nor does it stand over a lit city: it takes the first clear spot beside the route's last city, or hides while there is none. |
| A sent journey and the next Game State        | The panel shows the sent journey until the new state is shown; the new state clears it.                                                                                                                         |
| The staged route and the last-journey line    | Impossible: only the deciding seat drafts, and its board never draws the last journey in Live View.                                                                                                             |
| A staged pick and History View                | History View wins: entering it hides every pick, scrim and popup; leaving it shows the live offer again, rebuilt from the live state.                                                                           |
| A staged pick and another seat's action       | Impossible in this title: only the deciding seat acts, and every action is sequential.                                                                                                                          |
| A staged pick and a new Game State            | The new state clears every staged selection (default `keepsLocalSelections`), so a build popup or route never outlives the state it was made on.                                                                |
| Pans, zooms, full screen and any other intent | Coexist: they change only the view. A staged pick survives them.                                                                                                                                                |
| The building zoom and a hand move             | The hand wins: a pan, zoom or key focus lets the zoom go until the next turn or held spotlight change, and the turn's end leaves the view where the hand put it.                                                |
| The building zoom and the phone's pan         | Impossible: the pan follows Riding offers only, and the zoom building offers only.                                                                                                                              |

## Shared visual state

The session owns these. Components read them and call its methods.

- **Build stage** (`hex`): the picked hex while its railroad popup is open. Producer: a press on a lit hex with two or more railroads. Consumers: the hex's ring, the popup. Lifetime: a button press (sends), Undo, a second press on the hex, a new Game State. It is registered as the session's local selection, so the shared Undo pops it before it undoes any action. Validity: only in `Building`, for the deciding seat, while the hex is still in the offer. History View: hidden.
- **Journey draft** (`start`, then stacked `links`, each named by the city it reaches and its railroad, then `railroadChoice`: the city whose link popup is open). Producer: presses on lit cities, link popups, presses on cities the route reaches (a cut, pushed as one stacked value). The hexes sent are the first realisation of the named route, and a city is lit for a railroad when some realisation of the whole route reaches it. The route is realised a link at a time: while the next link can follow the hexes already chosen it takes the first way that does, with no search, so every city lit from those hexes realises, whatever the cap. Only a city that needs an earlier link to go another way starts a search, which steps only into hexes from which the link's city can still be reached and, once a link's first way fails, tries its other ways only where they leave every hex a later link must have; it gives up after `REALISE_EXPANSION_CAP` steps, which only a route whose later links contend for the same hexes, with no hex one of them must have, can reach, and that city stays unlit. Consumers: the map's route line, the grown start passenger, the lit set and chevrons, the map's Ride button, the panel's route and payments, the seat cards' pending "+$N" chips. Lifetime: Ride (sends), Undo pops, a press on the start with no link clears, a new Game State clears. Validity: only in `Riding`, for the deciding seat; a draft is re-checked against the displayed state and reads as empty if any link no longer holds. History View: hidden.
- **Sent journey** (`sentJourney`: the draft and preview just sent). Producer: Ride. Consumer: the journey panel, while the transition plays. Cleared when the new Game State is shown.
- **Journey preview** (derived, never stored): links per railroad, the fare, each seat's payment, from the draft and the shares. Consumers: the panel, the seat cards' pending chips, the map's link count. It uses the logic package's payment helper, so the preview equals what `RideJourney` pays.
- **Board offer** (derived, never stored): from the displayed state, the valid actions for this seat and the stages. Effects allowed: lit set, marks, chevrons, scrim holes, which popup is open (and, under a look option, ghost cubes). Consumers: every map layer. History View and other seats: empty.
- **Railroad spotlight** (`railroadId`): producer, the railroad cards (the build panel's cards on a phone and in full screen) and seat cards' held shares; consumers, the track layer, which fades every other railroad's cubes, and the building zoom when held. Cleared by a second press or pointer leave (hover only). Survives Game States and history navigation.
- **Transcontinental line shown** (a yes or no, this viewer's own, not game state): producer, the toggle in the Transcontinental chip's description, offered only while the displayed state has a claim; consumers, the line layer, the chip's gold outline, and the claim's animator, which fades the settled line as its coin lands unless it is shown. Starts hidden; survives Game States and history navigation, not a reload.
- **Bonus description open** (a bonus id or none, this viewer's own, not game state): producer, the bonus chip in the turn line (a press opens its description, a second press, a press anywhere outside the chip and the description, or Escape closes it); consumers, the chip (`aria-expanded`) and its description. One at a time. Closed when a Game State arrives in which another turn is under way (or the game has ended) and when the board is pressed, so it never stays over the deciding seat's offer. Opening it takes no game action. Starts closed; not kept across a reload.
- **Animation transients**: a travelling locomotive, a cube sliding to its new slot, a travelling passenger, a coin chip. Producer: the state-change animators. Consumers: the flight layer and the track layer. Scoped to the action being animated; cleared on its timeline's end or teardown.

## Render ownership

The board is **one SVG** (`docs/phone-rendering.md`, One board surface). Back to front:

1. Ground and map: the sea as a rounded card with its 45° hatch, plain hexes on their grid, mountain and city hexes as pale-edged patches, each city's cream centre inset in its grey, mountain peaks, the thin sea-edge line.
2. Look options for a lit hex (a wash, or a lit city painted cream); nothing with the look picked.
3. In the sea: the capacity marks and the map's title.
4. Cities: start bands, Yellow joining a band where it may start now.
5. Start cues as dashed outlines (a look option; nothing with the look picked).
6. Bonus pills at each city's bottom centre (none while riding).
7. Track: cubes (other railroads' faded under a spotlight), this turn's bars.
8. The claimed Transcontinental line (thin gold, while the viewer shows it with the toggle in the chip's description), then the last journey line through its cubes over its halo (not drawn for the seat now deciding, in Live View).
9. **Scrim**: one path, the land's outline with holes at the lit hexes (even-odd fill). It is a plain translucent fill, not an opacity or filter effect, and never covers the sea.
10. Impassable sides (Canada, France): a red line on a pale casing along each, over the bands, cubes, lines and the scrim, so no band, cube or dark hex hides one and a pick never dims it. It takes no presses.
11. Passengers waiting (wooden meeples), but not a picked journey start's: over the cubes, since each stands above its own city's cubes with its head in the hex above, over that hex's cubes where it reaches them, and over an impassable side's red line where a head or an arm reaches one (Caen's across the Seine; Nicolet's and Sorel-Tracy's on Canada's river). Two passes of the passenger layer, the unlit cities' (`cities="unlit"`) then the lit cities' (`cities="lit"`), both above the scrim. A lit city's passenger is never dimmed: its head stands in the hex above, outside its own hex's hole. In a pick, an unlit city's passenger dims itself, its whole figure, with the scrim's own fill laid over its shape (a fill's own alpha on one shape, as the scrim is).
12. The start legend, above the scrim so it is never dimmed.
13. Staged route: its line, the cubes it rides drawn again, the start passenger grown in the middle of its hex, and the link count on the last city.
14. Picks: the picked ring, the journey start's ring, marks and chevrons (the board offer).
15. Flight layer: travelling locomotives, passengers and coins.
16. Hit targets: transparent hex shapes carrying hex ids, the only map elements that take presses.

The board holds nothing but the map and what stands in its sea: the railroad cards, the round tickets and the Transcontinental chip are HTML outside the scaling (The table). Popups (the railroad buttons, and the map's Ride button) are HTML in the `Floater` against a reference point at the hex, outside the SVG, so their text stays crisp at any zoom (`docs/phone-rendering.md`, HTML text under zoom). The view's moves (the building zoom, the Riding pan and the phone's rest) follow from the `ScalingWrapper`'s `overlay` snippet (`components/BoardViewFollower.svelte`). Rings, marks and chevrons are sized by `viewScale()` so they stay readable at any zoom. The board draws no seat initials: the claimant in the Transcontinental chip is HTML outside the scaling (The table), at a fixed screen size. No SVG or CSS filters, masks or endless animations on the board.

## Game log and history

- **One entry per player turn**, grouped (`docs/game-ui-layout.md`, Group), each a card with the seat's dot and its name in plain text (the dot is enough to say whose it is):
    - a share ("Ana took ■", the railroad as a square of its colour);
    - a building turn that grows as placements arrive, each railroad's count before its colour square ("Ana built 2 ■ 1 ■ · Chicago $2 · Denver $5"), closing at its end; a turn that ended with nothing legal left says so, one that used up its placements gives no reason;
    - a journey in three lines, "Ana made a journey:", its ends ("New York → Albany"), and "4 total cities – Fare $4", then a small table of every seat it paid (dot, name, amount, fare and dividends together, most first), with no link counts per railroad;
    - a pass.
- **Interstitials:** a new round is a banner across the list with that round's ticket; each phase a small label under it. A round's end is a small card: "Round N ends", the new turn order as a table (place, dot, name, money) and the railroad made available. Both jump.
- A railroad is always its colour square in a line of text, its name kept for screen readers; elsewhere in text, a long route is its ends and "via N cities".
- **Skipped:** `SkipShare` is folded into its seat's entry; `FinishBuilding` closes its building entry rather than standing alone.
- **Jump** on round and turn entries. **Replay** on building turns (placements land in order) and journeys (the passenger travels). Shares, passes and the end of round have nothing to replay on the map.
- Text is built from each action's input and metadata (the logic package records bonuses, payments, the new order and the railroad made available).

## Design choices

- **U-1 Board picks** for hexes and cities, lit only when legal. _Alternative:_ a panel list of hexes. Rejected (the principle, rule 4).
- **U-2 Hex first, railroad second, and only when ambiguous.** Most presses place at once; the popup appears only when two railroads may go to one hex, or link the same pair of cities on a journey. _Alternative:_ choose a railroad in the panel, then its hexes. Rejected: a building turn often mixes railroads (R-3.2.2), and each switch would cost a press.
- **U-3 Each placement is sent at once** and undone one at a time, matching the logic's one action per placement.
- **U-4 Journeys built city by city**, and cut back by pressing a city already on the route. _Alternatives:_ pick only an end city and let the engine route (takes the railroad choice from the player, R-3.3.2); or a route list in the panel (unreadable with many routes).
- **U-5 An unlit press does nothing.** A near miss never acts. _Earlier:_ it opened the hex's detail beside it; the playtest of 2026-11-16 removed the detail cards, since the map draws what a hex holds.
- **U-6 Railroad colours are rule colours, never seat colours.** Cubes always carry the railroad colour and no letter; every accessible name carries the railroad's name; the cards, buttons and colour squares in text draw none (the colour is the label). Seats appear only as dots, discs, chips, bars and card edges.
- **U-7 The last journey is cleared for the seat now deciding** and stays drawn for everyone else until the next action, so a seat that looked away sees what was carried, while the seat choosing never reads the last ride as part of its own. History View draws it for everyone. _Alternative:_ keep it for every seat until the deciding seat's first action. Rejected: the next seat read the old route as its own.
- **U-8 A seat with no journey passes by itself** (R-3.3.1), as a System Action of the Riding state. _Earlier:_ a Pass button as the only choice; a press with one possible answer is no choice.
