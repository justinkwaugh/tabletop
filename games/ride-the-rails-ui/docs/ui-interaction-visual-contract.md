# Ride the Rails: the table, the map and the panel

This contract fixes what players see and press. It follows `docs/ui-interaction-visual-contract.md`, `docs/game-ui-layout.md`, `docs/user-interactions.md`, `docs/phone-rendering.md` and the game UI animation skill. Rules are cited as `R-x.y` (`games/ride-the-rails/docs/rules-digest.md`), house rulings as `R-x.y-Hn` and implementation rules as `R-X.n` (`games/ride-the-rails/docs/rulings.md`). Colours, shapes and fonts are in `games/ride-the-rails-ui/docs/art-plan.md`.

The code and its tests are the source of truth: where this file and they differ, they win and this file is corrected.

The choices a player would notice are gathered in [Design choices](#design-choices), each with the alternative not taken.

## The principle

1. **The table is for looking.** A press on anything that is not lit shows it: a hex or city opens its detail beside it; a railroad's card (on a phone, its pile in the build panel), or a share row in a seat card, spotlights that railroad's track.
2. **A press enlarges.** A detail stays until the next press, Escape or its close control. That press does nothing else.
3. **The panel chooses.** Every choice without a place on the map is a button in the action panel: the share to take, Done building, Ride, Pass.
4. **Departure: a lit hex is picked on the map.** Placing a locomotive and choosing a journey are choices of a place: a hex among 386, a city among 51. Listing them in the panel would make the player read a map as a list, and `docs/game-ui-layout.md` binds players to act where the thing is. So a hex or city is picked by pressing it, but only while it is lit. A press on an unlit hex keeps rule 1 (it shows the hex), so a near miss never places anything. When a pick needs a follow-up (which railroad), a popup beside the hex asks it and the panel confirms nothing more: the popup's choice sends the action.

### Images and data

There are no images. The map, cubes, passengers, supply piles, seat and railroad cards and cover are drawn in code from the logic package's map data and the game state (`art-plan.md`). A railroad's piece, which the rules call a locomotive, is drawn as a flat rounded square in the railroad's colour with a dark edge and no letter. The board is a light, printed board on the table in the site's light and dark themes alike: cream land on a thin grid, a hatched sea in a navy frame, grey cities, serif names. The palette, start mark, hex colouring, bonus mark, piece, passenger, start cue and the railroads in a lit hex are each one pick in `utils/boardLook.ts` (`art-plan.md`, Palette).

### Seat card

The players tab shows one card per seat, in turn order. It is a **paper card** on the table: edged on the left in the seat's colour, a seat-coloured disc, the name and turn position, then one row per railroad the seat holds, in railroad order (Red, Blue, Orange, Yellow, Purple, Black), each row showing the shares as a small cube with the count beside it, and the money as the card's large figure. A railroad not yet held has no row. The acting seat's card has an accent ring (3 px, with a 1.5 px dark halo) drawn inside its edge and its seat-colour bar, so the side panel, which clips what overflows it, shows the ring whole. Pressing a share row spotlights that railroad (Railroad spotlight). While the deciding seat drafts a journey, each seat it would pay shows a pending "+$N" chip under its money (dividends plus fare, from the Journey preview). The card takes the board's paper and ink (`--rtr-panel-raised`, `--rtr-ink`; `art-plan.md`, Palette).

## Visual intents

### Pick conventions

- **Lit** (a bright amber ring on a dark edge just inside the hex, over a soft amber fill): a hex or city the deciding seat may press now, in a building turn and a journey alike. While a map pick is open, a scrim (the paper's ink at 45%) dims the rest of the map; lit hexes show through holes in it. Panel-only steps never dim the map.
- **Lit for a start only** (a fainter amber ring over the same fill): a city where every placement it offers is a railroad's start. While Black is unstarted, its start (R-4.2.5-H1) lights almost every city, so start-only cities are drawn quieter than hexes that extend a railroad's network, and the extensions stand out. A hex that offers both a start and an extension takes the full ring. Both are equally pressable.
- **Picked** (the same ring, heavier): a staged hex or city. Pressing it again unpicks it.
- **A journey's start** (a navy ring on a white edge and a navy start flag): a marker, never a pick. Navy is a marker colour, so the start cannot be read as a city to press next; pressing it still clears the draft.
- **Next city of a journey** (the lit ring, plus a chip at the city's lower right per railroad that can make the link: a disc in the railroad's colour with a chevron in its ink).
- **Ghost cubes.** A hex lit for a build shows a ghost of each railroad that may go there, in the slot its cube would take: a pale square of the railroad's colour with a dashed edge in it (a second railroad in the next free slot; where fewer slots are free than railroads, the ghosts share the first, smaller and spread apart). The ghosts are the words-free statement of "which railroad"; the hex carries no name while lit. A city lit for a journey shows none: the railroads that link there already stand in it.
- **Gain and cost marks**, one wordless mark each at the hex's lower right, edged in the halo: a gold pill "$2" (Chicago), a green pill "$5" (a $5 city still to be had, when some railroad's placement there earns it), a darker gold pill "$12" or "$8" (some placement there completes the Transcontinental, with the price it would pay), and a rose "−1" (a mountain while no mountain has been used this turn: the limit would drop). Several marks stack outward. Marks come from the logic package's rule helpers, so a mark and what the engine pays never disagree.
- **Keyboard and touch.** Lit hexes are buttons in the accessibility tree, named by place and effect ("Chicago: place Orange or Black, $2"; a plain or mountain by the nearest city and the compass point it lies in from it, "Mountain west of Denver: place Red, −1"; where two hexes would share a name, both take a finer compass point and then their distance in hexes, "Plain two hexes west of Rapid City", so no two hexes share a name), reached by Tab in id order, pressed with Enter or Space. A lit hex's hit target is the whole hex. Nothing depends on hover: every hover effect also has a press.

### Look: hex detail

**Trigger:** a press on a hex or city that is not lit, at any time, in Live or History View. **Shows:** a popup beside the hex (during a map pick on wider screens, the side panel instead; see Coexistence and precedence) with its name (or terrain), capacity, the locomotives in it by railroad, the start colours it shows, its bonus and whether it is still to be had, and whether a passenger waits there. **Unaffected:** the scrim, the lit set, any staged pick. **Ends:** the next press anywhere, Escape, the close control, or a new Game State that moves the board.

### Look: railroad spotlight

**Trigger:** hovering (pointer) or pressing (touch, keyboard) a railroad's card (on a phone, its pile in the build panel) or a share row in a seat card; a second press, or pressing another railroad, ends or moves it. **Shows:** with no map pick open, every other railroad's track dims under the scrim and this railroad's track stays bright above it, its starts outlined. With a map pick open, the pick's scrim stays and this railroad's track gets an outline instead. **Unaffected:** lit hexes, staged picks, the panel.

### Take a share (`TakingShares`, the deciding seat)

The panel prompt reads "Take a share". The share buttons are the supply piles, one per railroad available this round (R-1.4) in railroad order, with no words: the pile draws the supply left, and the seat's own shares are dots in its colour. On desktop the railroad cards above the board are the buttons, and the panel keeps only its prompt and the order line; on a phone the buttons stand in one row below the prompt ([Phone layout](#phone-layout)). A railroad not yet available is not shown; one with an empty supply is a dashed ghost, disabled (R-1.1-H1). Each button's accessible name is the whole choice: "Take a Red share, 16 left, you hold 1", or "Red: none left". Pressing an enabled button sends `TakeShare` (one press), and the share's locomotive lifts from that pile. The map is not dimmed and nothing on it is lit: the railroad strip on the board is for looking only.

### Build: place a locomotive (`Building`, the deciding seat)

- **Lit:** every hex where some railroad the seat holds may be placed now (the placement test, rules R-3.2 and R-4.1 to R-4.2), with its ghost cubes and marks. Starts light only the start cities each railroad's rule allows (R-4.2); Yellow's western start lights the fixed westernmost set's cities that hold exactly one locomotive (R-4.2.3-H1, -H2). While Black is unstarted, nearly every city is lit for its start; those that offer only a start take the fainter start ring (Pick conventions).
- **Press a lit hex with one possible railroad:** sends `PlaceLocomotive` at once. The locomotive lands, any bonus is paid, and the lit set refreshes.
- **Press a lit hex with two or more:** the hex becomes picked and a popup beside it shows one button per possible railroad (U-2), with no words: the railroad's cube; a pill at its corner only when that placement earns a bonus ("+$5" green, "+$2" gold, "+$12" or "+$8" for completing the Transcontinental; a Black start in an empty $5 city uses the bonus up, so it shows none, R-4.3.2-H1); and a small peak at its foot when the hex is the turn's first mountain, which lowers the limit (R-3.2.4). Each button's accessible name says the whole choice: "Place Orange, 8 left, pays $5", "Start Black, 6 left, pays nothing: a start uses up the $5", "Place Purple, 22 left, mountain: one fewer placement this turn, completes the Transcontinental: pays $12"; the popup is named "<place>: which railroad?". Choosing a button sends `PlaceLocomotive`. Undo, or pressing the picked hex again, closes the popup.
- **Panel:** the prompt "Place locomotives", then "2 of 5 placed", and after a mountain "Mountain used: limit 4" (R-3.2.4). On a phone it is one line instead: the count "2/5" ("1/4" after a mountain; its accessible text says it in words), the supply piles of the railroads the seat may place now (`buildableRailroads`, the set that lights the hexes, so a railroad that empties or has no legal hex drops out), and the button. A press on a pile holds that railroad's spotlight and narrows the zoom, as a card does on desktop; placed locomotives lift from these piles. One button: Done building, which reads Build nothing before the first placement (R-3.2.1 "may"). When nothing more is legal the turn ends by itself (`FinishBuilding`), and the panel goes to the next view.
- **This turn's placements** are marked for every viewer with a thin seat-coloured bar under each locomotive placed this building turn, so others can follow the turn as it happens. The bars clear when the building turn ends.
- **Zoom to the lit hexes** (the deciding seat's view only, desktop and phone; `BuildZoom` and `buildZoomRequest` in `utils/boardFocus.ts`). When the turn's offer first lights hexes, the board zooms to fit them all: their boxes' union, a hex width wider on every side and kept inside the board. It never zooms in past a hex 64 px wide (`BUILD_ZOOM_MAX_HEX_PX`) nor past the board's largest zoom, and never out below the view's resting scale: the whole map on desktop, and on phones the middle third's scale, a hex at least 37 px. A view that rests on the whole map moves only for a fit that draws a hex at least 1.1 times as wide as the rest does (`MIN_BUILD_ZOOM_GAIN`); a smaller gain would cut the board's edges for a hex a pixel or two wider, so the whole map stays, and it stays through the turn's next lit hexes while every one is on it. On a phone, lit hexes too far apart to fit at the resting scale keep it, centred on those nearest the view's centre, as many as fit together. Building stays hex first (U-2): there is no railroad step.
    - **Held spotlight:** pressing a railroad's card (on a phone, its pile) or a seat card's share row narrows the zoom to that railroad's lit hexes, and releasing it returns to all of them. A railroad with no lit hex this turn moves nothing. A hovered spotlight never moves the view.
    - **After a placement:** the view stays while every lit hex is still whole inside it (for lit hexes too far apart to fit, those nearest its centre), and fits again only once one has left it, so the view does not jump at every press.
    - **By hand:** a pan, a zoom, or a key focus that pans to a hex lets the zoom go until the next turn or the next held spotlight change.
    - **Turn end** (Done building, or `FinishBuilding`): the view returns to where it was before the turn's zoom: the whole map on desktop, the resting view on phones (the middle third). In hotseat play the next seat's zoom follows instead, and the view from before the first zoom is kept for the last seat's end. A pan or a zoom holds only until the next turn or held spotlight change, and the last turn's end returns to the view from before the first zoom even if that seat moved the view by hand. The next phase's picks span the map, so it opens on the view the table had before building.
    - With reduced motion every move jumps instead of gliding. Watchers, History View, Take a share and Riding keep their views; Riding keeps the phone's pan (Phone layout). Verified by `utils/boardFocus.spec.ts` and `tests/autoZoom.spec.ts`.

### Ride the Rails: choose a journey (`Riding`, the deciding seat)

A journey is a staged selection of two stages: the start, then the links (a stacked stage, `docs/user-interactions.md`, Stacked stages).

- **Start.** Lit: each city holding a passenger from which at least one link leaves. The panel prompt reads "Choose a passenger". Pressing one picks it: its passenger lifts slightly and the city takes the navy start ring and flag (Pick conventions).
- **Extend.** Lit: every city one more link can reach from the route's last city without reusing a hex; its accessible name names the railroads that can make that link, and a chevron chip per railroad shows them. Pressing a lit city with one railroad pushes that link; with several, a popup beside the city offers a cube button per railroad, with a gold "+$N" pill for what the journey would then pay the seat, and an accessible name that says it ("Link by Orange, you +$5"). Each link takes its shortest way (fewest hexes; a tie goes to hex order) that leaves the later links possible, so a one-link draft never detours and keeps its hexes when a link is added unless the new link needs one of them. The route draws as a line through the cubes it rides (the link's railroad's cube in each hex), each link in its railroad's colour over a dark halo, those cubes drawn again on top, with a station dot on the cube at every city visited and the link count at the last city.
- **Panel:** the route as its cities ("Seattle → Portland → Boise"), "3 links · fare $4", then one chip per seat that a dividend reaches, in seat colour, with "+$N", and the seat's own total. **Ride · you +$N** sends `RideJourney`; it is enabled from the first link.
- **Undo** pops the last link, then the start, one per press; pressing the picked start again clears the whole draft.
- **No journey possible** (R-3.3.1-H1): the prompt reads "No passenger can travel: no link leaves any passenger's city" and the only button is Pass. Nothing is lit and the map is not dimmed.

### Last journey

After a journey, the seat now deciding starts its turn on a clean board: in Live View the last journey is not drawn for that seat. Every other viewer (the seat that rode included) sees it until the next action is made, through the cubes it rode, thinner than a staged route but over the same dark halo, so it reads in every railroad colour (Yellow on plain hexes included). The waiting view and the log still name the ride. In History View it shows the displayed action's journey, for every viewer. It never takes presses.

### Watching (waiting view)

For a seat that is not deciding:

- **Take a share:** "‹Name› is taking a share", the seats still to take in phase 1's reverse order, and the last share taken ("Ben took Blue").
- **Build:** "‹Name› is building · 2 of 5 placed", and the railroads they may build: only those with a legal placement now, so a railroad they hold with nowhere left to go is not listed. Their placements appear live with this turn's bars.
- **Ride:** "‹Name› is choosing a journey". After each journey, its line: "Ben: Seattle → Chicago, 6 links, fare $7; you +$3".
- Every waiting view ends with a line of seat chips in turn order with each seat's money, since money decides turn order (R-2.5.1).

### End of round and game end

- **End of round** has no view of its own: it happens in the last journey's cascade. The history description and the log show the new turn order, with each seat's money, and the railroad made available.
- **Game end:** the action area shows the final money in order, the winner or winners (R-6.2-H1: every seat tied for most), and each seat's shares.

### History View

The action area shows the history description for the displayed step. Nothing is lit, no scrim, no popups, no staged route; the last-journey line and this-turn bars follow the displayed state. Hex detail, railroad spotlight and the Transcontinental chip's toggle still work, since they only look; the claimed line follows the toggle wherever the displayed state has a claim.

## Coexistence and precedence

One semantic derivation, the **board offer** (Shared visual state), decides the lit set, the scrim, the marks and the popups. Map layers draw its result.

| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A map pick (build or journey) and hex detail  | Coexist. A press on an unlit hex shows its detail and changes no stage; a press on a lit hex picks and closes any detail.                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| The railroad popup and hex detail             | Coexist apart: during a pick the hex detail opens in the side panel, and the railroad popup stays beside its hex, so the two never cover each other. On phones, where the side panel is off screen, the detail opens beside its own hex on the side away from the railroad popup, or beside the popup when its hex has no clear side. |
| A map pick and railroad spotlight             | Coexist. The pick's scrim and lit set win; the spotlight only outlines its railroad's track.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| Railroad spotlight and hex detail             | Coexist.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| The railroad popup and Undo                   | Undo closes the popup first (it is the latest manual stage).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| The staged route and the last-journey line    | Impossible: only the deciding seat drafts, and its board never draws the last journey in Live View.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| A staged pick and History View                | History View wins: entering it hides every pick, scrim and popup; leaving it shows the live offer again, rebuilt from the live state.                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

A staged pick survives them.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

## Shared visual state

The session owns these. Components read them and call its methods.

- **Build stage** (`hex`): the picked hex while its railroad popup is open. Producer: a press on a lit hex with two or more railroads. Consumers: the hex's ring, the popup. Lifetime: a button press (sends), Undo, a second press on the hex, a new Game State. It is registered as the session's local selection, so the shared Undo pops it before it undoes any action. Validity: only in `Building`, for the deciding seat, while the hex is still in the offer. History View: hidden.
- **Journey draft** (`start`, then stacked `links`, each named by the city it reaches and its railroad, then `railroadChoice`: the city whose link popup is open). Producer: presses on lit cities, link popups. The hexes sent are the first realisation of the named route, and a city is lit for a railroad when some realisation of the whole route reaches it. The route is realised a link at a time: while the next link can follow the hexes already chosen it takes the first way that does, with no search, so every city lit from those hexes realises, whatever the cap. Only a city that needs an earlier link to go another way starts a search, which steps only into hexes from which the link's city can still be reached and, once a link's first way fails, tries its other ways only where they leave every hex a later link must have; it gives up after `REALISE_EXPANSION_CAP` steps, which only a route whose later links contend for the same hexes, with no hex one of them must have, can reach, and that city stays unlit. Consumers: the map's route line, the lit set, the panel's route and payments, the seat cards' pending "+$N" chips. Lifetime: Ride (sends), Undo pops, a press on the picked start clears, a new Game State clears. Validity: only in `Riding`, for the deciding seat; a draft is re-checked against the displayed state and reads as empty if any link no longer holds. History View: hidden.
- **Journey preview** (derived, never stored): links per railroad, the fare, each seat's payment, from the draft and the shares. Consumers: the panel, the seat cards' pending chips, the map's link count. It uses the logic package's payment helper, so the preview equals what `RideJourney` pays.
- **Board offer** (derived, never stored): from the displayed state, the valid actions for this seat and the stages. Effects allowed: lit set, ghost cubes, marks, scrim holes, which popup is open. Consumers: every map layer. History View and other seats: empty.
- **Hex detail** (`hexId`): producer, a press on an unlit hex; consumer, the detail popup beside the hex, or the side panel during a map pick on wider screens. Cleared by a second press on the hex, a press on a lit hex, any action sent, Escape, close, or a Game State that changes that hex; a press on another unlit hex moves it there. Kept across History View navigation when the hex exists in both states.
- **Railroad spotlight** (`railroadId`): producer, the railroad cards (the phone build panel's piles) and seat cards; consumers, the track layer and the scrim. Cleared by a second press or pointer leave (hover only). Survives Game States and history navigation.
- **Transcontinental line shown** (a yes or no, this viewer's own, not game state): producer, the Transcontinental chip, a toggle only while the displayed state has a claim; consumers, the line layer, and the claim's animator, which fades the settled line as its coin lands unless it is shown. Starts hidden; survives Game States and history navigation, not a reload.
- **Animation transients**: a travelling locomotive, a travelling passenger, a coin chip. Producer: the state-change animators. Consumers: the flight layer only. Scoped to the action being animated; cleared on its timeline's end or teardown.

## Render ownership

The board is **one SVG** (`docs/phone-rendering.md`, One board surface). Back to front:

1. Ground and map: the hatched sea, plain hexes on their grid, mountain and city hexes as pale-edged patches, mountain peaks, the navy frame.
2. Cities: start bands, East Coast capacities, bonus pills, names (the city greys are in the ground layer).
3. Start cues: the dashed outline of each city a newly available railroad could start in now, for every seat.
4. Passengers waiting (wooden meeples), and the dashed outline of each one taken.
5. Track: cubes, this turn's bars.
6. The claimed Transcontinental line (thin gold, while the viewer shows it with the chip), then the last journey line through its cubes over its halo (not drawn for the seat now deciding, in Live View).
7. **Scrim**: one path, the map's outline with holes at the lit hexes (even-odd fill). It is a plain translucent fill, not an opacity or filter effect. With a spotlight and no pick it covers track only, and the spotlit railroad's locomotives are drawn again above it.
8. Staged route line, the cubes it rides drawn again, and station dots.
9. Rings, ghost cubes and marks (the board offer).
10. **City names**, every city's, lit or not, above the track, the route lines and the picks, so neither a cube nor a ring, ghost cube or mark in a neighbouring hex covers a name. A lit city's marks sit low and right of its centre, clear of its own name, and presses pass through the names to the hit targets. The board holds nothing but the map: the railroad cards, the round tickets and the Transcontinental chip are HTML outside the scaling (The table).
11. Flight layer: travelling locomotives, passengers and coins.
12. Hit targets: transparent hex shapes carrying hex ids, the only map elements that take presses.

Popups (the railroad buttons, and hex detail outside a map pick) are HTML in the `Floater` against a reference point at the hex, outside the SVG, so their text stays crisp at any zoom (`docs/phone-rendering.md`, HTML text under zoom). The view's moves (the building zoom, the Riding pan and the phone's rest) follow from the `ScalingWrapper`'s `overlay` snippet (`components/BoardViewFollower.svelte`). During a map pick on wider screens the hex detail renders in the side panel instead, so it never covers the railroad popup; on phones it stays a popup and its placement (`clearOfChoice` in `utils/popupPlacement.ts`) keeps it clear of the railroad popup. Rings, marks and city names are sized by `viewScale()` so they stay readable at any zoom; ghost cubes stand in their slots at the cubes' size. The board draws no seat initials: the claimant in the Transcontinental chip is HTML outside the scaling (The table), at a fixed screen size. No SVG or CSS filters, masks or endless animations on the board.

## Game log and history

- **One entry per player turn**, grouped (`docs/game-ui-layout.md`, Group): a share ("Ana took Red"); a building turn that grows as placements arrive ("Ana built 5: Red ×3, Blue ×2 · Chicago $2 · Denver $5") and closes at its end, with the reason when it ended by itself; a journey with its cities, link counts per railroad, the fare and each seat's dividend; a pass.
- **Interstitials** at each round and phase from the round and phase managers' series.
- **Skipped:** `SkipShare` is folded into its seat's entry; `FinishBuilding` closes its building entry rather than standing alone.
- **Jump** on round and turn entries. **Replay** on building turns (placements land in order) and journeys (the passenger travels). Shares, passes and the end of round have nothing to replay on the map.
- Text is built from each action's input and metadata (the logic package records bonuses, payments, the new order and the railroad made available).

Silent restoration moves nothing.

- Every motion has an explicit timeline position; independent motion starts at 0.
- A full-action replay of a building turn plays each placement in turn, including the closing `FinishBuilding`; history does not exit before the last motion settles (terminal system-action batches).
- Motion stays still around it: only the small travelling element repaints. Lit rings are static, never pulsing.
- Undo while a journey plays takes back the journey itself, not the route already sent, and the motion ends at once.
- The board holds only the map, so each flight lifts from and lands on what the table shows beside it (`animators/flightPlaces.ts`): a locomotive lifts from its railroad's supply pile (its card on desktop; on a phone the share button's pile, or the build panel's pile; a phone with neither on show, a watching seat's, lifts it from above the board); a share lands on the seat's card; coins land on the seat's money, on its seat card or in the waiting view's seat line, whichever is most in view, at the part the view shows; the Transcontinental's coin lands on its chip. Where nothing shows the target (the History tab is open or the side panel is folded), a seat's flight lands at the board's edge beside the side panel. Locomotives and coins are drawn in one layer above the whole table (`TableFlightLayer`), in board units that follow the board on screen; on a phone so are the map's own flyers, clipped to the board's view, and coins and a share's locomotive are drawn no smaller than they can be read.
- SVG motion tweens a plain point and writes `translate(...)` on a centred outer wrapper (`docs/phone-rendering.md`, Moving SVG under zoom).

## Phone layout

At 375 × 812 and below `sm`:

- **Header:** one compact line: whose turn, "R3 · Build", the Transcontinental chip ("$12/$8", or the claim's medallion and the claimant's dot, then a button that shows or hides the line), Undo. Phones show no round tickets.
- **Action area:** the prompt on one line; buttons wrap. The journey panel collapses to "3 links · fare $4 · you +$6" with a toggle that opens the per-seat chips. Take a share's buttons are wordless piles in one row, 5 px apart, the piles drawn as large as lets the row fit (at most a 10 px cube). The build panel is one line (Build).
- **No railroad cards and no rail:** the board takes the game column's whole width (343 px at 390 × 844). The piles show only where they are used: Take a share's buttons and the building seat's piles in the build panel; none while riding or watching. The seat cards in the Players drawer show the holders, and their share rows hold a railroad's spotlight. Flights and the end of round play on the panel's piles (Animation plan).
- **Board:** its extents are the same as on desktop, the map data plus a thin rim of sea (`BOARD_MARGIN` in `utils/boardGeometry.ts`, 6 board units; `art-plan.md`, Board). Fitting the whole map would make a hex about 11 px wide. A cover fit (`coverBelowScale`) that only fills the view's height leaves a hex under 37 px once the header and the action panel take their share, so the board rests on the middle third at a scale where a hex is at least 37 px (`MIN_RESTING_HEX_PX`, or fills the view's height, if that is larger). At 390 × 844 the view on a building turn is 343 × 594 and the board at rest is taller than it as well as wider, so it pans both ways, north–south as well as east–west (`utils/boardFocus.ts`). The view moves by pinch, drag, wheel and the zoom buttons; no control names a part of the map. The resting view keeps its place and scale as the view resizes until the view is moved by hand or to an offer. A building offer is fitted for the deciding seat instead ([Build](#build-place-a-locomotive-building-the-deciding-seat)), so the two never fight. When a new Riding offer (a turn's start, or the next pick) lights hexes that all lie outside the resting view, the board pans to them at its scale, centring as many as fit, nearest the view's centre first, and jumping rather than gliding with reduced motion; it stays put while a lit hex is in view, once the view has been moved by hand during the offer, and in history (`litRevealRect`, verified by `tests/litInView.spec.ts`). It is the same in full screen. Full screen keeps play going by rendering the action area in the wrapper's toolbar while modal. Zoomed out to the whole map, city names, which would be about 4 px high, are left off; a press shows the city.
- **Popups** open below the hex, clamp inside the viewport and flip above the hex when there is no room below. The hex detail during a pick is one of them: the side panel is off screen on phones, so it opens beside its hex and keeps clear of the railroad popup (Coexistence and precedence).
- **Keyboard focus:** focusing a hex out of view scrolls nothing, neither the page sideways towards the side panel nor the board's own boxes; the board pans the hex into view instead (`utils/focusScroll.ts`). Verified by `tests/pressing.spec.ts`.
- **Touch:** every hover effect has a press. Lit hit targets are the whole hex, and no more: a tap goes to the hex under the point where the finger lifts, even where the browser would move it onto a lit neighbour, so at the whole map's scale a tap beside a lit hex opens the unlit hex's detail. Verified by `tests/pressing.spec.ts` at desktop and phone width.

## Design choices

- **U-1 Board picks** for hexes and cities, lit only when legal. _Alternative:_ a panel list of hexes. Rejected (the principle, rule 4).
- **U-2 Hex first, railroad second, and only when ambiguous.** Most presses place at once; the popup appears only when two railroads may go to one hex. _Alternative:_ choose a railroad in the panel, then its hexes. Rejected: a building turn often mixes railroads (R-3.2.2), and each switch would cost a press.
- **U-3 Each placement is sent at once** and undone one at a time, matching the logic's one action per placement.
- **U-4 Journeys built city by city.** _Alternatives:_ pick only an end city and let the engine route (takes the railroad choice from the player, R-3.3.2); or a route list in the panel (unreadable with many routes).
- **U-5 An unlit press shows the hex.** A near miss never acts.
- **U-6 Railroad colours are rule colours, never seat colours.** Cubes always carry the railroad colour and no letter; the hex detail and every accessible name carry the railroad's name; the cards and piles draw none (the colour is the label). Seats appear only as markers, chips, bars and card headers.
- **U-7 The last journey is cleared for the seat now deciding** and stays drawn for everyone else until the next action, so a seat that looked away sees what was carried, while the seat choosing never reads the last ride as part of its own. History View draws it for everyone. _Alternative:_ keep it for every seat until the deciding seat's first action. Rejected: the next seat read the old route as its own.

## Verification scenarios

Each gives the start, the input, the result, the result after Undo or exit, and how it is checked. "Auto" is a session or component test; "Manual" is a person at desktop and at 375 × 812.

**Take a share**

1. **Round 1, 4 seats, phase 1.** The last seat in turn order decides first. Only Red's and Blue's piles show, as the share buttons (the cards on desktop), enabled and named "Take a Red share, 27 left"; Orange to Black are not shown; no railroad name is drawn; the map not dimmed. Press Red: the locomotive lifts from Red's pile and flies to the seat's card, and Red's pile shows one cube fewer ("26 left"). Undo (before the next seat acts): the share returns. Auto.
2. **Empty supply.** With Red's supply at 0, Red's button is a dashed ghost, disabled, named "Red: none left"; Blue still enabled. Auto.

**Build**

3. **First placement.** Round 1, a seat holding Red only: lit are the six East Coast cities, each with a Red ghost cube; the rest dims. Press New York: the locomotive lands, the panel reads "1 of 5 placed", New York's neighbours that can take Red light. Undo: the locomotive leaves, the six cities light again. Auto.
4. **Red and Blue never share an East Coast city.** Blue started in Philadelphia: a seat holding Red sees Philadelphia unlit. Auto.
5. **Two railroads possible.** A seat holding Red and Blue presses a hex both may enter: the hex is picked, a popup shows a Red and a Blue cube button with no words, named in full. Undo closes the popup and places nothing; a second Undo undoes the previous placement. Choosing Blue places Blue. Auto.
6. **Mountain limit.** 4 seats (limit 5), 4 placed, no mountain yet: no mountain hex is lit and plain hexes are. With a mountain placed earlier this turn, the panel reads "Mountain used: limit 4", and the fourth placement ends the turn by itself (`FinishBuilding`). Auto.
7. **Bonus marks and pay.** A lit Chicago shows "$2"; placing there adds $2 and a coin flies. A lit, empty Denver shows "$5" for an adjacent railroad; a Black start in an empty $5 city shows no pill on its popup button (its name says "pays nothing: a start uses up the $5") and pays nothing. Auto.
8. **Transcontinental.** Purple's line from San Francisco waits beside Denver, where Orange's line from Chicago ends, and Red runs from New York into Chicago: Denver shows "$12"; placing Purple there pays $12 once, the coin flies to the Transcontinental chip in the turn line, which then shows the claim, the line settles in thin gold and fades as the coin lands, and no hex shows the mark again. The chip is now a button: a press shows the line in thin gold, another hides it (`aria-pressed`); the choice is the viewer's and outlasts the turn. Placing Purple instead on Orange's line in the plain hex east of Denver, the mark reads "$8". Auto.
9. **Yellow's starts.** Round 3: Chicago and the westernmost occupied cities holding exactly one locomotive light with Yellow ghost cubes, and every seat sees those cities' dashed Yellow start cues, which follow each placement until Yellow's first. After a Yellow start in Chicago, the western start still lights the set fixed at that placement, even after other railroads build further west. Round 5, a seat holding Black and Red with Black unstarted: nearly every city is lit; cities offering only a Black start take the fainter start ring, and hexes extending Red take the full ring. Auto.
10. **Build nothing.** A seat presses Build nothing with 0 placed: the next seat's turn starts. Undo before they act restores the turn. Auto.
11. **This turn's bars.** Another seat watches a building turn: each new locomotive has the builder's bar; the waiting view lists only the railroads with a legal placement now, and a railroad that runs out of places drops from the list; when the turn ends the bars clear. Manual.

**Ride the Rails**

12. **Start and extend.** Lit: passenger cities with a link out. Press one: it is picked and the cities one link away light. Press one with a single railroad: the link draws, the panel shows "1 link · fare $2" and dividend chips. Undo removes the link; a second Undo unpicks the start. Auto.
13. **Railroad choice on a link.** Two railroads link the head to a city: the popup offers both with what each pays the seat. Choose one: the link draws in its colour. Auto.
14. **No loop.** A city already on the route, or reachable only through a used hex, is never lit. Auto.
15. **City by city.** The panel suggests no route and no button fills one: the seat names every link. Ride sends the shown route and the money paid equals the preview. Auto.
16. **Pass.** No passenger city has a link: Pass is the only button, nothing is lit. Auto.

**Round and game end**

19. **Game end.** Round 6's last journey: the summary shows final money and both seats tied for most as winners. Auto.

**Looking, history and phones**

20. **Hex detail during a pick.** During a building pick, press an unlit hex: its detail opens in the side panel (on phones, beside the hex, on screen) and no stage changes; with a railroad popup open, the popup stays beside its hex while the detail shows in the side panel (on phones, beside its own hex), and the two never overlap.
