# Ride the Rails art plan

Every visual, its source, and the budget it must meet. What players see and press is fixed by the UI contract (`games/ride-the-rails-ui/docs/ui-interaction-visual-contract.md`); ids are those of the logic package's data (`games/ride-the-rails/docs/rules-interpretations.md`, Ids and coordinates).

**One source: drawn in code from data.** The title ships no publisher art and no supplied images. The map is drawn from the hex and city tables the logic package transcribes from the printed board (`src/data/usaMap.ts`), never from an image of the printed board. Cubes, passengers, money, markers, the round tickets, the start legend, and the seat cards are SVG and CSS built from a small shape vocabulary; the cover photographs that board. A railroad's piece, which the rules call a locomotive, is drawn as a flat rounded square, still called a cube. MarraCash (`games/marracash-ui`) is the model for a title drawn wholly in code, and Magna Grecia's board layers (`games/magna-grecia-ui/src/lib/components/board/`) for a hex map.

## Palette

**The look is chosen in one place.** It is a light board drawn flat, like a printed map, on a pale mist table: the mist palette (the chart board's cream land on a thin warm grid, a 45° hatched sea as a rounded card edged by a thin line in a deeper sea, mountains and grey cities as whole-hex patches, each city's middle cream inside a rim of its grey, no city names), a band of start colours inside each city with Black on every one, a newly available railroad's colour joining the band where it may start, bonus pills at a city's bottom centre, flat square cubes, a flat natural-wood meeple for a passenger, lit hexes as holes in a light scrim with nothing drawn in them, and a journey's start marked by its navy ring alone. Those are twelve picks (`palette: 'mist'`, `startMark: 'band'`, `hexFill: 'start-cities'`, `bonus: 'pill'`, `piece: 'flat-square'`, `passenger: 'meeple'`, `startCue: 'band'`, `litRailroads: 'none'`, `litHex: 'hole'`, `cityCentre: 'cream-inset'`, `mapFrame: 'sea-line'`, `journeyStart: 'ring-only'`), each a named constant in one module, `src/lib/utils/boardLook.ts` (`BOARD_LOOK`). Switching a pick is a one-line change there. The options not picked stay drawn in code for comparison (`LOOK_OPTIONS`): the chart palette, the navy frame (8 or 3 wide) or no frame, dashed start cues, ghost cubes in a lit hex, a deeper scrim, a thin white ring, a pale wash or a cream city for a lit hex, a grey city centre, and a pennant or a badge on a journey's start; the module also lists the options considered and not drawn.

Colours are CSS custom properties in `src/app.css` of the UI package, scoped to `[data-game-ui="ride-the-rails"]`. The tokens every palette shares (railroad colours, ghost cubes, coins and pills, cubes, the passenger, the pick colours, the sea's edge) sit on the title root; each palette's own (table, panels, land, sea, frame, city greys, pills, ink) sit in a block `[data-rtr-palette='<name>']`, and the table carries `data-rtr-palette` from `BOARD_LOOK.palette`. There are two blocks, one per column below; the chart palette is the first board's warm cream table, and mist keeps its board on a pale neutral table, lighter than the sea so the map's edge reads, with paler cards edged to match. A new palette is a new block and a new column in the table below. Some meanings in the table name things the picked look no longer draws (ghost cubes, the $5 ring, a used $5 pill, a dashed start cue, a taken passenger's outline, the navy frame); their tokens stay for the look options and the code that still reads them. The board is a printed board on the table: it keeps one look in the site's light and dark themes, and the panels and seat cards take its paper. Each token is also a Tailwind colour of the same name (`bg-rtr-panel`). Components use these tokens and never a raw colour; `palette.spec.ts` fails on a raw colour in any component.

Two meanings are fixed across titles: **amber says "yours to act on"** (`--rtr-accent`) and **rose says "costs or threatens"** (`--rtr-cost`). Neither is ever a seat colour or a railroad colour.

The scrim is the paper's ink at 25% (`#2A272240`), laid over the land only, never the sea: on a light board it greys the land without hiding it, every railroad's cube is still told from the dimmed plain (`ink.spec.ts`), and the lit hexes, holes in it with nothing drawn on them, stand out by being the only undimmed land. Orange (`#E8801E`) and Yellow (`#F2D634`) were pushed apart after the playtest: Orange redder, Yellow brighter.

The railroad colours are rule colours: they name the railroads in the rules (R-1.1) and appear only on railroad things (cubes, start bands, route links, chevrons, the railroad cards and share buttons, the colour squares that stand for a railroad in text, the start legend). Each has an ink for text on it as a token too, `--rtr-railroad-<name>-ink`, set to `--rtr-ink-light` or `--rtr-halo`, whichever meets the contrast (Seat colours and colour blindness); the $5 pill's amount takes `--rtr-five-ink`, set to `--rtr-ink-light`.

### Seat colours

Seats take their colours from a set of their own, kept apart from the six railroad colours (Red, Blue, Orange, Yellow, Purple, Black) and from the amber pick colour, so a seat's bar, chip or marker is never read as a railroad or as "yours to act on". The game offers seats only these colours, and `gameSession.colors` assigns them; there are five, one per seat at the most (R-1.2). They are the platform colours Green (shown as Teal), Pink, White (shown as Lime), Brown and Gray, given these values by `definitions/seatPalette.ts`, the runtime's `playerColorPalette`, and `definitions/colorizer.ts`. White is drawn lime because white barely showed on the paper cards. Each is one value in both themes: initials and edges take their ink by contrast (Seat colours and colour blindness).

| Lime        | `#94C43A` | Yellow, 36                      | dark, 8.4:1            | Light on a plain hex (1.8:1) and a raised card (2.0:1): a dark edge |

The seat card's disc carries a dark edge, so Lime and Pink still read on the paper.

`src/lib/fonts/BigShouldersDisplay-Bold.woff2` is instanced at weight 700 from the family's variable font and subset to Latin. It is declared under the title's own family name, "Ride the Rails Display" (`definitions/fonts.ts`), and loaded through `CustomFont` in `GameTable`; the `rtr-display` class sets it.

`src/lib/fonts/RideTheRailsSerif-Bold.woff2` (21,044 bytes) is a Modified Version of Libre Baskerville, instanced at weight 700 and subset to Latin, so under OFL section 3 it is renamed and does not carry the Reserved Font Name. Its source is the family's variable font, `games/preussische-ostbahn-ui/src/lib/fonts/LibreBaskerville.woff2`, whose licence is copied beside it as `LibreBaskerville-OFL.txt`. Its name table calls it "Ride the Rails Serif" (family, ID 1), "Ride the Rails Serif Bold" (full name, ID 4) and `RideTheRailsSerif-Bold` (PostScript name, ID 6, and in the unique ID, ID 3); the source has no IDs 16 or 17. It keeps the copyright (ID 0) and the licence notice and URL (IDs 13 and 14) as the source states them. It is declared as "Ride the Rails Serif" and loaded the same way; the `rtr-serif` class sets it. With city names gone from the board, nothing measures its letters any more.

It is made with fontTools 4.60 (with `brotli` for WOFF2), from `games/ride-the-rails-ui`; `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH` stamps the source's own modified date (15 October 2025) in the `head` table, so the commands give the same bytes every time:

```sh
export SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH=1760535429
python3 -m fontTools.varLib.instancer --update-name-table -o serif-700.ttf \
    ../preussische-ostbahn-ui/src/lib/fonts/LibreBaskerville.woff2 wght=700
python3 - <<'EOF'
from fontTools.ttLib import TTFont
font = TTFont('serif-700.ttf')
for name_id, value in {
    1: 'Ride the Rails Serif',

python3 -m fontTools.subset serif-700-renamed.ttf \
    --unicodes='U+0000-00FF,U+0131,U+0152-0153,U+02BB-02BC,U+02C6,U+02DA,U+02DC,U+0304,U+0308,U+0329,U+2000-206F,U+20AC,U+2122,U+2191,U+2193,U+2212,U+2215,U+FEFF,U+FFFD' \

```

## Board

**Geometry from data.** One `utils/boardGeometry.ts` with a spec turns each map's data in the logic package into board units: a `BoardGeometry` per map, built once, each cell placed by the platform's `calculateHexGeometry` for the map's orientation, with size `s` (centre to corner, 40 units). The USA's hexes are pointy-topped: a hex's centre is `x = dcol × (√3 s / 2)`, `y = row × 1.5 s`. A flat-topped map's hexes stand in columns: `x = col × 1.5 s`, `y = drow × (√3 s / 2)` (`games/ride-the-rails/docs/rules-interpretations.md`, Ids and coordinates). Both are plus the margin. A hex printed over two cells (R-8: Berlin) has each cell placed so; its centre is their mean, its box their union, and its outline the cells' shared outline, which the ground, the hit target and the scrim's hole draw, while rings inside the edge are drawn in each cell. Hex and city ids name their map, so a hex's centre, box and cells are looked up by id alone; everything else the board draws comes from the displayed state's map (the session's `boardFrame`). The marks inside a hex turn with its map's orientation (`utils/hexFrame.ts`): the start band's corners, the bonus pill's place, the mountain's peak, the sides a capacity mark tries and the cube slots. Every slot is the same either way up but Chicago's two rows, which stand at −9 and 8 on a flat hex, which is shorter, so they clear the pill at its bottom edge. Nothing is measured from a picture. The board draws no city names: the start bands, cubes, passengers and pills say what a city is for, and every lit city's accessible name, the log and the panels name it in text (both Portlands as "Portland (Oregon)" and "Portland (Maine)").

**Extents from data.** A map's board extents are the bounding box of its hexes' corners, every cell of each hex counted, either way up, plus a band of sea on every side (`BOARD_MARGIN` in `utils/boardGeometry.ts`, 40 board units): the board is `width = map width + 2 × 40` by `height = map height + 2 × 40`, its land starting 40 in from its top-left corner. The desktop fit is the smaller of 940 / width and 564 / height, never past 1. Nothing else widens the board. The extents are the same at every width.

**The sea** is a card with rounded corners (radius 16) filling the board under the land, its 45° hatch one path of lines 7 units apart clipped to the card (`--rtr-sea-line` on `--rtr-sea`); a pattern fill of the same lines doubled the board's repaint time in WebKit, the path costs nothing measurable. No coast line is drawn: the hatch against the cream is the coast. A thin line in a deeper sea (`--rtr-sea-edge`, 3 wide) runs just inside the card's edge, its corners following the card's, so nothing pokes out past them (`mapFrame: 'sea-line'`; the navy frame, `--rtr-frame`, is an option). No state borders, rivers or lakes are drawn: the map data has none and no rule uses them. The scrim never covers the sea.

**In the sea**, drawn in board units and never dimmed:

- **Capacity marks** (R-4.1.3, R-4.1.4, R-8): beside each hex with its own limit, a city or (on the expansion maps) another hex that holds one locomotive, just past the first side of its hex that faces open water (east first and then round by the north on a pointy hex, the north-east first on a flat one) where the mark stays 6 clear of every mark placed before it (the cities in the map's order, then the other hexes), a white outline square (20 units, corners 3, stroke 2) with the most locomotives it holds in the serif, also white: "1" beside each East Coast city, "6" beside Chicago, further out, clear of its passenger's head. Cities hold no capacity mark of their own (`board/CapacityMarks.svelte`, `utils/cityMarks.ts` `capacityMarks`).
- **The title**: "Ride the Rails" in the display face (52 units) over the map's printed name ("USA") in the serif (26 units, tracked), white, in the open water the map's layout gives, on the USA north-east of Michigan (`board/MapTitle.svelte`, `utils/mapLayouts.ts`).
- **The start legend** (R-4.2): a panel-paper card with a card edge standing on the corner the map's layout gives, on the USA in the sea south-west of California, right of where the zoom control stands when the board is fitted (500 wide, narrower than the usual 610, to stay clear of it and of the land), headed "First locomotive", one row per start rule in the order the map introduces its railroads, each the cubes of the railroads that share it and its words: "Different East Coast cities" (Red, Blue), "An East Coast city or Chicago", "Chicago or the 3 westernmost developed cities", "A West Coast city", "Any city with room". The rows come from the logic package's `RAILROAD_INTRODUCTIONS`, so they follow the map, the expansion maps' start rules included: on Australia, Black's row reads "Any city with room but Perth and Brisbane", the names from the map's long-distance cities. It is drawn above the scrim (`board/StartLegend.svelte`, `utils/mapLayouts.ts`).

**The expansion boards** (R-8) add looks of their own, drawn in code like the rest, nothing copied from the printed boards (`utils/terrainMarks.ts`):

- **Limit marks inside a hex**: a hex with its own limit and a side on open water has its mark in the sea, as the USA's East Coast cities do (In the sea, Capacity marks): Perth, Brisbane and Australia's four long-distance hexes, and Toronto. Where a hex has no side on open water (Paris and Berlin, whose "6" has nowhere else to go, and the six Paris entry hexes), the mark stands inside it: a city's at the top left of its own cell, another hex's "1" at its top centre. The mark is the capacity mark's cube outline inked (`--rtr-ink`, 15 units, corners 2.2, stroke 1.6) on a pale ground (white at 75%), its number in the serif (11.5 units), so it reads on any fill.
- **Cities with no limit** (Toronto, Paris, Berlin) are drawn as Chicago is: its blue-grey, their cubes in its slots, their "6" in the sea where a side opens on water and in the hex where none does.
- **City bonuses**: one gold pill per city, as Chicago's, wider for a longer amount (each character past the second one figure, 8.3 units): Perth "$50", Brisbane "$20", Paris "$10", Berlin "$3", and Toronto's two prices in one, "$3/$2" (build-in, start).
- **The start legend's text** starts past the widest row's cubes, never left of where the USA's two put it, so a row of five printed-colour cubes stays clear of its words. Each expansion map's legend stands where its board prints the railroad introduction panel when the open sea there holds it, and its title, which no board prints, in the largest open sea left.
- **The Alps** (Australia, no track): the mountain fill and peak, crossed by the sea's 45° hatch (lines 7 apart, on the sea's own lattice, 2.2 wide) in the peak's brown (`--rtr-alps-hatch`), drawn as lines each cut to its cell, the patch edge drawn again over them; no pattern, mask or clip.
- **Impassable sides** (Canada's river and lake shore, France's estuaries): a red line along the whole side (`--rtr-impassable`, 8 wide), round-ended, on a casing in the plains' cream (2 wide either side), in a layer of its own over the bands, cubes, journey line and scrim, so no band, cube or dark hex hides it and a pick never dims it, and under the passengers, the staged route, the picks, flights and hit targets, taking no presses. A passenger whose head or arm reaches a side stands over its line: Caen's across the Seine into Le Havre, Nicolet's and Sorel-Tracy's on Canada's river. The red is brighter and more saturated than the Red railroad's (ΔE 30 from it) and stands at 3:1 or more on the plains and the sea; the casing parts it from a black band or a Red cube (3:1 or more) and from an orange band (2.5:1, with the red's hue). At 8 wide it reads across a 6.4 start band and is about 1.4 px at the phone's whole-board fit; the line reaches 6 into a hex, so it covers at most the outer corner of the second of two cubes, about 3 units of it. A side along the shore runs between a hex and a cell off the board (`board/ImpassableLayer.svelte`).
- **The ring round Paris** (France): the six entry hexes tinted pale navy on the plains' grid (`--rtr-entry`), each with the in-hex "1", their outside edged in navy (`--rtr-pill`, 2.4 wide, the sides that face neither another entry hex nor Paris), so the ring reads as one group: only one of these. Lille, Valenciennes, Sedan and Metz stand on the board's land border, where the margin past them is no sea, so the map's layout puts their "1" in the hex.
- **Berlin** (Germany, one city over two cells): one outline round both cells, one start band round it (its colours split top to bottom from the middle, as a single cell's), one cream middle inside it (each side moved in as a cell's are), and one ring when it is lit or picked. Its cubes alternate between the cells, its own cell first: one each full size, two each full size, then Chicago's small size at five or six. Its passenger and its "6" stand in its own cell, its "$3" pill at the bottom of the second. A plain or mountain is named by its distance to the nearer cell.
- **Long-distance hexes** (Australia, one locomotive, the turn ends there): an ochre patch (`--rtr-long-haul`) with a buffer stop at the bottom, the end of the line: two inked rails with two ties across them, round-ended, running up into a stop bar in the impassable sides' red (`--rtr-impassable`, 3.0:1 on the ochre and 4.2:1 on a city's cream middle) edged in ink, about 14 by 15 in all. Perth and Brisbane, which close the chains, take a gold pill ("$50", "$20") and no passenger, with the white "1" in the sea. A placement there ends the turn too, so each carries the same buffer stop, at the top of the hex above its one cube, since the pill holds the bottom. No railroad starts there, so they draw no start band, only the city's rim, and never light as a start.

**Beside the board.** On desktop a row of railroad cards stands between the action area and the board, outside the scaling, centred, after a mark that says what the cubes are for; the round tickets and the Transcontinental chip sit in the turn line. On a phone there are no cards: the board takes the whole width, the supply stands in the action panel where it is used (Take a share's buttons, the building seat's cards), and the turn line shows the current round's ticket and the chip (UI contract, The table and Phone layout). The turn order panel is gone: the seat cards carry each seat's place and money.

**Layers**, back to front, as the contract's render ownership sets them: the sea card and its hatch; hexes (plains on their grid, then mountains and cities as pale-edged patches, each city's cream centre, the mountains' peaks) and the sea-edge line; the lit-hex look options (none drawn with the look picked); the capacity marks and the title; cities' start bands; dashed start cues (an option, none drawn); bonus pills; track (cubes) and this turn's bars; the claimed Transcontinental line, then the last journey line over its halo; the scrim; the impassable sides' red lines; the unlit cities' passengers (in a pick each with the scrim's fill laid over it), then the lit cities'; the start legend; the staged route, its grown start passenger and link count; picked and start rings, marks and chevrons; the flight layer; hit targets. All in one SVG.

**Hit targets** are transparent hex polygons carrying the hex id, one per hex, in the top layer.

## Cards and tiles

The game has no cards or tiles, and nothing on the map opens a detail: the board draws what a hex holds. Two drawn faces carry what the map does not:

- **Seat card** (a paper card, `PlayerState.svelte`): edged on the left (6 px) and marked with a disc in the seat's colour, the name, six share slots (a held share is its cube, 18 px; an empty slot a dashed square), and the money as the large figure on the right; no turn position and no caption. The acting seat's card has a 3 px ring in its seat colour inside its edge. The cards stand in the phase's order and slide when it changes.
- **Game end** (the action area): the winners' dots and line, then a small table of place, dot, name and final money.

## Icons and symbols

The panel and the log name a seat by its name, in plain text where a seat dot already marks whose line it is, and a railroad by a small square of its colour with its cube's edge (`RailroadName.svelte`), its name kept for screen readers; a waiting view's "May build" line keeps a chip with the cube and the name. A building turn's counts stand before their squares ("built 2 ■ 1 ■"); an amount is plain `$` text. The railroad cards' share-and-track mark (`ShareTrackMark.svelte`: a share certificate, a slash, a locomotive) is the one icon. The board's shapes (cube, meeple, mountain, pills, coins, chevrons) are drawn by the components above; none is an SVG symbol shared with the text.

## Seat colours and colour blindness

- **One source.** Every seat-coloured element reads `gameSession.colors`, whose values come from the seat set (Palette, Seat colours); no seat colour is written anywhere else in the title. The platform's colour-blind setting reaches every seat element.
- **Colour alone on the board.** The board draws no seat initials, so on it (this turn's bars) colour is all that tells seats apart, which is why the set keeps its members well apart from one another as well as from the railroads and amber.
- **Railroads never rely on colour alone.** Cubes carry no letter, and the railroad cards, share buttons, legend and colour squares draw no name (the colour is the label); each card's and button's accessible name names its railroad, every colour square in text carries the name for screen readers, and every lit hex and popup button names its railroads in its accessible name. Red against Orange and Blue against Purple are the pairs at risk; with the hex detail gone, those accessible names are what settles which is which.
- **Seats and railroads never share a shape.** Seats are discs, dots, bars, chips and card edges; railroads are cubes, colour squares, start bands, route links and chevrons.
- **Ink by contrast.** A letter or initials on a fill is dark or white, chosen from the fill's luminance so contrast is at least 4.5:1. Letters take `--rtr-railroad-<name>-ink`. Seat initials take the seat palette's `text`, white or `#1B1B1B`. Text on the table, the panels, every city grey and every pill meets WCAG AA (4.5:1), a used $5 pill's included; the passenger's edge and a cube's edge stand at least 3:1 from every ground they sit on.
- **Checks.** `ink.spec.ts` checks the letter contrast on every railroad fill in every palette, the text, pill, passenger and cube-edge contrasts above, and the initials contrast on every seat colour; `palette.spec.ts` checks that no two railroad colours are closer than a set colour difference (CIE76 ΔE 30), and that every seat colour keeps that difference from each railroad colour, from amber and from the other seat colours.
