# Ride the Rails art plan

Every visual, its source, and the budget it must meet. What players see and press is fixed by the UI contract (`games/ride-the-rails-ui/docs/ui-interaction-visual-contract.md`); ids and counts are those of `components.md`.

**One source: drawn in code from data.** The title ships no publisher art and no supplied images. The map is drawn from the hex and city tables the logic package transcribes from `components.md`, never from an image of the printed board. Cubes, passengers, money, markers, the round track, the seat cards and the cover are SVG and CSS built from a small shape vocabulary. A railroad's piece, which the rules call a locomotive, is drawn as a flat rounded square, still called a cube. MarraCash (`games/marracash-ui`) is the model for a title drawn wholly in code, and Magna Grecia's board layers (`games/magna-grecia-ui/src/lib/components/board/`) for a hex map.

## Palette

**The look is chosen in one place.** It is a light board drawn flat, like a printed map: the chart palette (cream land on a thin warm grid, a 45° hatched sea in a navy frame, mountains and grey cities as whole-hex patches, serif names), a band of start colours inside each city, bonus pills, flat square cubes, a flat natural-wood pawn for a passenger, dashed start cues for a newly available railroad, and ghost cubes in a lit hex. Every one of those eight picks is a named constant in one module, `src/lib/utils/boardLook.ts` (`BOARD_LOOK`). Switching a pick is a one-line change there; the module lists the other options considered and where each would be drawn.

Colours are CSS custom properties in `src/app.css` of the UI package, scoped to `[data-game-ui="ride-the-rails"]`. The tokens every palette shares (railroad colours, ghost cubes, coins and pills, cubes, the pawn, the pick colours) sit on the title root; each palette's own (land, sea, frame, city greys, panels, pills, ink) sit in a block `[data-rtr-palette='<name>']`, and the table carries `data-rtr-palette` from `BOARD_LOOK.palette`. A new palette is a new block and a new column in the table below. The board is a printed board on the table: it keeps one look in the site's light and dark themes, and the panels and seat cards take its paper. Each token is also a Tailwind colour of the same name (`bg-rtr-panel`). Components use these tokens and never a raw colour; `palette.spec.ts` fails on a raw colour in any component.

Two meanings are fixed across titles: **amber says "yours to act on"** (`--rtr-accent`) and **rose says "costs or threatens"** (`--rtr-cost`). Neither is ever a seat colour or a railroad colour.

The scrim is the paper's ink at 25% (`#2A272240`): on a light board it greys the land without turning it dark, a cube's dark edge still stands at least 1.5:1 from the dimmed plain, and the lit hexes, undimmed and ringed in `--rtr-lit-ring`, stand clear of the rest.

The railroad colours are rule colours: they name the railroads in the rules (R-1.1) and appear only on railroad things (cubes, ghost cubes, start bands and cues, route links, strip entries, share rows). Each has an ink for text on it as a token too, `--rtr-railroad-<name>-ink`, set to `--rtr-ink-light` or `--rtr-halo`, whichever meets the contrast (Seat colours and colour blindness); the $5 pill's amount takes `--rtr-five-ink`, set to `--rtr-ink-light`.

### Seat colours

Seats take their colours from a set of their own, kept apart from the six railroad colours (Red, Blue, Orange, Yellow, Purple, Black) and from the amber pick colour, so a seat's bar, chip or marker is never read as a railroad or as "yours to act on". The game offers seats only these colours, and `gameSession.colors` assigns them; there are five, one per seat at the most (R-1.2). They are the platform colours Green (shown as Teal), Pink, White, Brown and Gray, given these values by `definitions/seatPalette.ts`, the runtime's `playerColorPalette`. Each is one value in both themes: initials and edges take their ink by contrast (Seat colours and colour blindness).

| ----------- | --------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Teal        | `#15736E` | Black, 39                       | white, 5.7:1           | 4.8:1 on a plain hex, 5.6:1 on a seat card                                   |
| Pink        | `#F2A0D6` | Purple, 40                      | dark, 8.8:1            | Light on a plain hex (1.7:1), so a dark edge there                           |
| White       | `#F4F4F0` | Blue, 69                        | dark, 15.6:1           | Barely shows on a plain hex or a seat card (1.1:1): always a dark edge there |
| Brown       | `#6E4126` | Black, 37                       | white, 8.6:1           | 7.4:1 on a plain hex                                                         |

Contrast is WCAG contrast against the plain hex (`--rtr-plain`) and the seat card (`--rtr-panel-raised`). The seat card's disc and the turn order's markers carry a dark edge, so White and Pink still read on the paper.

`src/lib/fonts/BigShouldersDisplay-Bold.woff2` is instanced at weight 700 from the family's variable font and subset to Latin. It is declared under the title's own family name, "Ride the Rails Display" (`definitions/fonts.ts`), and loaded through `CustomFont` in `GameTable`; the `rtr-display` class sets it.

`src/lib/fonts/RideTheRailsSerif-Bold.woff2` (21,044 bytes) is a Modified Version of Libre Baskerville, instanced at weight 700 and subset to Latin, so under OFL section 3 it is renamed and does not carry the Reserved Font Name. Its source is the family's variable font, `games/preussische-ostbahn-ui/src/lib/fonts/LibreBaskerville.woff2`, whose licence is copied beside it as `LibreBaskerville-OFL.txt`. Its name table calls it "Ride the Rails Serif" (family, ID 1), "Ride the Rails Serif Bold" (full name, ID 4) and `RideTheRailsSerif-Bold` (PostScript name, ID 6, and in the unique ID, ID 3); the source has no IDs 16 or 17. It keeps the copyright (ID 0) and the licence notice and URL (IDs 13 and 14) as the source states them. It is declared as "Ride the Rails Serif" and loaded the same way; the `rtr-serif` class sets it. Its letters' advance widths are a table in `utils/boardGeometry.ts` (`SERIF_ADVANCE_EM`), so a name's room on the board is worked out from the font, never measured at run time.

It is made with fontTools 4.60 (with `brotli` for WOFF2), from `games/ride-the-rails-ui`; `SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH` stamps the source's own modified date (15 October 2025) in the `head` table, so the commands give the same bytes every time:

```sh
export SOURCE_DATE_EPOCH=1760535429
python3 -m fontTools.varLib.instancer --update-name-table -o serif-700.ttf \
    ../preussische-ostbahn-ui/src/lib/fonts/LibreBaskerville.woff2 wght=700

font = TTFont('serif-700.ttf')
for name_id, value in {
    1: 'Ride the Rails Serif',
    3: '2.005;pyrs;RideTheRailsSerif-Bold',

    font['name'].setName(value, name_id, 3, 1, 0x409)
font.save('serif-700-renamed.ttf')
EOF
python3 -m fontTools.subset serif-700-renamed.ttf \
    --unicodes='U+0000-00FF,U+0131,U+0152-0153,U+02BB-02BC,U+02C6,U+02DA,U+02DC,U+0304,U+0308,U+0329,U+2000-206F,U+20AC,U+2122,U+2191,U+2193,U+2212,U+2215,U+FEFF,U+FFFD' \

    --output-file=src/lib/fonts/RideTheRailsSerif-Bold.woff2
rm serif-700.ttf serif-700-renamed.ttf
```

## Board

**Geometry from data.** One `utils/boardGeometry.ts` with a spec turns the logic package's map data into board units. Hexes are pointy-topped with size `s` (centre to corner): a hex's centre is `x = dcol × (√3 s / 2)`, `y = row × 1.5 s` (`components.md`, Coordinates), plus the margin. Each city also has a label anchor (above, below, left or right of the hex) in a small table in the same file, chosen so no two labels overlap at the fitted desktop scale; the spec checks every city has one. Nothing is measured from a picture.

**Extents from data.** The board's extents are the bounding box of the hex set in the logic package's map data, plus a fixed margin on every side, one constant in `utils/boardGeometry.ts` measured in hex sizes. Nothing else widens the board. The margin gives the outermost city labels, rings and marks room at the map's edge, and gives popups an anchor that stays on the board; on desktop the margin panels sit in it, at the map's empty lower corners. The extents are the same at every width; on phones the margin panels move above the board and the margin keeps only its edge room.

**The sea** fills the board under the land as a 45° hatch, one path of lines 7 units apart (`--rtr-sea-line` on `--rtr-sea`); a pattern fill of the same lines doubled the board's repaint time in WebKit, the path costs nothing measurable; no coast line is drawn, the hatch against the cream is the coast. A navy frame (`--rtr-frame`, 8 wide, corners 12) runs just inside the board's edge. No state borders, rivers or lakes are drawn: the map data has none and no rule uses them.

**Margin.** On desktop the margin panels sit in the board's fixed margin, at the map's empty lower corners (UI contract, The table): the lower left (the Pacific, below row 12) the round track and the turn order track; the lower right (the Atlantic, below row 13) the railroad strip and the Transcontinental claim's medallion. The geometry spec checks that no margin box covers a hex. Phones leave the margin out: the round badge and the railroad entries form a strip above the board, ended by the claimed medallion, a gold disc with its amount ringed in the claimant's colour (UI contract, Phone layout).

**Layers**, back to front, as the contract's render ownership sets them: the hatched sea; hexes (plains on their grid, then mountains with their peak and cities as pale-edged patches) and the frame; cities (start band, East Coast capacity, bonus pill, name); start cues; passengers and the outlines of those taken; track (cubes) and this turn's bars; the claimed Transcontinental line, then the last journey line over its halo; the scrim; the margin; the staged route; rings, ghost cubes and marks; the flight layer; hit targets. All in one SVG.

**Hit targets** are transparent hex polygons carrying the hex id, one per hex, in the top layer.

## Cards and tiles

The game has no cards or tiles. Two drawn faces stand in for "enlarging":

- **Hex detail** (a popup): the hex's name or terrain, its capacity, its cubes as the same cube at a larger size, its start colours, bonus, and passenger.
- **Seat card** (a paper card): edged and marked with a disc in the seat's colour, the name and turn position, a row of cubes per railroad held, and the money as the large figure.

## Icons and symbols

The panel and the log name things in words: a railroad by its name in its colour (`RailroadName.svelte`), a seat by its player name, an amount as plain `$` text, and link counts as "Red ×2". The board's shapes (cube, pawn, mountain, pills, coins) are drawn by the components above; none is an SVG symbol shared with the text.

## Seat colours and colour blindness

- **One source.** Every seat-coloured element reads `gameSession.colors`, whose values come from the seat set (Palette, Seat colours); no seat colour is written anywhere else in the title. The platform's colour-blind setting reaches every seat element.
- **Colour alone, zoomed out.** Below the zoom level where seat initials are drawn, colour is all that tells seats apart, which is why the set keeps its members well apart from one another as well as from the railroads and amber.
- **Railroads never rely on colour alone.** Cubes carry no letter; the railroad strip and share buttons carry each railroad's name, the hex detail lists railroads by name, and every lit hex and popup button names its railroads in its accessible name. Red against Orange and Blue against Purple are the pairs at risk; a press on a hex settles which is which.
- **Seats and railroads never share a shape.** Seats are discs, bars, chips and card headers; railroads are cubes, ghost cubes, start bands and cues, and route links.
- **Ink by contrast.** A letter or initials on a fill is dark or white, chosen from the fill's luminance so contrast is at least 4.5:1. Letters take `--rtr-railroad-<name>-ink`. Seat initials take the seat palette's `text`, white or `#1B1B1B`. Text on the table, the panels, every city grey and every pill meets WCAG AA (4.5:1), a used $5 pill's included; the pawn's edge and a cube's edge stand at least 3:1 from every ground they sit on.
- **Checks.** `ink.spec.ts` checks the letter contrast on every railroad fill in every palette, the text, pill, pawn and cube-edge contrasts above, and the initials contrast on every seat colour; `palette.spec.ts` checks that no two railroad colours are closer than a set colour difference (CIE76 ΔE 30), and that every seat colour keeps that difference from each railroad colour, from amber and from the other seat colours.

## Specs that guard it

- `palette.spec.ts`: every colour of the Palette is a declared token in every palette, and every declared colour is in the Palette; every palette declares the same tokens; no component names a raw colour; railroad colours stay apart; seat colours stay apart from them, from amber and from each other; the display face and the serif are set in the families their fonts are declared under.
- `images.spec.ts`: the title ships no image or font but the cover, the display font and the serif, each font a small WOFF2 beside its licence; the cover's size, encoding and weight; no base64 image; no SVG with a script, event attribute, `foreignObject` or external reference.
- `boardGeometry.spec.ts`: every hex and city in the logic's map data has a position and a label anchor, no position names a hex the data lacks, the extents are the data's bounding box plus the fixed margin, every letter of a city name has its serif width, no label overlaps another or covers a city's marks, a cube or a margin box, and every margin box lies inside the board and covers no hex.
- `trackLayout.spec.ts`: every capacity has square slots for its cubes, each at its size and place, inside its hex (Chicago's front corners all but) and clear of its city's bonus pill and pawn; ghost cubes take the next free slot, or share the first free one, smaller and spread.
- `ink.spec.ts`: railroad ink, text, pill, pawn and initials contrast, a cube's edge laid over every ground, and every railroad's cube told from the plain under the scrim (by its fill or its edge, at least 1.5:1).
- `utils/boardLook.spec.ts`: the eight picks, each one of the options drawn; a palette block for every palette option, and the table carrying the pick; the hex colouring of every scheme.
- `utils/cityMarks.spec.ts`: the $5 pill green until used up, then grey and struck through; Chicago's $2 never used up; the pill's size and place; the start band's spans, paths and separators, and a plain city's Black band; where the pawn stands.
