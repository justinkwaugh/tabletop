# South African Railroads UI interaction visual contract

## Table layout

- **Action area.** The turn header spans the table. Below it the action panel comes first, then the action strip, then the map. Directions for the current decision appear only in the action panel, never on the map or the strip.
- **Action strip.** A round cell leads the strip: "Round X of 6" (or "Final Round"), and a station clock with the action track's value "of 35". The four action boxes follow. Each shows an icon, its name, a short line of terms (the full rule is its tooltip) and its player slots. A player in a slot shows as their initial in a box of their colour. A bubble on each box's top-right corner shows its action-track advance, or ↺ for Pay Dividends. During Choosing Action, every viewer sees the bubble turn red on each box the turn player could take that would pay dividends at once: Pay Dividends, or a box whose advance takes the track past 35. The clock's rim and ticks turn red when such a box is not Pay Dividends. On phones the strip is one row of four tiles with short names ("Dividends", "Develop", "Offer", "Build") and no terms. The round cell becomes a line above the tiles, with a bar showing the track's progress to 35, and the action panel lists each box's rule as a guide under the prompt.
- **Map.** The map carries no controls other than its targets: links, external connections and developable settlements. The legend's base and developed incomes come from the map's data. A Final Payoff panel states the payoff formula. The round and the action track are not drawn on the map: the action boxes' timer shows them. Where a map moves its free or automatic metro development from place to place, a numbered list gives the order and underlines the one that develops next. A map whose rules differ from South African Railroads lists the differences behind the ? button; Texas Special also prints them on the board, in a panel sized to its text. The game's logo, the title over a short length of track, sits between the map and the board's lower border. A developed settlement is surrounded by a gray ring, and a developed metro area rings its current income cell. Everything placed on the board comes from the map's board layout (`utils/boardLayout.ts`, one entry per map), and the artwork that is not data comes from that map's terrain component: the South African sea, and on Texas Special faint regional tints, rivers, dotted state lines and state names, with a few small ink drawings (a longhorn skull, a windmill, pines, derricks, the Gulf and a compass rose) in corners no track crosses. South African Railroads, Veld Railroads and Pennsylvania Railroad draw theirs from their layout's `artwork` in the same manner and ink: faint tints, rivers given beside the settlements on their banks so they follow each map's positions, and a few drawings in open paper. South African Railroads and Veld Railroads share the veld, the Vaal and the Orange, a compass rose, thorn trees, a Kimberley diamond, a Barberton headframe, the Drakensberg and a ship on the Indian Ocean, which Veld shares too. Pennsylvania Railroad has the Great Lakes and the Atlantic shore, the Ohio and the Hudson, a compass rose, pines, a lighthouse, a ship, the Liberty Bell, a steel mill, a coal headframe and a wheat sheaf.
- **Player tickets.** Each investor is an Edmondson railway ticket. A plain stripe in the player's colour runs the full height of the ticket's left edge. The player's name and "Cash $N" head the ticket. Below it, one line per railroad held shows a certificate per share and "+$N", what those shares would pay at the next dividend. A "Next Dividend +$N" total follows. When the sixth payout is next, the lines show the final payoff and the total reads "Next Final Payout". The total is followed by fine print with the player's score if the game ended now. The acting player's ticket has a border in their colour.
- **Investors sidebar and railroad pane.** The player tickets come first in the sidebar. One card for each railroad follows them, or, on screens 1280px and wider, the cards stand in their own pane to the right of the map. A card is a ledger page: the treasury sits in the railroad's band, a stack of the shares still for sale shrinks as they sell ("N left") and is stamped ALL SOLD once empty, and income, value, dividend per share and final payoff per share share one money column on alternately shaded rows. A closed railroad outside the initial offering reads "Opens when track reaches" its home (the ZASM: Johannesburg). The other closed railroads read "Opens in the initial offering".
- **Palette.** The board is a warm paper sheet with a soft shadow, lying on a cool blue-grey table. Ink, borders and buttons are slate navy, not brown, and tickets, cards, the prompt and the action boxes are white stock. Railroad and player colours and the gold of selectable things are unchanged.
- **Type.** Libre Baskerville sets names on the map, headings and prose. Oswald, registered as "SAR Oswald", sets the display headings in condensed capitals: player names, railroad names, the round cell, the base names on the map and the titles of the logo, Final Payoff and special connection panels, and the payoff formula. Bitter, registered as "SAR Bitter", sets the tickets, ledgers, bubbles and figures, using tabular numerals where amounts stack.
- **Texas Special furniture.** Maps with a special connection show its panel: the northern and southern railroads, the cost and the bonus, and two "+20 value" boxes that take a cube of each founder once it is founded. Maps with more than one metro area in Automatic Metro Development print the order under the logo, the next one bold and underlined; on a map with a free metro area the note is headed "Free Metro Area", since that next one is also the free one. Texas Special, Pennsylvania Railroad and Veld Railroads list short notes on where their rules differ from South African Railroads in a dialog opened by a ? button beside Undo in the turn header; Escape, the close button or a click outside closes it. Texas Special also prints its notes in a panel on the board. Natural resources are yellow hexagons, unnamed as on the printed map. A player with Leave Town cubes has a ticket line of grey cubes and their "+$N".
- **Waiting view.** While another player acts, or in History View, the action panel lists every action of the last step: the last player action and the system actions it set off, such as a free first link or the dividend the action track triggered. A line naming the player being waited on follows.
- **Auctions.** Every player sees the auction in the action area: the share as a certificate, then a seat card per bidder in bidding order with their bid. The bidder to act lifts with a gold border, and a pass is stamped PASSED. During the initial offering the railroads still to come follow under "Up next". The bidder gets a stepper whose amount can also be typed, and Pass. On phones the certificate shrinks and the seat cards drop to their own row.

## Visual intents

- **Choose an action.** On the acting player's Choosing Action turn, the boxes they may take get a gold border and a pale gold fill and become buttons. A player appears in a box only once they have chosen a first action. Every locomotive starts in Construct Track, but the boxes stay empty until each player's first choice.
- **Choose a building railroad.** When the builder must pick among railroads, those railroad cards get a dashed gold border and become clickable. The action panel offers the same railroads as buttons, so the choice works when the sidebar is out of view. When a player who controls no buildable railroad must hand the build over, the cards and buttons are shown even when only one railroad can build. A lone railroad of the builder's own is chosen automatically and nothing is highlighted.
- **Build track.** Buildable links and external connections are highlighted on the map. The action panel states the costs, and each buildable link shows the income it would add to the railroad. After the first link of a possible double build is chosen, a cube in the builder's colour sits on it until the second link or a single build is chosen, and the railroad cards and player tickets show incomes and dividends as if it were built. A free first link with only one option is laid automatically.
- **Develop a settlement.** Developable settlements get a ring on the map. The action panel states the cost of a second development. Under each developable settlement a chip per serving railroad, in its colour, shows the income the development would add to that railroad. After the first development the panel offers "Decline", naming the metro area Automatic Metro Development will raise.
- **Leave town.** On maps with Leave Town, before the first development, every settlement the player may leave gets a wider dotted red ring, and the action panel offers a "Leave X" button for each. Where a settlement can also be developed, the develop ring takes the click and the panel's button leaves.
- **Found the special connection.** When the building railroad can found the Texas Special, the action panel offers a button per partner railroad with the cost. Founding replaces the build.
- **Offer stock.** Railroads with an unsold share are highlighted in the sidebar. The action panel offers them as share certificates, and the player's own shares as certificates under "Yours".

Targets are static. No target animates, because an endless animation over the board made WebKit redraw it every frame and reload the page on phones. Board pieces cast unblurred offset shadows rather than filtered ones for the same reason.

## Coexistence and precedence

Only one intent is live at a time, because each belongs to exactly one machine state. During any auction, the railroad being bid on keeps the selected border.

## Shared visual state

The session owns the build selection (railroad and first link), the railroad choices, and the players placed on the boxes. The boxes, the cards, the map and the action panel only read them.

- **Lifetime:** the build selection clears in `beforeNewState`. Undo pops the most recent manual stage first, and an automatic railroad choice is never popped.
- **Validity:** a stored railroad applies only while it is still among the current options and a construction state is active. History View shows no targets, because `canAct` is false there.

## Render ownership

Settlement names are placed once per map from its static data (`utils/mapLabels.ts`). Each keeps its printed board position when that is clear. Otherwise it takes the nearest clear spot around its icon. A clear spot avoids every icon, link box, track line, external connection, base name, legend, score track and other name. Metro areas carry their names on a plate attached to their development grid. A layout that lists `namedSettlements` names only those settlements; every current map names them all. Where track crowds every side of a grid, its plate slides along a side, still touching the grid. Track converges on the grids from every side, so the plates sit above the track like the grids do.

## Verification scenarios

- With the action track at 31, the turn player's Develop Settlements and Pay Dividends bubbles are red and the clock is red; at 20 only Pay Dividends' bubble is red.
- Construct a track link that sends the track past 35: other players' waiting view shows the build, then the dividend.
- Start a game and finish the initial offering: the action boxes show no players. After the first player chooses Construct Track, only their initial appears there.
- As a player who controls no buildable railroad, choose Construct Track: every buildable railroad is highlighted in the sidebar and offered as a button, even when only one can build.
- `src/lib/model/session.spec.ts` checks the build selection and Undo order and the auction view against a real session.
- `src/lib/utils/mapLabels.spec.ts` checks that every name stays clear of icons, link boxes, track lines and other names.
