# Oath: picks, panels and the table

## Visual intents

- **Choose a starting site (Setup, R-1.23.1):** while the seat on the clock has not placed its pawn, every faceup site the engine accepts wears the amber pick ring and a "start here" chip, and the rest of the map dims. When exactly one site is legal it is taken for the player rather than picked, and nothing lights. The panel shows the hand from the start (R-1.20 deals before R-1.23 places): the site and the card to keep are tapped in either order, the discard order follows once both are chosen, and the pawn appears when that Action is processed. Unaffected: seat cards, the rail, the tracks.
- **Choose a Travel destination (R-5.6):** with Travel chosen, every affordable destination wears the ring and a chip with its Supply cost and any toll; the rest of the map dims. A tap sends the Travel. Unaffected: seat cards, the strip of cards at each site, the rail.
- **Target a Campaign (R-5.5.2):** with a defender chosen, the sites that defender rules (the Bandit Crown's empty sites included) wear the ring and a "target?" chip; a tap toggles the site into the target set, which paints a rose outline and a "target" chip. Sites the defender cannot lose dim. The panel lists every other target (relics, banners, the pawn, and with Relic Hunter declared the relics at chosen sites) and dims the ones the rules refuse with what is already chosen. The panel sends the Campaign. Unaffected: seat cards, the rail.
- **Campaign out of turn (Sneak Attack):** the asked player's question offers Campaign and Pass. Campaign replaces the question with the Campaign panel, its defender the player the card names and its cost no Supply; the map offers targets exactly as in Target a Campaign. "Back to the question" returns to the question with nothing sent. While the Campaign runs, the turn banner names who is campaigning out of turn and whose turn is held.
- **A free action comes next (R-10.2):** while a power's free Travel or Campaign is due (Second Wind once a Sneak Attack is passed, Brass Horse, a free Campaign after a Muster or a Search), the engine offers only that action, giving it up, or ending the Act Phase; the grid dims the rest, a line above it says why beside a Give it up button, and a dimmed tile tapped gives the same sentence.
- **Why an action is unavailable:** hovering an Act Phase tile writes its cost and summary into the one line under the grid; tapping a dimmed tile writes the engine's reason there instead, read from the displayed state. A hover on another tile replaces the reason, and a newly shown state clears it, so it is gone once the action is offered. Nothing on the board changes.
- **Move warbands onto your site (R-6.5):** with Move warbands chosen, your own site wears the ring when warbands may go from your board onto it, and the Board counter on your seat card wears the ring for the opposite direction. The map does not dim and no Travel price shows. A tap chooses the direction; a move of one is sent, otherwise the panel asks how many.
- **Act on a card at your site (R-5.2, R-5.3, R-5.4, R-6.3):** with Muster, Trade, Recover or Peek chosen, the legal denizens or relic slots in the site's strip wear the ring and rise above their neighbours; the map does not dim. A tap sends the Action, or for Trade selects the single legal option as an Auto Draft Entry and sends.
- **The Reliquary, known to the Scepter's holder (R-6.4-H1):** the Grand Scepter's holder sees the face of every covered Reliquary space on the seat card and in the open seat, from setup on; everyone else sees backs, except a space they were shown. When the Scepter changes hands the new holder sees all four at once, and the former holder keeps what they saw. No Peek targets the Reliquary.
- **Let another player peek (R-6.1, R-9.4, R-6.6.1):** at any time, except while the game waits on this player's own decision, the player's seat card offers Let another peek (and in their Act Phase the grid's chip does too); opened, it lists each facedown adviser of this player's, and, for the Grand Scepter's holder, each Reliquary space, with a button for every player who may be shown it (any other player for an adviser, an Exile for a Reliquary relic). A tap sends the Action. Nothing on the board lights. The history names an adviser to the player shown and to its holder alone, and a Reliquary relic to the Exile shown alone; the Scepter's holder already knows it. In the shown player's own seat, the adviser then shows its face, dimmed as a facedown card and marked "facedown; shown to you", on the seat card and in the open seat, until its holder discards or plays it; every other seat still sees a back.
- **Take a banner (R-5.4.2):** with Recover chosen, a banner this player can afford wears the ring and a "pay N" chip wherever it lies: on the rail while unclaimed, or on its holder's seat card.
- **Play a facedown adviser (R-6.1):** with the action chosen, this player's playable facedown advisers wear the ring on the seat card. One legal adviser is taken for the player rather than picked; the panel then offers the placements.
- **Search a source (R-5.1.1):** with Search chosen, the world deck and this region's discard pile wear the ring and a price chip when the engine offers them.
- **Choose a card:** wherever a panel asks for a card (the card to keep at setup, an adviser to discard, a When Played card, the Wild Mounts substitute, False Prophet's adviser, a relic or banner prize, exchange terms, a modifier, warbands standing at a site), the options are a row of cards: a tap picks one and rings it, a tap on a picked card untaps it, a multi-pick takes no more than its limit, and Back untaps as it unwinds; hovering or holding enlarges one. A card the chooser may not see is drawn as its back. Suits, counts, favor banks, players and sites keep their own controls.
- **Inspect a card:** a mouse hovering a faceup card, or focusing a pickable one, shows it enlarged in the centre of the screen; on touch, a hold on a pickable card or a tap on an inert one opens the same view and keeps it until the next tap. A site card's enlarged view lists what is at the site. The seat card's Reliquary spaces (a known relic, or the trait an uncovered space shows), its goal card and its banners enlarge the same way; no part of the table enlarges anything in place. Nothing else changes.
- **Open a seat:** the name pill on a seat card opens that seat at a readable size over the table; the backdrop, Escape or the pill again closes it.
- **Warbands on a card:** the warbands standing on the Obsidian Cage or on a revealed Vision show as colour counts on the card, on the seat card, in the open seat and in the enlarged card. A Vision carrying a player's warband also appears in that player's goal column, tagged "shared".
- **Who rules:** an empty site shows the bandit figure; when the Bandit Crown's holder rules it through them the figure wears a ring in the holder's colour, shaped like the pick ring with a light inner and dark outer edge, and its title names them. The enlarged site lists who rules it; an enlarged denizen at a site lists who rules the card, which differs from the site during the Grand Mask wearer's turn.
- **Answer another player's request (R-6.5.a, R-6.5.b, R-5.5.2.a):** a warband move that needs permission, a Citizen's choice to join a defence, and the defender's leave for that Citizen each put the asked player on the clock with a question panel naming who asks for what, and two answers: Allow or Refuse (Join or Stay out for the Citizen). Allow is dimmed with the engine's reason when the board no longer allows the move. Other seats see whom the game is waiting on. Nothing moves and nothing is rolled until the answer is sent.
- **Choose the defending side's losses (R-5.5.6.a):** when the attacker wins and the defending force leaves a choice, the Chancellor (for an Imperial defence) or the defender is on the clock with one slider per group of the defending force and a count of chosen against owed; "Kill these warbands" is dimmed with the engine's reason until the count is right. A defeated attacker chooses their own losses in the sacrifice step, and only when their force holds more than one group; a loss with only one possible pick is made by the engine.
- **Stack the Pilgrimage draw (Law Glossary "Discard"):** the asked player's question shows the drawn denizens as cards; each tap puts the next card down, labelled with its place in the stack, and the last untapped card goes on top. "Stack them" sends the answer once the order is fixed; Back untaps the last card and never sends anything. Other seats see only that the player is answering, never the cards.
- **What only you saw:** after an action that showed its actor a card nobody else saw (a peek, or the relic they sent to the bottom of the relic deck), a notice above the action panel pictures those cards, captioned with the card that showed them, until the next Action by anyone. The actor's own discards and kept card are never in the notice; the history row pictures those to the actor. Other seats see neither.
- **Undo:** the turn banner offers one light button labelled Undo, the platform's own as Estates uses. With a Manual Draft Entry in progress (the staged action or any panel's picks) it steps back one pick, as Back does, and its tooltip says so; otherwise its tooltip names the last Processed Action it would reverse (the history names it too), and it stops at the first one that revealed information (R-X.3).
- **Browse history:** the history controls above the side tabs on a wide screen, and the phone controls on a narrow one, move History View backward and forward. The table shows the displayed state; the action panel gives way to that state's turn banner, and nothing on the board is offered.
- **Fit the panel:** the panel above the board is scaled down, never scrolled and never clipped, to a fraction of the column so the map keeps its size; it refits when its content grows or shrinks, including growth that lands while a fit is being read (an image finishing, a step's cards arriving), and a fit that ran short overlaps the board until the next read rather than hiding cards.
- **Waiting on a send:** while an Action is being sent or the next state is being shown, every panel control is disabled and every draft reads as empty, so each panel shows its first step and the board offers nothing. The send ends every draft whether or not it is accepted, and a newly shown state does too; a state update that shows no new state gives the picks back.
- **What the panels do not offer:** a Rest power whose choice is not a favor bank is shown refused with that reason; a machine state with no panel says so in the panel's place.

Every board tap has a keyboard path: a pickable card is a button, and focus opens the same preview a hover does. An inert card cannot be inspected from the keyboard.

## Coexistence and precedence

One semantic derivation in the session decides what each site means (start here, travel, target, move onto your site, or nothing) and whether the rest of the map dims; the sites layer draws that result and nothing else.

- Setup's site pick exists only in the Setup machine state on the seat's own turn, and takes precedence over every other board offer while it exists.
- Only one Act Phase action is staged at a time, so the ring means one thing per screen: the staged action decides which layer offers targets and the others offer nothing.
- There is one Let another peek picker. In this seat's own Act Phase it is the staged action: the seat card's button stages it exactly as the grid's chip does, replacing any staged action and its picks as choosing another tile would, and the picker shows in the action panel, not on the seat card; the button then reads Cancel and leaves it. Choosing another tile closes it. Outside this seat's Act Phase the seat card's button opens the picker on the seat card and stages nothing, and a staged action cannot exist there.
- The Campaign panel follows the valid actions, not the phase: while Campaign is chosen and the engine offers it, the panel takes the place of every machine-state panel, the question included, until it is sent, cancelled or left with "Back to the question".
- Map dimming belongs to choosing among sites: Setup, Travel and Campaign dim the rest of the map; Muster, Trade, Recover, Peek and Move warbands never do.
- The Campaign target outline coexists with the ring and sits outside it.
- The enlarged card sits above the open seat, which sits above the table, so hovering a card inside the open seat still enlarges it. With a mouse the preview is inert and never takes a pick; on touch it takes the next tap and nothing else does.
- An open seat covers the table: a press outside it closes it rather than picking, and the offers are unchanged once it closes. Its own cards are inspectable, never pickable.
- Back and Undo unwind Manual Draft Entries only; an Auto Draft Entry (the Setup action, the only Trade option, the defender a Sneak Attack names, a one-die attack pool) is never something they return to. The only legal start site and the only legal adviser are derived rather than stored, so there is nothing to return to either. A panel's picks unwind before the staged action under them. With no manual entry left, Undo reverses the last Processed Action.
- History View wins over every pick: no board offer, no action panel and no send exists while it is shown. The enlarged card and the open seat keep working on the displayed state.

## Shared visual state

The session owns the drafts and the open seat below; the card preview is a model-level value of its own. Components read them and call their methods. Every draft is held as the player's last pick and read back only while it still applies, so a stale value offers nothing rather than something illegal. The session clears every draft in `beforeNewState()`, before a new state is published, and on every send. While an Action is being sent (`processingActions`) or the next state is being shown (`updatingVisibleState`), the session's live seat is empty, so every draft, the staged action included, reads as empty and no board layer offers a pick; the shared `busy` disables every panel control, and Back and Undo do nothing.

- **Staged action and its stages (action, the Chancellor's split of a short setup bank, modifiers, warband move, site (a Travel's destination waits here while its way to pay is asked), card, discard order, relic slot, banner, option, When Played picks for an R-6.1 play, a banner's bid, the People's Favor's start bank).** Producer: the action grid, the panels and the board taps. Consumers: every panel and board layer that offers a pick. Effects: which layer lights and what the panel asks. Lifetime: Back pops the highest manual stage; a send, cancel, or any newly shown state clears them. Validity: each consumer asks the engine again on the displayed state. History View: nothing is offered, and the drafts are cleared when a state is shown.
- **Setup site, adviser and discard order.** Producer: Setup's taps. Consumers: the map and the Setup panel. Effects: the start ring, the hand shown, the discard taps. Lifetime and validity as the staged selection; the site must still be legal and the card still in hand.
- **Declared modifiers and their picks (R-7.4).** A stage of the staged action, after the action and before what it acts on. Producer: the modifier picker. Consumer: the send of Travel, Muster, Trade, Search and Recover. Effects: the ticked cards and their pickers. Lifetime: declaring or dropping a modifier clears the picks made under it (site, card and the rest); Back takes the last modifier declared, one at a time, after any pick made under it; leaving the action (Back past it, cancel, a send) or a newly shown state clears them. Validity: only modifiers the engine lists as usable for the staged action are sent.
- **Campaign defender, battle plans, targets and attack dice.** Producer: the Campaign panel and the site taps. Consumers: the panel, the sites layer and the dice row. Effects: the target ring and outline, the panel's lists, the dice lit. A staged flow in that order. Lifetime: Back takes the last target, then the last plan, one at a time, then the defender; choosing another defender clears the plans, targets and dice; a plan dropped drops the targets it alone allowed and keeps the dice; the defender a Sneak Attack names and a one-die pool are Auto Draft Entries, so in a Sneak Attack Back with no pick left leaves for the question; cancel, "Back to the question", a send or a newly shown state clear all four. Validity: the defender must still be one the engine accepts, or the one a Sneak Attack names; a plan must still be usable; a target must still be offered for that defender and those plans; the dice are held between one and the warbands on the board.
- **Warband move.** A stage of the staged action. Producer: the direction tap on the map or the seat card. Consumer: the count question. Lifetime: Back returns to the direction; a send, cancel or newly shown state clears it. Validity: the direction must still be one the engine offers.
- **Let another peek picker.** Producer: the seat card's button and the grid's chip. Consumers: the seat card and the action panel. Effects: the picker's list and the button's Cancel. In this seat's Act Phase it is the staged action and lives and dies with it; elsewhere it is the seat card's own flag. Lifetime: a send, Cancel, another tile, or a newly shown state closes it. Validity: it shows only while the engine offers Let another peek to this seat.
- **Adviser being played.** Producer: a tap on a facedown adviser, or the only legal one. Consumers: the seat card and the placements. Lifetime and validity as the staged selection.
- **Panel drafts** (Search's kept card, the choices beside its placement, the placement, the advisers discarded to meet the limit, the When Played picks and the discard order; a question's answer in progress and the Pilgrimage stack; the Citizenship offer's Exile, relic space and terms; the spoils; the attacker's plans when Citizens were asked to join first, the defending side's plans, and each declared plan's picks; the attacker's sacrificed or defeated warbands; the Campaign's skull-loss order; the Wake's favor steps and site power; the Rest powers' banks; the Action powers' choices; the Citizenship recolor; the defending side's losses). Producer: the panel's controls. Consumer: that panel and its send. Each is a staged flow over the shared helpers, in its own stage order. Lifetime: Back pops the last pick (one discard or one stacked card at a time). The spoils, the defending side's plans and losses, the Wake, the Rest powers, the Action powers and the Citizenship recolor each hold every pick as one entry, so one Back or Undo clears that panel's picks together and its defaults return; a send or a newly shown state clears them, so no pick survives into the next question or to the next seat in a hotseat game. Validity: a pick is read back only while the displayed state still offers it, and one that depends on another (a prize on its player, terms on their counterparty, a second play on the kept card, the relic space on the chosen Exile) applies only while that other pick is unchanged.
- **Card preview.** Producer: a hover, focus or hold on a card. Consumer: the preview layer. Validity: the preview reads its owner's current props, so it pictures what the owner now shows. Lifetime: the owner's leave or blur, the owner unmounting, or on touch the next tap. The board's card lists are keyed by card (a denizen by its id, a relic by its slot, a site by slot and card), so a card that leaves its space unmounts its owner and the preview closes. It is one value per tab, since there is one pointer; it never enters game state and follows the displayed state in History View.
- **Open seat.** Producer: a seat's name pill. Consumer: the seat overlay. Validity: it opens only a seat of the game on screen. Lifetime: the backdrop, Escape or the pill, and the session, so another table opens with no seat open. It shows the displayed state in History View.

Silent restoration replaces the displayed state and every derived offer follows it without motion.

## Render ownership

- The board is one fixed surface scaled by the shared scaling wrapper; each layer owns the pick chrome of the thing it draws. The sites layer owns site cards, their tokens, the dimming of the map and site chips; the strip layer owns denizens, relic slots, their empty outlines and their rings; the pieces layer owns warbands and pawns and is never a hit target; the tracks layer owns the printed spaces, the discard counts and the deck spaces.
- A pickable board card widens its hit area by twelve screen pixels on each side, drawn nowhere. The board reads its on-screen scale whenever the wrapper's transform changes, not on a timer.
- The enlarged card and the open seat are mounted after the table layout, outside its scroll columns, the board's wrapper and the fitted panel, because a transform is the containing block for fixed layers. They cover the whole viewport rather than the table, so each pads itself by the device's safe-area insets: the open seat is capped at the dynamic viewport height less those insets and scrolls inside itself, and the enlarged card is sized from its layer's measured safe area, with the card and the boxes under it scaled together when they would not fit.
- One frame per panel: the action panel's container draws the only frame around a step, and the panel inside it draws none. Groups within a panel are set apart by spacing, a heading or a faint top divider, and a note by a tint; a border marks only a control, and the amber border its picked state.
- The side column has no background of its own: the history controls, the side tabs and the chat sit on the site's dark page in the platform's own colours, and Oath passes them none. Oath's own panels in the tabs paint their own backgrounds.
- Warband counts on a card are drawn by one component from game state, wherever the card is drawn. A seat's warband counts come from one reading shared by the seat card and the open seat: Imperial seats count purple from the one Imperial bank, an Exile their own colour.
- Every card-valued choice is drawn by one row component from the options its panel's draft computes; the panel owns what a pick does, and the row owns only the ring, the untap and the enlarge.
- The Reliquary is drawn by space, each space by its own slot, on the seat card and in the open seat alike.
- Actor-only facts (what a peek showed, a relic sent to the bottom of the relic deck, a pile deposit's cards, a Search's discards) are shown only when the action's player is this player, never because the client holds the data: a Local game or Host View holds every field. The notice, the history row and the questions that show a relic picture each card, enlarged on hover or hold.
- Hidden information is rendered by rule, not by data availability. This player's hand is drawn as cards where a panel asks for it; on the seat card and in the open seat every hand is a row of backs, its Visions with the Vision back by the public `handVisions` count. A relic is drawn as a back unless this player's own peeks name it. A facedown site shows a back and no denizen or relic spaces. A discard pile shows its count and the back of its top card; the world deck shows the back of its top card and no count; the relic deck shows a back and no count. Another player's facedown adviser is a back drawn from the public row, the Vision back where the row says `vision`; this player's own is a dimmed front named from their own list, even in a Local game or Host View. The history names a kept or played card only when the engine recorded that the play showed it. Every facedown card draws the back the state publishes for it, never one assumed by the drawing site: the world deck's top by `topCardBackType`, each discard pile's top by its recorded back, a facedown adviser by its row on the seat card, in the open seat, in a power's choice row and in an exchange's terms, a hand by `handVisions`, and relics and sites by their own kind. A Vision whose back shows always shows the Vision back, wherever it is drawn. A card is drawn facedown only by naming the back it shows, so no drawing site has a default back; before the opening deal the world deck has no top card and shows none.
- Motion: none is state-derived. History rows appear and leave without animation, keyed by action, in engine order. The only motion is hover feedback and the turn pulse on the active seat's border. The Supply marker snaps to its circle.

## Viewport

The platform's table layout (`DefaultTableLayout`) owns the bottom safe-area inset, as the shell above it owns the top one; Oath adds no inset and no viewport height of its own inside the table. Oath relies on that layout for the solved column height the fitted panel and the board divide between them, and for the bottom (and side) padding that keeps the board, the side tabs and the phone history controls clear of the home indicator.

## Verification scenarios

1. Setup, Chancellor: the game opens with the pawn at the top Cradle site and the hand offered; no site lights. Tap a card to keep and one of the other two; the Action is sent and the next seat is on the clock. Manual.
2. Setup, an Exile: every legal site lights with "start here" and the rest dims, and the panel shows the hand from the start. Tap a card to keep, then a site (or the other way round): the ring leaves the map, the kept card stays marked, and the discard taps follow. Back unwinds the card, then the site, returning the lit map; Undo does the same. Manual.
3. Setup, Undo around a send: before the send, Undo reads "your last pick" and steps back through the picks as Back does. After the send, no Undo is offered for the choice: its discard went into the concealed pile (R-X.3), and the next seat is on the clock. Manual.
4. Travel: choose Travel; the affordable sites light with their costs and the rest dim. Back or Undo returns to the grid with nothing lit; a tap travels and the ring leaves. Manual.
5. Campaign: choose a defender; the sites they could lose light. Tap two; both wear the rose outline and appear ticked in the panel. Tick Relic Hunter and a relic at a chosen site; untick the plan and the relic leaves the ticked set. Back untaps the last target, then the next, then the plans one at a time, then clears the defender and the map; Cancel leaves the Campaign. The target lists are automated; the flow is manual.
6. Play an adviser with two facedown advisers: both light; tapping one shows its placements; Back returns to the lit advisers. With one facedown adviser the placements show at once and Back leaves the action. Manual.
7. Inspect: hover a denizen; the enlarged card appears and closes on leave; move straight to the next denizen and the preview does not blink off. On touch, hold a pickable card and release: the preview stays and the card is not picked; tap anywhere to close. A card removed from the table while enlarged closes its preview. Manual.
8. Hidden information: as an Exile, the Chancellor's Reliquary shows four backs; as the Chancellor, holding the Scepter, all four show faces from the start, and the Exile's view still shows backs. Uncover one space by a Citizenship: the seat card and the open seat both show the other three in their own places. The decisions are automated; the rendering is manual.
9. History: after several actions, new rows appear at the top in engine order, and a row naming a site gives its printed name, or "a facedown site in the <Region>" while it is facedown. Step backward with the desktop controls: the action panel gives way to the displayed turn banner, nothing lights on the board, and a tap on a card only inspects it. Step forward to the end: the live panel returns with no draft left over. Manual.
10. A draft over a new state: start a Travel with a modifier ticked; let another seat's Action be processed (hotseat); the grid returns with nothing ticked. Start Travel again: the modifier is not declared. Manual.
11. Sneak Attack: after another player's Campaign ends, the holder is asked; tap Campaign: the panel shows the named defender, no Supply and no defender list, and the target sites light; "Back to the question" returns to Campaign and Pass, and so does Back once no target or plan is left. Declare: the banner names the campaigner and the held turn, and the turn player's Act Phase resumes after the Campaign. Pass sends the answer and the turn resumes. The panel's opening rule and the banner's names are automated; the flow is manual.
12. Second Wind first: once a Sneak Attack is passed and a free action is due, only the free action's tile is lit, the line above the grid gives the reason beside a Give it up button, and a dimmed tile tapped repeats it; using or giving up the free action clears the line. The decision is automated; the rendering is manual.
13. Bandit Crown and Grand Mask: with the Crown held, empty sites show the bandit figure in the holder's colour, titled with their name; with the Grand Mask on an Exile during their turn, the enlarged denizen at a purple site names the wearer, and the Imperial players again after the turn passes. The decisions are automated; the rendering is manual.
14. Obsidian Cage: after the Cage takes survivors, the counts show on its thumbnail, in the open seat and in its enlarged view; use its Action with two colours ticked and a count each, and both moves are sent. False Prophet: the Vision under an Exile's warband shows on both seats, "shared" on the Exile's. Picks and counts are automated; the rendering is manual.
15. What only you saw: use Dowsing Sticks or Family Heirloom; the notice and the history line name and picture the relic for the actor and another seat's line does not; the notice leaves when the actor acts again. A discard by a power names its region to all and its cards to the actor alone. The facts are automated; the notice's placement is manual.
16. The questions: False Prophet (reveal, facedown with an adviser to discard at the limit, discard), Wild Mounts (a Beast card instead, or the plans); each button is disabled with the engine's reason when refused, and a second question after the first starts with nothing picked. The adviser choice is automated; the panels are manual.
17. Pilgrimage stacking: play Pilgrimage with two or more denizens at your site; the question shows the drawn cards to you while another seat sees only that you are answering. Tap them: the place labels appear and Back untaps. Stack them: the pile count rises by the number drawn and the history names the cards to you alone. The answer's positions are automated; the panel is manual.
18. Move warbands: choose it with warbands on your board; your site wears the ring with no price chip and the map does not dim, and the Board counter lights for the other direction. Tap your site with several warbands: the panel asks how many; Back returns to the direction. Manual.
19. Act on a card at your site: choose Muster; the legal denizens rise and ring, the map does not dim; Back returns to the grid. Trade with a card allowing one option sends at once. Manual.
20. Take a banner and search a source: with Recover chosen, an affordable banner shows "pay N" on the rail or its holder's seat; with Search chosen, the deck and your pile show their prices. Back leaves either with nothing lit. Manual.
21. The Reliquary and the Scepter: as the Grand Scepter holder with a relic at your site, choose Peek: only the strip's relic lights, never a Reliquary space, whose faces already show. Take the Grand Scepter in a Campaign: the new holder's seat card shows all four faces at once, and the former holder's still does. The decisions are automated; the rendering is manual.
22. Open a seat while a pick is live: with Travel chosen, open another seat; its cards enlarge on hover and do not pick; a press outside closes it and the lit destinations are still offered. Manual.
23. Panel fit: in a Search with a Land Warden second play, choose a second card so the panel grows; the panel refits within the column without clipping. Manual.
24. Rapid interruption: hover a card and, before the preview settles, let another seat's Action be processed; the preview closes if its card left the table, and the new state's offers show with no leftover ring. Automated with a mouse and under touch emulation (`tests/contractScenarios.spec.ts`).
25. Picks and Undo: in a Search, keep a card and choose a placement; Undo steps back to the placements, then to the drawn cards. With no pick left, no Undo is offered, because the Search drew from the concealed deck (R-X.3). Let another seat's Action be processed mid-pick (hotseat): the panel starts again with nothing picked. The session's Back, Undo, new-state reset, updating window and history gate are automated; the panel is manual.
26. Let another peek: with a facedown adviser, on another player's turn, open Let another peek from your seat card and show it to one player; the turn and the clock do not move.
