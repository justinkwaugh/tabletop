# Oath: picks, panels and the table

## Visual intents

- **Choose a starting site (Setup, R-1.23.1):** while the seat on the clock has not placed its pawn, every faceup site the engine accepts wears the amber pick ring and a "start here" chip, and the rest of the map dims. When exactly one site is legal it is taken for the player rather than picked, and nothing lights. The panel lists those sites under their regions as framed rows with their pictures: a tap selects one, ringed and tagged "start here", and a tap on another moves the choice. The panel shows the hand from the start (R-1.20 deals before R-1.23 places): the site and the card to keep are tapped in either order, the discard order follows once both are chosen, and the pawn appears when that Action is processed. Unaffected: seat cards, the rail, the tracks.
- **Choose a Travel destination (R-5.6, R-7.1.4, R-11.12):** with Travel chosen, the staged bar reads "Travel", and every destination the engine accepts wears the ring on the map with a chip of what it costs, and the rest of the map dims; a destination the player cannot afford is not offered. A chip reads "2 Supply" where the one way to pay is Supply alone, and otherwise each way as its number and symbols, with "or" between two ("1+[favor]", "2 or 0+[secret]", "0+[secret]"); on a phone's map a token in a chip is no taller than the number beside it. In a wide panel (a desktop) the panel lists the destinations under their region's name, in the board's order, as tiles: each tile is the site's picture at card shape (its back while facedown) with its name and its cost. The three regions stand side by side as columns, like the board, each region's tiles stacked down its column with the picture beside the name. A long name wraps and is never cut short. A destination with one way to pay is one button, the whole tile; its cost reads its Supply ("0" when a token is paid and no Supply) and, for a toll, the favor and the payee's name chip ("2 Supply + 1 [favor] to <chip>"; two tolls "+ 2 [favor] to <chip> <chip>"; a toll the bandits rule "+ 1 [favor] burned"), and a flipped secret "+ [secret]". The card that asks for a toll is named in the History, whose Travel line names each toll's favor, its payee and its card ("; 1 [favor] to <name> (Toll Roads)", "; burned 1 [favor] (Toll Roads)") and a flipped secret ("; flipped 1 [secret]"). A destination with more than one way to pay (Way Station's favor for no Supply, the Buried Giant's flipped secret) is the same tile with one button per way under its name, each as wide as the widest label, and the tile itself is not a button. The tiles are sized from the panel's unshrunk box, so fitting the panel never changes their layout. A tile or a way's button sends the Travel. On a phone, held upright or sideways, the lit map is the menu: the panel is one line, "Tap a lit site.", and the board takes the height the panel gives up. Each chip sits wholly on its card, and a 28 px magnifier straddles each lit site's top-left corner at that size whatever the zoom, its tap area reaching out past the corner; it enlarges the site and never travels. A tap anywhere else on a lit site with one way to pay and no toll sends the Travel. A tap on one with more, or with a toll, rings it more heavily and opens its ways in the panel: a button per way ("2 Supply", "0 + [secret]", "2 Supply + 1 [favor] to <chip>") each as wide as the widest label, which Undo closes; a tap on another lit site moves the pick or travels there. Held upright, the site's name alone tops the panel, and under it the site's picture, as large as the width the ways leave, with the ways stacked to its right, level with its top. Held sideways, the site's picture, name and the ways stand on one row; a panel too narrow for the row puts the ways under the name. Held upright, the map is framed on one region's sites, at one zoom for every region: the region the pawn is in, or else the first in the board's order with a destination. A row of region chips along the map's top edge names each region with its count of destinations, a region with none not shown; a chip frames that region and chooses nothing. Held sideways, the map frames every site and there are no chips. When the step closes the board's view returns to where it was, unless a pan or zoom by hand took it over. Unaffected: seat cards, the strip of cards at each site, the rail.
- **Target a Campaign (R-5.5.2):** the panel is headed "Campaign" with its cost ("Free" when it costs nothing). With two or more defenders the rules allow it asks "Attack who?" over one button each, at the widest one's width, a player named by their colour chip; a lone legal defender is chosen for the player. With a defender chosen, one line reads "Against <chip>. Tap targets." ("Against the bandits." for the bandits) and the panel lists every target, grouped under "Sites" (those the defender rules), "Relics and banners" (with Relic Hunter declared, the relics at chosen sites, "+1 die") and "Pawn and favor" (the defender's pawn, named by their chip): each a framed row with its picture, the rows at the widest one's width, a tap adding it, ringed with a "target" tag beside its name (the tag's room kept while it is off), and a second tap dropping it. A target the rules refuse with what is already chosen is not listed until it can be chosen. "Attack dice" and "Battle plans" are headings like the targets'. "Declare" shows once a target and the dice are picked and every declared plan has its picks; a complete declaration the engine refuses shows the engine's reason in its place. On the map the sites that defender rules (the Bandit Crown's empty sites included) wear the ring and a "target?" chip, a chosen one the rose outline and a "target" chip; sites the defender cannot lose dim. The panel sends the Campaign. Unaffected: seat cards, the rail.
- **The Campaign's dice (R-5.5.3 to R-5.5.5):** from the Campaign's declaration until its spoils are placed, the action panel of every seat opens with one row naming the attacker and the defender, then the attack and defence pools as unrolled dice while the defending side answers, and once rolled the faces with their totals in swords and defense. The row is the same for the deciding seat and a waiting one, and it stays through the sacrifice, the defending side's losses and the spoils. The rail does not show them.
- **Campaign out of turn (Sneak Attack):** the asked player's question offers Campaign and Pass. Campaign replaces the question with the Campaign panel, its defender the player the card names ("Against <chip>.", with no defender list) and its cost "Free"; the map offers targets exactly as in Target a Campaign. Undo takes back the targets and plans one per press, and its next press returns to the question with nothing sent. While the Campaign runs, the turn banner reads "Your turn is paused" for the seat whose turn it is and "<seat>'s turn is paused" for every other seat; the dice row names the campaigner.
- **A free action comes next (R-10.2):** while a power's free Travel or Campaign is due (Second Wind once a Sneak Attack is passed, Brass Horse, a free Campaign after a Muster or a Search), the engine offers only that action, giving it up, or ending the Act Phase; the grid dims the other majors and lists no minor, a line above it reads "Free Travel next." ("Free Campaign next.", "Free Travel or Campaign next.") beside a Skip button, the free action's tile shows the Supply the engine charges for it, "no Supply", and a dimmed tile tapped reads "Free Travel first." ("Free Campaign first.").
- **The Act Phase grid, and why an action is unavailable (R-4.2):** the grid is headed by the seat's Supply ("Supply 3") beside End Act Phase. The six major actions are always listed, each with its cost ("2–4 Supply"), dimmed when unavailable; a minor action is listed only when it can be taken ("Adviser", "Use a power", "Peek", "Show", "Move warbands", "Offer Citizenship", "Exile Citizen", "Exile yourself"). Hovering a tile writes its short summary into the one line under the grid; hovering or tapping a dimmed major writes there instead why it cannot be taken, read from the displayed state, in short words for the causes the grid names: "Needs 2 Supply; you have 1.", "Nothing to draw.", "No card here.", "No empty card here.", "Needs 1 [favor]; you have 0.", "Needs 1 [secret] or 2 [favor].", "Vow of Poverty: no [favor] from Trade.", "Nothing to recover.", "Can’t pay for any of it.", "Nobody here to attack.", "Free Travel first.". A cause is named only when every card, pile or destination fails for it; mixed causes, and any other cause, keep the engine's sentence, which names each other seat by its colour chip and the reader as "you" ("your" for theirs). A tapped reason stays after the pointer leaves; a hover on another tile replaces it, except a hover on "Use a power" while it wears the ring for that reason (below), which keeps the reason and the ring; a newly shown state clears it, so it is gone once the action is offered. Nothing on the board changes. Every other reason a panel shows names each other seat by name and the reader as "you". With an action chosen, the bar above its menu reads the action's name, the word on its tile ("Trade", "Adviser", "Travel"); on a phone, Travel's line reads "Tap a lit site." instead.
- **A card that changes an action (R-7.4):** a major action that cannot be taken as printed stays dimmed. Tapping it gives the reason, and no card is named there: where a card the player can use and pay for would make it possible, the "Use a power" chip wears the amber ring, drawn inside its own edge with its border and words in amber, for as long as that reason shows; where no card would, nothing is ringed. The minor action reads "Use a power" and is offered when the seat has an "Action:" power to use or a modifier card that puts a row in an action's menu the menu lacks without it: a new choice, or one at another price, payment, gain or draw. A card whose condition only narrows the menu or that only adds something after the action (Hospitality, Hunting Party), and one the engine applies by itself, is not listed, and none is while a free action is due (R-10.2). Behind it the cards come first, under "Makes an action possible" (the action cannot be taken without the card) and "Changes an action" (it can), then the "Action:" powers under "Action powers". A card's entry shows the card, its name, the action it changes and its printed text, what using it costs ("put 1 [secret] on it", "free"), in rose what it brings beyond that ("ends your Act Phase", "sacrifices 1 warband from your board"), and one button, "<Action> with <card>". The button opens that action's menu with the card in use and sends nothing; the menu lists what the engine accepts with the card, at the price the card makes it, and no button there carries a card's name. Undo puts the card down and returns to the powers. In the menu a card cannot be put down while the action is possible only with it: of the cards that each make the action possible, one stays in use, whatever is picked. Where the action is possible without the card it can be put down, and the card is also offered inside the action's own menu as before. Undo returns to the powers from an action entered there for as long as a card is in use.
- **Move warbands (R-6.5):** with Move warbands chosen, the panel lists each move the engine allows as a row (from your board to your site, from your site to your board, to or from another Imperial player), with the owner's warband and one number button per count it allows; a number sends that move. On the map your own site wears the ring when warbands may go onto it, and the Board counter on your seat card wears it for the opposite direction; the map does not dim and no Travel price shows.
- **Muster (R-5.2, R-9.3):** with Muster chosen, the panel lists every card at the player's site a favor can go on: a row per card, in the strip's order, with its suit symbol and name, and one button showing what it places and the warbands it brings ("1 [favor] → 2 [warband]"), the warband in the colour the Muster draws, the Empire's purple for a Citizen. A bank that runs short gives what it holds, counted on the button, 0 included; that Muster stays listed, since the favor placed still blocks the card. A card the engine refuses is not listed. The cards that can take the favor wear the ring in the strip; the button sends the Muster with the declared modifiers. Undo leaves Muster.
- **Peek (R-6.3):** with Peek chosen, the panel lists each relic at the player's site they have not seen, by its space, with its back and a Look button that sends. A relic the player has already seen shows its face and is not offered, and with nothing left to see the grid does not list Peek. The listed relics wear the ring in the strip; the map does not dim.
- **Trade (R-5.3.2, R-9.3):** with Trade chosen, the panel lists every trade the engine accepts at the player's site: a row per card, in the strip's order, with its suit symbol and name once, and a button per option in that row, the cost and the gain as counts beside the secret and favor tokens ("1 [secret] → 3 [favor]"). A bank that runs short gives what it holds, counted on the button; a gain of 0 shows as a muted 0, with nothing under it, and the trade stays listed, since the token placed still blocks the card. The favor a Trade for secrets gains beside its secrets (Careless) follows them on the button ("2 [favor] → 0 [secret] + 1 [favor]") and in its accessible name ("and 1 favor"). The bank is the card's suit, so the button does not repeat it; the button's accessible name and tooltip give the whole trade in words. A card or option the engine refuses is not listed. The cards that can be traded with wear the ring in the strip; a tap on one only enlarges it; a button sends that Trade with the declared modifiers and the default tolls. Undo leaves Trade.
- **The Reliquary, known to the Scepter's holder (R-6.4-H1):** the Grand Scepter's holder sees the face of every covered Reliquary space on the seat card and in the open seat, from setup on; everyone else sees backs, except a space they were shown. When the Scepter changes hands the new holder sees all four at once, and the former holder keeps what they saw. No Peek targets the Reliquary.
- **Let another player peek (R-6.1, R-9.4, R-6.6.1):** at any time, except while the game waits on this player's own decision, the player's seat card offers Let another peek (and in their Act Phase the grid's Show chip does too); opened, it lists each facedown adviser of this player's, and, for the Grand Scepter's holder, each Reliquary space, with a button for every player who may be shown it (any other player for an adviser, an Exile for a Reliquary relic). A tap sends the Action. Nothing on the board lights. The history names an adviser to the player shown and to its holder alone, and a Reliquary relic to the Exile shown alone; the Scepter's holder already knows it. In the shown player's own seat, the adviser then shows its face, dimmed as a facedown card and marked "facedown; shown to you", on the seat card and in the open seat, until its holder discards or plays it; every other seat still sees a back.
- **Recover a relic or a banner (R-5.4):** with Recover chosen, the panel lists under "Relics" the relics at the player's site the engine lets them take, each with its picture (its back unless this player has seen it) and the price its site prints ("3 [favor] to [Order]", "burn 2 [favor]"), then the banners they can outbid, each with its least bid ("2+ [favor]"). A relic's button sends. A banner's button asks its price, "<Banner>: pay how many [favor]?", as a row of number buttons over every amount the engine accepts, the least picked first, and for the People's Favor the bank its favor starts returning to ("Return its [favor], starting at:") by the suits' symbols. "Recover" shows once the price and the People's Favor's bank are picked, and sends; its accessible name names the banner. A red line shows only when complete picks are refused. What the player cannot pay for is not listed. On the board the listed relics wear the ring in the strip, and a listed banner the ring and a "pay N" chip wherever it lies: on the rail while unclaimed, or on its holder's seat card.
- **Play a facedown adviser (R-6.1):** with the action chosen, the panel shows this player's playable facedown advisers as a row of cards; a tap picks one and rings it, and one legal adviser is taken for the player rather than picked. The panel then offers the placements the engine accepts as buttons (play to the site, turn faceup, reveal as a Vision, play the Conspiracy, discard); a placement the engine refuses is not offered. The panel declares no modifier on this play; in a game created at revision 2 or later a mandatory Search modifier (Book of Records) applies by itself, and the history row says what the play gained and names the card whose modifier changed it. In a game created at revision 3 or later, where Forced Labor demands a favor the play sends it unasked, with no line of its own in the panel; an adviser that cannot pay it is not offered.
- **Search a source (R-5.1.1, R-7.4):** with Search chosen, the panel lists every source the engine accepts: the world deck and the discard pile the Search would draw from, each with the back on its top, and a button with its Supply, the favor any toll takes and who gets it, and how many cards it draws ("draw 1, from the bottom" with Mushrooms). While a declared modifier names the pile the Search draws from (Errand Boy, Observatory), each pile it may name is its own row, and its button names that pile for the modifier and sends. One Search draws from one pile, so declaring one of them puts the other down. A modifier whose region is where the discards go (Bracken) adds no row and keeps its own pick. An empty pile or a source the player cannot afford is not listed. On the board each listed source wears the ring and its price chip.
- **Choose a card:** wherever a panel asks for a card (the card to keep at setup, an adviser to discard, a When Played card, the Wild Mounts substitute, False Prophet's adviser, a relic or banner prize, exchange terms, a modifier, warbands standing at a site), the options are a row of cards: a tap picks one and rings it, a tap on a picked card untaps it, a multi-pick takes no more than its limit, and Undo untaps as it unwinds; the magnifier in a card's corner enlarges it. A card the chooser may not see is drawn as its back. Counts, players and sites keep their own controls.
- **Choose a count:** wherever a number is chosen (the defending side's losses and the attacker's sacrifice, the Citizenship terms, an exchange's terms and the warbands it moves in, a power's counts, favor burned for secrets (from 1, "None" being the 0), a banner's price, the Chancellor's split of a short setup bank, a warband move), the numbers the rules allow are a row of buttons, 0 among them where it is legal, the picked one ringed, wrapping onto another line when long. A power's open count (Witch's Bargain) runs to the player's secrets or the favor they can use, whichever is more, the favor Vow of Kinship keeps in a bank included (R-7.1.2). A tap picks, and the panel's own button sends, except where the number is the whole choice (a warband move), which a tap sends. No count is a slider or a field.
- **Choose a bank:** wherever a favor bank is chosen (the Wake's People's Favor return, a Rest power in a game created before the turn-flow revision, a power's or a question's bank, the start of a banner's favor return), the options are the suits' symbols as printed beside the board's banks, in the board's order, each with its bank's favor count (an empty bank a plain 0 with no token, as on the board's bank discs) and its name for a screen reader: a tap picks one and rings it, and a choice that may be left empty untaps on a second tap. No bank is chosen from a list or by its name. Wherever a panel's text names favor, secrets or a suit (a prompt, a reason, a term, a cost or a gain), it shows the token with its count, or the suit's symbol, in place of the word, never both; the word stays as the image's alternative text, and the banners' names and the tooltips stay in words. The History rows draw favor, secrets, banks and counted warbands the same way, a warband in its owner's colour (the Empire's in the Chancellor's), read from the row's own record so a row looks the same all game: those a move names, the owner a Muster recorded (the Empire's for a Citizen), those an exile leaves Imperial, the one owner an Action power, a sacrifice or a choice of losses recorded, for a power that recorded none those of the seat it acted on, else the actor's own, as for a record that names no owner; a battle's losses to skulls and Supply stay words everywhere.
- **No default pick (a power's choices):** wherever a power's text asks for one pick (a bank, a card, a player, a site, a region; a Use a power, a When Played card from a Search or a facedown adviser, a modifier, a battle plan, a Rest power in a game created before the turn-flow revision), nothing is picked or ringed until the player taps, unless only one option is legal, which starts picked. The confirm (Use, Play) shows once every required pick is made, with the engine's reason in red only when the picks are complete and refused; an action's menu rows that wait on a modifier's pick show once it is made.
- **Enlarge a card:** a click or tap on any faceup card or site on the table, or on the magnifier of a card in a panel (or of a lit site, on a phone during Travel), shows it enlarged in the centre until the next press or Escape (rule 4). A site card's enlarged view lists what is at the site and, under that, what the site does: its Site Reference sentence in the Buried Giant card library's words, with the suits, favor, secrets and attack dice it prints drawn as their symbols. The sentence is only there; the site on the board carries no tooltip for it. The seat card's Reliquary spaces (a known relic, or the trait an uncovered space shows), its goal card and its banners enlarge the same way; no part of the table enlarges anything in place. Nothing else changes.
- **The goals (R-2.11, R-3):** the rail under the map shows every live win condition, read from the displayed state, under no heading of its own: a line naming the next player to win, how and when, as the table stands (a Wake, the end die after a named round with its odds, or the end of round 8); then a box for the Oath, one for each revealed or shared Vision, and one for each Citizen's Successor goal. A box carries its heading ("The Oath of Supremacy", "Vision", "Successor", with "met" or "not met" at its right), the symbol of what the goal asks for (sites, the People's Favor, relics and banners, the Darkest Secret, the Grand Scepter) in the Oath's amber, a Vision's white or the Successor's purple, a sentence naming the seat by its name chip ("Ann is the Oathkeeper", "No one is the Oathkeeper", "Your Vision of Conquest", "Bo is a Citizen"), and the standing: a counted goal is a disc per seat in its colour with its count, the goal's seat ringed (the title's holder, the Vision's seat, the Citizen); a held goal is the holder's ringed disc, or a dashed disc and "unclaimed". A disc's words are its tooltip and accessible name, never a caption. A tie never reads as met. A tap or click on the goals opens the same boxes at a readable size over the table; a hover does nothing. The backdrop, Escape or the close button closes it. The seat cards keep only their Visions and the Successor goal, and the holder's board keeps the Oathkeeper tile.
- **Focus the board:** a focus view in the board's toolbar (Full, Cradle, Provinces, Hinterland, Banks), or "Zoom the board here" on an enlarged site, zooms and pans the board to that region, the seven favor banks or that site's row (rule 5). Nothing else changes.
- **Full screen:** the board's corner control or the `f` key expands the board over the page with the action panel and the enlarged card inside it; Escape or the control again returns (rules 6 and 7).
- **Open a seat:** the name pill on a seat card opens that seat at a readable size over the table; the backdrop, Escape or the pill again closes it.
- **Warbands on a card:** the warbands standing on the Obsidian Cage or on a revealed Vision show as colour counts on the card, on the seat card, in the open seat and in the enlarged card. A Vision carrying a player's warband also appears in that player's goal column, tagged "shared".
- **Who rules:** an empty site shows the bandit figure; when the Bandit Crown's holder rules it through them the figure wears a ring in the holder's colour, shaped like the pick ring with a light inner and dark outer edge, and its title names them. The enlarged site lists who rules it; an enlarged denizen at a site lists who rules the card, which differs from the site during the Grand Mask wearer's turn.
- **Answer another player's request (R-6.5.a, R-6.5.b, R-5.5.2.a):** a warband move that needs permission, a Citizen's choice to join a defence, and the defender's leave for that Citizen each put the asked player on the clock with a question panel, headed "A question for you" for the asked seat alone, naming who asks for what, and two answers: Allow or Refuse (Join or Stay out for the Citizen), as wide as the longer label at every width and at least 44 px tall on a phone. Allow is dimmed with the engine's reason when the board no longer allows the move. Other seats read "Waiting on <chip>." Nothing moves and nothing is rolled until the answer is sent.
- **Answer an offer of Citizenship (R-6.6.1, R-6.6.2, R-9.3):** the Exile is on the clock with a panel that opens with the asker's name chip, "offers you Citizenship." (an Exile offering it to themselves reads "You offer yourself Citizenship."), over the binding exchange as symbols in two rows: "You get", the Reliquary relic as this player sees it (its back unless their own peek named it) with its corner magnifier, then the Chancellor's relics and banners as cards with magnifiers and favor and secrets as counts beside their tokens; and "You give", the Exile's side drawn the same way with its counts in gold, the row left out when the Exile gives nothing. One line follows: the Exile's warbands in their colour, an arrow, the warbands the Empire replaces in its colour, and when some leave play "· N Removed" in rose. The warbands are then pieces, grouped by place in words ("On your board", "At" the site), each a 44 px button. With Imperial warbands enough, every piece shows Imperial and none can be tapped; with none, every piece is faded in the Exile's colour in a rose outline over "Removed", and none can be tapped; when the Empire is short, the first pieces it can cover in the engine's order show Imperial, ringed over "Imperial", and the rest Removed: a tap on a Removed piece makes it Imperial, and at the Empire's count the oldest pick goes back to Removed; a tap on an Imperial piece untaps it. Accept sends the picks as the warband groups the engine reads; it appears once as many are picked as the Empire covers, and a red line shows only when a rule blocks it; Refuse sends the refusal. On a phone too the two answers are as wide as the longer label, at least 44 px tall. Other seats read "Waiting on <chip>."
- **Choose the defending side's losses (R-5.5.6.a):** when the attacker wins and which warbands die can change the outcome (the force mixes owners, or part of it stands on a board; in a game created before the turn-flow revision, whenever the force leaves a choice), the Chancellor (for an Imperial defence) or the defender is on the clock under the heading "Losses", with "Pick N to kill.", one row of number buttons per group of the defending force (a board named by its owner's chip, "your" for the viewer) and a muted count of chosen against owed; "Kill" (rose) shows once the count is right, and the engine's reason shows in its place only when a complete pick is refused. A defeated attacker chooses their own losses in the sacrifice step, and only when their force holds more than one group; a loss with only one possible pick is made by the engine.
- **The battle's words (R-5.5.3 to R-5.5.7):** each Campaign step's panel is headed by its step: "Battle plans", "Battle", "Losses", "Spoils". The battle plans, for the defender, an ally and the attacker after the Citizens, read "Tap the plans to use." over the cards, then "Use plans" (the primary, shown once a plan is picked with its picks) and "No plans" (always, sending none whatever is picked), at one width; the engine's reason for refusing a set takes its button's place. The battle, with nothing to sacrifice, reads "You win." or "You lose." over "Continue"; otherwise "Sacrifice N and win" (the primary) and "Sacrifice nothing", at one width, each shown, when the force holds more than one group, once its own picks are complete ("To win, sacrifice N:", "If you lose, N die:"); "Can't win: <reason>" shows only when a complete win is refused. The spoils list "<site> · N warbands" (the real 0), the relics and banners by name and "<chip>'s pawn and favor"; "Tap to put a relic on the bottom of the relic deck."; "Place warbands: N of M."; "Banish <chip> to:" with "nowhere" first; "Take spoils" (the primary) and "Take and burn N [favor]" (rose, the token at the text's height) at one width, or the engine's reason in their place. No panel restates a rule: the pools, the dice and the History say the rest. Every label stays on one line inside its button; on a phone each confirm is 44 px tall, and a pair that does not fit side by side goes one per line, still at one width.
- **A power's question (R-X.1):** the question put to a player is the card's name as its heading, the card it is about small beside one short line (the relic at stake, the Vision, the Conspiracy, else the heading's card; a tap enlarges it), in its own shape: an upright card 40 px wide, a wide one (a Vision's face) as wide as that card is tall, whole, and short answers; who used which power on whom is the History's, not the panel's. A player is their chip. The answers are as wide as the widest of them, never stretched across the panel, at least 44 px tall on a phone and one per line there when two do not fit; equal choices (Brass Horse's sites, False Prophet's and Inquisitor's plays) take the choice look at one width. An answer that needs picks (a count, a card, a player and terms, a take) shows once they are made, and the engine's reason shows in red only when a complete answer is refused, in place of that answer. Inquisitor's plays take their picks after the play, as a Search does: "Play it" opens "The Conspiracy: take a relic or banner?" when a player here holds a relic or banner it may take, and "Adviser, facedown" opens "Discard 1 adviser." (the pick ringed red) at the adviser limit; each step's "Play" shows once its picks are made, Undo steps back to the plays, and a play with nothing to pick is sent at once. A power's cost paid by the yes (Jinx, Relic Thief) is on it in gold ("Reroll · 1 [secret]").
- **Stack the Pilgrimage draw (Law Glossary "Discard"):** the asked player's question shows the drawn denizens as cards; each tap puts the next card down, labelled with its place in the stack, and the last untapped card goes on top. The line reads "Tap to discard; the last goes on top."; "Discard" shows once the order is fixed and sends the answer; Undo untaps the last card, one per press, and never sends anything. Other seats see only that the player is answering, never the cards.
- **History lines:** each row says what its action did from the record alone. A power row names the card, the cost printed on it ("placing a secret on it"), then what it did, every seat by name: "you" for the viewer, "their own" for the actor's own (Wolves on the user's own board) when no other seat is in the row, else the actor's name. An answer to a question names its card, then what the answer did, its seats and banks named the same way; an answer recorded with "you" in it keeps that word. A consent answer names the seat that asked. A Rest power row reads "rested with" the card, its bank by the suit's printed name. The Wake's row reads "began the turn", then each People's Favor step ("placed 1 favor on the People's Favor", "returned 1 favor to the Order bank") and the site's take ("took 1 secret from their site"), from the action's own record; a Wake that won the game took nothing from the site. A Search that stopped on a Vision says so to every seat; a Search recorded before that flag existed says so to its drawer alone. The spoils name the relics taken and the banners seized. The Oathkeeper title moving and the end die are rows of their own.
- **Major events in the History:** a Vision drawn (a world-deck Search that stopped on one, or Oracle's), a Vision revealed, the Oathkeeper title moving, the title flipping to Usurper, Citizenship taken or lost, a Campaign won or lost, and the end die are framed rows: a small uppercase heading naming the event (in the heading token, rose for the end die and a lost Campaign) with an aside such as the Vision's place on the track or the round, the row's sentence, the public picture (the drawn Vision's face to its drawer and its back to everyone else, a revealed Vision, the title on the side it shows, the relics and banners a Campaign took, the end die's face) and one muted line saying what follows (the Search cost stepping, with the Darkest Secret holder's 2 beside it, a Vision's goal, the Oath, who now is what). A finished game's top row names its winner and its ending with the title or the Vision that won it: the one whose goal the winner met, their own before one a warband of theirs shares (False Prophet), read from the final state. Each framed row is read from its action's record alone, the same for every viewer but for the drawn Vision; ordinary rows are unchanged, and no row moves.
- **A Vision was seen (R-5.1.2, R-9.4):** when a world-deck Search stops on a Vision, or Oracle draws one, every seat, the drawer included, is told over the board: a card in the Vision back's colours with the Vision back, "<seat> has seen a Vision" ("You have seen a Vision" for the drawer), several draws listed oldest first, and one button, the Oracular Pig with "The pig foresaw this", which clears it for that seat (as Escape does). Nothing else is named. It covers the board only, under a backdrop of its own, so the turn bar and the action panel stay readable and usable; full screen holds it. It shows live and, on opening the game, for every draw the seat has not cleared, remembered per device, so an undo never brings back a cleared one; not to spectators and not in History View. It appears and leaves with no motion.
- **What only you saw:** after an action that showed its actor a card nobody else saw (a peek, or the relic they sent to the bottom of the relic deck), a notice above the action panel pictures those cards, captioned with the card that showed them, until the next Action by anyone. The actor's own discards and kept card are never in the notice; the history row pictures those to the actor. Other seats see neither.
- **Undo:** the turn banner offers one button labelled Undo, drawn as the primary (a gold outline) so it is seen, and it is the only control that steps back: no panel shows a Back or a step-cancel (`docs/user-interactions.md`). It shows only while a press would do something and has no tooltip. With a Manual Draft Entry in progress (the staged action, any panel's picks, or the seat card's let-peek picker) a press takes back one pick, and the press that takes the last one closes the menu; otherwise it reverses the last Processed Action (the history names it), and it stops at the first one that revealed information (R-X.3).
- **The turn banner:** it names whose turn it is, not the seat the engine waits on: "Your turn" for that seat and "<seat>'s turn" for every other, then the turn's phase ("Wake Phase", "Act Phase", "Rest Phase"). A Search, a Campaign, a question, a request and the Oathkeeper title's choice keep the phase they interrupted; the action panel names the step and who must act. During Setup no turn has begun, so it reads "Setup" alone; between rounds it reads the Chancellor's roll (see The end die).
- **Browse history:** the history controls above the side tabs on a wide screen, and the phone controls on a narrow one, move History View backward and forward. The table shows the displayed state; the action panel gives way to that state's turn banner, and nothing on the board is offered.
- **Fit the panel:** the panel above the board is scaled down, never scrolled and never clipped, to a fraction of the column so the map keeps its size; it refits when its content grows or shrinks (a step's cards arriving) or its room changes, and a fit that ran short overlaps the board until the next read rather than hiding cards. Its natural height is read at full width without undoing the fit, so a change of room (a phone's address bar sliding) only rescales it, and the panel is fitted on its first frame and on a new step's first frame. Images in the panel have their size before they load, so one arriving changes nothing. A picture sized to the panel's width takes that width from the panel's unshrunk box, so the layout measured and the layout drawn are the same. Only a change of layout alone (the panel's width, a font arriving) is read a frame late: for that frame the panel keeps its previous fit, so a panel that fitted unscaled can be drawn for one frame past its box. Otherwise the visible panel is never drawn unscaled while it needs scaling.
- **Waiting on a send:** while an Action is being sent or the next state is being shown, every panel control is disabled and every draft reads as empty, so each panel shows its first step and the board offers nothing. The send ends every draft whether or not it is accepted, and a newly shown state does too; a state update that shows no new state gives the picks back.
- **The Wake (R-4.1):** a Wake with something to decide asks one choice at a time, in the question form: the People's Favor's steps, then the site's take. Its heading is "People's Favor" ("People's Favor (1 of 2)", then "(2 of 2)" in its place on the Mob side) or the site's name, the banner or the site card small beside it (a tap enlarges it), and its answers short buttons of one width, side by side while they fit, 44 tall or more: "Place [favor]" and "Return [favor]"; the banks tied for least as suit symbols after Return; "Take [favor]" or "Take [secret]" for what the site holds and prints, and "Take nothing". Nothing is picked before a tap. Every tap acts: it moves on to the next choice, and the tap that answers the last one sends the Wake; there is no confirm button. Each step already answered is a small muted line above ("Placed 1 [favor].", "Returned 1 [favor] to [suit].", the bank's name ("the Order bank") the symbol's alternative text); a step with one legal answer is taken without a tap and shown as its line. "End Wake Phase", the primary sized to its label, shows alone only when nothing is left to choose. Before the send, Undo takes back the last pick; after it, Undo takes back the sent Wake like any action. A red line shows only if the engine refuses the answered Wake.
- **The end of a turn (R-4.3, R-4.3-H1):** End Act Phase is a turn's last click: the Rest resolves by itself and the next seat's Wake begins, unless the seat holds a Rest power it can use. Then the Rest panel has no heading or sentence (the turn header names the phase): one row per usable power, the card with its corner magnifier, its name, then its buttons directly after, the names in one column, and one primary button sized to its label, "End Rest Phase". A bank power has a button for every bank it can name, in the board's order, each reading its suit and the favor it takes; an empty bank shows its 0 and can be tapped (R-9.3): the power is used and takes nothing. One tap uses the power with that bank; there is no separate Use. A power used this Rest is not listed (its History row says what it did), and using the last one ends the turn by itself. The Rest panel's choice buttons share one width, the widest button's content, are 48 tall and wrap, on a phone under the card's name. A game created before the turn-flow revision keeps the Rest panel's bank picker: no heading, sentence or end-die warning; no bank is picked until a tap (a power offering one bank has it picked); Use, in the control grey, shows once a bank is picked; and "End Rest Phase", or "Roll the end die" on the round's last turn from round five.
- **The end die (R-3.3, R-3.3-H1):** at the end of rounds five to seven with the Chancellor or a Citizen holding the title, the game stops between rounds on the Chancellor's roll. The turn banner reads "Your roll" or "<Chancellor>'s roll" with "End of round N". The Chancellor's panel, headed "End of round N of 8", says the Empire holds the title so they roll, then "A <roll> ends the game: you win as the Chancellor" (or "<Citizen> wins as your Successor"), the roll in rose; one primary button, "Roll the end die", and "The roll can't be undone." Every other seat and a spectator read "Waiting on <Chancellor>." and the same line. No die faces show before the roll. The last seat's Undo stays in its turn banner until the roll, and nothing before the roll can be undone after it. The result is the History's end-die row and the rail's End die box, which shows the face rolled ("last N") from the roll on; a roll that ends the game shows the game-end panel with the ending it recorded. A game created before the turn-flow revision rolls inside its last Rest, as it was recorded.
- **What the panels do not offer:** in a game created before the turn-flow revision, a Rest power whose choice is not a favor bank is shown refused with that reason; from the revision the Rest panel lists only the Rest cards it describes, each a bank power or a power with no choice. A machine state with no panel says so in the panel's place.

## Coexistence and precedence

One semantic derivation in the session decides what each site means (start here, travel, target, move onto your site, or nothing) and whether the rest of the map dims; the sites layer draws that result and nothing else.

- Setup's site pick exists only in the Setup machine state on the seat's own turn, and takes precedence over every other board offer while it exists.
- Only one Act Phase action is staged at a time, so the ring means one thing per screen: the staged action decides which layer offers targets and the others offer nothing.
- There is one Let another peek picker. In this seat's own Act Phase it is the staged action: the seat card's button stages it exactly as the grid's chip does, replacing any staged action and its picks as choosing another tile would, and the picker shows in the action panel, not on the seat card; the button keeps its words, and a second press on it, or Undo, leaves it. Choosing another tile closes it. Outside this seat's Act Phase the seat card's button opens the picker on the seat card and stages nothing, and a staged action cannot exist there; a second press on the button, or Undo, closes it.
- The Campaign panel follows the valid actions, not the phase: while Campaign is chosen and the engine offers it, the panel takes the place of every machine-state panel, the question included, until it is sent or Undo's last press leaves it (in a Sneak Attack, for the question).
- Map dimming belongs to choosing among sites: Setup, Travel and Campaign dim the rest of the map; Muster, Trade, Recover, Peek and Move warbands never do.
- The Campaign target outline coexists with the ring and sits outside it.
- Undo unwinds Manual Draft Entries only, one per press; an Auto Draft Entry (the Setup action, the defender a Sneak Attack names, a one-die attack pool) is never something it returns to. The only legal start site and the only legal adviser are derived rather than stored, so there is nothing to return to either. The seat card's let-peek picker closes first, and a panel's picks unwind before the staged action under them. With no manual entry left, Undo reverses the last Processed Action.

### Look on the table, choose in the menus

A press on the table means one of four things. **Choose** stages or sends a choice, and is the only one that changes the game. **Enlarge** shows a card at a readable size. **Focus** zooms and pans the board to a region, the banks or one site. **Open** puts a seat or the goals over the table. Each element has one meaning for a press, whatever the game is offering:

| Element | Click or tap | Hover |
|---|---|---|
| A card or site on the board, the rail, a seat card, the open seat or the open goals | enlarge | nothing |
| A lit Travel destination on a phone | choose: travel, or open its ways to pay | nothing |
| A row or button in the action panel and its menus | choose | lights the card or site it names on the board |
| The magnifier in the corner of a card in the action panel, or of a lit Travel destination on a phone | enlarge; a second press closes it | nothing |
| A seat's name pill, the goals, Let another peek, the board's toolbar and full-screen control, the history controls | open, focus or browse, as each says | nothing |
| The bare board | nothing | nothing |

The wheel, a pinch and a drag pan and zoom the board, as the shared wrapper does, and the platform's `f` shortcut turns full screen on and off. A mouse and a finger do the same things; there is no hover preview and no hold. The keyboard reaches the action panel's rows, buttons and magnifiers, the name pills, the goals, the toolbar and the history controls, where Enter or Space does what a click does; the cards on the table are not in the tab order, since every choice is in the panel (on a phone, Travel's lit sites are tapped on the map).

1. **The table is for looking.** No press on the board, the rail or a seat card stages or sends anything, whatever the game is offering, except Travel on a phone: there a tap on a lit destination travels, or opens its ways to pay (see Choose a Travel destination).
2. **The action panel is for choosing.** Every choice the engine offers is a row or button in the action panel's menus: the start site, a Travel destination (on a phone, the lit site on the map and its ways in the panel), a Campaign target, a card to Muster or Trade with, a relic or banner to Recover, a relic to Peek at, a Search source, a facedown adviser to play, a warband move. A row names what it acts on and gives its cost and gain; a choice the engine refuses is not listed, and the table does not ring it.
3. **The table echoes the menu.** The cards and sites the open menu offers wear the ring, with their chips (a Travel cost, "target"), and Setup, Travel and Campaign dim the rest of the map as before. Hovering or focusing a row lights the card or site it names; the ring itself never takes a choice, except a Travel destination's on a phone (rule 1).
4. **A press enlarges.** A click or tap on any faceup card or site on the table, or on the magnifier beside a card in the panel, shows it enlarged in the centre over a backdrop, at the size the card's shape allows, until the next click or tap anywhere, Escape, or its close button. The press that closes it does nothing else, and nothing under the backdrop reacts. A facedown card enlarges as its back. A card offered in the panel carries a magnifier in its corner: a press on the magnifier enlarges the card and a second press returns, and a press anywhere else on the card chooses it. On a phone during Travel a lit site is chosen the same way: its magnifier sits at its top-left corner.
5. **Focus is chosen, never a side effect.** The board's toolbar names the focus views, Full, Cradle, Provinces, Hinterland and Banks, and shows the current one; choosing one fills the board's view with it, Full fits the whole board, and a pan or zoom by hand clears the choice. An enlarged site offers "Zoom the board here", which focuses the board on that site's card, denizens, relics and warbands. Focus changes no offer, draft or choice, and a choice never changes the focus, except that Travel on a phone frames the map as it opens and gives the view back as it closes (see Choose a Travel destination).
6. **Focus, enlarge and full screen coexist.** A focused board's cards enlarge and its rings show exactly as at any other zoom. Full screen changes none of these rules: the action panel, the enlarged card and the focus views work inside the expanded board.
7. **What is open wins over what is under it.** The enlarged card sits above the open seat and the open goals, which sit above the table, so a card inside the open seat still enlarges. A press outside an open seat or the open goals closes it and does nothing else. Escape closes one layer at a time, the topmost first: the enlarged card, then the open seat or goals, then a Vision seen, then full screen. An Escape typed in a text field, such as the chat's, never clears a Vision seen.
8. **History View offers no choices.** No action panel and no send exists while it is shown. Enlarge, the open seat, the open goals and focus keep working on the displayed state.

## Shared visual state

The session owns the drafts and the open seat below; the card preview is a model-level value of its own. Components read them and call their methods. Every draft is held as the player's last pick and read back only while it still applies, so a stale value offers nothing rather than something illegal. The session clears every draft in `beforeNewState()`, before a new state is published, and on every send. While an Action is being sent (`processingActions`) or the next state is being shown (`updatingVisibleState`), the session's live seat is empty, so every draft, the staged action included, reads as empty and no board layer offers a pick; the shared `busy` disables every panel control, and Undo does nothing.

- **Staged action and its stages (action, the Chancellor's split of a short setup bank, modifiers, warband move, site (a Travel's destination, set and sent with its way to pay by one button, or on a phone set by a tap on a site with more than one way to pay, which opens its ways), card, discard order, relic slot, banner, option, When Played picks for an R-6.1 play, a banner's bid, the People's Favor's start bank).** Producer: the action grid and the panels. Consumers: every panel and board layer that offers a pick. Effects: which layer lights and what the panel asks. Lifetime: Undo pops the highest manual stage; a send or any newly shown state clears them. Validity: each consumer asks the engine again on the displayed state. History View: nothing is offered, and the drafts are cleared when a state is shown.
- **Setup site, adviser and discard order.** Producer: Setup's taps. Consumers: the map and the Setup panel. Effects: the start ring, the hand shown, the discard taps. Lifetime and validity as the staged selection; the site must still be legal and the card still in hand.
- **Declared modifiers and their picks (R-7.4).** A stage of the staged action, after the action and before what it acts on. Producer: the modifier picker, and the powers list when it opens an action with a card in use. Consumer: the send of Travel, Muster, Trade, Search and Recover. Effects: the ticked cards and their pickers. Lifetime: declaring or dropping a modifier clears the picks made under it (site, card and the rest); Undo takes the last modifier declared, one at a time, after any pick made under it, and from an action the powers list opened, the last card in use goes back to that list; a tap does not drop the last card in use of those that make the action possible; leaving the action (Undo past it, a send) or a newly shown state clears them. Validity: only modifiers the engine lists as usable for the staged action are sent.
- **Campaign defender, battle plans, targets and attack dice.** Producer: the Campaign panel. Consumers: the panel, the sites layer and the dice row. Effects: the target ring and outline, the panel's lists, the dice lit. A staged flow in that order. Lifetime: Undo takes the last target, then the last plan, one at a time, then the defender; choosing another defender clears the plans, targets and dice; a plan dropped drops the targets it alone allowed and keeps the dice; the defender a Sneak Attack names, a lone legal defender and a one-die pool are Auto Draft Entries, so in a Sneak Attack Undo with no pick left leaves for the question, and with a lone defender it leaves the Campaign; Undo past the Campaign, a send or a newly shown state clear all four. Validity: the defender must still be one the engine accepts, or the one a Sneak Attack names; a plan must still be usable; a target must still be offered for that defender and those plans; the dice are held between none and the warbands in the force. Once the declaration is complete (a target, the dice, and every declared plan's picks), the panel asks the engine about the declaration it would send, so plans that cannot all be paid from the one holding (from revision 3) show the engine's reason, such as "your battle plans cost 2 favor in all, you hold 1", in Declare's place.
- **Warband move.** A stage of the staged action. Producer: a number button in a Move-warbands row, which names the direction and the count at once. Consumer: the send, in the same press. Lifetime: it is set and sent together, so there is nothing for Undo to return to; a newly shown state clears it. Validity: the row and the count must be ones the engine offers, or the send is refused as a defect.
- **Let another peek picker.** Producer: the seat card's button and the grid's chip. Consumers: the seat card and the action panel. Effects: the picker's list and the button's `aria-expanded`. In this seat's Act Phase it is the staged action and lives and dies with it; elsewhere it is the seat card's own one-step draft, a Manual Draft Entry. Lifetime: a send, a second press on the button, Undo, another tile, or a newly shown state closes it. Validity: it shows only while the engine offers Let another peek to this seat.
- **Adviser being played.** Producer: a tap on a facedown adviser, or the only legal one. Consumers: the seat card and the placements. Lifetime and validity as the staged selection.
- **Panel drafts** (Search's kept card, the choices beside its placement, the placement, the advisers discarded to meet the limit, the When Played picks and the discard order; a question's answer in progress and the Pilgrimage stack; the Citizenship offer's Exile, relic space and terms; the spoils; the attacker's plans when Citizens were asked to join first, the defending side's plans, and each declared plan's picks; the attacker's sacrificed or defeated warbands; the Campaign's skull-loss order; the Wake's People's Favor steps and site take; the Rest powers' banks; the Action powers' choices; the Citizenship recolor; the defending side's losses). Producer: the panel's controls. Consumer: that panel and its send. Each is a staged flow over the shared helpers, in its own stage order. Lifetime: Undo pops the last pick (one discard or one stacked card at a time). The Wake holds each tap as its own entry, so Undo takes back its last pick, then the one before; the spoils, the defending side's plans and losses, the Rest powers, the Action powers and the Citizenship recolor each hold every pick as one entry, so one Undo clears that panel's picks together and the panel is as it opened; a send or a newly shown state clears them, so no pick survives into the next question or to the next seat in a hotseat game. Validity: a pick is read back only while the displayed state still offers it, and one that depends on another (a prize on its player, terms on their counterparty, a second play on the kept card, the relic space on the chosen Exile) applies only while that other pick is unchanged.
- **Card preview.** Producer: a click or tap on a card or site on the table, or on a panel card's magnifier. Consumer: the preview layer. Validity: the preview reads its owner's current props, so it pictures what the owner now shows. Lifetime: the next press anywhere (taken by the backdrop, so it does nothing else), Escape, a second press on the same magnifier, or the owner unmounting. The board's card lists are keyed by card (a denizen by its id, a relic by its slot, a site by slot and card), so a card that leaves its space unmounts its owner and the preview closes. It is one value per tab; it never enters game state and follows the displayed state in History View.
- **Menu pointer.** Producer: a menu row's hover or keyboard focus. Consumers: the sites layer, the strip layer, the tracks layer and the rail's banners. Effects: the site, card, relic, pile or banner the row names draws the bright ring a hover gives it; only a thing the table already rings lights. Lifetime: the row's leave, blur or unmount, so a new state, a send or Undo that removes the row lets go. One value per tab, since there is one pointer; it never enters game state, and History View shows no menu, so nothing is pointed there.
- **Board focus.** Producer: a focus view in the board's toolbar, "Zoom the board here" on an enlarged site, or Travel on a phone, which frames the lit sites and its region chips. Consumer: the board's scaling wrapper, and the toolbar's ring on the chosen view. Effects: the board's view zooms (up to 2x) and pans to the region, the banks or the site's row, fitted between the focus views along the top edge and the zoom buttons at the bottom, so nothing is under either. Lifetime: choosing the focused one again restores the view before it; a pan or zoom by hand clears it. Validity: the rectangles are the board's own geometry (`boardFocusAreas`), so they hold whatever the display holds. It never enters game state and works the same in History View.
- **Vision seen.** Producer: a public Vision draw in the actions. Consumer: the board's overlay. Validity: the draws after the action the seat last cleared, read from the actions. Lifetime: the seat's pig button or an Escape with nothing open over it (rule 7), which records the newest draw as cleared in the session's state and in the browser's storage for that game and seat, read once when the session starts.
- **Open goals.** Producer: a tap on the rail's goals. Consumer: the goals overlay. Validity: it shows the displayed state. Lifetime: the backdrop, Escape, the close button or another tap on the rail, and the session. It shows the displayed state in History View.
- **Open seat.** Producer: a seat's name pill. Consumer: the seat overlay. Validity: it opens only a seat of the game on screen. Lifetime: the backdrop, Escape or the pill, and the session, so another table opens with no seat open. It shows the displayed state in History View.

Silent restoration replaces the displayed state and every derived offer follows it without motion.

## Render ownership

- The board is one fixed surface scaled by the shared scaling wrapper; each layer owns the pick chrome of the thing it draws. The sites layer owns site cards, their tokens, the dimming of the map, site chips and, on a phone during Travel, each lit site's magnifier; the strip layer owns denizens, relic slots, their empty outlines and their rings; the pieces layer owns warbands and pawns and is never a hit target; the tracks layer owns the printed spaces, the discard counts and the deck spaces.
- The enlarged card and the open seat are mounted after the table layout, outside its scroll columns, the board's wrapper and the fitted panel, because a transform is the containing block for fixed layers. They cover the whole viewport rather than the table, so each pads itself by the device's safe-area insets: the open seat is capped at the dynamic viewport height less those insets and scrolls inside itself, and the enlarged card is sized from its layer's measured safe area: a wide card (a banner, a Vision's face, a site) up to 0.8 of that width and never past its source art (920, 1016 and 770 px), an upright or square card up to 460 px, both inside 0.82 of the height, with the card and the boxes under it scaled together when they would not fit.
- One frame per panel: the action panel's container draws the only frame around a step, and the panel inside it draws none. Groups within a panel are set apart by spacing, a heading or a faint top divider, and a note by a tint; a border marks only a control, and the amber border its picked state, except that a Travel destination with more than one way to pay keeps the same tile border as the others, and its buttons are the controls.
- The side column has no background of its own: the history controls, the side tabs and the chat sit on the site's dark page and are drawn in Oath's palette through the shared components' own colour props; Oath restyles no shared component from outside. The board's zoom controls keep the platform's colours, since the shared wrapper takes none. Oath's own panels in the tabs paint their own backgrounds.
- Full screen is the scaling wrapper's modal dialog, so anything drawn outside it is behind it: while the board is expanded (the dialog's role says so, read without an effect), the action panel, the goals overlay and the enlarged card are drawn inside the dialog, the panel docked above the board in the wrapper's toolbar and sized by the same fraction of the window's height; otherwise they are drawn in the page. Each is drawn in one place at a time. The side column and the open seat stay outside. The Vision seen notice is drawn in the wrapper's overlay beside the focus chooser, so it covers the board only and full screen holds it. One Escape handler closes the topmost layer: the enlarged card, then the open goals or seat, then a Vision seen; with none open, Escape is the wrapper's, which leaves full screen.
- The focus views' chooser is drawn in the scaling wrapper's overlay as one slim line centred along the map's top edge, unscaled, on a desktop and in full screen, so it never covers the rail or the banks at the bottom. A phone (under 640 px wide) has no chooser; Travel's region chips are drawn in the same overlay and place on a phone held upright, while the step is open; an enlarged site's "Zoom the board here" still focuses its row there; the zoom buttons stay where the wrapper puts them, in its own colours. Each view's rectangle is derived once from the board's geometry: a region its name, discard pile, sites and every strip's full width; the banks the seven discs; a site's row its card and its strip.
- The Campaign's dice are drawn by one row at the top of the action panel, read from the Campaign in game state; the rail draws the goals, the banners and the end die. The goals on the rail and in their overlay are drawn by one component from one pure reading of the state (`goalBoard`), so the two never differ.
- Warband counts on a card are drawn by one component from game state, wherever the card is drawn. A seat's warband counts come from one reading shared by the seat card and the open seat: Imperial seats count purple from the one Imperial bank, an Exile their own colour.
- Every card-valued choice is drawn by one row component from the options its panel's draft computes; the panel owns what a pick does, and the row owns only the ring, the untap and the enlarge.
- A menu's row reads left to right: the suit or the card's picture, the name at a fixed width, then its buttons or its tag directly after the name, never justified to the panel's far edge; on a phone the buttons take their own line under the name. Travel's destinations are tiles, not rows (see "Choose a Travel destination").
- The Reliquary is drawn by space, each space by its own slot, on the seat card and in the open seat alike.
- Actor-only facts (what a peek showed, a relic sent to the bottom of the relic deck, a pile deposit's cards, a Search's discards) are shown only when the action's player is this player, never because the client holds the data: a Local game or Host View holds every field. The notice, the history row and the questions that show a relic picture each card, enlarged on a press.
- Hidden information is rendered by rule, not by data availability. This player's hand is drawn as cards where a panel asks for it; on the seat card and in the open seat every hand is a row of backs, its Visions with the Vision back by the public `handVisions` count. A relic is drawn as a back unless this player's own peeks name it. A facedown site shows a back and no denizen or relic spaces. A discard pile shows its count and the back of its top card; the world deck shows the back of its top card and no count; the relic deck shows a back and no count. The history names a kept or played card only when the engine recorded that the play showed it. A card is drawn facedown only by naming the back it shows, so no drawing site has a default back; before the opening deal the world deck has no top card and shows none.
- Motion: none is state-derived. History rows appear and leave without animation, keyed by action, in engine order. The only motion is hover feedback and the turn pulse on the active seat's border. The Supply marker snaps to its circle.

## Palette

Every colour an Oath component names is one of these tokens, declared once in `src/app.css` under the title's scope and used as utility classes (`bg-oath-surface`, `text-oath-text-muted`) or as variables in a component's style block. No component names a raw colour class; `palette.spec.ts` fails on one, and on a token class or variable that is not declared. The seat colours are the exception: a seat, its pieces, its name chip and its discs wear the player's colour, which is a game fact, from the colourizer. The printed board, cards and dice are art and keep their own colours.

## Viewport

The platform's table layout (`DefaultTableLayout`) owns the bottom safe-area inset, as the shell above it owns the top one; Oath adds no inset and no viewport height of its own inside the table. Oath relies on that layout for the solved column height the fitted panel and the board divide between them, and for the bottom (and side) padding that keeps the board, the side tabs and the phone history controls clear of the home indicator.
