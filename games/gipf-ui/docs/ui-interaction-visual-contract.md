# UI Interaction Visual Contract

## 1. Visual Intents

| Intent      | Trigger                                                                                   | What should be emphasized                                                                                       | What should be dimmed   | What must remain unaffected       |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| choose_dot  | The viewer may push                                                                       | Every dot with a legal push (cyan)                                                                              | Nothing                 | Pieces                            |
| choose_type | Tournament, a dot is chosen, and both a single and a GIPF-piece may be brought in         | The staged piece on its dot: a GIPF-piece; tapping it or the dot again lifts its top disc off, or drops it back | Nothing                 | Targets                           |
| choose_line | A dot is chosen                                                                           | The chosen dot (ringed); an arrow and dashed target ring on each first spot it can push into                    | Other dots' rings (40%) | Pieces                            |
| choose_row  | The viewer has rows to take (all while resolving; only rows of GIPF-pieces before a move) | Every piece in a row's reach (dashed red ring)                                                                  | Nothing                 | Dots, targets                     |
| shape_row   | A row is chosen                                                                           | Pieces to be taken (solid red ring), GIPF-pieces kept (green ring)                                              | Nothing                 | Other rows keep their dashed ring |

## 2. Visual Primitives

- **Choice marks** (`Board.svelte`, `.under` and `.over`): the dot rings, target rings and arrows are two-tone as in MATRX GIPF, DVONN and LYNGK, a dark band (`.under`) under a cyan core (`.over`, `--gipf-mark`), so they read on the board and on light and dark pieces alike; the dashed target rings have each dash outlined on its own (shorter square-capped dark dashes), so what is under shows between dashes. Row marks stay red and green. The active player's tray keeps orange (`--gipf-target`).
- **Entry dot** (`Board.svelte`, `.dot.entry`, `.dot.chosen`): once a dot is chosen, the other dots' rings fade (`.dot-ring.faded`, 40%). Inputs `session.entryDots`, `session.chosenDot`.
- **Push target** (`Board.svelte`, `.target`; path from `board/marks.ts` `pushArrow`, click area `stackOutline`): an arrow lying on the board from the staged piece's top to near the first spot's centre (`ARROW_START`, `ARROW_REACH`, as in MATRX GIPF), so it marks where the push goes, and a dashed ring on the first spot. Every arrow's tail is as far from its dot on screen as an upright one's, so slanted ones, which the tilt shortens less, reach as far back over the staged piece. Inputs `session.targets`.
- **Row mark** (`Board.svelte`, on a layer above the pieces, drawn on each piece's top face so no mark reaches a neighbour): a dashed red ring just inside the face for a row you can choose (`.row-mark.role-row`); once a row is chosen, a spot on each piece (`.mark-spot`, red going, green kept), and on each GIPF-piece in the row the crown ring recoloured and thickened (`.crown-mark`) for keep or take. Your own GIPF-pieces start kept and the opponent's start taken; tapping one toggles it. Positions come from `BoardView.topFace` in `board/geometry.ts`. Inputs `session.rows`, `session.removing`, `session.keep`, `session.rowGipfPieces`.
- **Piece** (`PieceShape.svelte`, shape in `board/pieceShape.ts`): MATRX GIPF's disc seen at the board's tilt, rounded edges and a flat top with a groove whose lips and floor are rounded; each tone's disc is a pre-rendered WebP (`scripts/render-looks.mjs` from the `/looks` route, `board/lookUrls.ts`; rerun after changing the shape). A GIPF-piece is two discs, the top one resting 0.24 of a thickness above the lower, a dark crease between them; each disc is its own node so it can drop on its own. Light for White, dark for Black. Never carries interaction state.
- **Piece shadow** (`StandingPiece.svelte`, `StagedPiece.svelte`; stops from `board/softShadow.ts`): a radial-gradient ellipse under each piece whose stops follow the edge a 2.5-unit Gaussian blur gives its footprint, with a darker contact ellipse beneath. No filter, so phones paint it cheaply (`docs/phone-rendering.md`).
- **Board theme** (`app.css`; grid lines from `board/boardLines.ts`): one light board: pale blue play area bounded by the outermost grid lines, muted blue table, black lines and dots. No intersection marks.
- **Piece badges** (`KindBadge.svelte`, placed by `board/badge.ts`; off by default, see Board toggles): each GIPF-piece carries a G on a white badge with a black letter, 9px across on a 390px-wide phone, as in DVONN, at the far right of its top face, as DVONN's height badge does; single pieces have none. On the board it is drawn over the piece's marks, in two nodes beside the piece registered with the animator (`badge` slides with the piece, `badgeBody` rises and falls with its body); on the incoming and staged piece it is drawn on the top disc (`PieceShape`'s `onTop`), so it drops and lifts with that disc.
- **Coordinate labels** (`Board.svelte`, `.coordinate-label`, from `board/coordinates.ts`): each column's bottom and top point (A1…A5 to I1…I5), beyond the column's ends. They can be hidden (see Board toggles).
- **Board toggles** (`OverlayToggle.svelte`, in `GameTable.svelte`'s overlay): a row of round chips at the board's bottom right, as in GIPF with Potentials: A1 for the coordinate labels, then a G in a ring for the piece badges, then two turning arrows for the far side, the board seen half a turn round (`viewOf(true)`), so pieces hidden behind others come in front; labels stay beyond their columns' ends. Each is the player's own title preference (`showCoordinates`, `pieceBadges`, `turned`); coordinates on by default, the piece badges and far side off. A chip that is on shows its glyph in full ink with a thin cyan ring (`--gipf-toggle-on`) round it, as in LYNGK.
- **Action panel height** (`ActionPanel.svelte`, `components/holdTallest.ts`): the panel keeps the tallest height it has reached at its current width, so a line that wraps for a long name does not move the board when it shrinks back. A new width starts over; a remount (the end panel, a new game) starts over too.

## 4. Shared State Contracts

- **Animation scope**: the board animates only `own-action`, `other-action`, `undo`, `history` and `exploration` transitions; page loads, server syncs and view switches show the board at rest. Without an action (undo, state-only history) the animator reads a push from the two boards (`board/inferPush.ts`) and slides the line forward or back, the pushed piece returning to its dot on the way back; otherwise pieces whose spot is left empty fade, and pieces put back, such as a taken row an Undo or a step back returns, drop back in where they stood, their lift-off played backward (0.18s). Back on its dot, a piece your own Undo took back stays there staged (`returned`, then the restaged selection), unless the view passes to the other player as the Undo lands (the harness's Non-active view); any other push taken back, another player's Undo or a history or exploration step, lifts off from its dot as it arrives (slide 0.14s, lift from 0.06s for 0.12s), so the whole change plays in one timeline. All within 0.2s. The drop-in of the piece you are about to bring in, onto the chosen dot, is a local GSAP timeline (`StagedDrop`): it explains a staged choice, not a committed move. A switch of view while a move plays stops it (`abandon`): the move is played to its end at once, the switched state shows unmoved, and the rest of that presentation's replay plays nothing. Turning to the far side mid-move lands the move and its state at once (`interrupt`), since its motion was laid out in the old view.
- **Moving SVG** (`animators/svgPose.ts`): every moving node tweens a plain pose (`x`, `y`, `scale`) that writes `translate()` and `scale()` as it is set; opacity stays on the node. GSAP writes SVG transforms as `matrix()`, whose offset Firefox 140 leaves out of the board's view-scale zoom. `tests/motion.spec.ts` checks no board transform is a `matrix()` through a drop, a push, its Undo and a history step each way.

- **Staged selection** (`selection`: `dot`, `gipf`, `row`, `keep`): manual choices only; single options (one piece type, one row that must be taken) are derived, never stored, so Undo skips them. `keep` is a stacked stage, so each tap on a GIPF-piece in the chosen row is its own Undo step. The selection belongs to the player who made it (a restaged push to its pusher): anyone else viewing, as the harness's Non-active view is after an Undo, sees none of it and Undo passes it by. Registered with the session's `localSelections`, which pops it on Undo and clears it whenever a new state is published; on an Undo of your push, `beforeNewState` then restages the push's dot, and its GIPF choice if there was one to make. A tap on the board that misses every enabled `role="button"` lets go of a chosen dot (`letGo`, on a `role="presentation"` wrapper round the board; Undo is the keyboard's way); rows and keep toggles are left alone. A player's tray counts their reserve less the piece they have staged (`reserveLeft`); a step that brings a piece in sets the reserves it leads to as it starts (`landingReserves`, until the state lands, carried through a replay), so a piece entering the board is never counted in its reserve too. The staged piece lifts off only when the player's own choice or Undo removed it (`releasedByPlayer`, reset by `beforeNewState`, false in History View and for anyone but the selection's owner); when a published state or History clears the selection, it goes without motion.
- **Pushes known to the session** (`GipfGameSession`): a step back takes back `undonePush`, the push on screen whose index is the new action count; a history step forward knows `historyPush`, set by `onHistoryAction`. The animator uses one to tell which dot a piece came from, only when it turns one board into the other. `undoingPush`, set by `willUndo`, makes an Undo your own only while it is the push undone (`restagedPush`, by index); `beforeNewState` restages that push and the animator leaves it staged.
- **Stopping a move** (`GipfGameSession.moveInterrupter`, set by `BoardAnimator`): `beforeNewState` abandons the move on `view-switch`, and `toggleTurned` and `togglePieceBadges` interrupt it. Either plays the step's shared timeline to its end (`progress(1)` on the context's timeline), so the move lands without waiting out its length. The animator tells an abandoned presentation's later steps by `presentingState`.
- **Board drawn during a move** (`BoardAnimator`, read by `Board.svelte`): `pieces` (the board each replayed action leaves, until the state lands), `entering` (the piece coming in from its dot, cleared after each step), `returned` (the piece an Undo took back onto its dot, until the state lands) and `pushedIn` (your pushed piece stays off its dot until the state lands). Each but `entering` is a writable `$derived` over `session.gameState`, so publishing the new state resets it.
- **Target lists**: derived from the displayed state; empty when the viewer cannot act, including in History View.

## 5. Forbidden Couplings

- Do not derive legality in components; read the session lists.
- Do not store auto-selected piece types or rows in `selection`.

## 6. Interaction Matrix

| Intent A    | Intent B   | Can coexist?                                 | Winner / combination rule                             |
| ----------- | ---------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| choose_dot  | choose_row | yes, before a move with a row of GIPF-pieces | Dots and pieces are different elements                |
| choose_line | shape_row  | yes                                          | Targets are on empty first spots; row marks on pieces |

## 7. Regression Checklist

- Push: dot shows arrows and the other dots fade; tapping an arrow pushes the chain one spot; tapping off the choices on the board lets go of the dot.
- Rows: dashed marks on every row; choosing one turns its marks solid; your GIPF-pieces start kept and the opponent's taken, and tapping one toggles it.
- Tournament type: while a GIPF-piece may be played, the chosen dot drops a GIPF-piece disc by disc; tapping the staged piece lifts its top disc off, tapping again drops it back, even mid-drop, without a snap.
- Crossing rows: tapping the shared piece again switches row.
- History View: no marks or targets.
- Staged piece: letting go or Undo of the dot lifts it off; opening History or a switch of view removes it without motion.
- Undo of a taken row: its pieces drop back in where they stood.

## 8. Change Rules

When touching interactive visuals:

1. identify the intent being changed
2. identify the primitives involved
3. verify whether shared state contracts are being widened
4. update this document if a new interaction or coupling is introduced
