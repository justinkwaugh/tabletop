# Santiago visibility interaction contract

## Visual intents

Private money displays a question mark for other players during play and a numeric balance for the current Player Perspective. Spectators see only question marks. Public Money and EndOfGame show all balances. The tile supply always displays the public remaining count, including when the actual bag is concealed.

Private-money Games disable entry into Exploration in every perspective and phase, including ordinary hotseat, Host View, and legacy Games with the configuration option. Public-money Games can enter projected Exploration and return to their source.

History uses 12-pixel outlined dots centered on the timeline, including action and game boundary entries. Player names designated white render at full brightness in panels, bidding, history, and status text.

## Tile reveal

The tile strip to the board's left is rendered in every phase so the board never shifts sideways; outside a round's bidding and planting it holds only the draw pile, and once the bag is empty a dashed outline keeps the pile's place. Each round opens in TileReveal. The tile strip shows only the draw pile with its public count; once dealt, the round's tiles sit above the pile for the rest of the round. For the round's first bidder in the current Player Perspective, the pile is a button with a hover scale, and the action bar above the board prompts them to click it. Only the table's tile column is animated; hit targets and layer ordering elsewhere are unchanged. Every other perspective, history navigation, and the hotseat non-active view show the same pile as a static count. Clicking sends RevealTiles through the host; the local optimistic attempt is skipped because the draw reads the concealed bag. The reveal is the round's only Undo barrier, so the toolbar's Undo stays available to whoever acted last in the previous round until the pile is clicked.

## Tile deal animation

The Game Session's tile deal animator owns the motion when tiles appear; the table and the action bar both consume it. On a RevealTiles action it renders the `to` state's tiles in their slots as a hidden override, holds the persistent draw pile at the first slot, then on the shared action timeline deposits a tile there and slides the pile down one slot at a time; each tile appears beneath the resting pile and flips from the pile's back design to its face as the pile moves on. The pile finishes at its home below the tiles. It renders above the tiles while it travels. Without an action, as in state-only history navigation, the tiles fade in face-up within the fallback budget and the pile does not move. Tiles disappearing, as when navigating backward past the reveal, unmount reactively with no motion. Both overrides last exactly one action and clear in afterAnimations; the pile's count updates when the reactive state publishes. The action bar's wrapper is always rendered, empty when there is nothing to show, so its height can be measured.

## Canal candidates

Every spot where a canal could go during canal building, and for the player holding a personal canal during extra irrigation, is staked out: a wooden stake set in from each end of the segment, clear of the spring and of canal joints, with cream twine strung between their tops that sags and sways gently, each spot at its own phase. Small pennants hang along the twine, riding its sway and each fluttering at its own phase; on vertical runs they hang to the side. An unbribed spot's pennants are plain cream in slightly varied shades, from the moment spots are first offered; a bribed spot's pennants take its bribers' colors, with the state and without a transition, so committing, undoing, or restoring a bribe never animates them. Each bribe also shows as a pill in its contributor's color, above a horizontal spot's twine, clear of the twine on its stakes, or beside a vertical one on the side away from its pennants; on the board's top edge pills go below the twine and on its right edge to the left, and a horizontal spot's row of pills shifts inward to stay on the board, since there is no room outside the board for them; the overseer deciding can click a spot's pills, or its twine, to accept it, and clicking an unbribed spot rejects every bribe and builds there. An unbribed spot shows its cost to the bank as a grey pill in the same place only while hovered, since the action bar already states it. Hovering a clickable spot lightens its twine. The selected bribe spot's twine tightens gradually, with its pennants, through one transitioning tension that scales the twine's sag, its sway, and every pennant's drop, and turns gold, its pennants take the selecting player's color one after another from the end where water would enter, as local selection feedback outside AnimationContext, and the selection, with the bar's bribe controls, holds until the next state publishes, after which the twine relaxes just as gradually. The stakes and twine never take pointer events; each clickable spot keeps its wide transparent hit line underneath. The sway and pennant flutter stop when the OS prefers reduced motion.

## Canal construction

The Game Session's canal build animator owns the motion when a canal is built, by an overseer decision or a personal canal. On the action timeline the staked-out spots that the resulting state will no longer offer fade out, while spots still offered stay as they are; after the state publishes, newly offered spots fade in as local, non-semantic entry, which also happens whenever spots appear after any transition the animators run, as on Undo or a perspective switch; a silent restoration runs no animators, so spots it reveals appear without motion. The board draws the canals of the build step's `to` state as a pre-reactivity override, so junction shapes are already final, and clips the new canal with a rect that sweeps from the end already joined to the spring or an existing canal to its open end, so water flows into the new ditch. Both overrides hold until that state publishes, so a chain of system actions after the build cannot drop them between steps; each build step overwrites them with its own state, so several builds arriving together each flow in turn. A transition that fails clears them as well. Without an action, as in state-only history navigation, the fade and fill run within the fallback budget; removing a canal, as on Undo, has no motion.

## Bribes and planted fields arriving

When a proposal adds a bribe, the Game Session's bribe pop animator draws the step's bribes as a pre-reactivity override and pops each new pill from nothing with a `back.out` overshoot on the action timeline, centered on the pill; its hover enlargement lives on an inner group, so the two never share a transform. When planting adds a field, the field pop animator draws the step's squares and remaining tiles as pre-reactivity overrides, so the planted tile has left the strip as its field lands, and pops the field with its farmers the same way; that square overflows its cell and sits above its neighbours only while it pops. Both overrides hold until the state publishes. Without an action the pills and fields grow in within the fallback budget; removing them, as on Undo, has no motion.

## Action bar transitions

The action bar is rendered from one view of the visible state and the current perspective, so it always shows exactly one of: spring placement, bidding, the reveal prompt, planting, neutral placement, canal building, or extra irrigation, or nothing. On every state change the Game Session's action bar animator works out the bar of the state the transition ends in, from the perspective the session will present once that state is published (in hotseat, the next active player), even when one action brings a chain of system actions such as auto-passes in Extra Irrigation. When that bar has a different height, the change waits for the action's own motion: on the final timeline the bar swaps to the new content as a pre-reactivity override that holds until the state publishes, is inert so its controls cannot be used before they apply, and hands focus back afterwards to a control in the bar that had it, its wrapper tweens from the old height to the new one so the board and tile strip slide instead of jumping, and, when the bar is showing a different kind of content, the new content fades in; the same content updating in place never blanks. Tags inside the bar's rows, such as Overseer, must not change a row's height. The bidding table keeps an Overseer pill, the same OverseerPill component and dimensions as the player panels', shaded with that player's color, in every row, hidden unless that player is the overseer, so its column never changes width; a bid that settles the overseer wipes that player's pill in from left to right at the point the bar settles, and it simply appears without an action. Until then the text above the board and the board itself stay still. When the height is unchanged the new content simply arrives with the published state. Without an action, as on Undo, the resize runs at once within the fallback budget and nothing fades. The wrapper is always rendered, empty and zero height when there is nothing to show, and contains its content's margins so its measured height is its laid-out height.

## Ambient motion

From time to time a small flock of birds flies in from either side of the board, settles on one planted field that has not dried out, sometimes spreading over a neighbouring living field as well, pecks and idles where it stands, now and then sends a single bird on a short flight to a neighbouring living field, and leaves together once one bird departs. The Game Session's bird director owns this as local presentation outside AnimationContext: it runs its own frame loop only while a flock is present, writes each bird's SVG transform directly, and uses reactive state only for the flock's presence. Visits begin only after 30 to 90 seconds of idle time in a visible tab, never while viewing history or when the OS prefers reduced motion. Any change that disqualifies a field a bird occupies, or entering history, startles the flock away at once; hiding the tab removes it without motion. Birds paint in a pointer-events-none SVG above the board, clipped to the board surface so they emerge from and vanish at its edge rather than over the surrounding padding or the tile strip, and never gate interaction.

Small glints drift along built canals and pop now and then at the spring, more often the more canals there are; each lasts 1.2 seconds, they never take pointer events, their removal timers stop when the board unmounts, and they are hidden when the OS prefers reduced motion. A static warm sun wash lights the board from the upper left: a soft-light blended layer above the board, canals, and birds, clipped to the board surface, with pointer-events none, that brightens and warms everything beneath it without changing what is drawn. Palm trees sway gently from their base, each square's palm at its own pace and phase, as pure CSS motion outside AnimationContext; the sway stops when the OS prefers reduced motion.

## Coexistence and precedence

EndOfGame disclosure takes precedence over private-money presentation. History uses the displayed state's phase, so navigating back before the end restores private presentation. Perspective changes replace the permitted state; values learned through Host View must not remain in a subsequent Player or Spectator representation.

## Shared visual state

These behaviors derive from displayed Game State, persisted configuration, and the Game Session's perspective. They add no transient selection. The bird layer's only transient state is the set of bird ids for the current flock: it is written when a flock spawns, cleared when the flock has left, is startled away, the tab hides, or the layer unmounts, and is never written per frame. The Game Session owns Exploration availability; the shared exploration control consumes that value.

## Render ownership

The players panel owns balance presentation. The table owns the tile-supply count and reads the public count through the hydrated state. The shared control owns the disabled Exploration button. Changing visibility does not change board hit targets or layer ordering.

Paper texture paints behind content within an isolated stacking context. It may texture a panel background, but must not darken player names or other foreground content. The history panel owns its custom dots and suppresses Flowbite’s default markers only within that panel.

## Verification scenarios

- With a planted field on the board, summon a flock from the console: birds enter from the board's edge without popping in, settle inside the field, peck in place, occasionally hop fields one at a time, and leave as a group. Summon again and step into history: the flock startles away, as it does when the field dries out. Hide the tab mid-visit and return: the flock is gone and a new idle timer runs. With reduced motion on, summoning returns false. Manual browser verification; the simulation and scheduling are covered by unit tests.

- Start a private-money protected Game and select the acting player: exactly their balance is delivered, three opponent badges show question marks in a four-player Game, the public tile count remains visible, and a submitted bid reduces the owner's money. Automated browser test, at desktop and narrow viewport sizes.
- Switch to Host View and then Spectator: canonical bag and balances are available only in Host View; Spectator restores an empty bag representation and four question marks. Exploration stays disabled. Automated browser test.
- Start a Public Money protected Game as Spectator: all balances are delivered, the bag remains concealed, and Exploration samples a playable complete bag. Submit a bid there and exit: the original action count and concealed bag return. Automated browser test.
- Complete a private-money Game, navigate backward and forward through history: end-game money is public, earlier opponent balances remain omitted, and tile order never enters projected history. Automated logic conformance test.

- Create a Game: the first bidder sees the draw pile and the reveal prompt with no bid controls; clicking the pile leaves a face-up tile where it stood and slides it down the strip leaving one at each of the next three slots, ending below them; once the deal finishes the bar above the board grows so the board and strip slide down and the bid controls fade in, and the count drops by four. Step backward and forward through that reveal in history: backward the tiles vanish, forward they fade in quickly, and Shift-forward replays the deal. In a later round, after the last personal-canal decision, Undo remains available to that player until the pile is clicked, and disappears once it is. The reveal click and the reappearance of bid controls are covered by the automated browser test; the count drop and Undo barrier are manual browser verification.
- Create a Game and place a bid: panel and inline names retain full-white text; History shows one 12-pixel dot per entry aligned with the timeline. Verified in Chromium with rendered-pixel and geometry checks.
