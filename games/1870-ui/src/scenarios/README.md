# 1870 scenarios

The dev harness lists these under **Scenarios**; loading one again starts it over. `scripts/build-scenarios.mjs` writes them from the recorded game in `games/1870/test/fixtures`; build the logic package first.

## Bridge for sale

The Missouri Pacific's first operating round, the Mississippi River Bridge Company still with a player (p. 9).

- St. Louis offers only tile #5, facing west and southwest. Other river hexes offer tiles that stay on one bank.
- Buying the bridge for $20–$40 opens the river, and offers its free lay at home in the private powers, besides the ordinary lays.

## Price protection

A player has sold shares and ended their turn, so the company's president may buy them back or let the price fall (p. 13).

- As the president, the action panel asks to Protect or Decline, with both prices.
- Protecting pays the bank, leaves the price, and passes play to the president's left.

## Company shares

A president's stock turn with a company that may redeem (p. 16).

- **Company action** in the stock panel lists the shares it may redeem: a Market share while there is one, otherwise each holder's, who may refuse.
- Redeeming is the turn's only action.

## Connection run

A company has just become able to run a train from its home to its destination, between companies' turns (p. 22).

- The action panel offers to place the destination station or keep it on the charter.
- The header names that company, not the next one to operate.

## Connection route

The same company choosing its routes.

- **Find routes** returns one train from home to destination and no further, with any other trains running as usual.
- Earnings offer pay or withhold, no half dividend; a paid run moves the price right, a withheld one not at all.

## Late game

A company's track step in phase 6, opened without history: bottom off-board values, reached destinations, destination stations beside their cities.

## Port token

A company owning the Gulf Shipping Company on its turn, its Cattle token already on the map (p. 9).

- Its private powers offer an open port and a closed port, each on the five port cities.
- An open port leaves the private open and adds $10 for other companies; a closed port closes the private.
