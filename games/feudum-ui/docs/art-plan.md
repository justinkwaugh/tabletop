# Feudum art plan

Every visual, its source, and the budget it must meet, with the UI contract (`ui-interaction-visual-contract.md`: "Render ownership", "Palette", "Phone layout"). Component ids are in `games/feudum/docs/components.md`.

**The publisher's art, through an asset map.** The art lands with the title's permission entry. Components ask a typed map (`src/lib/art/assetMap.ts`) for an image by id. A file lives in `src/lib/art/supplied/` as `<id>.webp` or `<id>.png`, found with `import.meta.glob`, and when it is absent the component draws a plain labelled fallback, so the tree builds, passes and plays with the folder empty. The files were committed together with the `PERMISSIONS.md` entry that covers them, in one commit. Anything that takes a seat colour is drawn unless the publisher printed it in the colour the seat shows (see Seat colours), and every highlight, ring and animation layer is always drawn. "Supplied assets" below lists both sides.

The drawn forms in this plan are our own work and are the fallback for every supplied id. None is traced, sampled or copied from the publisher's art.

Notes on the table:

- **Two meanings are fixed across titles.** Amber (`accent`) says "yours to act on" and rose (`danger`) says "costs or threatens". Nothing decorative is amber beyond `frame`, and nothing is rose except a labelled chip, a refusal reason, or text that names a cost. A rose chip always carries a label and the halo. The pink starting rings of the printed board are `map-ink`, so no pink or rose appears on the map outside a chip.
- **`seal` is a game piece.** It always has its crown and never a halo, so it is not read as a cost, and a cost is not read as a seal.
- **Eight tokens** (`terrain-line`, `parchment-shade`, `wood-piece`, `wood-dark`, `map-ink`, `halo`, `dim`, `good-iron-edge`) are named here with their values; the contract's palette table lists them with their meanings.

### Seat colours

Not tokens. Every seat-coloured element (pawn cube, influence marker, player disc, reeve dot, an action card's band and back, ribbon, wax seal on the seat panel, Knight row, seat chip) reads `gameSession.colors` through `model/seatPaint.ts`; no seat colour is written anywhere in the package but the colorizer. Where the publisher printed the element in the five seat colours it may show the print instead (below); otherwise it is drawn. The five seats are red, blue, yellow, green and purple (R-1.1). The default colorizer resolves them to red-700, blue-600, yellow-300, green-600 and purple-600; the colour-blind colorizer swaps in its own set, and the art follows with no change.

- **Ink by contrast.** Text or an icon on a seat fill is `halo` or `text`, chosen from the resolved colour's relative luminance (`model/seatPaint.ts`) so contrast is at least 4.5:1.
- **An edge on every seat fill.** Every seat-coloured shape has a 1 unit dark edge (`map-ink`), or a light edge when the fill is near black, so a seat colour stays off the terrain.
- **Never colour alone, and no letters.** No piece carries a seat's letter: pawns, markers, discs, reeves, a controlled monster's disc and the seat panel's wax seal are colour only, so the table stays uncluttered. Every one of them names its seat in its accessible name and its tooltip (`owner` in `model/seatPaint.ts`: "Ana's influence marker", "Ana's knight pawn", "Ana's behemoth"); a piece no seat stands behind, a dummy Knight row's, is named by its colour ("red influence marker"). A player who needs more than colour turns on the platform's colour-blind palette, which reaches the title through `gameSession.colors`: every seat's pieces are then drawn in that palette's colours, never the print. A card back carries a crown and the seat name on the panel.
- **Roles never take a seat colour.** A seat colour is a fill only: never a ring, chip or button. Yellow (default `yellow-300`) sits close to `accent`, and red (`red-700`) close to `seal`; yellow is never the only cue for a lit pawn since the ring is over `halo` and thicker than the edge, and the seal always has its crown.
- **Printed colours.** The publisher printed the action cards, the influence markers, the player discs and the reeves in the five seat colours (`PrintedColours` in `assetMap.ts`). A seat paint carries `printed` only while the fill the session shows is that colour's own under the default colorizer; under the colour-blind colorizer, or for a colour never printed, it is absent and the element is drawn in the colour the palette chose, so the art never overrides a palette.
- **A check.** `palette.spec.ts` resolves the five seats under both colorizers and fails if any two are closer than a set difference, or if a seat colour is that close to `danger`. The yellow and `accent` pair is the one entry on its allow-list, with the reason above.

## Board

**Geometry source.** One `boardGeometry.ts`, in board units, with a spec. A board unit is one CSS pixel at scale 1; the surface is 2500 × 953, the printed board's proportions, with nothing under it. Nothing is measured from a picture at run time. The table holds a position for each id in the logic package's board data and no other id:

- **22 spaces** (`space.<region>.<n>`), each a centre point, its four piece slots and its N/S/E/W letter anchors. `components.md` gives each space's region, letters, starting-space flag and a short note on where it lies ("upper big island, NW end", "central", "river"), but no numeric coordinates; the positions were fitted once by hand to the printed board, in board units, so a piece lands on its printed space when the map's file is there. The spec fixes the result.
- **41 routes** (13 road, 8 ship, 9 submersible, 8 flying machine, 3 ferry), each a cubic path between its two space centres with stored control points (a straight line where nothing crosses it). With the map's file there the print shows the routes at rest and these paths carry only the lit chrome. Where two media join the same pair of spaces the paths run side by side at a fixed lateral offset in medium order.
- **6 region outlines**, polygons hand-authored round each region's spaces, in the board's own order.
- **6 guild areas**, three down each side, each in its own local units (500 × 354, the printed panel's proportion) so the bench draws the same component at another scale: the Alchemist (top), Merchant (middle) and Farmer (bottom) on the west edge, the Knight (top), Noble (middle) and Monk (bottom) on the east edge. Local positions for each guild's goods spaces (Merchant 20, Alchemist 15 plus the vessel space, Knight 15 sections, Noble 6, Farmer barn, purse and 4 chickens, Monk rosary and coffer) and its 3 track spaces.
- **Tracks:** the 150 score positions round the board's edge, the 7 voyage spaces, 3 service spaces, 5 epoch rays, 5 region-tile chart rows, 6 region stacks, 5 wine barrels, the die and the supply's corner, where flights to and from the general supply begin and end.

**Layout** (board units): the score ring a band about 60 wide round the whole edge; west guild column x 60 to 452; the map in the centre x 452 to 2050, y 60 to 893, its top the epoch sky to y 150 and its bottom the strip of stacks, chart and barrels; east guild column x 2050 to 2440. The table and its spec are the source.

**Layers**, back to front, as the contract's render ownership sets them. Each layer owns the pick chrome of what it draws and dims its own unlit elements from the board offer. There is no global scrim.

1. **Ground:** the table, the frame, terrain fills with hatching per region (waves, hills, trees, dunes, cracked earth, peaks), the epoch sky.
2. **Routes:** the five media, each with its own line style and glyph so none is told by colour alone.
3. **Locations:** location discs with their silhouette and guild icon, the letters, the double start ring.
4. **Space contents:** landscape tiles, goods, abandoned vessels, influence markers as ruler, subject and serf, immunity seals.
5. **Pieces:** pawns with their vessels, monsters, the Move trail and ghosts.
6. **Guild areas:** each guild's grid, track, markers and reeves.
7. **Tracks:** stacks and chart, the die, voyage, service, barrels, the score ring and its discs.
8. **Supply card** (over the board, from the Supply button): the writ deck, haversack, supply counts, the start player.

**Hit targets** are transparent shapes carrying the model's ids: a circle per space and per marker slot, a rounded rectangle per guild grid space, track space, stack, barrel and area, a pill per chip. A target's diameter is at least 54 board units, which is 44 CSS px at the phone's pick-framing scale (about 0.8, the cover fit raised by the contract's 2.5×). Where a printed shape is smaller, the hit shape is larger than it and is clipped against its neighbours, so two targets never overlap. A piece target sits above the space beneath it; an unlit element does nothing on a press except enlarge, as the contract says.

## Pieces and tokens

A piece is one component in `components/pieces/`, its root an SVG that stands in HTML or nests in the board's SVG. Its forms live in `utils/pieceShapes.ts`, shared with the cover script. A piece with an asset id shows its file when the folder holds one and otherwise draws the form below, with its label on the root either way. A piece without an id takes a seat colour, or has no printed form to show, and is always drawn. A motif the board repeats many times may be defined once and placed with `<use>`. Slot positions (where a marker, good or pawn sits relative to its space) live in the board's geometry, never in a component.

## Cards and tiles

Three face components, each taking a `faceShown` decision from the perspective rule (`model/perspective.ts`) and never from whether the data is present. Enlarging shows the same component at a larger size, never a second asset. A back never names its face: when `faceShown` is false the component draws the back only, and nothing in its markup carries the face's id or file.

- **`pieces/ActionCardFace.svelte`** (11 kinds, 5 colours: 55 cards; a viewBox of 250 x 380). **While the seat shows a printed colour** and the folder holds it, a face is the whole printed card in that colour, `card.action.<kind>.<colour>`, frame, border and print together, and a back is `card.back.<colour>`, which names no face. The print is cut at its line to the card art's proportion, so it stands whole and centred in the card's box, never stretched, with a narrow strip either side. Over the print the face draws only its number, on the frame's foot, and on a compact face its short name across the foot, over the print's name line in its other languages, where the print's own name is too small to read. A standard or full face draws no name plate: the print carries the name, and a plate on its title would cover the print's top symbols. The accessible name ("Influence card, A") names the card at every size. **Otherwise** the seat colour fills the card as a band round an art window, and the card's number sits on the band. With a file, `card.action.<kind>` fills the window, one image per kind whatever the seat. Without one, the window is parchment with the card's name (title font), its number, its top-right symbols from the card's data (`x2`, `crossed-x2`, `not-last`, `either-or`, `vp` with its value, `vp-variable` "?", and `epic-voyage` and `region-tile`), the regular action, and the special ability's name, needs as icons and effect. **The drawn back** takes the seat colour, with a parchment rim and a crown.
- **`pieces/WritFace.svelte`** (8 mandates, 8 charters). With a file, the writ's own id. Without one, a mandate shows two rows, A and B, each with the goods turned in and its wording; mandates 6 and 8 show their guild icons under their wording; a charter shows its wording with its values. The back is `writ.back` or a drawn crowned writ, the same for all sixteen. A sealed writ is that back with a wax seal to others, and a dimmed face marked "sealed" to its holder.
- **`pieces/TileFace.svelte`** (24 region tiles). `tile.front.<numeral>` or a drawn numeral, in the region's terrain token when the caller gives a region; turned, `tile.back.<landscape>` or a drawn landscape. The printed front carries only the numeral, so a tile's region comes from where it stands. The same component draws a tile on a space, in a stack, in a panel row that offers it (28 px) and enlarged.
- **`pieces/SealFace.svelte`** (one disc, two sides). Red side up it is one crowned disc for everyone, its holder included, and its value is not in the markup; flipped, a bead shows its value to all. It never has a halo.

**Sizes.** A face draws at three tiers by the width it is given, not by a second asset (`utils/cardShape.ts`). **Compact** (under 60 CSS px, the seat panel's small faces): band, number, name abbreviated, symbols. **Standard** (the hand row and the panel): adds the action line. **Full** (180 CSS px and up, enlarged): adds the special ability and its need. A supplied image carries its own printing, so at compact only the abbreviated name is added over it. Enlarged is capped at 300 CSS px wide on a desktop, and on a phone is the screen width less its gutters.

**Perspective.** The owner sees faces, others see backs with a count; a set-aside card is dimmed for the owner and not shown to anyone else; a sealed charter as above. A magnifier on a face enlarges it and picks nothing.

## Cover

The catalog title image is the supplied cover, `src/lib/art/supplied/cover.webp`, read by `info.thumbnailUrl`. It is shown at 150 px high in the compact card and 340 px high in the large card. It is 544 × 680 (4:5), lossy WebP, at most 120 KB. 680 px is twice the large card's height, so it is never upscaled and carries no excess under the image-assets gate. No cover is drawn.

**Production.** `scripts/make-cover.mjs` places the image on a page from `utils/coverPage.ts`, renders it in Chromium and writes the WebP; it reads colours from `app.css`, so it contains no raw colour. `src/lib/art/cover-README.md` says how the cover is made again.

**The supplied cover.** The id `cover` is the catalog's image when its file is present. It is the publisher's box front, with nothing drawn over it, since the print already says the title. Save the box front as `box-front.png` (or `.jpg`, `.webp`) in the git-ignored `src/lib/art/cover-source/` and run `node scripts/make-cover.mjs --titled`: the script takes `box-front.*` when it is there and the rulebook's cover, `rulebook-cover.*`, otherwise, and writes `src/lib/art/supplied/cover.webp`. The crop rule (`coverPlacement` in `utils/coverPage.ts`) keeps the printed title whole. The box front is landscape, and its title runs across 58 % of its width (`BoxFrontTitle`), more than a full-height 4:5 crop shows, so the title and a 16-unit margin each side fill the cover's width, centred across it, and the image stands centred top to bottom between two bands of `surface`. A source without a known title is scaled to the cover's full width and centred top to bottom; the rulebook's cover loses about 5 % of its height at each end, and its title stays whole. A supplied scene that carries no title of its own takes `--art` alone and stands behind the drawn parchment band. `src/lib/definitions/definition.ts` reads the supplied file and, where the folder holds none, gives the catalog an empty thumbnail: no drawn cover stands in for the publisher's, and the site's cards show no picture for the title. It finds the file through `src/lib/art/cover.ts`, which looks for the cover alone, so the UI entry the site loads for its library carries neither the asset map nor the logic tables the map is built from.

## Contract notes

Where this plan adds to `ui-interaction-visual-contract.md`:

- **Aspect ratio.** The board is 2500 × 953 (about 2.6:1), with nothing under it, so 375 px wide fits at about 145 px tall. The contract says the same.
- **Bead.** The contract draws a bead as a pale disc with its value; this plan keeps that and adds a teal-and-gold stripe band to echo the physical bead.
- **Coins.** One `shilling` token (silver) for both the 1 and the 5 in the drawn form; the 5 is told by size, rim and numeral, not colour. The supplied coins are silver and gold and print no numeral, so a coin's label carries its value.
- **Action card size.** A viewBox of 250 x 380, so the art window keeps the printed proportion.
- **One image per kind, or the printed card.** A supplied card's art window is the same for every seat, the band and back carrying the seat; while the seat shows a printed colour the whole printed card in that colour stands in for both.
- **The upright guild.** The trade picker shows a guild's area turned a quarter clockwise, so its print, which lies on its side on the board, reads upright: 310 px tall on a desk, 210 px on a phone. It is the same `GuildArea` at another scale; rings in `accent` over the offered places say which the cards beside it sell, and take no press. Each card draws its offer with the pieces' own art (a vessel, a good, a marker in the row's colour, a seal), and the shilling token beside its price.
- **The nourish picker.** Each pawn row draws the pawn's cube in its seat's colour (the same `PawnPiece` as the map) and each feed as its good's cube: food, and sulfur for wine. A rose row (`danger` border on `danger-soft`) is a pawn left unfed; a feed with nothing left is dimmed. The summary pays in the same cubes, and on a phone the send carries them in place of the words. The map's payment chips carry the good's cube beside "1 food" or "1 wine".
- **The hand draft.** A drafted card stays among the eleven at 40 % opacity with its slot number in an `accent` disc, numbered in `ink`, at its centre. The plan's slots are the same `ActionCardFace` at 64 px, a slot not yet filled a dashed `frame` outline at the card's 250 × 380 proportion; each slot carries its number in a smaller `accent` disc at its top left. The pouch's two choices draw their goods with the map's own cubes (`GoodToken`, 22 px): saltpeter and sulfur. The chosen one has the `accent` border and a 2 px `accent` ring on `primary`; one that cannot be chosen is at half opacity. On a phone the art stands over its short name.
- **Numeric positions.** `components.md` gives no numeric board coordinates, only region, letters and a placement note per space, so `boardGeometry.ts` is authored, not rescaled from a given table.

## Specs that guard it

- `palette.spec.ts`: every colour is a declared token and every used token is declared; the declared set equals the contract's table and this one, with this plan's values; the seat colours under both colorizers are mutually distinct and clear of `danger` (yellow against `accent` allow-listed), with ink at 4.5:1.
- `imageCoverage.spec.ts`: every id in the asset map, present or not, has a component, a labelled fallback and a file slot; the folder holds no file the map doesn't know; the cover's size, format and budget.
- `definitions/entry.spec.ts`: the UI entry reaches only the title info and the cover.
- `emptyArt.spec.ts`: with the folder empty, the state of every commit before the art lands, every id falls back and the catalog gets no cover: its thumbnail is the empty string.
- `faces.spec.ts`: a back is identical across its faces and names none, with and without files; a seal hides its value from its holder; tiers by width; a rose chip's label and halo; a seal without one.
- `faces.spec.ts`, printed cards: in a printed colour every kind is its whole printed card and the back is that colour's, the same for all eleven kinds and naming none; a paint in no printed colour, or a printed colour the folder lacks, keeps the band and the drawn back; the name over the print at the two small tiers, the number at all three.
- `faces.spec.ts`, printed pieces: in a printed colour a marker, disc and reeve are the printed piece of their own kind, with no letter on it; every seat piece, printed or drawn, names its seat in its accessible name and tooltip; a paint in no printed colour, or a file the folder lacks, draws the piece in the seat fill.
- `model/seatPaint.spec.ts`: a seat paint names its printed colour under the default colorizer only, never under the colour-blind one, for a colour never printed, or for a fill that is not the colour's own; a paint names its seat ("Ana's"), or a colour with no seat behind it by that colour.
- `fonts.spec.ts`: every font file has its licence text beside it, and only the families this plan names ship.
- `boardGeometry.spec.ts`: every space, route, region, guild-area part, track space and barrel in the logic's data has a position, and no position names an id the data lacks; each space lies inside its region's outline; no two space discs overlap; each route's ends are its two spaces' centres; there are 150 score positions in the stated order, none sharing a lane; no two hit targets overlap and none is under 54 units.
- `boardGeometry.spec.ts`, on the printed panels: the print places the same ids the drawn areas place, each inside its panel; a guild reads the print only when its own panel is in the folder; ranks, bins, cells, scrolls, inventions, carts and the 17 beads stand apart; the bench's presses lie on the panel and never on one another.
- `guildArea.spec.ts`: each area lays over its own printed panel at board and bench scale, leaves the prices, inventions, hens and beasts to the print, still stands every piece state holds, says the counts the print can't, keeps a concealed seal unread and veils an unused apprentice space.
- `tests/printedArt.spec.ts`, in the browser with the files there: each panel's print fills its area and the ring the board; on the bench the Knight's markers stand inside their rows' presses, and on the trade picker's upright board the market's goods stand inside their bins' rings and the rosary's beads on the panel; each printed seat piece is centred in the box the drawn one filled, with no letter and its seat in its accessible name.
- `map/mapLayers.spec.ts`: the ground, routes and locations; the markers, seals, goods and abandoned vessels on a space, each where the rules put it; pawns on their vessels and monsters, in seat paint.
- `tracks/trackLayers.spec.ts`: the score ring and what the printed board already holds.
- `supplyCard.spec.ts`: the Supply card's groups, each count from state, the deck's notes for the viewer alone, and the start player.
- The cover budget: the supplied cover, where the folder holds it, is 544 × 680 and at most 120 KB, and no drawn cover is committed beside it.
- `utils/coverPage.spec.ts`: a scene without a title stands under the title band; a supplied cover that prints its own title is the whole cover, with nothing drawn over it.
- Readiness gates 1 and 2 pass on the first run.
