# Feudum art plan

Every visual, its source, and the budget it must meet, with the UI contract (`ui-interaction-visual-contract.md`: "Render ownership", "Palette", "Phone layout"). Component ids are in `games/feudum/docs/components.md`.

**The publisher's art, through an asset map.** The rights holder has agreed to the use of Feudum's graphical assets when hosted on https://boardtogether.games. The UI draws every board, piece, card, tile and icon through an asset map from component id to file. During development the files live in a git-ignored folder in this package. They are committed together with the `PERMISSIONS.md` entry that covers them, in one commit, and never before it. Anything the publisher's art does not supply, such as highlights, rings and animation layers, is drawn in code. The sections below that describe drawn forms are the fallback until the asset map replaces them.

Notes on the table:

- **Two meanings are fixed across titles.** Amber (`accent`) says "yours to act on" and rose (`danger`) says "costs or threatens". Nothing decorative is amber beyond `frame`, and nothing is rose except a labelled chip, a refusal reason, or text that names a cost. A rose chip always carries a label and the halo. The pink starting rings of the printed board are `map-ink`, so no pink or rose appears on the map outside a chip.
- **`seal` is a game piece.** It always has its crown and never a halo, so it is not read as a cost, and a cost is not read as a seal.
- **Eight tokens** (`terrain-line`, `parchment-shade`, `wood-piece`, `wood-dark`, `map-ink`, `halo`, `dim`, `good-iron-edge`) are named here with their values; the contract's palette table lists them with their meanings.

### Seat colours

Not tokens. Every seat-coloured element (pawn body, influence marker, player disc, reeve dot, card border, ribbon, wax seal on the seat panel, Knight row, seat chip) reads `gameSession.colors`; no seat colour is written anywhere in the package. The five seats are red, blue, yellow, green and purple (R-1.1). The default colorizer resolves them to red-700, blue-600, yellow-300, green-600 and purple-600; the colour-blind colorizer swaps in its own set, and the art follows with no change.

- **Ink by contrast.** Text or an icon on a seat fill is black or white, chosen from the resolved colour's relative luminance (`utils/colorLightness.ts`, as MarraCash does) so contrast is at least 4.5:1.
- **An edge on every seat fill.** Every seat-coloured shape has a 1 unit dark edge (`map-ink`), or a light edge when the fill is near black, so a seat colour stays off the terrain.
- **Never colour alone.** A pawn is told by its seat's initial on its base; markers, discs and reeve dots carry theirs; a Knight row carries the initial at its head; a card back carries a crown and the seat name on the panel.
- **Roles never take a seat colour.** A seat colour is a fill only: never a ring, chip or button. Yellow (default `yellow-300`) sits close to `accent`, and red (`red-700`) close to `seal`; yellow is never the only cue for a lit pawn since the ring is over `halo` and thicker than the edge, and the seal always has its crown.
- **A check.** `palette.spec.ts` resolves the five seats under both colorizers and fails if any two are closer than a set difference, or if a seat colour is that close to `danger`. The yellow and `accent` pair is the one entry on its allow-list, with the reason above.

## Board

**Geometry source.** One `boardGeometry.ts`, in board units, with a spec. A board unit is one CSS pixel at scale 1; the surface is 2500 × 1000 for the board and 150 more for the margin, 2500 × 1150 in all. Nothing is measured from a picture. The table holds a position for each id in the logic package's board data and no other id:

- **22 spaces** (`space.<region>.<n>`), each a centre point, its four piece slots and its N/S/E/W letter anchors. `components.md` gives each space's region, letters, starting-space flag and a short note on where it lies ("upper big island, NW end", "central", "river"), but no numeric coordinates; the positions are authored once from those notes and the connection list, in board units, so adjacent spaces are roughly where the printed board puts them. The spec fixes the result.
- **42 routes** (13 road, 8 ship, 10 submersible, 8 flying machine, 3 ferry), each a cubic path between its two space centres with stored control points (a straight line where nothing crosses it). Where two media join the same pair of spaces the paths run side by side at a fixed lateral offset in medium order.
- **6 region outlines**, polygons hand-authored round each region's spaces, in the board's own order.
- **6 guild areas**, three down each side, each in its own local units (about 500 × 300) so the bench draws the same component at another scale: the Alchemist (top), Merchant (middle) and Farmer (bottom) on the west edge, the Knight (top), Noble (middle) and Monk (bottom) on the east edge. Local positions for each guild's goods spaces (Merchant 20, Alchemist 15 plus the vessel space, Knight 15 sections, Noble 6, Farmer barn, purse and 4 chickens, Monk rosary and coffer) and its 3 track spaces.
- **Tracks:** the 150 score positions round the board's edge, the 7 voyage spaces, 3 service spaces, 5 epoch rays, 5 region-tile chart rows, 6 region stacks, 5 wine barrels, the die and the margin slots.

**Layout** (board units): west guild column x 0 to 540; the map in the centre x 590 to 1910, y 140 to 760; east guild column x 1960 to 2500; the epoch sky above the map y 0 to 130; stacks, chart, die, voyage and service below the map y 770 to 1000; the score ring a 56 unit band round the whole board edge. The margin sits below at y 1000 to 1150. These are starting numbers; the table and its spec are the source once written.

**Layers**, back to front, as the contract's render ownership sets them. Each layer owns the pick chrome of what it draws and dims its own unlit elements from the board offer. There is no global scrim.

1. **Ground:** the table, the frame, terrain fills with hatching per region (waves, hills, trees, dunes, cracked earth, peaks), the epoch sky.
2. **Routes:** the five media, each with its own line style and glyph so none is told by colour alone.
3. **Locations:** location discs with their silhouette and guild icon, the letters, the double start ring.
4. **Space contents:** landscape tiles, goods, abandoned vessels, influence markers as ruler, subject and serf, immunity seals.
5. **Pieces:** pawns with their vessels, monsters, the Move trail and ghosts.
6. **Guild areas:** each guild's grid, track, markers and reeves.
7. **Tracks:** stacks and chart, the die, voyage, service, barrels, the score ring and its discs.
8. **Margin:** the writ deck, haversack, supply counts, the start-player marker.

**Hit targets** are transparent shapes carrying the model's ids: a circle per space and per marker slot, a rounded rectangle per guild grid space, track space, stack, barrel and area, a pill per chip. A target's diameter is at least 54 board units, which is 44 CSS px at the phone's pick-framing scale (about 0.8, the cover fit raised by the contract's 2.5×). Where a printed shape is smaller, the hit shape is larger than it and is clipped against its neighbours, so two targets never overlap. A piece target sits above the space beneath it; an unlit element does nothing on a press except enlarge, as the contract says.

## Cards and tiles

Three face components, each taking a `faceShown` decision from the perspective rule and never from whether the data is present. Enlarging shows the same component at a larger size, never a second asset. A back never names its face: when `faceShown` is false the component draws the back only, and nothing in its markup carries the face's id.

- **`ActionCardFace.svelte`** (11 kinds, 5 colours: 55 cards; a viewBox of 250 × 350). Parchment, the seat colour as a border and a corner ribbon. The card's name (MedievalSharp) and its number in the hand's fixed order. Top-right symbols, drawn from the card's data: `x2`, `crossed-x2`, `not-last`, `either-or`, `vp` with its value, `vp-variable` ("?") and the Move and Improve extras `epic-voyage` and `region-tile`. The regular action in one line (IM Fell English). The special ability's name and its need as an icon (a pawn character, a good, a barrel sulfur). The back is the seat colour with a crown and a parchment rim.
- **`WritFace.svelte`** (8 mandates, 8 charters). A mandate shows two rows, A and B, each with the goods turned in boxed and its reward as tokens; mandates 6 and 8 show their three guild rewards each under its guild icon and "3 star" as the `star` symbol. A charter shows its name, its values and, for a kingdom charter, a small map built from the board's region outlines and space positions with the qualifying spaces dotted. The back is a crowned writ; a sealed charter wears a wax seal.
- **`TileFace.svelte`** (24 region tiles). The front and back described under pieces; the same component draws a tile on a space, in a stack and enlarged.

**Sizes.** The component draws at three tiers by the width it is given, not by a second asset. **Compact** (under about 60 CSS px, the seat panel's small faces): frame, border, number, symbols, name abbreviated. **Standard** (the hand row and the panel): adds the action line. **Full** (enlarged): adds the special ability and its need. Enlarged is capped at 300 CSS px wide on a desktop, and on a phone is the screen width less 32 px, 343 at 375 wide. The viewBox is 250 units wide, so text on the full tier is at least 11 units, 13 CSS px at the phone enlargement.

**Perspective.** The owner sees faces, others see backs with a count; a set-aside card is dimmed for the owner and not shown to anyone else; a sealed charter is a back with a wax seal to others and a dimmed face with "sealed" to its owner. A magnifier on a face enlarges it and picks nothing.

## Icons and symbols

Symbols used in panels, chips, badges, log sentences and token text are drawn SVG in `SymbolDefs.svelte`, in `currentColor` where they take the ink of where they stand and in their own token where they are a game piece. Each has a name the token text uses, so the table and the sentences share one vocabulary, as Oath's `TokenText` does. A good, shilling, seal, bead, vp or vessel named in a panel or history line is drawn as its symbol; its word is the image's alt text.

## Cover

The catalog title image is `src/lib/images/cover.webp`, read by `info.thumbnailUrl`. It is shown at 150 px high in the compact card and 340 px high in the large card. It is 544 × 680 (4:5), lossy WebP, at most 120 KB. 680 px is twice the large card's height, so it is never upscaled and carries no excess under the image-assets gate.

**Composition.** One image that reads at both heights:

- A dusk-indigo sky (`surface`) over a green hill, with a keep on it in the centre, a crown on its pennant. This silhouette and the crown carry the title at 150 px.
- A band of parchment across the lower third with the title "Feudum" in MedievalSharp, in `ink`, its capital height 14 % of the image. That is 21 px at 150 high and 48 px at 340 high, and the title is whole at both.
- The six guild icons as medallions in an arc over the keep, which are ornament at 150 px and legible at 340 px.
- Five pawns, one per default seat colour, standing along the hill's foot under the band. At 150 px they are a row of coloured marks.
- No text but the title; no amber and no rose beyond what the palette uses decoratively.

**Production.** `scripts/make-cover.ts` composes an SVG from the same shape modules the board uses, with the title converted to outlines so it needs no font at render, renders it once and writes the WebP. It reads colours from `app.css` and the five default seat colours from the colorizer's table, so it contains no raw colour. A short `src/lib/images/README.md` says how the cover was made and that rerunning the script regenerates it.

- **Board ground:** one image as layer 1 under the existing layers, with `boardGeometry.ts` rebased to the image's pixels (a board unit becomes one image pixel) and re-derived from the logic data; terrain fills and hatching in `TerrainDefs.svelte` are removed, routes and locations stay.
- **Card, writ and tile art:** an art window on `ActionCardFace`, `WritFace` and `TileFace` (the symbols in `CardDefs` and `TileDefs`), one image per id. Frame, name, number and text stay drawn so they stay translatable and legible at compact size.

## Contract notes

Where this plan adds to or departs from `ui-interaction-visual-contract.md`, for the contract to settle in the same change that builds the UI:

- **Aspect ratio.** The contract says the board is about 2.5:1. The board proper here is 2500 × 1000 (2.5:1), but the contract also puts the margin inside the scaled surface, so the fitted surface is 2500 × 1150, about 2.2:1: 375 px wide fits at about 172 px tall, not 150.
- **Bead.** The contract draws a bead as a pale disc with its value; this plan keeps that and adds a teal-and-gold stripe band to echo the physical bead.
- **Coins.** One `shilling` token (silver) for both the 1 and the 5; the 5 is told by size, rim and numeral, not colour.
- **Numeric positions.** `components.md` gives no numeric board coordinates, only region, letters and a placement note per space, so `boardGeometry.ts` is authored, not rescaled from a given table.

## Specs that guard it

- `palette.spec.ts`: every colour is a declared token and every used token is declared; the declared set equals the contract's table; the seat colours under both colorizers are mutually distinct and clear of `danger` (yellow against `accent` allow-listed).
- `boardGeometry.spec.ts`: every space, route, region, guild-area part, track space and barrel in the logic's data has a position, and no position names an id the data lacks; each space lies inside its region's outline; no two space discs overlap; each route's ends are its two spaces' centres; there are 150 score positions in the stated order, none sharing a lane; no two hit targets overlap and none is under 54 units.
- `pawnShape.spec.ts`, `locationShape.spec.ts`, `markerShape.spec.ts`, `goodsShape.spec.ts` and the other shape specs: slot positions are inside the disc's neighbourhood and distinct, every character, good, vessel, monster and disc face has a shape.
- `symbols.spec.ts`, the image coverage spec for a title with no image files: every name token text can build is defined in `SymbolDefs`, every guild, good, vessel, monster, region, landscape and card-symbol id has a symbol, and every `<use href>` in the package resolves to a defined `<symbol>` or `<g>`.
- `cardFaces.spec.ts`: each of the 11 action cards, 16 writs and 24 tiles renders at all three tiers from its data; a back's markup contains no face id.
- The cover budget: `cover.webp` is 544 × 680 and at most 120 KB.
- Readiness gates 1 and 2 pass on the first run.
