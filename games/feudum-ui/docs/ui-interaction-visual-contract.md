# Feudum: the table, the map, the guilds and the panel

This contract fixes what players see and press. It follows `docs/ui-interaction-visual-contract.md`, `docs/game-ui-layout.md`, `docs/user-interactions.md` and the game UI animation skill. Rules are cited as `R-x.y` (`rules-digest.md`), house rulings as `R-x.y-Hn` and implementation rules as `R-X.n` (`rulings.md`), in `games/feudum/docs/`. Hidden information follows `games/feudum/docs/visibility.md`. Action, machine-state and component names are the engine's (`components.md` ids).

Its code and tests are the source of truth: where this file and they differ, they win and this file is corrected in the same change.

## The principle

Oath's principle, with three stated departures:

1. **The table is for looking.** A press on a card, a writ, a region tile, a guild area, a seat or a stack shows it at a readable size or opens what the table knows of it. That press changes nothing in the game.
2. **A press enlarges.** The enlarged view sits over everything until the next press, on the piece or beside it, or Escape. No close button stands over it; the enlarged piece is itself the close press, named Close.
3. **The panel chooses.** Every choice with no place on the board is a row or a button in the action panel: a card, a mode, a writ, a good, a count, yes or no.
4. **Departure: a lit thing on the map is picked on the map.** `docs/game-ui-layout.md` binds this: players act where the thing is. A space, a piece, a marker, a landscape, a region stack or a guild area is picked by pressing it, but only while it is lit. An unlit element does nothing on a press except enlarge, as in 1. The reason: nearly every Feudum card targets one of 22 spaces or the pieces on them, and a list of those in the panel would not show where they are.
5. **Departure: the guild bench is an enlarged view that chooses.** A guild function moves goods, seals, beads and markers between two guild areas (R-4.6). Those grids (a 5 × 4 market, six scroll spaces, four Knight rows of six spaces) are too small to press at any fitted scale, and two of the six neighbouring pairs (Alchemist → Knight, Monk → Farmer) lie on opposite edges of the board. So once a guild function is staged, the two areas it touches are drawn side by side at a readable size in the **guild bench**, and the function's picks are made there. The bench draws the same components as the board; it is the board's own guild areas enlarged, never a second picture.
6. **Departure: the hand is chosen by pressing the card.** In hand selection and in card play the action panel shows the seat's cards as card faces, and a press on a card picks it. Each card carries a magnifier at its corner that enlarges it and picks nothing. The reason: the hand is the panel's choice (3), and a face is the clearest row for it.

## The table

### What is on it

The board keeps the printed board's arrangement: the map in the centre, three guild areas down each side, the tracks round and under the map. Players moving between the physical game and this one find each thing where it was. Where the print is supplied it is the ground: the map, the score ring and frame, and each guild's panel, with state, hit targets and picks fitted over it; each is drawn in code where its file is absent.

- **The printed guild panels** are the guild areas' ground at board scale, on the bench and upright in the trade picker. The pieces stand on the panel's printed spaces, the bench's presses cover those spaces, and the picker's rings ring them. A Knight row's column is narrower than a finger at bench scale, so a row that takes a press reaches past its print to 44 px across, as far as a pressing neighbour allows; its ring stays over the printed column (S68). The area draws only what the print can't show: the pieces, the veil on an apprentice space below its player count, and the counts (purse, coffer, rosary total, barn against its limit). A concealed seal stays a crowned back on its printed scroll.

### Drawn in code

A piece, face or disc the publisher's art supplies shows it through the asset map, and draws its own labelled fallback when the file is absent (`art-plan.md`, "Supplied assets"). Everything else, and anything that takes a seat colour but was not printed in the colour the seat shows, is SVG generated from data. The fonts are OFL. The forms below are the drawn ones.

- **Geometry from data.** Every space, route, guild grid space, track space, stack and barrel has a position in one board geometry table in board units; routes are drawn along stored control points. A spec checks that every id in the logic's board data has a position and no position names an id the data lacks. Nothing is measured from a picture at run time.
- **Terrain.** Each region is a filled shape in its terrain token (sea, island, forest, desert, badlands, mountain) with a light hatching motif per terrain (waves, hills, trees, dunes, cracked earth, peaks) drawn once in a `<defs>` and repeated.
- **Routes.** Each medium has its own line and glyph so none is told apart by colour alone: road a solid tan path; ship a dotted line with a small sail glyph at its midpoint; submersible a dash-dot line with a periscope glyph; flying machine a dotted arc lifted off the ground with a wing glyph; ferry a rope-dashed line with a ferry glyph and the "2s" fare. Where two media join the same pair of spaces their lines run side by side.
- **Spaces.** A location disc shows its kind as a silhouette (outpost tower, farm field, town roofs, feudum keep) and a feudum its guild icon (sheep, coins, gear, axe, crown, cross). A starting space wears a double ring in map ink. A ruled space wears a ring under its disc in its ruler's colour, on a dark keyline so a pale seat colour reads on the pale map; it keeps the map art and the disc untouched and sits clear of the pawns above and the markers at the lower left, and the site card names the ruler. Influence markers stand off the disc at its lower left: the ruler nearest it, a subject under the ruler in the same colour, a serf beside the subject in the other colour, an immunity seal as a small crowned red disc under its marker.
- **Pawns and pieces.** A pawn is the wooden cube it is on the table, in the seat colour, seen in three-quarter view (the front face square-on, the top and right side going back from it), with its character's guild icon printed pale with a dark outline on the front face, as on the printed cubes; a pawn on a vessel stands on the vessel's hull. A drunk pawn tilts and carries a small sulfur drop. Monsters are their own silhouettes (behemoth, sea serpent) with a seat-colour collar when controlled. An influence marker, a player disc and a reeve are the printed wooden pieces while the seat shows one of the five printed colours and the file is present (a disc its blank face, a reeve the face printed with its point); otherwise they are drawn in the seat colour. No piece carries a seat's letter: each names its seat in its accessible name and tooltip ("Ana's influence marker", "Ana's knight pawn"), and the platform's colour-blind palette, which reaches the title through the session's colours, redraws every seat's pieces in its own colours for a player who needs more than colour. Pawns are always drawn, since the print does not show a pawn's character. Every space keeps one anchor, so a piece is always in the same place: up to four pawns or monsters in an arc above the disc, clear of it, more as a count chip over the arc; the markers at the lower left; a landscape at the right; abandoned vessels at the left edge. A space against the map's edge mirrors the anchor or sets its vessels where there is room. Every wooden piece (a pawn, a marker, a player disc, a reeve), printed or drawn, has a dark outline and a small shadow under it, so no seat colour is special-cased and every piece lifts off the gold discs and the pale map; both are drawn as shapes, not filters, so a phone renders them.
- **Goods.** Each good has a silhouette as well as its colour: saltpeter a white crystal, sulfur a yellow nugget, iron a dark ingot, wood a brown log, food a green sheaf. Goods on a space stand on one fixed plate below the disc, at its lower right (its lower left on a space mirrored against the map's edge), grouped by kind: one token per kind in the same order everywhere (saltpeter, sulfur, iron, wood, food), with a count badge when more than one lies there, so "how many iron" never needs counting cubes or telling two dark cubes apart. Goods in a grid show one token per good up to four, then a token with a count.
- **Tokens in text.** A good, shilling, seal, bead, vp or vessel named in a panel or a history line is drawn as its symbol, so the table and the sentences share one vocabulary.
- **Card, writ and tile faces** are components that lay out their data (below). Enlarging shows the same component at a larger size.

### Seat panel

Each seat has one **seat panel**. On a desktop the seat panels stand in the Players tab and nowhere else; on a phone the seat strip stands in for them, and a chip opens the full panel as a sheet. Every view of a seat reads one derivation of it, so they never differ.

The panel's look is the game's signature (`docs/game-ui-layout.md`): a **sealed charter roll**, a parchment sheet hung from a wooden roller, the seat colour as its ribbon and wax seal. On a desk it is about 120 px tall: the ribbon, then three lines, each piece a small face with its count beside it. A piece the seat may hold none of (seals, beads, tiles, vessels, the pouch good) is left out at zero; the always-held kinds show their zero dimmed. Every piece has an accessible name and a tooltip that says it in words.

- **Ribbon:** the turn-order numeral, the name in the seat colour, the start-player crown when the seat holds the marker, the turn pulse while the seat decides, then the score. Once the seat serves or rules a feudum, a **shield** on the ribbon carries its military discs covered, filled when the seat is a vassal; its tooltip reads "Service 1/3 · vassal", then the voyage space ("voyage 4") once the seat has one (R-3.7.5, R-4.9.2). Before either, no shield and no voyage.
- **Cards line:** for other seats the unplayed hand is a stack of card backs with "4 in hand"; the set-aside cards are not shown, since which they are is hidden. While the seat's own desk shows its hand (its card-play turn), its panel reads "4 in hand" alone, so the hand is never shown twice; at any other time (another seat's turn, an answer, round end, history) the own panel shows the hand's faces. While hands are chosen the line reads "choosing", for every seat. Then the played cards face up in play order; a card used other than as regular carries a corner badge (special, forfeited, replayed, Déjà Vu, flipped as Defend), and its tooltip names the use ("Tax card, special"). In Host View another seat's hand shows its faces; set-aside cards never stand on the panel.
- **Supply line:** the five goods by kind and shillings, always; then king's seals as a concealed crowned disc with the count, and beads with their values, when held (R-5-H1).
- **Holdings line:** influence markers in supply and free discs (of 3, R-1.1), the wine barrel as three staves filled by its sulfur ("wine barrel: 1 of 3 sulfur"), and the writs, always; then collected region tiles (each a small face), vessels by type and the pouch good, when held. Other seats' writs are one back per kind with its count, sealed charters as backs with a wax seal once final scoring seals them (R-4.4.3, R-4.4.4). The owner sees unsealed writs' faces and sealed charters' faces with "sealed"; its deck knowledge ("you know 2 under the deck") is the writs' tooltip. The pouch good is saltpeter ("extra action") or sulfur ("sequential"), dimmed once spent; before the hand is finalised the owner sees the drafted good outlined. Once a seat has finalised and until every seat has, its pouch is sealed: the owner sees its good outlined under a lock with "sealed", every other seat a lock and "hidden until all finalise", whether or not a good went in (R-3.1-H1). At the reveal the pouches on every panel turn over together, edge on to face up, in one 420 ms turn (none with reduced motion).
- **Pawns** off the board are the body's tooltip ("2 pawns in supply").
- **Guild places** are not on the panel: the guild standings' rails beside the guild areas hold every seat's places.

Rules for the panel:

- **Enlarge.** A press on any card, writ face, writ back, tile or bead in a seat panel enlarges it in the centre until the next press or Escape. A back enlarges as a back.
- **Open a seat.** The ribbon opens that seat at a readable size over the table: the same panel, larger. The backdrop, Escape or the ribbon closes it.

### Guild standings

Every seat's place in every guild, read from the state alone (`model/guildStandings.ts`; it recomputes no rank, since the tracks already hold the places the rules gave, R-4.5.3, R-4.5-H1), stands as a **rail beside each guild's area**. At the desk the six rails stand in two columns beside the board, the West guilds' to its left and the East guilds' to its right, each level with its area (`standings/GuildRails.svelte`, `standings/GuildRail.svelte`); the board gives up the columns' width (at 1440 × 900 it is about 844 px wide). On a phone there is no margin to spare, so a guild column's view carries its three rails over the map's inner edge, each level with its area, and the view's frame takes them in; the Map view has none, and the seat strip's Guilds chip opens the whole table as a sheet (Phone layout).

- **A rail** shows only what exists: the guild's icon and name; its master boxed, with the larger influence marker and stars (★N, R-4.5.1) and a ringed dot for a reeve (R-4.7), or "no master yet" beside an empty marker outline; then the lower places held, Journeyman then Apprentice (R-4.5.3), smaller; last, muted, the seats with stars and no place, in turn order. Empty lower places are not drawn, so early in the game a rail is its heading and one line; they appear as ranks fill.
- **The table** (the phone's sheet): one row per guild in ring order (Farmer, Merchant, Alchemist, Knight, Noble, Monk), headed by its die face with no badge, the guild's name in the row's accessible name; the track's places from the top, Master, Journeyman, then Apprentice at four or five seats only (at two or three the column is not there), the master's outlined, an empty place a dot; then Others.
- **Others:** a seat with stars and no place is "no marker" when it has no influence marker in supply (R-4.5.2), otherwise "outranked" (R-4.5.3, R-1.4.9).
- **Words.** Markers carry no letters, so every place has an accessible name: "Farmer master: Ana, 3 stars, reeve", "Farmer journeyman: vacant", "Farmer, no place: Bram, 1 star, outranked", "Farmer, no place: none"; an unheld master on a rail is named "Farmer master: vacant". A held place or an Other carries its words as its tooltip, and a press says them, at the rail's foot or in the line under the table's heading, for a screen with no hover; a second press on a rail's place clears it. The press changes nothing else.

### Cards, writs and tiles

- **Action card face:** while the seat shows one of the five printed colours and its file is present, the whole printed card in that colour, with the card's number on the border's foot and, at the compact size only, its short name again over the print's foot (at standard and full sizes the print names the card, and its accessible name does too); its back is the printed back in that colour, the same for every face. Otherwise the seat colour as a band round an art window, with the supplied art when its file is present; drawn, parchment with the card's name and number, its symbols in the top-right corner (x2, crossed x2, not-last, either/or, vp), the regular action in one line, the special ability's name with its need as an icon (a pawn character, a good, a barrel sulfur). The back is the seat colour with a crown.
- **Writ face:** a mandate shows its two alternatives as two rows, A and B, each with the goods it turns in boxed and its reward in tokens; mandates 6 and 8 show their three guild rewards each under its guild icon and "3★". A charter shows its name, its values, and for a kingdom charter a small map of the board with its qualifying spaces dotted. The back is a crowned writ.
- **Region tile:** front shows the region and the epoch numeral; back (as a landscape) shows the landscape kind (orchard, silver mine, archery butt, sulfur mine) and its resource.
- **Seal and bead:** a seal is a red disc with a crown, the same for everyone; a bead is a pale disc with its value "+3".

### Symbol key

The menus draw pieces in place of words, so the table carries its own key. A **Key** button stands beside the board's zoom controls, at the board's lower-left corner, on a desk and a phone alike, and in full screen. A press opens a compact card over that corner, inside the board's view, scrolling inside itself where the board is short: each guild's die face, the five goods, the shilling, the king's seal and sulfur in a barrel; the four location discs; the three vessels; a region tile, a writ, an influence marker, a player disc and a pawn in the viewer's colour (a spectator's in the first seat's); then the marks: a rose chip, "a cost or threat"; an amber chip, "yours to take"; and a die's badge, "rank it gives". Each piece stands beside its name. A second press or Escape closes it. The key is for looking: it offers no target and changes no offer.

### Supply

The general supply has no strip under the board. A count shows where it limits a choice: a tile's goods choice reads each good with the haversack's count ("iron 15"); Explore's primary reads "Explore: draw 3 of 16 writs", the deck counted; a mandate's vessel reward reads "Alchemist → ship · 1 left", or "none left" beside its refusal. For the whole picture a **Supply** button stands beside the Key button, at the board's lower-left corner, on a desk and a phone alike, and in full screen. A press opens a card titled "General supply" over that corner, inside the board's view, scrolling inside itself where the board is short. Its groups, each a labelled group: the writ deck (its count, the "seen" note, the viewer's own "you know 2 under the deck", and "Open the deck" for the deck's stack view where the table offers it); the haversack, by good; the king's seals; the vessels, by kind; the targets; the shillings, which run short (R-1.1-H1); the location discs; the influence markers, by seat in the seat's colour; the start player, the crown with the seat's disc and name. Each piece is drawn with its count and its name. Escape, a press on the card, or a press anywhere off it closes it; opening it closes the key, and the key closes it. It is for looking: counts only, it offers no target and changes no offer.

The zoom controls, Key and Supply make one row at the board view's lower-left corner, and the row stands under the board's frame, never over the score track. The board's view fits the board with an empty band under it, 128 board units tall and scaled with it, so the row has room at every fit: about 40 px at a phone's cover fit, more on a desk. At rest, and zoomed in and panned as far as the board's lower edge goes, no score-track space lies under the row. The Key and Supply cards still open over the board, above the row.

### Site card

A press on a location disc that nothing lights opens a card beside it (above it on a phone) listing everything on that site, the busiest sites included, each row its pieces and its words: the title ("Badlands 4 · farm"); the ruler, subject and serf by name ("Ruler: Ana"), each with its marker and an immunity seal where one lies; the pawns as their cubes, then one line by seat ("Ana: Knight, Monk · Bram: Farmer"); a monster and whose it is; the landscape; the goods by kind, each its token and its count; abandoned vessels. On a desk, a pointer over the disc shows the card while it stays there. A second press on the disc, its close button or Escape closes it; it gives way to a popup, an open seat, a stack view, a sheet, an enlarged piece and the bench, and opening any of those closes it. A lit disc keeps its press for the pick. It is for looking: it shows only what the table shows, offers no target and changes no offer.

## Visual intents

### Pick conventions

- **Lit** (amber ring): a target the deciding seat may press now. The rest of the map dims while a map pick is open. Panel-only steps never dim the map.
- **Picked** (heavier amber ring): a staged pick. Pressing it again unpicks it.
- **Halo.** Every ring, outline and chip is drawn over a dark halo, so a seat colour, a terrain or a red seal never stands in for it.
- **Chip:** a short note on a lit target: a cost ("1 wood", "2s"), a gain ("+3 vp"), a consequence ("starve", "pinned") or a role ("ruler", "off"). It is anchored on its target and never covers another lit target. Rose chips carry a cost or a threat and always carry a label and a halo; amber chips carry what is yours to take. A chip may be drawn as art: a cost in goods as the goods themselves, each with its count (Migrate's food: the food cube and "1"); an Improve's step as the disc the place shows, an arrow, the disc it becomes, then the good or seal it costs with its count. Its words stay the chip's accessible name ("farm to town · 1 iron"). Rose still means a cost.
- **Art first, words second.** Wherever a menu offers a good, a piece, a disc or a guild, the row shows the piece and only the words the art can't say (a count, a short caption). The full words stay its accessible name and tooltip ("Writs 1", "Town disc, back: farm"). When a pick lights places with chips, the tray's stage line says once what the chips show, so no chip needs its own words.
- **A guild choice is its die.** Wherever a seat picks a guild or a character, each choice is that guild's progress-die face: its one icon in ink on light wood, 72 px in the panel and 56 px in a popup on a desk, 50 px on a phone. When the pick gives a rank, a badge on the die's corner says which: M, J or A, or a dimmed dash for none (R-4.5). There are no rank words and no pawn on the die; the guild's name is a caption under it on a desk, and the press's accessible name and tooltip say both ("Farmer, journeyman"). A choice that gives no rank of its own (a feudum's guild) has no badge. A popup of dice stands them in rows of three. A picked die is ringed in the accent; a touch screen's "On board" preview stands beneath it.
- **Being decided** (neutral dashed outline, no dimming): what the deciding seat is choosing among, shown to every other seat and in History View, wherever it is public.
- **A lone legal choice** is auto-selected (`auto` stage). Undo never returns to it, and an auto selection never completes a send: the send is always a press.
- **Dimmed with a reason.** A card, function or button the engine refuses in the displayed state is dimmed; a press on it gives the reason in one line ("Conquer can't be your last card: forfeit it.", "No farm under 10 goods."). A dimmed map element does nothing.

### Confirming a send that cannot be undone

A press that completes an undoable Action sends at once. A step whose Action will set `revealsInfo` (R-X.2) stages instead: the panel shows one primary button naming the Action with "can't be undone". Those are: `FinaliseHand`; `Explore`; `Harvest`; an `Improve` that takes a tile (its landscape was hidden, R-1.4-H2); a `Conquer` or `StarveThePeople` that asks the target's owner; `AnswerAttack`; `AnswerStarve`; a `GuildAction` or `Feast` whose preview reaches a pull threshold, uses the hidden-bead draw (Monk trade, Farmer pull, Monk push), flips a seal (Monk pull, Noble push) or spills at random (a Farmer trade over the barn's limit with beads on the chickens); `KeepWrits` with Shovels & Swords; `PlaceDisc` onto voyage space 2, 4 or 6; a `PlayMandate` that draws goods or flips a bead. Which of these will reveal comes from the logic package's preview of the staged input, never from the UI's own reading of the rules (see [What the model supplies](#what-the-model-supplies)).

### Setup

- **`ChooseStartingGoods`** (R-1.4.8). The setup seat's panel shows the five goods with the haversack's count of each. Presses pick three different goods (tap list); a good the haversack lacks is dimmed. When sulfur is picked a popup beside it asks "To supply" or "To barrel". The primary button reads "Take saltpeter, sulfur (barrel), iron". Others see "‹Name› chooses starting goods" and the haversack counts.
- **`PlaceStartingPawn`** (R-1.4.9). The panel shows the six characters as guild dice, each badged with the rank it would give now (M, J, A, or a dash for none, from the guild tracks, R-4.5). A press picks a character; the six starting spaces light on the map. A press on a lit space sends. Picking the space first is equally allowed: either order, Undo steps back one press. Others see the starting spaces outlined as being decided.

### Hand selection (`SelectingHands`)

- **Drafting.** Every seat drafts at once, locally (R-3.1, R-3.1-H1). The panel has one heading and one caption, "Round 2 · choose your hand" and "everyone at once · pouch goods shown when all have finalised", with the seats at the line's end (see Finalising). Under them, the seat's eleven cards in a grid of two rows (six, five) and, right of the grid, **tray A**: one framed tray, its labels in one column and its rows in the other, every row on the slots' left edge. Its first row is the **plan**, labelled "Hand" over its count ("2 of 4", "5 of 5" with saltpeter): one numbered slot per card the hand needs (four, five with saltpeter). The slots have no caption; "play in any order" is in their accessible name ("Your hand · play in any order"). A press on a card fills the next slot; the card stays in the grid, dimmed, with its slot's number on it. A press on a filled slot, or on the dimmed card, takes the card back and the slots after it close up. Its second row, labelled "Pouch" over "one or none", is the **pouch** as two choices stacked, each as wide as the slots, each its good's art, a short name and what it does: "+1 card · opens a fifth slot", "2 in a row · once this round"; the full words ("Saltpeter: 5 cards", "Sulfur: two cards back to back") are each choice's name. Neither is chosen at first, and no pouch good is the default; a press chooses one in place of the other and rings it, and a second press on the chosen one clears it. One the seat's supply lacks is dimmed and says why in place of what it does ("You hold no sulfur.", "Barrel sulfur can't go in the pouch.", R-3.3), once; its press adds nothing. Its third row is the send (see Finalising), as wide as the slots, with the Conquer warning under it when there is one. Choosing saltpeter makes the count 5 and opens a fifth slot; dropping it with five cards picked leaves the hand at five, over, until one is dropped. The at-will chips stand under the eleven. On a desk the whole draft stays under about 350 px.
- **Conquer warning** (R-3.13-H1). A hand with Conquer where every other card could be played before it, so that it may end last, carries the line "Conquer may end up your last card; it would be forfeited." It is a warning, not a refusal.

### Move

The action area carries no line for the roll.
- **Dawn** (`BeginDawn`, `ReplenishMap`, `ReplenishGuilds`, R-4.12). The action area shows the **dawn sheet** to everyone while the dawn's actions play: one row per seat with guild ranks, landscapes, active regions and disloyalty (in rose), and the total; then the landscape fills, the map's new goods and (III) the guild refill. It stays, under the next decision, until the first press of that decision that is not the landscapes' own; a writ discarded at any time (R-4.4-H5) is no such press.
- **`CollectLandscapes`** (`CollectingLandscapes`, R-4.3.4, R-4.12.3). The asked serf's tended landscapes with resources light, each with "collect 3 · 1 to the ruler". Presses pick (tap list); the primary reads "Collect 2" or "Leave all". Others see the order row.
- **End of round** (`EndRound`, R-2.2.5). The start-player crown passes, the cards return to the seat panels, spent pouch goods leave. The next hand selection opens. After the V dawn there is no end of round: final scoring takes its place (R-6.1-H2), so the crown, the pouches and the played cards stay where they are.

### Game end

The game-end summary replaces the action area: one column per seat with what it scored before the V dawn, the V dawn, epic voyage by section, large empires (with sets), shillings, each sealed charter now face up with its score beneath it as "5 vp", the total, and the winner with the barrel tie-break when the vp were tied (R-6.2, R-6.3), from `ScoreGame`'s metadata. The tie is called broken only when the barrel left fewer winners than were tied; level barrels read "Tie on vp and on barrel sulfur: … · the win is shared", and a barrel that drops some but leaves two or more adds "still tied, the win is shared". The History's ScoreGame line words it the same way.

### Watching (waiting view)

The waiting view always says who decides and what, then the live state of a shared step, then what the previous seat just did, from the Action's input and `metadata`; a writ another seat than the turn's discards at any time (R-4.4-H5) is not that, while the turn player's own discard, down to the writ limit or not, is. Its rows: the finalise row, the combat tally (attack and defence; who answers is the waiting line's to say), the nourish row, the landscapes row and the dawn sheet. They are the same rows the deciding seat sees above its panel. A seat's own draft of its hand stays in the action area while it waits in `SelectingHands`; the waiting line sits above it.

### Hidden information, as others see it

Hidden things are drawn by rule, never because the client holds the data. A Local game or Host View holds every field; the perspective decides.

- **Hand** (R-3.1, R-5.1): other seats see backs and the count; nothing shows which cards are set aside. A drafted hand is never shown before it is played, and finalising shows only the pouch good.
- **Writs** (R-4.4.4): others see backs and counts; sealed charters as sealed backs. Enlarged, a back stays a back.
- **A writ draw**: others see the drawn count as backs and how many are kept; the writs put under are never shown to others.
- **Deck knowledge**: the owner's note on the deck in the Supply card and as the writs' tooltip in the seat panel; nothing for others.
- **Seals** (R-5-H1): every seal red side up is the same crowned disc for everyone, its holder included. A flip to a bead shows its value to all.
- **Asks**: whether a seat holds Defend is never shown, and no prompt, button, delay or line on another seat's screen depends on it. The asked seat is never auto-answered.

## Coexistence and precedence

One semantic derivation in the session, the **board offer**, decides each board element's role: lit, picked, being decided, dimmed or plain, and its chip. Every board layer draws that result and nothing else. The bench reads one derivation of its own, the **bench offer**, from the same stages.

- **One staged action at a time.** The staged card or at-will action decides which layer lights targets. The at-will row and a staged card never coexist.
- **Lit and being decided never meet.** The board offer shows lit targets only to the deciding seat and the outline only to others. In `SelectingHands` every seat drafts, but drafting lights nothing on the board.
- **Chips.** One chip per target. A cost or threat (rose) wins over a role, which wins over a gain.
- **Dimming** belongs to an open map pick. Panel-only steps and the bench never dim the map; the bench covers it.
- **A popup** (a Move way, a character, a guild function, a feudum's guild, a harvest mode) sits beside its anchor and over the board. The board under it keeps its offer; a press outside it does nothing and closes nothing. Undo removes it.
- **The bench and the map.** The bench is open only from a staged guild function until its send or Undo. A Knight trade's tame and a Farmer trade's farm are map picks; for those the bench closes to a slim header ("Knight trade: tame the behemoth") over the board, and Undo reopens it.
- **What is open wins over what is under it.** The enlarged card sits above the open seat, the bench and the stack view, which sit above the table. Escape closes one layer at a time: the enlarged card, then the open seat or stack view, then a site card, then full screen. Escape never closes the bench; Undo does.
- **History View offers no choices.** No panel, no lit target, no bench and no send. The being-decided outline, enlarge, open seat and focus keep working on the displayed state.
- **Undo** (`docs/user-interactions.md`): it pops the highest manual stage first (a Move step, a picked good, a popup's choice, the card), then undoes the Undo Candidate. It is hidden when neither exists, so it is hidden right after a barrier.
- **Hover** on a panel row brightens the board elements it names, only those its press would light. Under an open pick a dimmed element stays dimmed. Touch has no hover, and no information depends on it.
- **Asks interrupt nothing local.** When another seat's Conquer asks this seat, any local draft this seat holds belongs to no open decision and is cleared with the new state; the ask's panel replaces the waiting view.

## Shared visual state

The session owns these values. Components read them and call its methods. Every draft is read back only while it still applies, so a stale value offers nothing.

- **Hand draft** (`SelectingHands`). One stacked stage: the cards (a tap list) and the pouch good as each press left them, so Undo removes the last press of either kind. Producer: card and pouch presses. Consumers: the panel, the finalise button. Lifetime: the seat's `FinaliseHand`; Undo. It **survives** `other-action` and `reconciliation` transitions, keyed by the round, so another seat's finalise never clears it and a kept draft is never submitted in another round. It is cleared on undo and history transitions.
- **Turn draft** (`PlayingCards` and its follow-ups). Stages: card → mode (regular, special, forfeit; the two-cards toggle) → the card's targets and popups → cover (a tile for a short payment). Producer: hand, mode and button presses, lit-element presses, popups. Consumers: the panel, the board offer, the bench offer, the send. Lifetime: the send; Undo; every new displayed state. Validity: each stage must still be legal in the displayed state, or it offers nothing.
- **Move draft** (a Move card once the turn draft hands it off). Its own draft, registered ahead of the turn draft so Undo pops it first; a card, replay or mode press ends it. Stacked steps: each press pushes one step (piece, to, way), and Undo pops the last. Then Godspeed and pilfer as plain stages.
- **Bench draft.** Picked items (a tap list), the count toggle, a popup's choice. Lifetime: as the turn draft.
- **Follow-up drafts** (kept writs, favours, saltpeter and weapons, nourish choices, landscapes): each read back only in its own state and only for its named decider.
- **Board offer** and **bench offer.** Derived, never stored, from the displayed state, the valid actions and the stages. Effects: ring, chip, outline, dimming, ghosts and trails. Empty in History View except for the being-decided outline, and empty while `updatingVisibleState` is true.
- **Menu pointer.** Producer: a panel row's hover or keyboard focus. Consumers: board layers. Effect: the bright ring on what the row names. Lifetime: the row's leave, blur or unmount.
- **Card preview.** Producer: a press on a card, writ, tile or bead anywhere, on a guild area on the board while it is not lit, or a magnifier. Consumer: the preview layer, a guild area upright as the trade picker shows it, turned a quarter clockwise, 640 px tall by 453 px wide (on a phone, the phone's width less its margin wide), the others at 420 px wide (the phone's width less its margin). Nothing inside the enlarged piece answers the pointer, so no part's tooltip (the guild panel's name, a bead's) stands over it. A face-up card, writ or guild area reads a short note of its rules beside it (under it on a phone): the action and the special ability, a mandate's reward or a charter's scoring, a guild's trade, pull and push. The notes are this game's own words from the rules digest and the rulings, one per id in `rulesText.ts`; a back, a tile and a bead have none. The note is for reading, and a press on it closes nothing. It reads its owner's current props, so it shows a back where the owner shows a back. Lifetime: the next press (on the enlarged piece, which is a button named Close, or beside it), Escape, or the owner unmounting. It has no close button of its own.
- **Site card.** Producer: a press on an unlit location disc, or a desk pointer over one. Consumer: the site card layer. Lifetime: a second press, the close button, Escape, the pointer leaving (for the hovered card), or any other layer opening; a pressed card wins over a hovered one. It shows the displayed state, History View included.
- **Open seat** and **stack view** (a region stack's tiles by epoch, or the deck's seen writs). Producer: a ribbon, a seat chip, a stack or the deck. Lifetime: the backdrop, Escape, the close button. They show the displayed state, History View included.
- **Board focus.** Producer: the focus chooser, or a map pick opening on a phone. Consumer: the scaling wrapper. A pan or zoom by hand clears it. It never changes an offer.
- **In-flight pieces.** Producer: the state-change animators. Consumer: the flight layer. Lifetime: the running timeline; cleared on teardown and interruption. Never read for game meaning.

Silent restoration replaces the displayed state; every derived offer follows it without motion.

## Render ownership

- **Board surface.** One SVG, scaled by the shared scaling wrapper (`expandable`, `coverBelowScale`) with the guild standings' rails in the same row, so they scale together (Guild standings). Layers in order: ground (terrain, frame, the epoch sky); routes; locations (ruled rings, discs, letters, start rings); space contents (landscapes, goods, abandoned vessels, markers, immunity seals); pieces (pawns with vessels, monsters); guild areas (each owns its grid and its track); tracks (stacks and chart, die, voyage, military, barrels, score ring); motion (a running animator's tokens, none at rest, with a seal's token under the markers). Each layer owns the pick chrome of what it draws and dims its own unlit elements from the board offer. There is no global scrim.
- **Pieces on the board** sit in a centred outer wrapper for motion, with world placement on a separate node, so GSAP owns the wrapper's transform.
- **Moves within the board** (a pawn's path, goods from one guild area to another, a tile from a stack to the map, a marker from a track to a space) stay inside the board SVG and its coordinates, so they scale with it.
- **Flight layer.** A good, a writ, a tile or a marker moving between a seat panel (or seat chip) and the board crosses the scaling wrapper. One flight layer, mounted above the table layout and outside the wrapper, and inside the full-screen dialog while it is open, draws the moving copy from the two elements' screen rectangles. While it flies the source is hidden and the target not yet shown.
- **The bench** is drawn over the board's container, outside the scaled SVG, at a scale fixed for legibility. It draws the guild areas with the same components the board uses. While it is open the board's own guild areas keep their state and take no presses. Where the art is not supplied a Knight row is drawn as a short, wide band, so its press reaches 44 px down the same way, between the rows above and below it (S68).
- **Seat panels** sit outside the wrapper, in the Players tab on a desktop only; on a phone the seat strip stands in for them. The open seat and the enlarged card are mounted after the table layout and pad themselves by the safe-area insets.
- **Full screen** is the wrapper's modal dialog. While it is open the action panel is drawn in the wrapper's toolbar, and the bench, the popups and the enlarged card inside the dialog. Each is drawn in one place at a time.
- **Hidden things** are drawn by one card component, one writ component and one seal component, each taking a `faceShown` decision from the perspective rule, not from data presence.
- **The finalise, nourish and landscapes rows and the combat tally** are each drawn by one component, read from `flow` in the displayed state, in the panel and the waiting view alike. **The dawn sheet** is drawn by one component too; `flow` holds no dawn, so it reads the dawn's Action on the displayed state.

## Game log and history

Built from each Action's input and `metadata` only (`docs/DESIGN.md`, Actions). A seat's line is past tense after its name ("You moved", "Ana moved"). Space names read as region and number ("Desert 3").

- **Lines, by Action:**
    - `ChooseStartingGoods`, `PlaceStartingPawn`: the goods and barrel choice; the character, space and rank taken.
    - `FinaliseHand`: "Ana finalised · pouch sulfur". The cards are named in the actor's own log only.
    - `RevealPouches`: one line for every pouch good, each with its seat and its short name, then the seats without one: "All finalised · pouch goods revealed: Ana sulfur 2 in a row · Bram, Cleo: none"; "All finalised · no pouch goods" when no seat committed one (R-3.1-H1). The seats are the game's, fixed when it is made, so the line reads nothing of the state.
    - `Migrate`: the character and space, or "off"; Distant Kin.
    - `Move`: each piece's path with its ways ("monk Forest 2 → Island 1 by ferry (2s) → Island 2 by road"), vessels boarded, placed or left, Godspeed, the pilfer and where it came from, and the rank change when a pilfered target gives a waiting seat its marker.
    - `Influence`: markers placed and each space's new ruler, serf or subject; Money is Influence.
    - `Improve`, `TendLandscape`: the step, the disc and feudum guild, the tile taken and points, or the fallback tile, or why none was taken ("Forest tile not yet legal, none taken"; "no tile left" only when the region's stack is empty, R-4.3.2); the landscape and whether collected.
    - `Explore`, `KeepWrits`: "drew 3, kept 1, 2 under the deck"; the actor's own log names them. Shovels & Swords.
    - `Harvest`, `ChooseFavours`: the goods drawn, by kind, and the favours.
    - `Tax`: shillings, and the Reinforcements marker or "declined Reinforcements".
    - `Conquer`, `AnswerAttack`, `CommitAttack`: the target, "Ben flipped Defend" or "Ben let it stand", saltpeter and weapons, the strengths, the result, points and markers returned.
    - `StarveThePeople`, `AnswerStarve`: the marker, Royal Immunity or not, chicken beads returned.
    - `GuildAction`, `Feast`: the guild and function, items moved with their places (a krud barrel's markers by row, and no barrels clause when it added none, R-4.6-H8), completions, points, the payment split, reeves, rank changes, the writ count drawn, a drawn bead's value, a flipped seal's value, spilt goods.
    - `PlaceDisc`: reeve, service or voyage, and where.
    - `PlayMandate`, `DiscardWrit`, `SpendRegionTile`, `PlaceVessel`, `ReclaimImmunitySeal`: the writ named (a discard is named in the owner's log only), the good, the vessel.
    - `Nourish` and `NourishPawns`: food and wine per seat; pawns unfed and returned; the rank changes once, on the one that ends nourishing (R-4.5-H1).
    - `RollProgressDie`: every face in order, the tile removed and the epoch that dawned; the action area does not repeat it.
    - `BeginDawn`, `ReplenishMap`, `ReplenishGuilds`, `CollectLandscapes`: the dawn sheet's rows, each total signed as it falls (+5 vp, -6 vp); goods placed; beads flipped.
    - `EndRound`: pouch goods paid and the start player passed, folded into the round's last entry.
    - `ScoreGame`: the summary's rows.
- **Rank changes** are named on the Action that caused them ("Ana became Merchant master; Ben dropped to journeyman").
- **Grouping:** one entry per hand selection (a line per finaliser); one entry per turn, growing as its card, a declared second card, follow-ups, asks and at-will Actions arrive, while a writ another seat than the turn's discards at any time (R-4.4-H5) is its own entry, between turns or in the middle of one, and the turn's entry goes on growing after it; one entry for nourishing; one for the progress roll; one per dawn. Bookkeeping System Actions (`StartRound`, `AdvanceTurn`) are skipped.
- **Interstitials:** epoch (from the epoch field) and round (from the round manager).
- **Jump** on a round, a turn and a dawn: a round shows the table as it began, a turn and a dawn as they ended. **Replay** on entries that move things on the board, over the entry's whole span: turns with Migrate, Move, Influence, Improve, Tend, Harvest, Conquer, Starve, a guild function or a feast; the progress roll; nourishing with unfed pawns; dawns.

- **The supply's corner.** The general supply is drawn only in the Supply card, so a piece that leaves or reaches it (a writ from or to the deck, a seal from or to the pile, a good or a coin from or to the haversack) flies from or to one point inside the board at its lower-left corner (`SupplyCorner`), under the Supply button when the whole board is in view.
- System Actions that follow one user Action play in sequence on one timeline, each tween at an explicit position. Example: a `Move` lands, `AdvanceTurn` passes, the next turn's pulse begins.
- Interaction is gated while `updatingVisibleState` is true.
- Replay plays the entry's whole span, contiguous System Actions included.
- **Fast fallback.** Without an action every context-owned path is bounded at or below 200 ms: pieces and tokens interpolate straight to their new places, flips and fades are cut to that bound.
- **Reduced motion.** With reduced motion every animator follows each step with no tween; the order of the steps and their history lines are unchanged.

- **Board.** The board is about 2.6:1, with nothing under it. Fitted to 375 px it would be about 145 px tall and its spaces too small to tap. It rests at a cover fit, the board and the zoom row's band under it filling the view's height, and opens on the map.
- **Focus chooser.** Four chips over the board's top edge: Map, West guilds, East guilds, Tracks. This departs from Oath, which has no chooser on a phone, because Feudum players look at every area every turn. - **Tracks sheet.** The tracks lie scattered round the printed board, and framing them all leaves them too small to read on a phone. The Tracks chip instead opens a bottom sheet, up to 80 % of the dynamic viewport, that redraws them at full size in one column: epoch and round, the region tile stacks and chart, the progress die, the krud barrels, the voyage track, military service and the score track. The sheet is for looking: it offers no target, and a pick that lights a track (a voyage disc, a service disc) frames that track on the board instead.

## What the model supplies

- **Offers and previews come from the rules.** The logic package exposes, for a staged input, the legal targets of the next stage (pieces, ways, spaces, guild functions, bench items, writs, covers) and a preview of the outcome: points, completions and thresholds, the payment split, reeve points, combat strengths, harvest totals, the writ count an Explore draws, and whether the send will set `revealsInfo`. The UI draws these and re-derives no rule.
- **History lines read `metadata`.** Each Action records the values its line names that the input does not carry: Move's ways, vessels and pilfer source; role changes from Influence; the disc and tile taken; goods drawn; payments split; rank changes; combat strengths and result; bead and seal values; the die's faces and the tile removed; each dawn row; nourish results; pouch goods paid; the final breakdown with charters revealed.
