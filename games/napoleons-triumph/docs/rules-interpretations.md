# Napoleon's Triumph rule interpretations

The implementation follows the Simmons Games rulebook (2007) with its three official errata, read in the light of the designer's and the experienced players' answers in the BoardGameGeek rules forum. This file records the reconstructed data, the rulings chosen where the rulebook leaves a question open, and what is not yet implemented. The worked examples of rule 11 are covered by `src/model/attackFlow.spec.ts`; movement and set-up by `src/model/movement.spec.ts` and `src/definition/playthrough.spec.ts`; secrecy by `src/definition/visibility.spec.ts`.

## Reconstructed data

- **Map.** `src/components/austerlitzData.ts` is generated by `scripts/build-map-data.py` from a transcription of the board: 168 locales (printed numbers 1 to 170; 145 and 146 do not exist), 281 passable approaches and 12 impassable ones, capacities, hills, objective stars, set-up locales, 20 entry roads and 279 road crossings. `src/components/austerlitz.spec.ts` pins the counts.
- **Checked against the designer's file.** The transcription was first made from a scan of the published board and then checked against the vector map file the designer released into the public domain in 2026, which carries a layer of locale numbers and stores every approach as a symbol with its penalty marks. Locale numbers, the width and penalty marks of all 281 passable approaches, all capacities, hills, set-up locales and the main road agree with it. It corrected four things: Welspitz (88) carries a green, a red and a black star; a French entry road at Ottmarau (139) had been missed; about twenty borders are crossed by local roads the scan trace had missed; and two pairs of locales taken for impassable neighbours are not neighbours (next item).
- **Approach terrain.** A symbol belongs to the locale it is printed in and penalises attacks made into that locale.
- **Impassable approaches and rivers.** Locales are adjacent when they lie on opposite sides of the same approach. Twelve approaches between two locales are impassable. Along the streams and ponds each bank has its own impassable approach with marsh or water between; locales that face each other across such water (114 and 115, 124 and 151, 151 and 159 and others) share no approach and are not adjacent, which matters only for the rule that halts a corps marching by road.
- **Roads.** A road joins two locales where it crosses the side they share. Road stretches inside a locale that do not touch are different roads, so a road move cannot change from one to the other there (rule 10, "connected roads"). Which borders a road crosses comes from the vector roads of the public file. Stretches count as connected when they touch in the vector file or were judged connected on the scan, where junctions sitting exactly on a border had been reviewed by eye; roads that run out of sight under the printed tracks connect nothing.
- **Order of battle.** 36 French and 44 Allied units with the printed corps minimums; Bernadotte may enter from 11:00AM on 1 December and Davout from 8:00AM on 2 December.

## Rulings

- **Errata.** A move by road may pass through a locale too small to hold it but never through a full one. Two artillery Unit Moves combine only when both batteries lead the attack. A locale with three stars counts for all three colours.
- **Hidden blocks.** A unit's face is known to its owner. A face the enemy has seen stays visible until the end of that player turn; then the faces of units standing together in the same position and corps are shuffled among them, so the enemy cannot follow a block it once saw. A unit standing alone stays known. The fixed battery is always face-up.
- **Defence declaration.** The defender names the defending pieces and, in the same decision, the leading units. The leading units stay hidden from the attacker and are shown only if the attack is pressed, which is what steps 2 and 4 of rule 11 amount to; it saves a hand-off between the players in the middle of every attack.
- **Threats.** A threat names only the attack approach. It is legal when some piece could still make an attack across it, cavalry riding up a road included. The attacker picks the command, the pieces, the width and the leading units after the defender has answered.
- **Attack by road.** Only cavalry may attack by road. If the defender stands the attack must be a feint; if the defender gives ground the cavalry moves in, and the vacated locale counts as a locale entered. Cavalry that attacks or feints by road is turned face-up.
- **Zero leading units.** Either side may name none. With no attack leader the defender loses nothing; with no leader on either side nobody loses anything. A Guard Attack must name a Guard leading unit.
- **Result 0.** The attacker wins a tie against reserve defenders when it attacks with more units than were named to defend, the French winning an equal count; blocking defenders win every tie. Commanders are not counted.
- **Approach penalties.** The infantry −1 and the printed penalties apply only against defenders blocking the approach, once per attack, and not at all when the attacker names no leading unit. An obstructed approach is never a −1: it stops cavalry of both sides from leading or counter-attacking.
- **Artillery.** A battery leads only from the attack approach, by Unit Move or Detach Move. An artillery-led attack moves nobody, costs the attacker nothing and cannot be counter-attacked; its losses cannot take an army's morale below 1. The same approach may not be bombarded in consecutive rounds unless the guns fire down from a hill. One further attack, not led by artillery, may cross the approach afterwards in the same turn.
- **Losses.** Combat losses fall only on the pieces named in the attack; excess is ignored. A unit that takes a loss is shown. Guard infantry that takes a loss becomes ordinary infantry. Morale falls by the strength the loser of the attack actually lost, counter-attack losses included only when the defender loses.
- **Defeated attackers.** The attacking pieces fall back where they came from and every attacking corps keeps one unit, of its owner's choice; the rest are detached.
- **Advance after a feint or a held defence.** At least one named defender in reserve steps up to block the approach. The fixed battery never advances.
- **Retreats.** Every piece in the locale retreats and is shown. Artillery is eliminated. Reserve infantry not named in the attack loses one step between them, two across a wide approach; the foot and horse blocking each other approach lose one or two by that approach's width. A loss is capped at the strength present. Each corps keeps one unit and the others are detached; units go to any adjacent locales with room that are not enemy-occupied and not the attack locale, and units with nowhere to go are eliminated. A retreat before combat is a retreat in full.
- **Elite commitment.** Heavy cavalry and the Guard cost their army morale the first time one of them leads, counter-attacks, retreats or takes a loss. Showing a Guard unit to announce a Guard Attack does not by itself commit the Guard. Commitment cannot take morale below 1; a defeated Guard Attack costs three more and can.
- **Stop rule.** An enemy corps of two or more units in any locale sharing an approach with the locale entered, impassable approaches included, stops a corps of two or more units moving by road.
- **Objectives.** A locale is held by a corps with at least one infantry or artillery unit that can trace connected road back to a main-road entry of its own side; any enemy piece in the entry locale or on the way breaks the path.
- **Night.** At nightfall each army regains half the morale it has lost, to a limit of four. There are no attacks in the night round.
- **The Santon (optional).** While the fixed battery stands in the Santon's reserve, French pieces defending from that reserve get the printed approach penalties and nothing else: no infantry −1 and no automatic tie.
- **Morale auction (optional).** Players bid morale in turn; a pass gives the other player the choice of army, and the bid comes off the starting morale of the army chosen.

## Not yet implemented

- **Continuing a cavalry road move.** After a retreat before combat lets road cavalry into the defence locale, the rules let it ride on within its allowance and threaten again. Here its move ends in the locale it took.
- **The 2007 special case for a cavalry corps of two or more units threatening an enemy corps of two or more units by road.** The stop rule is applied as written; the contested exceptions are not modelled.
