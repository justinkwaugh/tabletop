# Napoleon's Triumph rule interpretations

The implementation follows the Simmons Games rulebook (2007) with its three official errata, read in the light of the designer's and the experienced players' answers in the BoardGameGeek rules forum. It was also checked, section by section, against the community's Annotated Rules v1.4 (David Ells and Rick Mathews), which print the rulebook verbatim with clarifications; where those annotations and the rulings below part ways, the entry says so. This file records the reconstructed data, the rulings chosen where the rulebook leaves a question open, and what is not yet implemented. The worked examples of rule 11 are covered by `src/model/attack.spec.ts`; attacks by road and the fixed battery by `src/model/roadAttack.spec.ts`; command limits, the forced advance, attaching and the auction by `src/model/commands.spec.ts`; movement and set-up by `src/model/movement.spec.ts`, `src/model/reinforcementRoads.spec.ts`, `src/model/setup.spec.ts` and `src/definition/playthrough.spec.ts`; secrecy by `src/definition/visibility.spec.ts`.

## Reconstructed data

- **Map.** `src/components/austerlitzData.ts` is generated by `scripts/build-map-data.py` from a transcription of the board: 168 locales (printed numbers 1 to 170; 145 and 146 do not exist), 281 passable approaches and 12 impassable ones, capacities, hills, objective stars, set-up locales, 20 entry roads and 281 road crossings. `src/components/austerlitz.spec.ts` pins the counts.
- **Checked against the designer's file.** The transcription was first made from a scan of the published board and then checked against the vector map file the designer released into the public domain in 2026, which carries a layer of locale numbers and stores every approach as a symbol with its penalty marks. Locale numbers, the width and penalty marks of all 281 passable approaches, all capacities, hills, set-up locales and the main road agree with it. It corrected four things: Welspitz (88) carries a green, a red and a black star; a French entry road at Ottmarau (139) had been missed; about twenty borders are crossed by local roads the scan trace had missed; and two pairs of locales taken for impassable neighbours are not neighbours (next item).
- **Approach terrain.** A symbol belongs to the locale it is printed in and penalises attacks made into that locale.
- **Impassable approaches and rivers.** Locales are adjacent when they lie on opposite sides of the same approach. Twelve approaches between two locales are impassable. Along the streams and ponds each bank has its own impassable approach with marsh or water between; locales that face each other across such water (114 and 115, 124 and 151, 151 and 159 and others) share no approach and are not adjacent, which matters only for the rule that halts a corps marching by road.
- **Roads.** A road joins two locales where it crosses the side they share. Road stretches inside a locale that do not touch are different roads, so a road move cannot change from one to the other there (rule 10, "connected roads"). Which borders a road crosses comes from the vector roads of the public file. Stretches count as connected when they touch in the vector file or were judged connected on the scan, where junctions sitting exactly on a border had been reviewed by eye; roads that run out of sight under the printed tracks connect nothing. Two junctions the scan review had taken to sit on a border lie just beyond it in the vector file, so the roads they join are separate roads on the near side: in Pratze Berg (106) the road from 108 and 123 and the road that cuts its corner from 94 meet only in 121, and in locale 90 the roads from 101 and from 80 cross only in 102. No road therefore takes a piece from 123 through 106 to 94. The two French entry roads at locale 140 come out from under the Allied command track separately, one running on to 131 and one to 147, and are two roads.
- **Order of battle.** 36 French and 44 Allied units with the printed corps minimums; Bernadotte may enter from 11:00AM on 1 December and Davout from 8:00AM on 2 December.

## Rulings

- **Errata.** A move by road may pass through a locale too small to hold it but never through a full one. Two artillery Unit Moves combine only when both batteries lead the attack, and never so as to take the last units from a commander. A locale with three stars counts for all three colours.
- **Hidden blocks.** A unit's face is known to its owner. A face the enemy has seen stays visible until the end of that player turn; then the faces of units standing together in the same position and corps are shuffled among them, so the enemy cannot follow a block it once saw. A unit standing alone stays known. The fixed battery is always face-up. The rulebook has units shown during an attack or a retreat turned face-down again as soon as it is over (rule 11, step 11), and the annotated rules repeat that. Keeping them visible until the shuffle shows the opponent nothing a player with a good memory would not have, since a physical block can be followed by eye until it is shuffled; it is a deliberate departure from the letter of the rule.
- **Taking an action back.** An action that shows its player an enemy block they could not see cannot be undone: pressing an attack against leading units, or an attack or counter-attack whose losses fall on a hidden block. Nor can the choice of army after the morale auction, because the armies are dealt the same way whichever is chosen.
- **Defence declaration.** The defender names the defending pieces first (rule 11, step 2) and the leading units only after the attack is pressed (step 4), as two decisions. Which pieces defend is told to the attacker at once, without their faces; a leading unit is turned face-up when it is named, and naming none shows nothing. Pieces blocking the defense approach all defend, so the game names them. A unit named in a defence may, for the rest of that turn, defend only that approach again; that it was named stays known to both players until the turn ends.
- **Threats name only the approach.** The attacker picks the command, the pieces, the width and the leading units after the defender has answered. The game keeps, for the attacker alone, the command and pieces he had in hand when he threatened, as where his declaration starts from; it is never shown to the defender and binds nothing.
- **Commands spent on an attack.** Every command that takes part in an attack is counted when the attack or feint is declared: two Corps Moves combined in one attack take two of the Allies' five corps commands, and two batteries firing together take two independent commands. An attack that would overspend either limit cannot be declared.
- **Attack by road.** Only cavalry may attack by road, by one Corps Move or one Unit Move, from reserve along a road that goes on across the attack approach, and only with road movement left to enter the defense locale. Cavalry already in the attack reserve chooses at its declaration between an ordinary attack and a road attack. One cavalry unit of a larger corps makes a road attack by Unit Move, leaving its corps and its commander behind; it goes by Corps Move, taking the commander along and detaching the rest, only when the player asks for that, or when it is the whole corps. If the defender stands a road attack must be a feint and ends in the attack locale, so a threat that only cavalry by road could carry out cannot be pressed; if the defender retreats the cavalry moves in, and the vacated locale counts as a locale entered. No road attack may cross an approach across which an attack was turned back earlier in the turn. Cavalry reinforcements may threaten and attack this way as they come on, riding in from their entry road, but not before the round the Time Track gives their corps. From a locale on the map there is never more than one road by which to attack another locale by road (`src/components/austerlitz.spec.ts` checks every one), so the road is not asked for. Only reinforcements coming on can have two, where two entry roads meet in the entry locale; one of them then leaves open every road beyond that the other does, and the game takes that one.
- **Riding on.** Cavalry that took a locale by road may at once, under the same command, ride on along its road, end blocking an approach its road crosses, or threaten again across such an approach; each new threat runs the attack procedure afresh, and the attacker may instead carry it out with other pieces by a new command. Its move stops when its road allowance is used up, or when a corps of two or more units has had to halt beside an enemy corps: it then enters no further locale and makes no further threat, but following the designer's ruling it may still end blocking an approach of the locale it stands in that its road crosses. Giving any other command ends the road move. Following the designer's ruling the cavalry is turned face-up only when its road move is over, so a defender facing a second threat has not seen it.
- **Riding on and the second command of a reinforcement.** French reinforcement cavalry riding on in its turn of entry still has its second command (rule 10), and may take it at any point in place of riding on: to move, on foot or by road with a fresh allowance of road, or to attack. The new command ends the ride and the cavalry is shown. It is the only way such cavalry presses an attack, since an attack by road must be a feint: when the defender stands it may feint riding on, at no command, or press by its second command as an ordinary attack from the reserve it stands in. When the defender retreats it follows up riding on, or by its second command, on foot or as a new road attack. Its threat records, for the attacker alone, which of the two it had in mind; that binds nothing.
- **Cavalry corps by road.** A corps of two or more units that would have to halt in the attack locale, because an enemy corps of two or more units is adjacent to it, cannot attack from there, not even to feint; a single unit can, as a Unit Move or as the one unit its commander takes along. Once a retreat has broken the enemy corps up, the whole cavalry corps may ride in.
- **Threats.** A threat must be one the attacker could carry out whether the defender stands or retreats: by a piece in the attack locale that can still be commanded, by cavalry riding on, or by cavalry that can ride up a road, stop in the attack locale and go on across the approach. Pressing an attack needs the same: the attacker may press only if a piece in the attack locale could attack, which for the fixed battery means it may fire across that approach now. Otherwise, as with a threat made by cavalry up a road, the attack can only be a feint.
- **Naming the fixed battery.** The French player names any battery on the map and chooses which approach of its set-up locale it blocks (rule 6, step 9). The French always have one: a set-up that keeps every artillery unit off the map with Bernadotte and Davout is not accepted. Step 9 repositions the battery "if it is in reserve", so putting it on an approach is free only from reserve; a battery whose corps already blocks an approach either stands with the corps there or goes to another approach as one of the six detachments, as does a battery detached to another locale. The player always chooses the battery's approach: a battery put in its corps's locale is not taken to stand on the approach the corps happens to block. With the Santon rule in use the fixed battery must set up on the Santon, in reserve, and no other set-up is accepted. This is a table ruling: rule 18 only says what follows if the battery sets up there. No corps sets up on the Santon, so the battery is detached to it from a corps within two locales and counts as one of the six detachments. The French batteries are all alike, so the set-up table starts with one of them already on the Santon.
- **Which commander a detached unit comes from.** Rule 6 has the French assign every unit to a commander (step 7) and then detach up to six (step 8), so a detached unit counts towards the minimum and the eight of the commander it left, and has to stand within two locales of him. Nothing after set-up depends on which commander that was. The player therefore arranges only where units end up and is never shown a detached unit as part of a corps. The game takes a detached unit as coming from the nearest commander on the map that can still count it, or from another within reach when that is what lets every corps meet its minimum. A set-up is accepted when some such reading satisfies steps 6 to 9, which is exactly when the player could have made it by assigning and then detaching: so a corps may stand below its printed minimum when detached units within two locales make up the difference, and a commander with detached units that can only be his may lead fewer than eight. The engine's `DeployArmy` still records the assignment and the detachments separately.
- **The fixed battery.** It takes commands to fire or to feint where it stands and never changes position: it cannot be an attacking piece unless its fire leads the attack, it holds its ground after a retreat before combat only if it could have fired, and it never advances. It can be attached to a corps like any other unit, which changes who commands its fire and nothing else; as the last unit of a corps it can be given no command at all.
- **Zero leading units.** Either side may name none. With no attack leader the defender loses nothing; with no leader on either side nobody loses anything. A Guard Attack must name a Guard leading unit.
- **Pairing leading units.** Two units lead an attack together only if they are of one type; two units in reserve lead a defence, and two units counter-attack, only if they are of one type and one corps. Guard infantry pairs with line infantry to lead a defence but not to attack or counter-attack.
- **Result 0.** The attacker wins a tie against reserve defenders when it attacks with more units than were named to defend, the French winning an equal count; blocking defenders win every tie. Commanders are not counted.
- **Approach penalties.** The infantry −1 and the printed penalties apply only against defenders blocking the approach, once per attack, and not at all when the attacker names no leading unit. An obstructed approach is never a −1: it stops cavalry of both sides from leading or counter-attacking.
- **Artillery.** A battery leads only from the attack approach, by Unit Move or Detach Move. An artillery-led attack moves nobody, costs the attacker nothing and cannot be counter-attacked; its losses cannot take an army's morale below 1. The same approach may not be bombarded in consecutive rounds unless the guns fire down from a hill. One further attack, not led by artillery, may cross the approach afterwards in the same turn. After a retreat before combat a battery stays in place only if it could have led the attack, which rules out artillery on the Santon other than the fixed battery (rule 18). Artillery fire that scores nothing against a blocked approach still leaves the approach open to a second attack that turn, as the annotated rules' flow chart has it, although the text also bars any further attack after "a loss for the attacker".
- **Losses.** Combat losses fall only on the pieces named in the attack; excess is ignored. A unit that takes a loss is shown. An odd loss between two leading units is placed by the other player (rule 11, step 9); the game places it itself only when the two have the same face and stand in the same corps, or are both detached, so that nothing distinguishes them. Guard infantry that takes a loss becomes ordinary infantry. Morale falls by the strength the loser of the attack actually lost, counter-attack losses included only when the defender loses. The annotated rules say leading artillery takes a loss only when nothing else in its position is left to take it. The rulebook's own wording puts the leading artillery among the "other defending units" the defender chooses from, its Attack Example 3 says the defender "does not have to" put the loss on it, and the forum's later consensus is that the choice is free; the implementation follows those and not the annotation.
- **Defeated attackers.** The attacking pieces fall back where they came from and every attacking corps keeps one unit, of its owner's choice; the rest are detached.
- **Advance after a feint or a held defence.** At least one named defender in reserve steps up to block the approach. The fixed battery never advances. A commander goes forward when every unit standing with it advances and may stay only with a unit that stays, so a corps is never left as a commander alone; a unit that advances without its commander is detached. When only one unit can step up and nothing is left to choose (it is detached, or it is the last unit standing with its commander, who goes with it), the advance is made at once without asking the defender.
- **Retreats.** Every piece in the locale retreats and is shown. Artillery is eliminated. Reserve infantry not named in the attack loses one step between them, two across a wide approach; the foot and horse blocking each other approach lose one or two by that approach's width. A loss is capped at the strength present. Each corps keeps one unit and the others are detached; units go to any adjacent locales with room that are not enemy-occupied and not the attack locale, and units with nowhere to go are eliminated. A retreat before combat is a retreat in full.
- **Elite commitment.** Heavy cavalry and the Guard cost their army morale the first time one of them leads, counter-attacks, retreats or takes a loss. Showing a Guard unit to announce a Guard Attack does not by itself commit the Guard. Of two units named to lead the defence of a wide approach, the one a narrow attack passes by is "no longer considered a leading unit" (rule 11, step 5): it was shown with the other at step 4, but it is not committed. Commitment cannot take morale below 1; a defeated Guard Attack costs three more and can. Each commitment, and the defeat of a Guard Attack, is recorded on the action that caused it with the morale the army really lost, which is less than the rule's figure when the army is near its last point.
- **Reserves closed by a march.** A corps of two or more units closes every reserve it marches into by road to "other units" for the rest of the turn (rule 10). The units that made the march are not other units: they may go back into a reserve their own march closed, together or one of them by a Unit Move, which only a French reinforcement corps can do, on the second command of its turn of entry. A unit that joined the corps afterwards, or stayed behind when it marched, is another unit.
- **Connected roads through the turn.** "In a single turn, a piece can only move along connected roads" (rule 10) holds across both commands of a French reinforcement on its turn of entry: its second move by road, a road attack and any riding on included, leaves the locale only by a road connected to the one that brought it there. A piece that began the turn in the locale takes any of its roads, and so do pieces moving together when none of them came by road. Which entry road a reinforcement comes on by therefore matters where several meet in one locale, as three do at locale 89.
- **Stop rule.** An enemy corps of two or more units in any locale sharing an approach with the locale entered, impassable approaches included, stops a corps of two or more units moving by road.
- **Objectives.** A locale is held by a corps with at least one infantry or artillery unit that can trace connected road back to a main-road entry of its own side; any enemy piece in the entry locale or on the way breaks the path.
- **After the game.** Every block is turned face-up for both players as soon as the game is decided (rule 16: "Units are turned face-up after the game for verification purposes").
- **Night.** At nightfall each army regains half the morale it has lost, to a limit of four. There are no attacks in the night round.
- **The Santon (optional).** While the fixed battery stands in the Santon's reserve, French pieces defending from that reserve get the printed approach penalties and nothing else: no infantry −1 and no automatic tie.
- **Morale auction (optional).** Players bid morale in turn, the first bidder drawn at random, or position zero when starting positions are assigned (the assignment never takes the auction's place); a pass gives the other player the choice of army, and the bid comes off the starting morale of the army chosen. The rules set no ceiling, so any bid is taken that would leave the larger army with a point of morale, and the winner may only choose an army the bid leaves with at least one.

## Left out

- **Team play (rule 19).** The game is for two players.
- **Combining two Corps Moves in one road attack.** The rules do not settle whether it is allowed; a road attack is made by a single command.
- **A morale handicap by agreement (rule 17).** Only the morale auction is offered.
