# Magna Grecia UI interaction visual contract

## Visual intents

- **Turn phases.** The action panel lays the turn out left to right as three labelled phases joined by drawn chevrons, with every action button the same height: "Two actions, or one enhanced ⁺ⁿ" (Roads, Cities, then Resupply), "Market" (Build market, Sell market) and "Finish" (End turn). Each button appears only while it has a legal target or allowance. A phase with nothing left is greyed and says why: the tile phase says Done (naming an enhanced action when one was taken), Skipped (a market action closed it first) or None available, and the market phase says Done or None available. Choosing a market tool while tile actions remain greys the tile phase to the status it will have once the market action is taken, with no separate hint.
- **Enhanced allowance.** Roads, Cities and Resupply buttons lead with the same icon as the round tab, then the label, then the count within the card's basic value in the tab's bold serif and, while the enhanced step is still open, a superscript "+n" for the extra that only an enhanced (sole) action allows. Build market and Sell market lead with the market icon, and Sell market adds a coin. On phone widths (under 640px) the button labels are visually hidden (kept for screen readers), except that the market buttons read "Buy" and "Sell", and the extra shows as a "(+n)" centred on the count instead of a superscript, with tighter button padding so Roads, Cities and Resupply fit on one line. Phone widths also hide the hint line, except the red "This cannot be undone." under an End turn confirmation, and shorten the Cities and Roads prompts to "Found or expand a city" and "Place a road tile" by hiding their detail. No text repeats what the buttons show: the count and superscript extra on each button, read with the phase label "Two actions, or one enhanced ⁺ⁿ" (its "+n" in the buttons' bold-serif superscript, as a key), already give both limits, so a chosen Roads, Cities or Resupply mode adds no hint row. The only tool hint is the one fact the buttons cannot show: when the basic allowance is used up and one more tile would make the action enhanced, "One more city tile (road) makes this your enhanced action and ends your tile actions". The resupply confirm button reads "Resupply n", and "Resupply n⁺" (a superscript +) once n passes the basic value. There is no star and no colour code; the extras stay in the text colour.
- **End turn.** A market action no longer ends the turn. When End turn is the only thing left, the prompt says the turn is complete and the End turn button turns solid gold with a slow glow (no glow under reduced motion). The End turn button's tooltip says what ending the turn does (starts the next round and reveals a new action card; starts the final round; or ends the game). When that cannot be undone (revealing the next card, or ending the game) End turn does not act at once: the prompt becomes "End turn and start the next round?" (or "End turn and end the game?"), a red hint says "This cannot be undone.", and the End turn button is replaced by a solid red "Yes, end turn" and a "Cancel". Cancel, Undo or choosing any earlier action backs out without ending the turn. There is no standing warning before the click. When ending the turn only starts the final round (the next card is already known), the hint line notes it and End turn acts at once.
- **Tool targeting.** On the acting player's turn the action panel offers Roads, Cities, Build market and Sell market for whichever have a legal target, plus Resupply and End turn. No tool is preselected at the start of a turn: the prompt asks the player to choose an action and no board space pulses until they pick one. The active tool alone decides which board spaces pulse as targets; every other space stays inert. Targets are focusable and activate with Enter or Space.
- **City placement.** City targets are filled a pale cream so they stand out from the land and show a brown outline of the city tile's temple and houses, drawn at tile size in the same colour as the road target arc; hovering one previews a ghost city tile in the player's colour: a founding tile with its temple where the placement founds a new city, and otherwise a tile whose houses sit on the terraces of the city it joins (`ghostCityLayout`, from the joined city's founding tile). Spaces that commit the player to further tiles use a fainter, dotted outline: a space beside a village (the next tile must cover it) and a plain space that founds a city which must reach a village this turn.
- **Pending village claim.** After a tile is placed beside a village, only that village is targeted, the tool buttons disappear, and the prompt asks for the village tile.
- **Pending founding.** After founding on a plain space, only spaces that extend the new city toward a village it can legally cover are targeted, the tool buttons disappear, and the prompt asks the player to keep building until the city covers a village.
- **Road targets.** Road targets use the same pale cream fill and carry a thick, wide arc glyph.
- **Road tile laying.** Choosing a road space opens the tile laying widget, modelled on the 18xx track picker. The legal tile shapes (straight, curved) fan out in an arc beside the space, above it unless the board edge forces another side. Choosing a shape moves it onto the space as a full-size preview in its first legal orientation; a single legal shape is auto-selected. Clicking the preview (or its rotate badge) cycles that shape's legal orientations. A red ✕ and green ✓ (28-unit radius, 1.75× the original size) sit on the side of the space away from the shape arc, below it unless the arc or the board edge is there: ✕ cancels, ✓ places the road. Nothing is committed until ✓.
- **Market targeting.** Build targets show a price tag with the cost (−n) above the place. Sell targets show a price tag with the value (+n) above the tile of each sellable market, one per market, so a player with two markets in one merged city chooses which one to sell.
- **Resupply.** The Resupply button toggles an inline picker bounded by the allowance and the staging area.
- **Oracle history rows.** A road or city action that turns an oracle adds "— an oracle turns to <player>'s city" to its history row, naming the player recorded in the action's metadata when the oracle turned, so the name stays after that city merges into another.

## Coexistence and precedence

- A pending claim or pending founding overrides the chosen tool: the City tool is forced and only the city tiles that complete it are targetable.
- The tile laying widget exists only while the Road tool is active; choosing another tool, ✕, Undo, or any published state closes it. Choosing a different road space moves the widget there and forgets the previous shape and orientation.
- Choosing a mode hides the tile actions it rules out: while the resupply picker is open only Resupply remains in the tile phase, and while Build market or Sell market is chosen the tile phase greys out with its status in place of the buttons, as it will read once the market action is taken (Done after a tile action, otherwise Skipped). Undo deselects the mode and the buttons return.
- While a city flow plays or pieces settle (`boardAnimating`) the target layer is unmounted, so no target, ghost city or tile laying widget sits over the pieces moving; it remounts with no hover once they settle.
- The resupply picker is its own mode: opening it deselects the Roads and Cities tools, closes any road being laid and hides every board target. Choosing a tool closes the picker; closing the picker (its button again, or Undo) restores the previously chosen tool. The picker closes when a new state is published.

## Render ownership

- **Targets, ghost city and tile laying widget.** The target layer owns every pulsing target, the ghost city preview and the tile laying widget. It renders above the pieces layer so previews cover placed tiles, and it is the only layer with hit targets on the board.
- **City flow.** A city tile poured into place is owned by `CityFlowAnimator` (`animators/cityFlowAnimator.ts`) on the shared `AnimationContext`, from the board diff (`utils/cityFlow.ts`), never from the action or transient UI state. While it plays, `CityFlowArt` draws the involved cities as one piece with `CityArt`'s parts, and its last frame matches `CityArt` exactly so the swap does not show. With an action (own, other players' and full-action replay) the tile pours out of the edges it shares with the player's own cities in a bulging front with a springy settle (500ms); a founding tile spreads from its centre as a round drop and its temple rises; when the tile merges cities each run of shared edges pours its own lobe, they meet along seams through the tile's centre that dissolve, and the absorbed city's temple sinks; houses that appear or go then ripple out from the surviving temple (about 0.9–1.15s in all). An opponent's neighbouring city is open land and keeps its border. Without an action (Undo, history steps) the same shapes play in at most 200ms, backwards when a tile is removed. A transition that changes more than one city tile swaps without a flow.
- **Roads, markets and oracles.** `PiecesAnimator` (`animators/piecesAnimator.ts`) owns their motion on the shared `AnimationContext`, from the board diff (`utils/pieceChanges.ts`), writing straight to the nodes the pieces layer registers. With an action: a placed road drops in (240ms); a built market rises out of the ground, its top climbing from the ground line with a small bounce (450ms), and a founding market waits for its city tile to settle first; selling stamps the X on (300ms); markets that wake rise to full height and markets that go quiet sink to the low disc (350ms); an oracle whose favour changes turns its precinct the short way to its new city while every tone blends from marble (or the old player's colour) to the new one, and when favour first arrives the teardrop's tail runs out from the round precinct to the road (and back when it goes) (650ms). Consequences (oracles, markets waking or going quiet) start once the piece that caused them has arrived: 0.45s into a city placement, 0.2s into a road. Without an action the same changes play in at most 200ms, and undone roads and markets fade or sink away. `MarketArt` always draws the sold X (hidden unless sold) so it can be stamped on before the sold state arrives.
- **Tile laying preview.** The widget draws the chosen shape full size on the space with a rotate badge when more than one orientation is legal; the chosen shape leaves the arc while it is on the space.
- **Cities (terraced polis).** `CityArt` draws each city as one piece from `cityLayout` (`utils/cityLayout.ts`), not tile by tile. Its tiles are a flat field of the player colour clipped to the city's hexes; along the outer edges only (none between the city's own tiles) a band in the colour darkened 22% toward warm dark `#2a1a0a` shows 4 units deep and a near-black (`#1d1a17`) 1.2 outline closes it. Inside, low-contrast house roofs (the colour lightened 16% toward `#fffaf0`, the lower half 7%, with a shadow darkened 16%) stand on oval terraces around the founding temple, spaced 12.5 apart from radius 25, broken by gaps, lanes and missing houses chosen by a deterministic hash, and kept 6.5 clear of the outer edges and clear of the temple. Because the terraces are centred on the founding tile in board coordinates, the houses run on across tiles and do not move as the city grows. The founding tile (the city's first space) carries a front-view temple (`TempleArt`: steps, six columns and a pediment in tints of the colour) 10 units above its centre. A merged city keeps only the surviving city's founding tile, so the absorbed city's temple disappears and its houses follow the survivor's terraces. The Cities button, round tabs, resupply picker and player card use a small hex in the player colour with the temple (`CityTileIcon`); the legend's City entry is a one-tile city.
- **Roads.** `RoadTileArt` draws the road as a 12-wide band in the player colour over a dark casing (`#2a1a0a`, 14.6 wide) and a soft shadow, so every player colour, yellow included, reads on the gold road tile. Every road leaves its hex edge head-on, at the edge's middle (curves bend through the tile centre; straight tiles are a gentle S that crosses the centre a little turned), so roads meet each other, a city's edge and an oracle's tail square, band to band. The light centre dashes (2 every 7) are spaced so each road starts and ends halfway through a gap, and run on evenly across tiles and into an oracle's tail.
- **Oracle sanctuary.** The pieces layer owns it. Each oracle keeps its opaque sand tile (`#ecdcae`, brown edge and dashed inner trim) so no village art shows beneath. On it lies a precinct drawn like a city field: a disc of radius 25 with a darker band inside its edge and a road-dark (`#2a1a0a`) outline. Unfavoured, the precinct is round white marble (`#fbf8f1`). Favoured, it takes the favoured player's colour and becomes a teardrop: its sides run tangent off the disc to the middle of the hex edge it points through, ending as wide as a road (band 12 inside 1.3 dark edges) with the road's dashed centre line, so the road that comes in there carries straight on into it. It points along the oracle's connection to the favoured city (`oracleViews`), not from any transient UI state. Upright on the precinct stands a small round temple (tholos: steps, four columns, a dome and finial, 88% size) drawn in the city temple's manner, as tints of the precinct colour with no ink; favoured, its tints are lifted toward white so it reads on every player colour. The legend's oracle is the unfavoured sanctuary. Colours and the drop's shape come from `oracleLook`, `oracleTone` and `oracleDrop` in `OracleArt`, which the animator shares.
- **Action card.** Below the turn header, the action panel (or the end-of-game panel) sits on a centred, rounded, lighter card with a soft shadow, floating above the page and set apart from the board and its round tab by a gap. When the viewer cannot act (another player's turn, or viewing history), the card shows the last action (or "Waiting for turn" / "Viewing history") and below it the current round's actions at text scale, drawn with the same `AllowanceList` as the tabs, so a waiting player can plan; on phone widths their extras show as "(+n)" like the phone buttons. Off phones the card spans the game column and never shrinks (`ActionCard`): it starts tall enough for the resupply picker (147px) and keeps the tallest height its content has needed, easing over 220ms if something ever needs more, so choosing a tool, opening a picker or confirming End turn never resizes the card or rescales the board below it; content sits centred at its top. A new column width (a window resize) starts the reservation over. On phones the board is sized by the screen width, so the card follows its content height there. The resupply confirm button has a fixed minimum width for its longest label, so the label growing to "Resupply n⁺" never moves the − and + buttons. Newly shown buttons, statuses, hints and the resupply picker fade up over 200ms; nothing animates out. Both are local presentation outside `AnimationContext`: they settle the control panel after selection or state changes, explain no game action, gate no interaction, and are off under reduced motion.
- **Phone layout.** On phone widths (under 640px) the turn header is 30px tall with 14px text, a smaller Undo button and no rule beneath it. The turn header, action card and Next strip have no side margins of their own, so they line up with the board frame across the game column; the header's text keeps a 4px inset. The board area always keeps at least 40% of the viewport height (`40dvh`); when the controls above need more room the game column scrolls instead of shrinking the board further.
- **Full screen map.** The board's zoom controls include the shared full screen button (also the F key; Escape or the button again leaves). Full screen is the shared `ScalingWrapper` modal dialog, so the turn header (with UNDO) and the action card are docked across its top on the page background, and the board fills the rest; a player can take a whole turn without leaving it. The players panel, history and chat stay on the page behind it.
- **Phone turn steps.** On phone widths the action card fills the available width (only its height animates) and shows one step at a time instead of all three phases: the tile step (tile buttons and a small "Skip ›" button beneath) while tile actions are open and not skipped; the market step (market buttons and End turn on one row, wrapping only if the screen is too narrow; no chevrons and no Market or Finish titles) after Skip, once tile actions are finished, or while a market tool is chosen; and only End turn once it is the only option. Skip is a manual turn-draft stage ahead of the tool, so Undo deselects a market tool first and then returns to the tile step; choosing a tile tool clears it and it is not carried into the next published state.
- **Round tab.** "Round X of N", with both numbers in the same weight, sits on a folder tab rising from the top-left of the board frame, followed by the round's player order as squares in each player's colour with their initial, the current player's square outlined. The current round's action counts are not repeated here; they are on the action buttons. The tab shares the frame's background and highlight, so it reads as part of the frame, and its right side curves down into the frame's top edge.
- **Upcoming tab.** A mirrored folder tab rises from the top-right of the board frame with a "Next" label, then the next round's actions (icon, count and superscript "+n") and player order, drawn with the same `AllowanceList` and `SeatSquares` components as the round tab, with the label and player order at the same size, the actions a little smaller, and all of it lightly dimmed (85% opacity) so it reads as solid but not as the current round. It is derived from `upcomingCard()` and is hidden in the final round and after the game ends, when the frame's top-right corner is rounded again. Its content is the shared `UpcomingRound` component. On phone widths (under 640px) the tab is hidden, the frame's top-right corner is rounded, and the same content shows in a full-width strip below the action card, outside the scaled board (`UpcomingStrip`): undimmed, a small "NEXT" header label above one row with the actions on the left and the player squares on the right with no divider, the extra shown as "(+n)" centred on each count as on the phone buttons.
- **Market state.** Markets are wooden cylinders (`MarketArt`) in the player colour, shaded left to right (darkened 18%, lightened 28%, the colour, darkened 38%) under a top lightened 16% with a rim lightened 40%. A tall cylinder is active (it scores its place's connections). A low disc of the same colour, half the height, is inactive: it no longer sits in, or is directly connected to, one of its owner's cities and scores nothing until it is again. A sold market is a tall cylinder in its full colour crossed by a small cream-white (`#fffaf0`) X with a dark outline, about half the piece's width. Each piece gets a thin dark outline and a drop shadow from the board's `mg-piece-outline` filter. Each market stands in the upper part of the lower half of the tile it was placed on and stays there when places merge; markets sharing a tile take separate slots. Activity comes from `isMarketActive` through `marketViews`, never from transient UI state. The market icon is the active cylinder.
- **Legend.** A compact single-column legend card floats over the sea in the board's bottom-right corner (there is no side panel), showing a starting (frontier) village, a village, an undeveloped space, a road, a city, an oracle (its unfavoured white temple), and active, inactive and sold markets, drawn with the board's own art in the viewer's colour (terracotta for spectators).
- **Player panel score.** The coloured banner carries the name, its capitals optically centred in the banner, and, on the right, a cream pill with the coin icon and the points to spend. Below it the road tiles, city tiles (each supply with the staging area in parentheses) and markets left are the card's main row, with large icons and numbers, since players watch them during play. The end-game score is a recessed footer under them: a faintly darker band with a hairline above, in smaller, muted text and icons, starting with a small-capitals "Score" label, then the coin icon and spendable points, the market icon and points from markets, the oracle icon and points from oracles (count × points in its tooltip), and "Total". There is no caption; only the Total's tooltip says it is the score if the game ended now.
- **Player colours.** Red `#c8461f`, yellow `#f5e04a`, a dark slate gray `#686d73` and a sky blue `#4a94d0`. Their lightness is spread (yellow lightest, then blue, red and gray) so all four stay apart on the land and for colour-blind players; the dark gray and the light blue cannot be confused. Red and gray carry white text, yellow and blue dark text.

## Verification scenarios

Scenarios 1, 4–9 and confirming a resupply were exercised manually in the single-game harness. Scenario 2, the mode and Undo paths of scenario 3, the empty tool selection at the start of a turn, the enhanced chip and hint of scenario 11, the market, End turn and round-end confirmation paths of scenario 10, and full screen are automated in `tests/turnControls.spec.ts`.

1. **Claim start and Undo.**
    - Start: a new 4-player game.
    - Input: choose Cities.
    - Expected: the 10 frontier villages are solid targets and the plain spaces beside them are dotted. Clicking a dotted space leaves only its village targeted, shows the claim prompt and hides the tool buttons.
    - Cancel: Undo removes the tile and restores the tool buttons and all city targets.
2. **Road tile laying.**
    - Start: the player has a frontier city.
    - Input: choose Roads, then a space where both shapes are legal.
    - Expected: straight and curved tiles fan out beside the space. Choosing curved moves it onto the space; clicking it steps through its legal orientations; ✓ places the road and closes the widget.
    - Cancel: ✕, or Undo with no shape chosen, closes the widget without placing anything; Undo with a shape chosen returns to the arc.
    - Replacement: choosing another tool or another road space while the widget is open closes it or moves it there.
    - Publish: the state published by ✓ closes the widget.
3. **Resupply picker.**
    - Input: open Resupply, step roads and cities, then confirm.
    - Expected: supply and staging counts update and the picker closes.
    - Cancel: Undo closes the picker without moving tiles.
    - Mode: while the picker is open no tool button is highlighted and no board space pulses; choosing Roads or Cities closes it, and closing it with its button or Undo restores the previously chosen tool.
4. **Market targets.**
    - Input: choose Build market.
    - Expected: price tags appear on eligible villages and rival cities, and clicking one builds the market, keeps the turn open with only End turn left (highlighted) and marks the tile phase Skipped if no tile action was taken.
    - Merge: a market built on a village or city tile stays on that tile when the place is covered or merged into a larger city.
5. **History navigation.**
    - Start: the Roads tool is active.
    - Input: step back in history.
    - Expected: no tool buttons, targets or ring are shown.
    - Returning to Live View restores the targets of the chosen tool.
6. **Game end.**
    - Input: play to the end.
    - Expected: the end panel lists winners with points, markets, oracles and totals, and no targets remain.
7. **Pending founding.**
    - Start: a new game with a card that allows at least two cities.
    - Input: choose Cities and click a dotted plain space that founds a city away from a village.
    - Expected: only spaces that extend the new city toward a village it can legally cover are targeted, the tool buttons disappear and the prompt asks the player to keep building; covering the village places the founding market and restores the tool buttons.
8. **Sell market.**
    - Start: the player owns an active market.
    - Input: choose Sell market.
    - Expected: a +n price tag appears on the tile of each of the player's active markets, including a market worth 0; clicking one lays that market on its side and leaves only the highlighted End turn.
9. **Silent history replay.**
    - Start: a game at least one round past a round whose card changed the turn order.
    - Input: click an action from the earlier round in the history list to replay it.
    - Expected: the player list switches order instantly when the replay starts and ends; it only slides when a live or stepped state change reorders it.
10. **Turn phases and End turn.**
    - Start: the first turn of a new game.
    - Input: choose Build market and click a price tag.
    - Expected: the market is built, the turn stays with the same player, the tile phase reads Skipped, no Roads, Cities, Resupply or market buttons remain and End turn is solid gold.
    - Round end: on the last turn of a round no warning shows beforehand. Clicking End turn asks "End turn and start the next round?" with the red "This cannot be undone." and offers "Yes, end turn" and "Cancel"; Cancel and Undo each return to the normal panel with the turn unchanged, and "Yes, end turn" starts the next round.
11. **Enhanced allowance.**
    - Start: the first turn of a new game.
    - Expected: Cities, Roads (once reachable) and Resupply show the basic count and a superscript "+1" (or "+n"); choosing Cities adds no hint row, since the button and phase label already give both limits.
    - Input: place cities past the basic value.
    - Expected: Roads and Resupply disappear and the tile phase reads "Done: enhanced cities".
12. **City flow.**
    - Start: a new game, the City tool chosen.
    - Input: found a city, expand it, then Undo.
    - Expected: the founding tile spreads from a drop into its hex and the temple rises; the expansion pours from the shared edge and settles; Undo drains the tile back within 200ms. No target or ghost shows while a flow plays, and the settled city matches the static art with no flash at the swap. Plan and timing cases (merge, opponent neighbour, reverse, multi-tile) are covered by `utils/cityFlow.spec.ts`.
13. **Roads, markets and oracles.**
    - Start: a new game; found a city whose founding market is placed.
    - Input: lay roads that connect the city to an oracle, sell the founding market, then Undo each step.
    - Expected: the founding market rises after the tile settles; each road drops in and only then does the oracle turn toward the city and take its colour; selling stamps the X on; Undo reverses each within 200ms. Change detection is covered by `utils/pieceChanges.spec.ts`.
