# MarraCash UI interaction visual contract

Covers the board highlights and previews used while a player takes their turn. Selections live in `MarracashGameSession`; the board only renders them.

### Touch screens

A finger has no hover, so hover highlights become taps. Each tap is judged by its own pointer type, so a touchscreen laptop gets taps from a finger and hover from its mouse.

- Tapping a customer count toggles its board highlight; tapping it again, or another one, replaces or clears it. Pointer hover is ignored for touch input. It is also a button that Enter or Space toggles. The shared `hoverOrTap` action owns this hover, tap and key handling.
- A History turn card has no tap highlight: a tap, like a click, replays the turn.
- Every selectable fountain is tappable across its whole cell. The table is laid out in its own units and shown up to 1.5 times that size, both when it rests at a fit on a large screen and when zoomed in; it is always drawn at the scale it is shown (the scaling wrapper's `renderAtViewScale`), so a phone never draws it larger than its screen needs. When a contain fit would draw the board below 45% scale, as on phones and portrait tablets, the board rests at a cover fit and pans; the zoom buttons sit at its bottom-left, in a strip kept clear below the bottom row of the queue, so the board starts right under the action panel. The header's **?** and **Undo** each fill the header's 36 px height, so they are full-height tap targets.

## Coexistence and precedence

| Intent A               | Intent B                                                        | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Choose a fountain      | Choose a shop                                                   | Coexist until a fountain is selected. Selecting a fountain removes the shop highlights, because an auction can't follow choosing a move.                                                        |
| Choose a destination   | Choose a fountain                                               | Exclusive: selecting a fountain removes the other movable fountains' halos under the overlay. Undo returns to choosing an action, where any fountain or shop can be chosen.                     |
| Preview a route        | Choose a destination                                            | The preview only exists while its route starts at the selected fountain.                                                                                                                        |
| Visitors walk          | Choose a destination                                            | Exclusive: choices are withheld while the session is busy, so the overlay, halos and preview are gone before the first pawn leaves and nothing can be chosen until the walk ends.               |
| Visitors walk          | History navigation                                              | History controls ignore input while an action is being applied or a history step is animating, so a walk always finishes and the view settles on the moved state; later steps start from there. |
| Trace a customer count | Spotlight the auction, Choose a destination, Choose an entrance | Coexist: every matching chip keeps its halo. Chips sit on the shop signs, which stay above the overlay, so every haloed chip shows at full strength.                                            |

During bidding, while waiting for others, and in History View, the session reports no available actions, so no intent shows and any staged choice is hidden. The auction spotlight comes from game state, not available actions, so it shows to every player during bidding, including in History View. A history step lands after a player action and before the system actions it triggers, so the step after an auction's last sealed bid shows the spotlight with every bidder's ✓ in the action panel.

## Shared visual state

- **Staged selection** (`fountain`, `destination`, `shop`, `refill` in the session):
    - Meaning: the player's manual choices before an action is committed. Only the moving fountain and the refill choice are staged; a destination or a shop acts at once.
    - Producers and consumers: board clicks and taps and the refill panel set it. Board highlights and the panels read it.
    - Lifetime: cleared by `resetAction()` before each new state is published, including each History View step. Undo removes the latest staged entry. `refill` holds the queue end and count together as one entry, so a queue pawn, which sets both, is undone by one Undo.
    - Validity: each derived value is undefined unless its action is currently available, so stale choices never render.
    - Eligibility: while the session is busy (an action is being applied or a state change animates), staged values derive as undefined, no fountain, shop, entrance or queue pawn is offered, and the staging methods ignore input, so nothing chosen mid-transition is silently cleared by `resetAction()`.
    - A single legal visitor count is derived, never stored, so Undo never consumes it.
- **Hovered route** (`Board`):
    - Meaning: the route to the destination fountain under the pointer or focus.
    - Producer: the destination `FountainSpot`s. Consumer: `RoutePreview`.
    - Lifetime: cleared on pointer leave or blur, and reset whenever the selected fountain changes or a new state starts publishing, so choosing a destination (whose spot unmounts without a pointer leave) never leaves a stale preview.
    - Validity: derived empty while a new state is publishing, or when the route doesn't start at the selected fountain.

- **Final round banner** (`finalTurnPlayerId` in the session):
    - Meaning: the queue has run out, so the game ends after the last seat in turn order finishes this round.
    - Producer: the game state's `finalTurnPlayerId()`, from `finalRound`, the end-of-game state and the turn order. Consumer: `FinalRoundBanner`, always first in the action panel.
    - Lifetime: from the moment the queue empties until the game ends; it names the last player ("The game ends after Bashir's turn."), or says "This is the last turn of the game." during that player's turn.

- **Outcome line** (`outcomes` in the session):
    - Meaning: who won a shop at auction, or completed an antique set, in the latest player action and the system actions that followed it. The action panel shows only instructions to the current player, led by this one short sentence ("Bashir won the red shop.", "Amira completed their antique set.") on the same line as the prompt. Payments, bids and move details are left to History.
    - Producer: derived from the visible actions by `moneyReport.ts` (`isOutcomeReport`). Consumer: `Outcomes`, rendered at the start of the active panel's prompt.
    - Lifetime: replaced by the next player action. An antique set appears with the step that ends the turn or starts an auction.
    - Validity: derived from the visible context, so History View shows the outcome of the step being viewed.
- **Visitor move presentation** (`movingVisitors`, `fountainVisitorOverrides`, `shopCustomerOverrides` in the session):
    - Meaning: the pawns in motion during a move, an undo or a history step, and the fountain crowds and shop customer counts as they stand mid-animation.
    - Producer: `VisitorMoveAnimator`. Consumers: `Board` draws the moving pawns and renders fountains and shops from the session's `visibleFountains` and `visibleShops`.
    - Lifetime: the moving pawns last for one animated action and are cleared after its animations; the count overrides last until `beforeNewState()` clears them as the new state publishes, so counts never fall back to the old state between the animation and the publish.

- **History highlight** (`historyHighlight`, derived from the highlighted action, and `highlightedHistoryActionId` in the session):
    - Meaning: where the highlighted History entry took place.
    - Producer: the lines of `HistoryTurnCard`: a mouse or pen shows a line's action while over it; touch input never sets it. Consumer: `HistoryHighlight` on the board.
    - Lifetime: ends when the pointer leaves the line, when the History tab unmounts, and whenever a new visible state publishes (a History View step, Undo or a live state), so it never outlives the board it described.
    - Validity: drawn on the board being viewed, which in History View is the viewed step.

- **Customer highlight** (`customerHighlight` in the session):
    - Meaning: the player and market colour whose customer count is highlighted.
    - Producer: the slots of `PlayerState`'s `CustomerCounter` through `hoverOrTap`, as for the history highlight. Consumer: `ShopTile`, through `ShopSign`, haloes the matching `PawnCountChip`s.
    - Lifetime: a hover highlight ends when the pointer leaves the slot; a toggled one stays until toggled again or another slot takes its place. Either ends when the Players tab unmounts. It survives History View steps and new states.
    - Validity: it names a player and colour, not shops, so it haloes the matching chips of whatever state is shown, including each History View step.

- **Draft bid** (`draftBid` in the session):
    - Meaning: the amount the seated player has dialled up in the bid panel but not yet placed.
    - Producer: `BidPanel`'s − and + buttons. Consumer: every mounted `BidPanel`, so the table's panel and the full-screen panel always show the same amount.
    - Lifetime: replaced by the next − or + press.
    - Validity: stored with its auction and bidder, and read as undefined (the minimum bid) once either no longer matches, so a new auction or the next hotseat bidder starts from the minimum while other players' bids arriving mid-draft leave it alone. It is always clamped to the current minimum and the bidder's cash.

- **Player aid** (`playerAidOpen` in the session):
    - Meaning: the player aid cards are open over the board.
    - Producer: the header's **?** toggles it; Escape or a click outside the cards closes it. Consumers: `GameTable` lays `PlayerAid` over the board area, inside the full-screen view while it is open, and the **?** shows its open state. The cards centre in that area when they fit and otherwise scroll from the first card, so none is cut off on a phone. While open, the aid holds keyboard focus (returned to whatever opened it on close), the covered board is inert so Tab can't reach its shops, and Escape inside it closes the aid without leaving full screen.
    - Lifetime: stays open across state changes and History View steps until closed.

- **Short-queue warning** (`showQueueTooShort` in the session):
    - Meaning: the last queue pawn clicked can't be read as one end and count.
    - Producer: `VisitorQueue`. Consumer: `RefillPanel`.
    - Lifetime: a few seconds, or until a pawn choice is made, a new state publishes, or refilling ends.

## Render ownership

- `Board` owns the layer order, in one SVG, so the board is a single bitmap; a second board-sized composited layer cost as much memory as the board itself and, with the board drawn without filters, saved no measurable repaint time. The **ground** comes first and never changes with play: the city (`CityWall`, the packed-earth street, `StreetWear`, `CityGates`), palms, every shop's stall and every fountain's basin, with no controls, halos, signs or visitors. Everything that changes is drawn **above** it, ignoring the pointer apart from its controls, each block that mounts or unmounts pieces in a group of its own. Every fountain is drawn twice, its visitors in place and its whole spot raised above the overlay, and raising it (selected, a destination, an entrance to refill, or any fountain during an auction) only swaps which copy is visible: in WebKit mounting a basin into the board made choosing a fountain about twice as slow (`tests/boardMounts.spec.ts`). Above the ground come the halos and controls of the shops and fountains that can be chosen, which are invisible targets over the ground beneath; then the shared overlay, which covers only the board and the towers that overhang its outer wall (with their shadows), so the queue margin keeps the page's own background; then the shop signs and fountain visitors, which never dim (during an auction, whole fountains); then whatever is lifted above the overlay (the auctioned or staged shop, the shops the previewed route enters, the selected fountain and its destinations, or the empty entrances awaiting a refill); then the route preview and any history highlight; and the visitor queue in the margin outside the walls. A route line runs from the edge of its origin fountain to the edge of its destination, never from or to a centre. The queue stands on a deep-red carpet runner (`QueueRunner`) that follows it round the margin under the visitors' feet, with fringed ends; its front end reads "FRONT – 55 WAITING" and its back end "BACK". The front is anchored at the top, the runner's front end stopping at the top gate's opening so the count waiting sits beside the gate visitors enter by: visitors fill the path from there at a fixed spacing, and as the queue shortens the back end recedes along the bottom, up the left side and along the top, its label reading upwards on the side with the letters standing on the board's edge. A label never bends round a corner; one that would starts just past it, clear of the runner coming into it. Each visitor keeps one element for the whole game, named by its place in the original queue (`queueShift.ts`), and `QueueAnimator` owns its position. When visitors are taken from the front, they fade out and everyone behind walks forward along the runner, round its corners, to close the gap; taken from the back, they fade out where they stand. Either way, once the line has settled the back label and fringe fade, the runner's back end rolls up behind the new last visitor (the roll thickening as it winds, slowing and swinging round corners, the carpet trimmed along the roll's line), then unrolls a short way, the roll shrinking to reveal the fringe at the new end, and the label fades in. While the line or runner moves, the refill buttons are disabled. Meanwhile the new visitors walk in to their entrance: they appear just outside its gate, fade in as they pass through it, and stroll single file to their final places round the fountain, the places farthest from the gate filled first, so no one passes through a visitor already standing; any visitors already there would first step to their places in the larger crowd. No one moves once placed, and the fountain shows its crowd again only when all have arrived, in the same places. Undo, state-only history and reduced motion place them at once. Undo and state-only history move the visitors still in line straight to their places within 200ms. The runner's shape and labels change with the published state, after the roll. The top row stands a little higher than the side column, so its runner keeps a strip of the table showing between it and the wall's battlements. The margin is wide enough, and the bottom row raised enough, that every visitor stands clear of the wall and its towers and the runner fits the table; with the queue empty there is no runner. Each pawn moving during a move animation is its own small composited element above the whole board, moved by a CSS transform and mounted only while the move animates, so a walk repaints nothing and costs no board-sized layer. The overlay blocks clicks on everything beneath it, so only lifted pieces are interactive; the preview and the moving pawns ignore pointer events so they never block a destination.
- The History tab sits on night indigo inside a brass frame and lists the game newest first as one warm-stone card per turn (`HistoryTurnCard`), built by the pure `historyEntries` (`utils/historyTurns.ts`) from the shown actions: a turn runs to its EndTurn, and as seats take turns in a fixed order, the turn's player and round follow from how many turns came before, so a turn with nothing to do still counts. Above each round's turns a brass divider reads "ROUND 3", or "FINAL ROUND" for the latest round once the queue has run out, with even space above and below; "GAME OVER" tops a finished game. A card's header, a shade darker than its body, holds the mover's mini sign (their standee outline and initial, the height of the name and time together), their name in El Messiri with how long ago the turn's latest action was beneath it, and on the right the mover's net income for the turn: green "+N", red "−N" or a muted dash, the sign, name and digits centred on the header's centre line. The body has one line per action in the body font: "Moved [pawns]", then one indented line per shop entered ("2 into Chiara’s [swatch] shop", "1 into your [swatch] shop") with the mover's income from it on the right, the whole income in their own shop or the cut in another's; a refill reads "[pawns] from the **front** entered **north**" (the entrance named by its gate, north, west or south, and the phrase from "entered" kept on one line); an auction reads "Won the [swatch] green shop for 250", or "Amira won …" when someone else won, then each bid beside the bidder's mini sign, highest first in table order, a pass reading "Pass", then any walk-ins and the auctioneer's cut, amounts shown only for the mover; an antique set reads "Completed the 2nd antique set" with its payout, or names its collector. Shops are a small square swatch in the market colour, never an ID. A footer strip, a shade darker again, gives every other player's net income from the turn beside their mini sign and name, and is left out when nobody else earned. The turn still in progress has a dashed brass outline; an auction still open reads "Auctioning the [swatch] red shop" with each bidder's mini sign followed by a green ✓ once they have bid or a muted … while they have not, and "2 of 4 bids"; no view ever sees an open bid's amount there. Numbers on cards are El Messiri, except within sentences. Clicking or tapping a card, or Enter or Space on it, replays the whole turn on the board (`gameSession.history.replayRange` from its first action to its last) and returns to where the viewer was; meanwhile the card has a solid brass outline and the list, including the open auction's bids and who reads as "you", holds still.
- Local presentation, with no shared state: the bid panel's − and + step 25 dirham per press and, held down, repeat after 0.5 s and speed up after eight steps, stopping on release, on leaving the button, or at the minimum bid or the player's cash (keyboard activation steps once); action panel buttons darken slightly on hover, a selected refill choice like a filled button.
- Players never see fountain numbers: fountains carry no number labels, and the history describes moves, refills and walk-ins without naming fountains ("You moved 6 visitors."); the History tab names a refill's entrance by its gate. Screen-reader labels keep the numbers so each fountain stays distinguishable.
- `GameTable` composes `HistoryControls` and `DefaultTabs` itself so they match the player panels: the history controls sit on solid night indigo, framed in brass in the sidebar and as a band with a brass bottom edge above the table on phones, with gold icons and muted ones when disabled; the tabs are low pointed-arch doorways standing on a brass threshold bar, lettered in El Messiri: closed tabs are dark wooden doors with gold lettering, the open tab glows with warm lamplight and dark lettering. The chat sits on the same solid indigo inside a brass frame, with parchment messages and a deep teal composer. Nothing in the sidebar lets the zellige show through, and the tiled teal stays the player panels' own.
- The action panel's body depends on who is looking. A player who is not acting gets `WaitingPanel`: the latest outcome sentence, then what is being waited for, built from public game state alone by `waitingStatus` (Concealed Cash hides other players' cash, so whether they can afford an auction counts only when the viewer may see it): "Chiara is choosing a second action: move visitors or put a shop up for auction.", "Chiara is choosing a shop to put up for auction.", "Chiara is bringing new visitors to an emptied entrance.", or during bidding "Bilal is auctioning the [swatch] yellow shop.". Outside bidding, a smaller line below gives the move or refill that ended the latest step. In History View, `HistoryStepPanel` describes the viewed step's player action; the state shown also holds what the game resolved just before it (an auction or an antique set), which comes first on its own smaller, muted line ("Bashir won the [swatch] green shop for 100."). Automatic passes and ends of turn are not described. While an auction is open, in the bidder's `BidPanel`, the waiting view and History View alike, a centred row below gives each bidder's mini sign followed by a green ✓ once they have bid or a muted … while they have not, then "2 of 4 bids" (`BidSeals`, as on the History tab's open auction). All of these use `ActionDescription`, one sentence in the History tab's words, led by the actor's tag: "put the [swatch] red shop up for auction.", "placed a sealed bid." (the viewer's own bid gives its amount, or "passed."), "won the [swatch] green shop for 250.", "moved [pawns]: 2 into Amira’s [swatch] shop, 1 into their own [swatch] shop.", "brought [pawns] from the front to the north gate.", "completed their 2nd antique set.". It gives no payments or bid amounts, so the panel stays short and the board keeps its size; the History tab keeps the full detail.
- `ActionPanel` is a parchment panel in a brass double frame. Its `Header` overlaps the panel's top edge: on the left a cartouche plaque, a hexagon with notched ends and a brass edge, filled with the colour of the player whose turn it is and lettered in El Messiri with that player's text colour ("Your turn" in the viewer's colour; teal with gold lettering for History and End of game); on the right UNDO and **?** as teal medallions with brass rims, the **?** filled with brass while the aid is open. The plaque and medallions are 34px tall, 30px on phones. Below the text the body leaves as much room as above it, counting from the frame; on phones the body starts a little lower so a name tag in its first line clears the plaque. Prompts set in `marracash-prompt` use El Messiri. Name tags (`PlayerTag`) are always set in El Messiri, whatever the text around them, with their capitals centred in the tag.
- The city is Marrakesh's sunlit salmon-pink rammed earth: the wall is a flat earth walkway with raised battlements along its outer edge and a low parapet on its inner edge, lit from the top-left and casting a soft shadow outward. Flat-topped square bastions, edged with battlements, stand at the four corners, beside each gate and at regular intervals along every wall. The street is pale rose-sand packed earth in one flat colour; `StreetWear` lays the wall's shadow along the street's edges. Gate passages are lightly shaded. The ground stays pale and quiet so the shops, fountains and pawns read clearly on it. The board's depth (battlement and fountain shading, cast and wall shadows) is drawn with plain shapes and gradients, and the board's only SVG filters are glows, each bounded to what it marks: every halo glows softly white round its piece (the `candidate-halo` class), and each route line, in a preview or the history highlight, has a soft glow in its own colour beneath it (`RouteGlow`). Every shadow is an unblurred offset shape with a faint edge, a half-opacity outline in its own colour, so it reads softer without a blur. The ground's noise textures and blurred shadows made each full repaint (zoom, pan, dimming) about twice as slow in WebKit, worst on phones, and the route preview's board-sized line glow cost about 7 ms on every frame of its dash animation. Filters may come back one at a time where they are worth their cost.
- `GameTable` paints the whole game area in night zellige (`tableBackground.ts`): deep indigo under a faint brass eight-point star lattice. It stays dark and quiet so every market colour of the queue pawns standing on it, and the teal player panels, read against it.
- Every shop is a matte market stall in its market colour, either a tent (`TentStall`) or a rug (`RugStall`), fixed per shop in `stalls.ts` so every market colour has both and the board looks the same each game; the kind carries no rule meaning. A tent is striped woven canvas sagging between four poles, with a ridge down its long axis and the far side in shade. A rug is a flat kilim with a diamond field, a woven border and fringed short ends, its edges wandering a little as a hand-woven rug's do, laid square. Both cast an unblurred shadow with a faint edge on the ground; neither has a sheen or highlight. A shop's halo and history highlight follow its own stall's outline.
- Every visitor, on the board, in the queue, on chips and in text, is the original's turned wooden figure in its market colour, one outline for all (`pawnShape.ts`): a low rounded knob on a thick, mushroom-like hat, a short head under the brim, a slight groove, then a rounded bell skirt flaring to a lip just above a wide foot. `PawnFigure` shades it: a highlight on the crown, the brim's shadow on the head, a soft groove, darker sides and foot, and fine creases where the brim meets the head and the skirt meets the foot.
- `ShopTile` draws each owned shop's `ShopSign`: a flat cardboard sign in the owner's colour, cut in a shape unique to their seat, with the customer count beside it. Its thin inner frame and small diamonds are cream, or a dark warm grey on a white sign, where cream would vanish; the same goes for `PlayerSign`. On a horizontal stall the sign and count share a vertical centre and sit centred together across the stall; on a vertical stall the count sits below the sign. That count and the route preview's entering counts use the same `PawnCountChip`: a chip with a pawn and a number on or beside a shop always means visitors of that colour in the shop. Crowded fountains use their own tally panel instead. Up to nine pawns stand in rows filled to a near-square, any short row in front. Each row sits on the half-step between the pawns of the row behind it, full rows going left, right, left, so no pawn ever stands directly behind another, and the whole cluster is centred on the fountain (`fountainPawns.ts`).
- `GameEndPanel` gives the winner line in bold, then one finisher per line as a three-column list: rank, name tag right-aligned, cash left-aligned. Equal cash shares a rank and the next rank skips (1, 1, 3); tied players are listed in seating order.
- `PlayerState` is a tiled panel in deep teal with a brass border, so the five market colours stand out against it rather than against the board's sand. On the left is `PlayerSign`: the same sign the player puts on their shops (`SignFace`, cut in their seat's shape, in their colour with their initial), edged in brass and as tall as the name and customer rows beside it, with a brass "n/6" shop-count badge over its lower edge. On the right are the name and cash, then `CustomerCounter`: a pawn and count per market colour on a darker band, every colour always in the same slot. A colour with no customers shows a dashed hollow pawn and a quiet count instead of fading. Below runs the antiques row: the viewer's own hand with "antiques n/5", a revealed set with its award position and payout ("1st set", "+725 د.م."), or a fan of five card backs with "5 hidden antiques". Turn is marked by a white ring and a glow in the player's colour, both within the padding round the list so the scrolling tab never clips them.
- Money is shown as an amount followed by the Moroccan dirham sign د.م. (`DirhamAmount`), never the word "Dirham"; screen readers hear "dirham". Where an amount ends a sentence, the sign's final dot doubles as the full stop. Player names, cash, card values and other figures on the panel use El Messiri (`marracash-merchant`); the sign uses Noto Naskh Arabic (`marracash-dirham-sign`); the panel's small labels keep the game's serif.
- Each antique card shows its colour's item (yellow signet ring, red brass lantern, blue teapot, purple perfume bottle, green curved dagger) above its value. Items are always drawn in their market colour, with a shaded side and a highlight. In the owner's hand a card not yet covered by a customer of its colour is glazed teal with a dashed border in the market colour; a covered card is parchment with a solid brass border. On the player aid's parchment, an uncovered card is a faint wash instead of teal glaze, its value in dark ink. Hands and revealed sets are shown sorted by market colour in the customer counter's order (red, blue, green, purple, yellow), then by value. Clicking anywhere on the owner's hand toggles a line below it listing, in that order with counts, the colours still missing a customer ("Missing: Red, 2x Purple"), or "Set complete" once every card is covered but the set is not yet paid. It is local to that player's panel, and only the viewer's own hand is ever drawn, so it reveals nothing about other players' cards.
- `PlayerState` shows a revealed antique set face up to every player, with its award position and the amount it paid; cards outside the paid best cards for that position keep their colour and have their value struck through, using the logic's `paidAntiques`.

## Verification scenarios

Scenarios naming a test are checked by the Playwright suite in `tests/`; the rest were checked manually in the dev harness, using scripted browser runs.

1. **Round 2 turn:**
    - At the start, both fountains with visitors and unowned shops are highlighted.
    - Selecting fountain 1 dims the table and leaves only fountain 1 and its destinations 2, 3 and 6 bright; the panel asks for the destination.
    - Hovering fountain 6 previews the route to it.
    - Undo, or clicking fountain 1 again, restores the start-of-turn highlights.
    - Hovering fountain 4 from fountain 8 lifts the yellow and purple shops its visitors would enter, each with a chip counting one visitor.
    - Clicking fountain 6 moves the visitors there.
2. **Entrance refill:**
    - After two moves that empty entrances 1 and 8, the board dims and both empty entrances are highlighted but not yet clickable; the queue stays bright.
    - Choosing the front of the queue and 3 visitors makes both entrances clickable and haloes the three incoming pawns; clicking the fourth pawn from the back instead selects the back and 4, and one Undo clears that choice (`tests/turns.spec.ts`).
    - With 5 or 6 visitors waiting, clicking a middle pawn shows the short-queue warning in the refill panel and changes nothing.
    - Clicking entrance 1 places the visitors and clears the selection.
    - The second refill starts from a fresh selection.
3. **Auction** (`tests/turns.spec.ts`): clicking an unowned shop starts its auction at once and spotlights it until the auction resolves. Undo, before anyone else bids, cancels it; a bidder's Undo takes back their sealed bid.
4. **Outcome line** (`tests/turns.spec.ts`): after the first auction the panel reads "Developer won the yellow shop. Auction an unowned shop." on one line, with no payments or bids; History lists the bids. The next player action replaces it.
5. **Visitor move animation:**
    - From fountain 3 holding blue, red, purple, green and two yellow pawns, with blue, purple, red and yellow shops owned along its routes, moving west to fountain 4 clears the highlights, then walks the pawns single file; purple, red and both yellows turn into their shops, whose chips count up, and blue and green join fountain 4 as fountain 3 empties one pawn at a time.
    - The south and north moves from the same setup branch only the matching shops along those routes.
6. **Undo a move:** Undo straight after that move glides every pawn back to fountain 3 in 200ms, out of the shops and fountain 4, and restores the earlier counts.
7. **Touch move** (phone, `tests/touch.spec.ts`): tapping a destination moves the visitors at once, as a click does; no **Move here** step appears.
8. **Tap toggles** (`tests/touch.spec.ts`): tapping a customer count haloes that player's matching badges and a second tap clears them; mouse hover still shows them only while over it. Tapping a History turn card replays the turn, its card outlined in brass until the replay ends.
9. **Short-queue warning after a choice:** with six visitors waiting, choosing the front two and then clicking a middle pawn shows the warning in place of the entrance prompt, keeps the front-two choice, and the prompt returns a few seconds later.
10. **Final round:** once the queue empties, the banner names the last seat in turn order ("The game ends after Bashir's turn."), and says "This is the last turn of the game." during that seat's turn.
11. **End standings:** a finished game lists one finisher per line; with cash 5500, 5300, 3700, 3700 the ranks read 1, 2, 3, 3, ties in seating order.
12. **Hold to bid:** a tap on + adds 25; holding + from 100 reaches about 350 after two seconds; holding − stops at the minimum bid.
13. **Turns end by themselves** (`tests/turns.spec.ts`, Admin in the harness): a second move that leaves no entrance to refill passes the turn on at once, with no Confirm turn. Undo then returns the mover to the state after their first move.
14. **History navigation** (`tests/history.spec.ts`):
    - With a fountain selected after a move into owned shops, stepping back hides the staged choice and every intent, glides the pawns home within the 200ms budget, and shows the open auction's spotlight, and the action panel describes that step ("Chadia placed a sealed bid.").
    - Stepping forward glides the pawns back out; go to current shows the turn with nothing staged.
    - Play history from that step replays the move as a full walk.
15. **Interrupted motion** (`tests/history.spec.ts`): pressing step backwards during a live walk, or step backwards and go to current during a replayed walk, lets the walk finish; the view settles on the moved state with no pawns left in motion and no errors.
16. **Reduced motion** (`tests/history.spec.ts`): with reduced motion requested, a move glides its pawns within the 200ms budget instead of walking, and the active player's border stops pulsing.
17. **Explore under Concealed Cash** (`tests/protected.spec.ts`): the Explore button is disabled in player and spectator views and enabled in Host View, where exploring starts without errors.
18. **Header** (`tests/turns.spec.ts`): the action panel's header reads "Your turn" or "[Player]'s turn" on the left ("History" in History View, "End of game" once the game has a result) and offers UNDO on the right whenever there is something to undo, followed by the **?** for the player aid; no Back button appears anywhere. The turn plaque takes the colour of the player whose turn it is, the viewer's own for "Your turn", and is teal for History and End of game.
19. **Undo after the turn passes on** (`tests/turns.spec.ts`, Admin in the harness): after the placement that empties the last entrance passes the turn on, Undo returns the previous player to the refill with the visitors back in the queue. In a hosted game that player's own Undo does the same until the next player acts; the harness's local seat is always the active player, so these tests reach it through Admin.
20. **Sealed bid corrections** (`src/actions/auction.spec.ts` in the logic package, `tests/turns.spec.ts`): with four players, A starts an auction and bids 300, B bids 200, C bids 150, A undoes, D passes, C undoes, A bids 100 and C bids 250; the auction stays open until that last bid, then C wins at 250. Each Undo takes back only that player's bid and keeps the others'.
21. **Player aid** (`tests/playerAid.spec.ts`): the **?** opens the Your Turn, Money and Antiques cards over the board; Escape or a click outside closes them; with Antique Cards off only Your Turn and Money appear. On a phone the first card starts at the top of the scrollable area. During an auction the fountains and shop signs are drawn above the dimming overlay.
22. **Full screen** (`tests/playerAid.spec.ts`): entering full screen shows the action panel above the board and the **?** opens the aid there; Escape then closes the aid and stays in full screen. A bid raised on the table carries into full screen and back (`tests/playerAid.spec.ts`).
23. **Aid keyboard focus** (`tests/playerAid.spec.ts`): opening the aid with Enter on the **?** focuses the aid, the board sits under an inert ancestor, and Escape closes it with focus back on the **?**.
24. **Earnings bubbles** (`src/lib/animators/earningsPopups.spec.ts`, `src/lib/utils/shopEarnings.spec.ts`): a move bringing two customers into another player's shop for 500, with a 200 cut, shows the owner +300 then the mover +200, and holds the move's timeline until the last bubble has risen away; reduced motion holds still bubbles for the same time. Checked visually in the dev harness, recorded as GIFs.
25. **Name lists** (manual): "Waiting for" lists player tags separated by spaces, with no commas, and tags have no border. An open auction's bidders show as a row of mini signs with ✓ or …, and the count follows them, centred in the action panel (`tests/turns.spec.ts` checks "0 of 4 bids", "1 of 4 bids" and back after Undo). A tag is only as tall as its line, so tags on wrapped lines keep a gap between them.
26. **History turns** (`src/lib/utils/historyTurns.spec.ts`, `tests/turns.spec.ts`, `tests/protected.spec.ts`): actions group into one card per turn with seats and rounds following turn order, including turns with nothing to do; the mover is credited a shop's whole income in their own shop and only the cut elsewhere, and other players' net income goes to the footer; a refill names its gate; only the latest round is final once the queue runs out. During the first auction the card reads "0 of 4 bids", then "1 of 4 bids" with no amount, in every protected view; once it resolves it reads "Won the … shop for 100" with four bids.
