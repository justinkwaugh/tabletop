# Medina UI interaction visual contract

## Visual intents

- **Choosing a piece.** On the acting player's turn the action panel is an arcade of the pieces they hold, each in a horseshoe arch seen from the front. No piece is chosen at the start of a turn. Choosing one turns its arch turquoise with a dark rim; choosing it again keeps it chosen; Undo takes it back. Pieces do not move on hover or selection: hover darkens the rim and shows a deeper turquoise, on enabled arches only. Arches with no stock or no legal place are dimmed and do not respond.
- **Legal places.** Only the chosen piece puts targets on the board: pale squares on free spaces, on the track for a wall, and on the roof of each building of a palace that can be claimed. Squares and the hover ghost sit at their true depth, so a nearer piece covers them as it would any piece. What can be clicked is an invisible area over everything, so a partly covered place stays reachable. Hovering or focusing a target brightens its square and previews the piece as a ghost; a target activates by click, tap, Enter or Space.
- **Keeping the tool.** A piece chosen for the first placement of a turn stays chosen for the second if it can still be placed, and is then an automatic selection: Undo goes straight to the placement. It never carries into another turn.
- **Scoring a palace.** Hovering any building or stable of a palace with a mouse, or tapping it on a touch screen, fades everything it does not score on and shows a tag with its owner's name and rooftop (or Unclaimed, or Neutral), its value, its size (buildings and stables, which decides the palace tile) and how the value is made up. Tapping elsewhere closes it on touch. A drag that pans the board never opens it, and panning or zooming the board closes it.
- **Shadows.** Pieces cast soft ground shadows to one side in the angled view. Top down has none.
- **Board view.** A Top down button at the top right of the board (the bottom right on phones) switches to the straight-down view and back, saved as the player's preference for the title. Both views play the same; panels and cards keep the front view of the pieces in either.
- **Since your last turn.** Pieces placed since the viewer's last turn are marked in the colour of whoever placed them: a dot on a building or stable, drawn over everything, with a fine dark edge (a firmer one on a rooftop, which can share its colour), a coloured square under a merchant, and the wall itself tinted. The acting player's own placements this turn are marked the same way; a waiting player's finished turn is not. Marks clear at the end of the game. In History View only the piece placed by the step on screen is marked, the same way.
- **Tiles.** Palace and tower tiles stay on the board. A tile held by a player carries a rooftop in that player's colour, not taken from their supply. A palace tile's rooftop also prints the size of the palace holding it, the size to beat; a palace tile held by a neutral palace carries a neutral rooftop with its size, since a larger palace must still beat it. A tower tile held by a neutral palace or nobody carries no rooftop. The player card shows tea tiles. A game without the Tea tiles option never shows the tea stack.
- **End of the game.** Each palace's score is printed on its rooftop. The end panel names players by rooftop and plain name, and says who wins.
- **Told panels.** In History View and for a waiting player the action panel is centred. These panels and the history tab name a player by their rooftop and plain name rather than a colour fill. In the history tab each turn opens with its name on a lighter band, setting it apart from the lines it describes. History tab entries are not clickable; the history controls step through the game, outlined in the same colour as their enabled icons.
- **Player cards.** Each palace colour has a door holding the building and the number in hand below it. The rest of the supply runs along the foot of the card as a darker band, edge to edge, with a divided cell for each kind of piece and its count; tea tiles add a cell only while held. With the Hidden pieces option, another player's card shrinks until the game is played to its end (a Called, Conceded or Stalled game stays hidden): it drops the row under its doors and the stable, merchant and wall cells, leaving rooftops and tea tiles in the band and each claimed colour's door outlined in the player's colour. A claimed colour turns the door's outline to the player's colour and adds a small rooftop, sitting on the number's baseline and no taller than it. A palace's score shows in its board popover and the rooftop's tooltip. The acting player's card reads "placing". A player who takes a tea break reads "tea" from then until their next turn starts, so everyone can see it.
- **Tea break.** The button shows only while it can be pressed: from the start of a two-piece turn until the second piece, for a player holding a tea tile.
- **Corner tiles.** A tower tile uses the printed tower in the palace tiles' engraved treatment, in green, with its number beside it as live type. While it holds merchants the tower and number sit smaller at the top, above a dark tray they stand on so they stay distinct from the tile; once they are taken, the tower fills the tile. The tile beside each tower is its only number.
- **Well bonus.** The spaces one straight space from the well, where a building or stable scores 4, carry a plain "+4" in a deep water colour for the whole game, with no square. It lies on the ground, so a piece placed there covers it.
- **Phones.** In portrait under 640px the city turns sideways on a board with a thin cedar edge. It rests fitted to the width and pans vertically, and zooming fully out shows the whole board. Tiles stay upright around it: a row above holds the top two towers with tea and the palace tiles between them, and the other two towers sit at the bottom corners. The zoom controls stay at the bottom left and the Top down button moves to the bottom right, on a strip of table left clear below the board.

## Coexistence and precedence

- A focused palace and legal targets can coexist: targets stay at full strength over the faded pieces.
- Changing the board view keeps the chosen piece, its targets and any focused palace.
- The ghost follows the target under the pointer among the current targets, and is cleared by placing: it never outlives its placement or appears over a placed piece.
- While a change is being presented (`updatingVisibleState`) targets are unmounted, so none sits over a piece landing.

## Shared visual state

| State             | Meaning                         | Producer                                               | Consumers                 | Lifetime                                                                               | Validity                                            |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| `activeTool`      | The piece being placed          | Arcade, as a staged selection                          | Action panel, targets     | Cleared on each published state; carried as automatic after the player's own placement | Only in its own turn, while stock and places remain |
| `focusedPalaceId` | The palace whose score is shown | Board hover or tap                                     | Board dimming, palace tag | Cleared on each published state                                                        | Read from the shown state                           |
| `pieceState`      | The state the pieces draw       | Game State, set to the target of a change as it starts | Board                     | Follows `gameState` once published                                                     | Always                                              |

## Motion

A placed piece fades in, in `actionTimeline`, for any change that has an Action, including replays, and its shadow fades in with it. A piece the player was previewing firms up from the ghost's opacity with the same sprite, so it does not appear to move or change; any other piece fades in from nothing. Changes with no Action, such as Undo or stepping back, show the target state at once with no motion.
